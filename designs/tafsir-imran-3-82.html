Tafsir Study

The Universal Creed

Tafsir, lessons, and practical applications of Surah Ali 'Imran 3:82

Prepared by Tarbiyyah | Drawing on Tafsir Ibn Kathir & authenticated sources

This verse is the cornerstone of Muslim creed regarding prophethood. It establishes a universal, unbroken chain of divine revelation — from Ibrahim to Muhammad — and uncompromisingly demands that believers accept all prophets without distinction or favouritism. Understanding its tafsir is essential for every Muslim seeking to navigate interfaith discourse, sectarian tensions, and the foundations of aqeedah.

1. The Verse

قُلْ آمَنَّا بِاللَّهِ وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَيْنَا وَمَا أُنزِلَ عَلَىٰ إِبْرَاهِيمَ وَإِسْمَاعِيلَ وَإِسْحَاقَ وَيَعْقُوبَ وَالْأَسْبَاطِ وَمَا أُوتِيَ مُوسَىٰ وَعِيسَىٰ وَالنَّبِيُّونَ مِن رَّبِّهِمْ لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُمْ وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ

Surah Ali 'Imran 3:82

"Say, 'We have believed in Allah and in what was revealed to us and what was revealed to Abraham, Ishmael, Isaac, Jacob, and the Descendants [al-Asbat], and in what was given to Moses and Jesus and to the prophets from their Lord. We make no distinction between any of them, and we are Muslims [in submission] to Him.'"

— Translation: Saheeh International

2. Tafsir Ibn Kathir

2.1 The Universal Creed of Prophethood

Imam Ibn Kathir (may Allah have mercy on him) opens his tafsir of this verse by noting that it is the fundamental creedal statement regarding the unity of Allah's message across time.[1] The command "Say" (قُلْ) is directed at the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ, but the response — "We have believed" — is spoken in the collective voice of the Muslim Ummah. This teaches us that whatever the Messenger of Allah ﷺ believes, the believers also believe. The Prophet does not speak from himself in matters of creed; he speaks what Allah commands, and the believers follow.

Ibn Kathir draws upon the hadith of the Prophet ﷺ narrated by Abu Hurayrah, in which he said:

أَنَا أَوْلَى النَّاسِ بِعِيسَى ابْنِ مَرْيَمَ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالْآخِرَةِ. الأَنْبِيَاءُ إِخْوَةٌ لِعَلاَّتٍ، أُمَّهَاتُهُمْ شَتَّى وَدِينُهُمْ وَاحِدٌ

"I am the nearest of the people to 'Isa ibn Maryam in this world and the Hereafter. The prophets are paternal brothers; their mothers are different, but their religion is one." Narrated by al-Bukhari in Kitab al-Anbiya' [60], Hadith no. 3443; and by Muslim in Kitab al-Iman [1], Hadith no. 236.

Sahih — Muttafaq 'Alayh

This hadith establishes the theological principle that underpins verse 3:82: every prophet received the same essential religion — the religion of Islam (submission to Allah alone) — though the specific laws (shari'ah) varied according to time and circumstance. Imam al-Nawawi (d. 676 H) explains in Sharh Sahih Muslim that the phrase "their religion is one" refers to the unity of tawhid and worship of Allah alone, while their mothers being different refers to the varied communities and legal codes they were sent to govern.[2]

2.2 The Chain of Prophets: From Ibrahim to Muhammad

Ibn Kathir meticulously enumerates the prophets mentioned in this verse, tracing a lineage of divine election:

  • Ibrahim (Abraham) — the patriarch, the father of monotheism, recipient of the scrolls (su-huf). His mention first establishes the Abrahamic foundation of all subsequent revelation.
  • Isma'il (Ishmael) — the son of Ibrahim, from whose lineage the Arabs descend and through whom the lineage of the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ continues.
  • Ishaq (Isaac) — the other son of Ibrahim, from whose lineage the Banu Isra'il (Children of Israel) descend, including Musa and 'Isa.
  • Ya'qub (Jacob) — son of Ishaq, father of the twelve tribes of Israel. His mention bridges the patriarchal era to the prophetic tradition of the Israelites.
  • al-Asbat (the Descendants) — a collective reference to the twelve sons of Ya'qub, representing the twelve tribes of Israel. Ibn Kathir notes that mention of a group after individuals indicates that the divine message was not limited to named prophets but extended to the guided communities they established.
  • Musa (Moses) — the giver of the Torah (Tawrat), the lawgiver, the one who spoke to Allah directly.
  • 'Isa (Jesus) — the giver of the Gospel (Injil), born miraculously, the Messiah.
  • al-Nabiyyun (the prophets) — a comprehensive term encompassing all prophets not explicitly named, including Dawud (David), Sulayman (Solomon), Ayyub (Job), Yunus (Jonah), Zakariyya (Zachariah), Yahya (John the Baptist), and the thousands of unnamed prophets sent to every nation.

Ibn Kathir, following Imam al-Tabari (d. 310 H), explains that the purpose of listing these names is not merely historical enumeration, but theological demonstration: Allah is showing the believer that the same hand that guided Ibrahim guides Muhammad, and the same message that was given to Musa and 'Isa is perfected in the Qur'an.[3]

2.3 The Prohibition of Distinction (La Nufarriqu)

The phrase "We make no distinction between any of them" (لَا نُفَرِّقُ بَيْنَ أَحَدٍ مِّنْهُمْ) is the theological fulcrum of this verse. Ibn Kathir identifies two forms of prohibited distinction:[4]

  • Distinction by rejection — believing in some prophets while denying others. This is the error of the Jews who rejected 'Isa and of the Christians who denied the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ. Allah says: "Indeed, those who disbelieve in Allah and His messengers and wish to discriminate between Allah and His messengers and say, 'We believe in some and disbelieve in others,' and wish to adopt a way in between — those are the disbelievers, truly." (Qur'an 4:150–151)
  • Distinction by elevation — exalting one prophet above others in a way that distorts their human status or divine role. Ibn Kathir notes that the Christians erred by elevating 'Isa to divinity, while some Jews diminished the status of Ibrahim and Isma'il.
Key Distinction
The verse does NOT say all prophets are equal in rank. Classical scholars unanimously agree that prophets vary in rank — some are Ulul 'Azm (those of strong resolve: Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, Muhammad). Rather, the verse prohibits favouritism that leads to rejection or distortion. See: al-Qurtubi, Al-Jami' li Ahkam al-Qur'an, Tafsir 3:82; Ibn Kathir, Tafsir al-Qur'an al-'Adhim, Tafsir 3:82.

Imam al-Qurtubi (d. 671 H) elaborates in his Al-Jami' li Ahkam al-Qur'an that the phrase "we make no distinction" means: "We believe in all of them equally in the sense that we do not reject any of them, and we do not elevate any above what Allah has elevated them." The believer honours every prophet according to the station Allah gave him.[5]

2.4 "And We Are Muslims to Him"

Ibn Kathir explains that the closing phrase — "and we are Muslims [in submission] to Him" (وَنَحْنُ لَهُ مُسْلِمُونَ) — is the summation of the entire verse.[6] The word Muslim here is not used as a religious label but in its original meaning: one who submits his entire being to Allah's will.

Imam Ibn Taymiyyah (d. 728 H), in Majmu' al-Fatawa [10/482–483], writes that the essence of Islam in this verse is the total surrender of the heart, tongue, and limbs to Allah's command. A person who claims belief in all prophets but refuses to follow the shari'ah of Muhammad ﷺ has not fulfilled this verse. True submission requires both iman (faith in the heart) and islam (outward submission to the law).[7]

3. Lessons from the Verse

3.1 The Continuity of Revelation

This verse teaches that revelation is not a series of disconnected events but a continuous thread woven by the same Divine Hand. From Ibrahim to Muhammad, the message is one: worship Allah alone. Imam al-Ghazali (d. 505 H), in Ihya' Ulum al-Din, writes that the believer who understands this continuity never despairs of divine mercy, because he sees that Allah has never abandoned humanity — He sent prophets to every nation, in every era, with the same essential guidance.[8]

3.2 The Error of Sectarian Exclusivism

The verse is a direct refutation of every group that claims exclusive access to truth. The Jews who rejected 'Isa, the Christians who rejected Muhammad, and — by extension — any Muslim group that claims prophets or saints as intercessors with exclusive rights, all violate this verse's command of non-distinction.

3.3 Respect for All Prophets Is Non-Negotiable

A Muslim cannot truly believe while mocking, diminishing, or dishonouring any prophet — including 'Isa, Musa, or Ibrahim. The Prophet ﷺ said:

لاَ تَخْتَصُّوا بِلَيْلَةِ جُمُعَتِكُمْ بِقِيَامٍ مِنْ بَيْنِ اللَّيَالِي، وَلاَ تَخُصُّوا يَوْمَ جُمُعَتِكُمْ بِصِيَامٍ مِنْ بَيْنِ أَيَّامِكُمْ، إِلاَّ أَنْ يَكُونَ فِي صَوْمٍ تَصُومُونَهُ

In a more relevant hadith, the Prophet ﷺ said regarding the prophets: "Do not prefer me over Musa, for the people will fall unconscious on the Day of Resurrection and I will be the first to regain consciousness, and behold, Musa will be holding onto the side of the Throne. I do not know whether he was among those who fell unconscious and regained consciousness before me, or if he was exempted because of his having fallen unconscious at the mountain." (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Prophets [60], Hadith no. 3408). This demonstrates the Prophet's own humility and the obligation of Muslims to honour all prophets equally without preference or diminishment.

Sahih — Agreed upon (Muttafaq 'Alayh)

3.4 Submission Trumps Identity

The verse closes with submission — not identity, not affiliation, not lineage. It is not enough to be born into a Muslim family or to carry a Muslim name. The believer must be actively submitting — one who submits — to the will of Allah. Imam Ibn al-Qayyim (d. 751 H) says in Madarij as-Salikeen that the perfection of faith is not in knowledge alone but in the transformation of that knowledge into willing, humble submission.[9]

4. 'Aqeedah Dimensions

This verse touches the foundational principles of Sunni Islamic creed as articulated in Sharh Aqeedah al-Tahawiyyah and the classical creedal texts:

  • Belief in All Prophets Is Obligatory — Imam al-Tahawi (d. 321 H) states in his Aqeedah: "We believe in all the prophets and messengers, and we do not distinguish between any of them." Denial of even one prophet is equivalent to denial of all, because all convey the same divine authority.
  • The Books Are From Allah — The verse mentions both unzila (what was revealed) and utiya (what was given), indicating that the original Torah, Gospel, and all previous scriptures were truly from Allah, even if their current textual forms have been altered by human hands.
  • Prophetic Inerrancy ('Ismah) — The fact that Allah commands belief in all prophets implies their trustworthiness. A prophet who could err fundamentally in conveying revelation would not be worthy of unconditional belief.
  • The Seal of Prophethood — While the verse honours all prophets, the fact that it is revealed to Muhammad ﷺ establishes his supremacy in the prophetic chain. The Prophet ﷺ said: "I have been given five things which were not given to any amongst the prophets before me..." (Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Prophets [60], Hadith no. 3353).

5. Practical Applications

5.1 'Aqeedah Integrity in a Pluralistic World

Modern Muslims navigate interfaith environments daily. This verse reminds us that while we respect all faiths and their adherents, our belief is exclusive in its content (tawhid) and inclusive in its attitude (respect for all prophets). We do not dilute our creed to win acceptance, but we also do not mock others' sacred figures.

5.2 Interfaith Dialogue with Theological Clarity

When engaging with Jews or Christians, a Muslim can affirm shared Abrahamic lineage — Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa are all ours — while clarifying that the Qur'an corrects the distortions that entered previous scriptures. This verse provides the scriptural basis for dialogue without compromise.

5.3 Guarding Against Sectarianism Within Islam

Just as this verse forbids distinguishing between prophets, it forbids the sectarian arrogance that divides Muslims into warring factions over trivial interpretations. The Prophet ﷺ warned: "The Jews split into seventy-one sects, the Christians into seventy-two, and my Ummah will split into seventy-three sects, all of which will be in the Fire except one..." (Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Kitab al-Iman [8], Hadith no. 2641, Hasan Sahih). The "saved sect" is defined by adherence to the Qur'an and the authentic Sunnah.

5.4 Qur'anic Consistency in Personal Belief

A Muslim's creed cannot be selective. One cannot believe in the prophethood of Muhammad ﷺ while doubting the miraculous birth of 'Isa, or affirm the miracles of Musa while questioning the Night Journey (Isra' wal-Mi'raj). This verse demands complete, unreserved acceptance of all divine communications.

5.5 Respect for All Prophets in Speech and Action

Mocking, caricaturing, or diminishing any prophet — even those not central to Islamic practice — violates this verse. The believer must guard his tongue and heart against casual disrespect for Ibrahim, Musa, 'Isa, or any prophet, including those not named in the Qur'an.

5.6 Submission as a Daily Practice

The verse closes with muslimun — submission. This is not a static identity but a dynamic practice. Every prayer, every business transaction, every word spoken is an opportunity to submit to Allah's will. Imam al-Ghazali calls this the "station of surrender" (maqam al-taslim), where the believer no longer argues with divine decree but accepts it with gratitude and patience.

6. Conclusion

Surah Ali 'Imran 3:82 is the foundation stone of prophetic theology in Islam. It demands that the believer accept every prophet Allah sent, from the first to the last, without distinction, rejection, or distortion. It anchors the Muslim in a lineage of divine mercy that stretches from the dawn of history to the final revelation.

Yet this verse is not merely a catalogue of names. It is a commitment. To say "We believe" is to enter a covenant — a covenant that binds the tongue to truth, the heart to sincerity, and the limbs to submission. It forbids the arrogance of exclusivism and the laxity of religious relativism. It calls the believer to a middle path: honouring all prophets while following only the final shari'ah, respecting all scriptures while relying only on the preserved Qur'an.

As Imam al-Ghazali writes, the perfection of this belief is not in words but in transformation. The one who truly believes that Musa spoke to Allah, that 'Isa was born of a virgin, that Ibrahim was thrown into a fire that became cool — such a person cannot help but submit. And in that submission lies the essence of Islam.

رَبَّنَا لَا تُزِغْ قُلُوبَنَا بَعْدَ إِذْ هَدَيْتَنَا وَهَبْ لَنَا مِن لَّدُنكَ رَحْمَةً ۚ إِنَّكَ أَنتَ الْوَهَّابُ

"Our Lord, let not our hearts deviate after You have guided us and grant us from Yourself mercy. Indeed, You are the Bestower." — Qur'an 3:8
