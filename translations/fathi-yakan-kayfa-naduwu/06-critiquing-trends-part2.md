### 1. The Law of the Pursuit of Profit

This law constitutes the essential contradiction between the socialist system and the capitalist system. The socialist system confines its economic goals to meeting human needs, while the capitalist system directs the economy toward realizing, hoarding, and amassing profits in the coffers of the project owners.

As soon as capitalism begins, it begins applying this law to the society in which it arises and operates. The first victims of this law are the workers and consumers, for it always strives to increase its profits at the expense of wages.

If we return to the early stages of the development of capitalism, we witness the brutal exploitation that capitalism practiced against workers, since its basic goal was first and last to extract the greatest possible amount of productive hours from the workers in exchange for the least possible amount of wages.

According to this law, the law of the pursuit of profit, the worker becomes a commodity subject to the law of supply and demand. There is no minimum or maximum wage; rather, its level is determined by the need of the industrial market first and by the number of workers second.

The law of the pursuit of profit drives capitalists and capitalist states to seek profits in every country and land on earth. All of us remember the human tragedies that colonialism committed and still commits through its financial and commercial influence in all corners of the earth.

### 2. The Law of Competition and Rivalry

The capitalists feed not only on the blood of the working class and the flesh of the inhabitants of the colonies, but they also devour one another. Every industrial project finds in similar industrial projects a competitor and rival. Therefore, it strives with all its might to eliminate that project and to attract customers and markets—and all of that out of love for acquiring profits.

If we wish to investigate the causes of all wars since the capitalist organization of society and the state became known, we trace the causes of every war back to this law: the law of competition and rivalry.

### 3. The Law of the Concentration and Monopoly of Production

The law of competition that drives peoples and nations to plunge into the waves of wars waged by capitalism and the capitalists also drives companies to struggle and fight against one another. Banks play a large role in achieving the aims of large companies in liquidating small or medium companies. In times of crisis, the banks withhold loans from small or medium companies and demand payment of their debts, thereby pushing them into bankruptcy and collapse.

### 4. The Law of Low Prices

This law calls for preserving profits while increasing them, by means of the mechanical expansion of production and the reduction of the workforce. What results from the lowering of the cost of the commodity through reducing wages arising from replacing workers with machines leads to a lowering of the price of the commodity on the scale of profits; thus, the capitalists preserve their profits without the lowering of the price of the commodity affecting the amounts of profit that come to them. All of this is because the problem has been solved by reducing the workforce and thereby lowering costs through dispensing with paying wages to workers whose place the machine has taken.
