## Introduction

# Introduction

Those working in the Islamic field today are in the utmost need of a *manhaj* (method) of *tablīgh* (conveyance) to which they adhere, and of intellectual and activist starting-points from which they proceed in the sphere of calling people to Islam. They need all of this to be of such precision and clarity that they can clearly see the path, its characteristics, and its conditions, so that they no longer wander blindly, or do harm to Islam while thinking that they are doing good.

Therefore, they need a *manhaj* that defines for them the path of *daʿwah* to Allah, how to address people and convince them, and the topics that are best raised before them and explained in accessible terms.

In my view, this *manhaj* consists of four sections:

**The First Section:**

It revolves around the obligation of *daʿwah* to Allah: that faith in Islam entails working for it, calling to it, and *jihād* in its cause. This in turn entails the establishment of a collective activist work; individual effort will not produce an Islamic reality or realize an Islamic society.

**The Second Section:**

It is a presentation of the style that should be adopted in calling people to Islam. The callers must address people according to their capacity to respond, and according to their level of understanding. Their guiding policy in that must be knowledge, wisdom, and sound conduct.

**The Third Section:**

It is a rapid overview of the major outlines upon which the Islamic *manhaj* rests, whether in the sphere of *ʿaqīdah* (creed) or in the sphere of *sharīʿah* (Divine Law). Taken together, these constitute the topics that are best raised for discussion before those whom one intends to call.

**The Fourth and Final Section:**

As for the fourth and final section, it is an affirmation of the necessity of establishing collective activist work for Islam. This collective activist work is the path the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, followed in building the first Islamic community. Every individual effort for Islam is bound to be lost unless it is tied to an activist organization. All of this obliges the believers in Islam to establish an activist organization, or to join an existing one.

As I offer this treatise to my brothers, the callers in every place, I ask Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, to record the reward, erase the mistakes, and realize the hope.

> And indeed, Allah is the Guide of those who have believed to a straight path. [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:54]

**The Author**

## Section 1: The Obligation of Daʿwah

# Preachers on the Sidelines of Daʿwah

## First Investigation: Preachers on the Sidelines of Daʿwah

Many of the preachers of Islam in this age do not fully understand the true nature of the objectives they are working for, nor the path upon which they are traveling, nor the real demands that Islamic work makes upon their lives, their time, their thought, their very selves, and their wealth. This is what often makes them poor examples of what they call people to, and unsuitable models of what they command others to do. Consequently, it makes them, in the life of the Islamic movement, a heavy burden and an obstacle that impedes its progress and hinders its launch.

Some of these people imagine that the costs of Islamic work fall from their shoulders if they publish a book, write an article, or deliver a lecture. Others believe that merely belonging to an Islamic movement, attending its gatherings, and revolving in its orbit is the furthest ambition and the ultimate wish.

These and those alike are, without doubt, living on the margins of Islamic work. They have not actually entered its true field nor lived in its real atmosphere.

The correct conception of the cost of Islamic work ought to be impressed upon the minds of the workers: that they are called to something called **sacrifice** (*al-taḍḥiyah*, التضحية). Sacrifice that transcends in its meaning the limits of its forms and descriptions; that means putting the interest of Islam above every other interest; that means, as well, revolving in the millstone of Islam whatever the circumstances and however great the sacrifices may be:

> They chose you for a matter; if you perceive it,
> Then guard with your very self the interests of the Muslims.

Working for Islam means working to demolish the *jāhili* society in every pillar of its pillars and to establish the Islamic society in every affair of its affairs. It means uprooting *jāhiliyyah* in its entirety—whether it be a *jāhiliyyah* of ideas, a *jāhiliyyah* of systems, or a *jāhiliyyah* of morals. It also means confronting the preachers of this *jāhiliyyah* and its defenders. And it means, finally, establishing the rule of Allah on earth and demolishing the rule of *ṭāghūt*.

A path such as this is a difficult, thorny path; objectives such as these are mighty, great objectives; costs such as these are weighty, heavy costs. Weak souls cannot bear their burden, and none can undertake them except one who dedicates his life to *jihād* in the cause of Allah, except one for whom Allah and His Messenger are dearer than all else, and except one who has purged himself of attachment to the goods of this world, its desires, its charms, and its temptations:

> But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination, then indeed, Paradise will be his refuge. [Qurʾan, An-Nāziʿāt 79:40-41]

The honor of working for Islam is not deserved by one who merely calls to it with his tongue, or is eloquent in speech. It is deserved only by those who live for Islam and die for it:

> [Such believers are] those whom neither commerce nor sale diverts from the remembrance of Allah and performance of prayer and giving of zakah. They fear a Day when hearts and eyes will overturn—so that Allah may reward them according to the best of what they did and increase them from His bounty. And Allah provides for whom He wills without account. [Qurʾan, An-Nūr 24:37-38]

And they are those who fight in the cause of Allah, so they kill and are killed:

> Indeed, Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties [in exchange] for that they will have Paradise. They fight in the cause of Allah, so they kill and are killed. [It is] a true promise [binding] upon Him in the Torah, the Gospel, and the Qurʾan. And who is truer to his covenant than Allah? So rejoice in your transaction which you have contracted. And it is that which is the great attainment. [Qurʾan, At-Tawbah 9:111]

The path to Paradise, O preachers, is hard and arduous. The merchandise of Allah is precious; it is obtained only by one who pays the price and settles the account. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ spoke truly when he said:

> "Whoever fears, sets out at nightfall; and whoever sets out at nightfall reaches the destination. Verily, the merchandise of Allah is precious; verily, the merchandise of Allah is Paradise." [Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Asceticism, Hadith 2349]

Shaddād ibn al-Hādī said: A man from the Bedouins came to the Prophet ﷺ and believed in him and followed him. He said, "I will emigrate with you." The Prophet ﷺ entrusted him to some of his Companions. When the expedition to Khaybar took place, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ acquired some spoils, divided them, and gave a share to the Bedouin. The Bedouin said to the Messenger, "I did not follow you for this, but I followed you so that I might be struck here"—and he pointed to his throat with an arrow—"and die and enter Paradise." He said, "If you are truthful to Allah, Allah will be truthful to you." Then they rose to fight the enemy. He was brought to him slain. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, "Is it he?" They said, "Yes." He said, "He was truthful to Allah, and Allah was truthful to him." [Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Asceticism, Hadith 2349; also narrated by al-Ḥākim in *al-Mustadrak*]

This, O preachers of Islam, is the path to Paradise: continuous work, persistent *jihād*, and eagerness for martyrdom:

> And those who strive for Us—We will surely guide them to Our ways. And indeed, Allah is with the doers of good. [Qurʾan, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:69]

# The Obligation of Da'wah to Islam

Calling people to Islam, commanding them with what is right, and forbidding them from what is wrong is a heavy responsibility incumbent upon every Muslim working in the Islamic field. They must rise to it and give it its due share of effort, thought, and time — nay, this very duty is the original, foundational mission of every *dāʿiyah* (داعية).

The Noble Qur'an exhorts the fulfillment of this duty in many of its verses, among them the saying of Allah, the Exalted:

> "And let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong, and those will be the successful." [Qur'an, Aal-i-Imran 3:104]

And His saying:

> "And who is better in speech than one who invites to Allah and does righteousness and says, 'Indeed, I am of the Muslims.'" [Qur'an, Fussilat 41:33]

And His saying:

> "So to that [religion of Allah] invite, [O Muhammad], and remain on a right course as you are commanded and do not follow their inclinations." [Qur'an, Ash-Shura 42:15]

And His saying:

> "And invite them to your Lord. Indeed, you are upon straight guidance." [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:67]

And His saying:

> "O Messenger, announce that which has been revealed to you from your Lord, and if you do not, then you have not conveyed His message." [Qur'an, Al-Ma'ida 5:67]

The Pure Sunnah is replete with what has been narrated from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, of *aḥādīth* (أحاديث) regarding the duty of rising to call to Allah and combating evils. We mention by way of example his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he is unable, then with his tongue; if he is unable, then with his heart — and that is the weakest of faith." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith]

And his saying:

> "O people, Allah says to you: Enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong before you call upon Me and I do not answer you, ask of Me and I do not give you, and seek My aid and I do not aid you." [Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Book of Supplications; Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of the Introduction]

And his saying:

> "Whoever conceals knowledge from that by which Allah would benefit people in a matter of religion, Allah will bridle him on the Day of Resurrection with a bridle of fire." [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of the Introduction]

And his saying:

> "What is the matter with a people who do not instruct their neighbors, do not teach them, do not admonish them, do not command them, and do not forbid them? And what is the matter with a people who do not learn from their neighbors, do not seek understanding, and do not take admonition? By Allah, a people must surely instruct their neighbors, teach them, admonish them, command them, and forbid them, and a people must surely learn from their neighbors, seek understanding, and take admonition, or I will hasten punishment upon them." [Al-Tabarani, *Al-Mu'jam al-Kabir*]

## That Without Which an Obligation Cannot Be Fulfilled Is Itself Obligatory

Furthermore, calling people to Islam, persuading them of it, and preparing them to submit to its principles and rulings are among the means by which an Islamic society is established and Islamic life resumed. Since the realization of a Muslim society is in itself an obligation, then by consequence every means of establishing and creating it is likewise an obligation.

Indeed, the matter goes beyond even this, for Islam today has no state that judges by it in its affairs and derives all its conduct from it. The rulings of Islam are suspended. Since recourse to the law of Allah is an Islamic duty, and the realization of this duty is dependent upon the existence of a state — and there is no Muslim state — then working to establish the Islamic state becomes, in turn, a *farḍ ʿayn* (فرض عين: individual obligation) upon every Muslim until the existence of that state is realized. Those who fall short in this are, by the *sharīʿah* (الشريعة), sinful (*āthimīn*), and nothing lifts the sin from them but their rising to the burdens of calling to Islam and their actual contribution, within the limits of their capacity and means, to preparing all the causes and means for the establishment of the Islamic state.

## Action Is the Brother of Faith and Its Fruit

Faith itself, unless it drives one to persistent action in spreading the principles through which faith is realized, is not faith. Faith is a conviction of the mind, a tranquility of the heart, and a *jihād* (جهاد) of the soul — "And We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you and the patient, and We will test your affairs." [Qur'an, Al-Ankabut 29:2] And the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truth when he said:

> "Faith is not by wishful thinking; rather, it is what settles in the heart and is confirmed by action." [Al-Daylami, *Musnad al-Firdaws*]

This being so, the callers to Islam should know that the first stage of action is the purification of the soul and its cleansing from defects and deviations, then mutual counsel in truth and patience, calling to Allah, and *jihād* in His cause.

The callers to Islam are required to exert their utmost effort in fulfilling the duties of *daʿwah* (دعوة), conveying it to people, and simplifying it for them according to their levels of understanding and comprehension. This is the trust with which they have been entrusted, that they may be heirs to the prophets. Then let them hear the words of the Greatest Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him:

> "There has never been a prophet whom Allah sent to a nation before me except that he had from his people devoted disciples and companions who took hold of his *sunnah* (سنة) and followed his command. Then after them came successors who said what they did not do and did what they were not commanded. Whoever strives against them with his hand is a believer; whoever strives against them with his tongue is a believer; and whoever strives against them with his heart is a believer. And beyond that there is not [even] a mustard seed of faith." [Sahih Muslim, Book on Government]

## Allah Is More Deserving of Being Feared

At times some callers avoid speaking in a situation where sincere counsel is obligatory, or where a bold, truthful word would be beneficial, for fear that doing so may expose them to people's displeasure and harm. There is no doubt that such conduct is a fruit of cowardice and fear, two traits that ought never find their way to a believer's heart. For Allah is more deserving that we fear Him, and Allah is more deserving that we dread Him. Let our constant and perpetual concern be His pleasure, even if it incurs the anger of all people:

> "O Allah, if You are not angry with me, I do not care." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Supplications]

The callers to Islam have been charged with bearing the legacy of prophethood and undertaking its burdens. Among the most important legacies of prophethood was confronting *jāhiliyyah* (جاهلية) in its various forms and manifestations, and combating evil in whatever battle array and movement it assumed — in fulfillment of the saying of Allah, the Exalted:

> "And say, 'The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills — let him believe; and whoever wills — let him disbelieve.'" [Qur'an, Al-Kahf 18:29]

And it is narrated from ʿUbadah ibn al-Ṣāmit that he said:

> "We pledged to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, to speak the truth wherever we are, and not to fear in Allah the blame of any blamer." [Sahih Muslim, Book on Government]

So let the callers to Islam beware of being silent where eloquence is due, or fearful where sincere counsel is required. And let them listen to what Abu Dawud and al-Tirmidhi narrated from ʿAbdullah ibn Masʿud, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said:

> "The first deficiency that entered Banu Isra'il was that a man would meet his fellow and say: 'O so-and-so, fear Allah and leave what you are doing, for it is not lawful for you.' Then he would meet him the next day and that would not prevent him from being his eating companion, his drinking companion, and his sitting companion. When they did that, Allah struck their hearts against one another." Then he recited: "Cursed were those who disbelieved among the Children of Israel by the tongue of David and of Jesus, the son of Mary. That was because they disobeyed and [habitually] transgressed. They did not used to forbid one another from the evil they did. Evil indeed was what they were doing." [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Knowledge; Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Book of Faith]

## Section 2: How to Call to Islam

# The Method of Da'wah and Addressing People

## How to Call to Islam

From the very nature of Islamic *da'wah* (دعوة) is that it is directed at people—all people—despite the differences of their ages, classes, cultures, environments, inclinations, and orientations. This obliges the callers to be wise and skilful in conveying their views and concepts to these various classes of people. Consequently, they must know where to begin and how. The successful caller is the one who gives every person what he needs of ideas and guidance, and strives to convince him of the idea and attract him to the movement in the style that influences him. This is the secret of the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him:

> We, the company of prophets, were commanded to lower people to their [proper] levels and to speak to them according to the measure of their intellects. [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Knowledge, Hadith 3656]

### Know the Person Before Calling Him

If the matter is so, then it becomes the duty of the caller to get to know the person he wishes to call to Islam: to know his ideas, concepts, perceptions, and to discover his ailments and problems. In doing so he will inevitably reach knowledge of the openings through which he can reach his soul. This will provide the caller with successful diagnosis and treatment, and thereby make his encounters, directions, and suggestions effective in impact and interaction.

### Where Do We Begin, and How?

Among the most prominent questions facing the caller in the field of his contact with new elements and his attempt to convince them of the Islamic idea is: How does he begin his call, and what should the beginning be? In truth, correctness in determining the starting point saves the caller a great deal of time and facilitates the process of convincing and attracting. In many cases the caller's failure to attract many elements to the call is due to his poor estimation of the starting point and his poor diagnosis of the ailment he seeks to treat: he begins where he ought to end, or ends where he ought to begin. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truly when he said:

> No one addresses people with a discourse that exceeds their intellects except that it becomes a trial for some of them. [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Zuhd, Hadith 3976; graded *hasan*]

### A Proposed Standard Methodology

Therefore there had to be a standard methodology by which the starting point is determined, and by which every caller knows how and where to begin.

In first meetings the caller should be keen to evaluate the element he wishes to call to Islam. Once he has done that, he is able to compare him with the standard model, determine his position in it, and the stage that corresponds to him; then follow him up from that very position according to the method designated for it.

In my view, the standard model should have its characteristics and stages determined as follows:

**First: the stage of building creed**, that is, forming the correct conception of the universe, man, and life. Among its conditions is the realisation of faith in Allah and the rest of the five pillars of faith. This stage is considered the fundamental stage in the formation of the Islamic personality, because it is the base from which the other stages branch, since the greater knot is such that when it is untied all other knots are untied.

Among the conditions of this stage is also the realisation of faith in Islam as a methodology free from the factors of human incapacity, because it is from One All-Knowing, All-Aware, All-Powerful, All-Mighty. And that it is the comprehensive, integrated methodology emanating from an authentic, independent cosmic philosophy. And that other methodologies are false in origin because they are of human origin, and this humanity makes them deficient, impotent, governed by the influences of caprice.

This requires a concentrated, conscious study of the Islamic idea, and a study of other trends and schools in order to refute them and expose their defects and shortcomings.

### From Theory to Practice

**Second:** The second stage to which the new element should be transferred, once his faith has become sound, his creed firmly rooted, and his understanding of Islam complete, is the stage of application—that is, the stage of embodying the theoretical truths of faith in correct Islamic conduct. This is the meaning of the Prophet's words, peace and blessings be upon him:

> Faith is not by wishful thinking or by adornment; rather it is what settles in the heart and is affirmed by deeds. [Musnad Ahmad, 4/324; graded *hasan*; also narrated by Ibn Hibban]

The nature of the method adopted by Islamic educational methodologies in building the Islamic personality is essentially based on concentrating the call of creed and deepening its foundations in the human soul before the devotional, legislative, and directive duties issue from it—and not the reverse, by any means.

That is because Islamic ethics, acts of worship, and transactions are but an expression of the creedal reality and an effect of it; and to the extent that this is firm and rooted, harmony and coherence between appearance and essence—that is, between beliefs and conduct—is realised.

What further confirms all this is that devotional duties and legal rulings came down in order only after the creedal conception had been firmly established and its foundations and principles implanted.

Therefore, among the requirements of this stage is calling the individual to take himself to what agrees with his creed and harmonises completely with the legislation emanating from that creed. This requires submitting to the rulings and commands of Islam in all his affairs, and fashioning his life according to the divine methodology, whether in the sphere of worship, behaviour, or conduct. Among its requirements as well is making Islam the measure from which one proceeds and to which one returns in all affairs of life.

**Third:** The third stage, once his creed is firmly rooted, his understanding complete, and his deeds sound, is the stage of engaging in the Islamic activist work. The Qur'anic and Prophetic evidence for the obligation of collective work for Islam is abundant and explicit. For the individual alone is unable to bear the responsibility of *da'wah* and its demands, to confront the *jāhili* reality and demolish it, and then to establish the Islamic society, create the Islamic state, and resume the Islamic way of life. All of these aims, and the efforts and resources they require, necessitate his participation in an activist organisation, his integration into it, and his cohesion with it.

Among the conditions of this stage are performing the pillars of Islam and adopting the authentic *Sunnah*, avoiding doubtful matters and shunning the forbidden, and watching Allah, Mighty and Majestic, in every small and great matter.

Among the conditions of this stage as well is the individual's undertaking the duty of reforming his family, colleagues, and those around him. He must realise that his own reform is no excuse before Allah for neglecting the reform of others, and that he must undertake the duty of commanding right and forbidding wrong even if he is alone and has no support. For faith should impel a person to speak the word of truth without fearing in that any blame or harm. I find no need to present legal proofs for what I say, because of the abundance of what has come in the Book of Allah and the *Sunnah* of His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him.

These are the principal stages which, by their interconnection and cohesion, compose a standard line that may be adopted or benefited from in the field of calling to Allah.

All the brothers can benefit from this approach in determining the features of the path they should follow while calling people to Islam as an idea and a movement. And Allah is the Guide to the straight path:

> And that Allah should guide a man through you is better for you than this world and all it contains. And say, "Work, for Allah will see your work, and [so will] His Messenger and the believers; then you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform you of what you used to do." [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:105]

## The Method of Da'wah and Addressing People

It should not be understood from what has preceded that the path for calling people to Islam, attracting them to it, and winning them over is a single path: the creedal path that begins with proving the existence of Allah and ends with proving that Islam is a way of life, that working to resume the Islamic way of life and establish Islamic rule is an obligation upon every Muslim.

What is meant here by the path of *da'wah* is the entry point through which the caller can reach the soul of the one being called, so that he is able to convince him of the idea and attract him to the movement.

In this matter and matters like it we ought to proceed from legal rulings which we take as a basis for our approach and conduct, so that Islamic work does not become arbitrary and spontaneous, controlled by emotions, caprices, and personal judgments, and thus depart from the limits and restrictions laid down by the *Sharīʿah* to protect it from excess and deviation.

It is very obvious that people today are in need of something that first attracts them to Islam, makes them feel its presence as a way of life, as an imamate, as a leadership, as a worship, amid the conflicting materialistic trends and forces. It is likewise obvious that this attraction is a preliminary step that precedes the process of convincing, indeed it is almost a first step in preparing people's minds and hearts to receive and be moved.

The guidance of Islam in this matter becomes clear when one follows the Qur'anic and Prophetic method in calling people to Islam. Among the methods the Noble Qur'an adopted repeatedly and consistently in order to attract the polytheists to its clear signs—and they used to put their fingers in their ears when they heard it—was its opening of the *sūrahs* with disconnected letters, which had the greatest effect in arousing the curiosity of the polytheists and drawing them to listen and receive.

The methods followed by the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, in calling people to Islam and attracting them to it were but a practical explanation of the noble divine guidance crowned by the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction. [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:125]

And His words:

> And whoever is given wisdom has certainly been given much good. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:269]

Among the wisdom of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, was his following of the indirect psychological rebuke, as when a young man came to him asking permission to commit fornication—the hadith is established in the books of *Sunnah*. And among his wisdom, peace and blessings be upon him, in guiding hearts and attracting them to Islam was his saying to one who came asking him about Islam and its duties: "Do not lie." And likewise among his wisdom in addressing people according to their intellects, and with matters that influence them, was his performing the miracle of moving the tree—as is recorded in the books of *Sīrah* and *Sunnah*—as a proof of the truthfulness of his prophethood before a Bedouin who asked him.

Therefore, among the wisdom in the method of calling people to Islam and attracting them to it is addressing them from where they will respond, and in the style by which they are moved.

For people differ in everything: they differ in intelligence and knowledge, just as they differ in temperaments and emotions. They also differ in ideas and perceptions, just as they differ in inclinations and orientations. All of this obliges the call and the caller to choose the entry point most suitable to their souls and the style most appropriate to their minds.

The keys by which people may be attracted to Islam as a prelude to convincing them are numerous and varied, and cannot be counted or confined. Sometimes that key may be a solution the caller provides for a livelihood problem, a service, or a cause he adopts. Sometimes the key may be a political stance taken by the movement that attracts people to Islam and presents it to them, at the very least, as an orientation worthy of study and thought. It may also be a purposeful visit to a friend, a talk in a mosque, a dialogue at a seminar, or an article in a book or newspaper.

Thus the methods by which people may be attracted to Islam and their minds and feelings prepared to receive and respond multiply.

The Islamic movement is able to attract people to Islam through every issue of theirs, small or great. The correct treatment of a political issue from the Islamic perspective and in the light of Islam can reveal to people the error of the policies being followed, even if only gradually, and thereby direct people's attention and stimulate their alertness to receive more Islamic ideas, concepts, and directions.

Thus it becomes clear that the movement can benefit from every issue that occupies people's interest as a means of critiquing the existing *jāhili* conditions, attracting people to Islam, making them aware of the need to realise its worth, study its ideas, adhere to its principles, and to the honour of working for it and of *jihād* in its cause.

# Direct Individual Contact

Direct individual contact is instrumental in winning new elements for the movement and in increasing the movement output of every member of the organization.

## The Concept of Individual Contact

By direct individual contact we mean every member of the organization undertaking the duty of deliberate, purposeful contact with new elements and striving to attract them first to the idea, and finally to the movement.

For every individual—each and every one—naturally lives within a society tied to its members by bonds of various kinds: ties of kinship, friendship, comradeship, common interest, and so on.

It behoves the bearer of the idea to try to benefit from all these relationships and circumstances in presenting his ideas and seeking to convince others of them. He thereby moves his ordinary, spontaneous relationships out of their accidental, haphazard sphere into the purposeful sphere that serves the Islamic idea and opens many doors and starting points before it.

Indeed, the Brother ought to make his whole life, in its entirety and its details, an expressive, embodied image of Islam, so that he is able to prepare around him a suitable atmosphere under all conditions and circumstances.

Every individual has an existence and an impact. His existence is not a substitute for his impact; rather, his impact indicates the value of his existence. Mere, spontaneous existence is a phenomenon shared by animals, plants, and humans alike. The value of a Muslim Brother lies not in his existence alone, but in his impact—that is, in what this existence produces and yields.

Hence it follows that the Brother—bearer of a message—must live his message and for his message. His message should be the axis of his thought and his concern, and through this, little by little, the meaning intended by the noble verse is realized:

> Say, "Indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds. No partner has He. And this I have been commanded, and I am the first [among you] of the Muslims." [Qur'an, Al-An'am 6:162-163]

## Individual Contact Is a Religious Obligation

The Muslim Brother must fully realize that the task of spreading *daʿwah* (دعوة), conveying Islamic concepts to people, and combating wrongs is a religious obligation and an individual responsibility that is not lifted from him merely by his affiliation with a group or his movement within its orbit.

The responsibility for Islamic work rests upon every Muslim, whether a movement exists or not. Thus, the purpose of activist organizations is to absorb individual activities, develop them, direct them, and gradually transform them into a mighty energy with which to confront the great issues that isolated individuals are unable to face.

The forms of individual address found in the Qur'anic and Prophetic commands affirm personal responsibility in bearing the burdens of Islamic *daʿwah* (دعوة). Examples include the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> And who is better in speech than one who invites to Allah and does righteousness and says, "Indeed, I am of the Muslims." [Qur'an, Fussilat 41:33]

> So to that [religion of Allah] invite, [O Muhammad], and remain on a right course as you are commanded and do not follow their inclinations but say, "I have believed in what Allah has revealed of the Qur'an, and I have been commanded to do justice among you. Allah is our Lord and your Lord. For us are our deeds, and for you your deeds. There is no [need for] argument between us and you. Allah will bring us together, and to Him is the [final] destination." [Qur'an, Ash-Shura 42:15]

> Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:125]

> And invite to your Lord; indeed, you are upon straight guidance [Qur'an, Ar-Ra'd 13:30]

And the words of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him:

> Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he is unable, then with his tongue; and if he is unable, then with his heart, and that is the weakest of faith. [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 170]

> All of you are shepherds, and all of you are responsible for your flocks. [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Judgments, Hadith 2554]

Many workers in the Islamic field mistakenly imagine that the duty of undertaking individual contact and spreading *daʿwah* (دعوة) requires one to have studied the sciences of the Qur'an and its exegesis, the sciences of *ḥadīth* (حديث) and its terminology, and the sciences of *sharīʿah* (الشريعة), its principles and branches, not to mention social, economic, and political culture—something that is unattainable except for a few individuals whose circumstances and capabilities permit it.

In my view, the matter is far simpler than that. When the Brother's understanding of Islam has become well-rounded, and he is able to express that understanding with clarity and simplicity, it becomes his duty to offer it at the very first step and to bear the responsibilities of a caller (*dāʿiyah*, داعية)—provided three conditions are met:

1. He knows the limit of his knowledge and works within its scope and at his level.
2. His conduct is consistent with the principles he calls people to.
3. He works to develop his Islamic culture until, with time, it reaches a good level that grants him greater openness, breadth, and productivity.

Therefore, the important thing is to begin. And beginning, like every beginning, may be the most difficult of difficulties for any work. Once a person gets past this stage, obstacles fall one after another, and Islamic output rises day by day.

## Obstacles on the Path

I find no explanation for the sterility, stagnation, and isolation afflicting many who are affiliated with activist organizations except one reason: their excessive fear and awe of people and society.

Such people either lack self-confidence, and so must treat themselves in this regard; or they fear for their lives and avoid exposing them to harm—the least that can be said of them is that they are weak and cowardly, and they need healing and treatment before they are fit to treat and heal others; or they are people of interests who exaggerate in preserving them even at the expense of Islam.

Fear of people cannot be an attribute of the believers, for faith by its nature gives a person boldness in the truth and courage in it. Allah, the Exalted, confirms this:

> Those to whom hypocrites said, "Indeed, the people have gathered against you, so fear them." But it [merely] increased them in faith, and they said, "Sufficient for us is Allah, and [He is] the best Disposer of affairs." So they returned with bounty from Allah and grace, untouched by harm. [Qur'an, Aal-E-Imran 3:173-174]

To this meaning the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed when he said: "I have been commanded to speak the truth, even if it is bitter," and when he said: "I have been commanded to speak the truth and not to fear, in Allah, the blame of any blamer." [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Tribulations, Hadith 3952; declared hasan]

Fleeing from the battle is a trait of the hesitant, wavering people who are heedless of His words, the Exalted:

> Say, "Never will we be struck except by what Allah has decreed for us; He is our protector." And upon Allah let the believers rely. [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:51]

And those who imagine that their fortresses protect them from Allah and His decree, forgetting the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> Wherever you may be, death will overtake you, even if you should be within towers of lofty construction. [Qur'an, An-Nisa 4:78]

It is to these and their like that the Noble Qur'an points with His words:

> Those who said about their brothers while sitting [at home], "If they had obeyed us, they would not have been killed." Say, "Then prevent death from yourselves, if you should be truthful." [Qur'an, Aal-E-Imran 3:168]

And His words:

> Say, "Indeed, the death from which you flee—indeed, it will meet you. Then you will be returned to the Knower of the unseen and the witnessed, and He will inform you about what you used to do." [Qur'an, Al-Jumu'ah 62:8]

As for those who fear that Islamic work will harm their interests, livelihoods, and sustenance, and therefore calculate the matter a thousand times over, these can only be slaves to the earth and its refuse. They ought to free themselves from anything that restricts their launch and shackles their movement.

The Brother is certainly required to work, earn, and not forget his share of this world, acting on the words of the Exalted:

> But seek, through that which Allah has given you, the home of the Hereafter; and [yet], do not forget your share of the world. [Qur'an, Al-Qasas 28:77]

But he must place the world in his pocket, not in his heart; employ it for good without letting it employ him for evil; and not let his attachment to it become a cause of turning away from *jihād* (جهاد) in the path of Allah.

## Benefits of Individual Contact

Direct individual contact has many benefits too numerous to enumerate. It suffices to point out here that personal contact allows the callers to become acquainted with the elements they seek to attract to *daʿwah* (دعوة) and to convey the idea to them closely. It also enables them, through this contact, to learn of their situations and problems, thereby facilitating diagnosis, guidance, and treatment—benefits not realized in the domains of collective contact, such as the direct impact that touches the very cause and treats the ailment itself.

Among the benefits of individual contact is that it places all the Brothers, without exception, before their responsibilities and duties. Thus Islamic work is not confined to a number of individuals who perform the tasks of guidance and instruction; rather, every member of the organization is required to perform his productive role within the limits allowed by his capabilities, capacities, and energies.

This has the effect of transforming the group into a working cell in which everyone works and produces, with no room for idleness and laziness.

Another of the advantages of individual contact is that it spares the movement many embarrassing situations sometimes imposed by political circumstances, and helps the callers to face all raised questions with objective discussion, simplification, and detail in atmospheres that general contact—such as celebrations, festivals, and lectures—do not provide.

Thus, individual contact is the fruitful, productive means that yields its fruit without clamor or noise, and brings the movement to its desired goal with the least effort and shortest time.

Finally, let the callers keep constantly before their eyes the words of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him:

> That Allah should guide a man through you is better for you than this world and all it contains. [Jami` at-Tirmidhi, Book of Manners, Hadith 2686; hasan]

## Good Method

There are several factors that help the caller to a great extent in the domains of *daʿwah* (دعوة), bring him fertility and fruit, and give him the ability to influence, interact, and diffuse his principles and ideas in every environment and at every level.

A good style is one of the sensitive, important factors that save the caller time and effort and bring him to the goal and what is required with the least cost and most ease.

The caller, in every domain of *daʿwah* (دعوة) and conveyance—in writing, in oratory and speech, in discussion, in popular, union, political, and student work—needs the good style that hits the mark and achieves the purpose.

Perhaps among the most important prerequisites for the caller to possess a good style in the domains of his *daʿwah* (دعوة) is his clear acquaintance with the environment that will be the field of his activity and work: he studies its conditions, problems, trends, inclinations, and desires, like a physician who observes the symptoms of the disease, its development, and its stages, then diagnoses its causes and motives with knowledge and understanding—knowledge of the disease itself and understanding of the means of cure.

The mature caller, like the successful physician, knows where and how to begin; and he does not begin before he has the ability to examine, diagnose, and treat, so that his work is not a series of failed experiments, hasty actions, and gilded styles that address people from where they will not listen, and come to them from where they do not feel, touching no wound in them.

The callers of Islam must be no less careful and concerned about their methods of *daʿwah* (دعوة) than others. They should not address toiling workers in the language of the graveyards, nor discuss passionate, atheist intellectuals with emotionalism; rather, they must make for every occasion a fitting word, motivated by the words of the greatest Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him:

> Speak to people according to their intellects. [Jami` at-Tirmidhi, Book of Knowledge, Hadith 2685; hasan]

Islam, in this age, needs callers who present its ideas and principles in an attractive, engaging style—callers who endear and do not repel, who clarify and do not complicate, and who do good as Allah has done good. How many pretenders have disfigured Islam under the pretext of calling to it, calling people to it while thinking they are doing good!

Hence the function of the callers is grave and their responsibility great; therefore, the elements of realism, positivity, and balance in the callers' methods must be present.

### Between Severity and Gentleness

Souls are naturally disposed to love those who do good to them, while harshness and severity sometimes push them to stubbornness, insistence, and aversion, making them cling to false pride. But gentleness does not mean flattery, duplicity, and hypocrisy; rather, it is giving sincere counsel and doing good with a gentle, effective style that opens hearts and expands chests—especially when the call is to a group of wrongdoers, for it is in no way fitting to confront them with rebuke and harsh reproach.

Do you not see the Noble Qur'an, in the course of divine guidance, addressing Moses and Aaron and instructing them to begin the tyrant Pharaoh with gentleness and goodness:

> Go, both of you, to Pharaoh. Indeed, he has transgressed. And speak to him with gentle speech that perhaps he may be reminded or fear [Allah]. [Qur'an, Taha 20:43-44]

Indeed, the Qur'anic indications and Prophetic allusions to kindness and the avoidance of harshness and cruelty confirm, beyond doubt, the effectiveness of this style and its educational value. Allah, the Exalted, says at the end of Surat an-Nahl, commanding His Prophet to adhere to wisdom in calling people:

> Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction, and argue with them in a way that is best. Indeed, your Lord is most knowing of who has strayed from His way, and He is most knowing of who is [rightly] guided. [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:125]

Ibn Kathir explained this by saying: "That is, whoever needs debate and argumentation, let it be with a good face, gentleness, and good address."

In Surat Aal-E-Imran, the Noble Qur'an points to the benefits of kindness and gentleness in winning helpers and supporters, and in the spread and embrace of the call:

> So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. [Qur'an, Aal-E-Imran 3:159]

In the commentary on this verse, it is related from Abdullah ibn Umar that he said: "I see the characteristic of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, in the earlier scriptures, that he is not vulgar, coarse, or loud in the markets, nor does he repay evil with evil, but he pardons and overlooks."

In the Prophetic biography there are practical models and explanatory events of the captivating, penetrating style with which the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, conveyed his call to people with eloquence. Abu Umamah narrated: "A young man came to the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, and said, 'O Messenger of Allah, do you permit me to commit fornication?' The people cried out at him, so the Prophet said, 'Bring him near, bring him near.' He came near until he sat before him. The Prophet said, 'Do you love it for your mother?' He said, 'No, may Allah make me your ransom.' He said, 'Likewise, people do not love it for their mothers. Do you love it for your daughter?' He said, 'No, may Allah make me your ransom.' He said, 'Likewise, people do not love it for their daughters. Do you love it for your sister?'—and Ibn Urfah added that he mentioned the paternal aunt, the maternal aunt, and the female in-law, saying at each one, 'No, may Allah make me your ransom.' Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, placed his hand on his chest and said, 'O Allah, purify his heart, forgive his sin, and guard his chastity.' Thereafter nothing was more hateful to him than that, that is, fornication." Narrated by Ahmad with a good chain. [Musnad Ahmad, 5:256; graded hasan]

The style of *daʿwah* (دعوة) should be renewed and evolving within the limits permitted by Islam. The flexibility of Islam requires working at the level of the age and with the various lawful means that ensure conveying Islam to people as a way of life, a message of life, in the most beautiful form and on the best footing.

This is the sense of flexibility in the words of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him:

> Wisdom is the lost property of the believer; wherever he finds it, he has more right to it. [Sunan Ibn Majah, Introduction, Hadith 251]

And his words:

> Take wisdom from whichever vessel it comes forth. [Sunan al-Darimi, Muqaddimah, Hadith 175]

## Section 3: Topics Worth Raising

# Critiquing the Current Reality

It is fitting for the caller (*al-dāʿiyah*, الداعية), in his first meetings with the new elements he intends to call to Islam, to raise the subject of critiquing the prevailing conditions under which the Ummah is living, and to clarify—so far as he can—his critique with objectivity, figures, and logic. The more he is able to touch upon the ills and diseases, to expose their causes and motives, and to explain their effects and consequences, the deeper will be his impact and the greater his influence upon those present.

I believe the caller should address this subject through the following points:

## First: The Corruption of Creed

The first catastrophe to befall the Ummah was the corruption of her *ʿaqīdah* (عقيدة) and her affliction with grievous materialist taints that planted doubt, materialist concepts, and irreligious opinions, thereby exposing the spirit of religiosity to violent and terrifying shocks. These shocks later paved the way for the emergence of atheistic trends (*ittijāhāt ilḥādīyyah*, اتجاهات إلحادية) and their dominance over the lands of the Muslims and their resources, and then for their war against Islam and its callers—at times by spreading rumors and fabrications, and at other times by inventing pretexts and justifications to uproot the callers and be rid of them.

## Second: Moral Deviation

One result of the corruption of *ʿaqīdah* that afflicted the Ummah was the collapse of her morals and the spread of immorality and vice among her.

Historical evidence confirms, beyond doubt, that among the causes of the fall and disappearance of nations is their immersion in the mire of vices and lusts, and their turning to pleasures and gratifications. Allah, the Exalted, says:

> "Indeed, those who like that immorality should be spread among those who believe will have a painful punishment in this world and the Hereafter." [Qur'an, An-Nur 24:19]

## Third: Failed Attempts at Liberation

This Ummah has witnessed, over the past quarter-century, failed attempts at liberation.

On the political level, the nationalist trends failed to establish any form of unity or union, even between two Arab countries.

On the economic level, the imported theories—capitalism and socialism—failed to produce a society of sufficiency and justice. Indeed, these theories dealt a deathblow to the national economy, leading to the destruction of commercial and financial life in more than one country.

As for the military level, the successive defeats suffered by the Ummah confirm, beyond doubt, the inability of the existing regimes to prepare the Ummah psychologically and materially to confront the colonialist challenges and to wage the battles of liberation.

## Fourth: The State of Loss Afflicting the Ummah

Thus the Ummah has come to be in a state of loss—utter loss. The conditions she lives in are extremely abnormal and deviant. Catastrophes and disasters descend upon her from every side.

The Ummah would not have reached what she has reached in vain. It is the harvest of her turning away from Allah, her rebellion against His sovereignty, her denial of His *manhaj* (منهج) in life, and consequently her return to the first *jāhiliyyah* (جاهلية) all over again.

## If the Cause Is Known, Wonder Ceases

The truth is that this relapse (*riddah*) that has afflicted the Muslims was the natural result of the conspiracies in which the tools of destruction collaborated against this Ummah from every quarter: from Shuʿūbiyyah (شعوبية) movements to materialist trends, from Zionist schemes to Masonic lodges, from foreign intelligence pens, and from the material resources and media apparatuses possessed by these forces and trends.

The Ummah has suffered grievously because of this and endured the utmost hardship, and she has seen the proof of it in every aspect of her life. Her social life has become putrid from the abundance of immoralities and abominations that have spread through it. Her economic life has become barren because of the failure of the man-made ruling systems and their inability to achieve justice and prosperity.

She has likewise seen the proof of it in her weakness, the disunity of her word, her division, and then her defeat in successive battles against a deformed state and a gang of deviants from every horizon.

Finally, after the materialist trends and man-made doctrines have gone bankrupt in solving the Ummah's problems and treating the impulses and deviations sweeping over her, Islam remains the only *manhaj* qualified to confront the challenges of materialist civilization, and the only orientation qualified to lead this Ummah—indeed, to nominate her for the leadership of humanity.

For the tyrannical materialism that has trampled under its path every ideal and value, and that has planted anxiety, disturbance, deviation, and abnormality throughout the inhabited world, cannot be confronted, repelled, and uprooted by anything except the logic of the *fitrah* (فطرة), for it is stronger than materialism and more capable of making man happy and providing him with tranquility.

The Ummah—after she has exhausted her precious energies in the whirlpool of her failed experiments, and after she has reached the misery and hardship she has reached because of her turning away from the voice of the *fitrah*—is called anew to express her original identity, called to appear before the world with the message of rescue and salvation, called to realize her Islamic self in the battle of self-realization.

And here it is fitting for the caller to critique the two global doctrines.

# Critiquing Contemporary Trends

It is fitting for the caller (*al-dāʿiyah*, الداعية), at this point, to critique the two global doctrines—Communism and Capitalism—with logic and objectivity.

## The Rise of Communism

Communism is the most violent form of socialism. It was founded by Karl Marx, a Jew born in Germany in 1818 and died in England in 1883.

Marx announced his communist views in 1851. He was confident of the collapse of the capitalist system, that the gap would widen between workers and employers, and that the result would be the establishment of a “dictatorship of the proletariat.” He declared these views in a document known as the *Communist Manifesto*.

Anyone who follows the details of Marx’s private life can clearly discern the factors that made him produce nothing but Communism as a solution to life.

The first factor was his failure in his private emotional affairs. In 1837 he secretly became engaged to a girl from an aristocratic family. When the matter was exposed to the family, they opposed the marriage project for class considerations. This was one of the first psychological shocks in his life—indeed, one of the first factors that ignited in him feelings of hatred toward the aristocratic, bourgeois society.

In 1838, Marx also engaged in a fencing duel at the Poets’ Club in Bonn against one of the bourgeois members of the club. His opponent overcame him and inflicted a serious wound on him. It seems that the thrust directed at him by that bourgeois did not leave an effect only on his eyebrow, but in the depths of his subconscious.

Then need and poverty, and the straitened circumstances of Marx’s family, engendered in him a raging resentment toward the rich and the wealthy, and made him constantly repeat a passage by Shakespeare in which he says:

> “O gold! O precious, glittering gold! You turn the white black, the ugly beautiful, evil good, the old young, the coward brave. This yellow, slavery-breeding thing is what ties and unties the sacred bonds.”

All these factors were able to work their profound effect in Marx, driven by the greatest factor of all: his Jewishness, producing Communism—or Marxism.

In addition to the above, Communism was also a reaction to the tyranny of feudalism and its dominance, and to the abominable conditions that Europe in general was experiencing. The peasants in that part of the world during the seventeenth, eighteenth, and nineteenth centuries suffered every kind of torment that caused them to flee into the jungles of Europe and the forests of Siberia.

Likewise, Communism was a reaction to the deviation of the Church from its original path, its siding with theocratic rule, its bolstering the authority of tyrants and rulers, and its revolving in their orbit—something that drove Marx to disbelieve in everything connected with the religious idea and religions, as will become clear.

Thus the particular factors in Marx’s life, combined with the general conditions of society, led to the birth of Communism—which we regard as a deviant reaction to deviant conditions, not as a useful remedy for the deviation of those conditions.

## Marxist Theory

Marxist theory rests on two fundamental principles: dialectical materialism and historical materialism—that is, the materialist interpretation of history. For this reason it is called historical materialism. It regards matter as the basis of everything in life, and humanity as driven in all its phases solely by the influence of matter.

### Dialectical Materialism

Dialectical materialism means that all existents are material, and that matter is in constant motion and continual development. This in turn means the rejection of all metaphysical factors that religions believe in, and the regarding of matter as the sole active, moving agent in this life.

This materialist theory is rejected by innate human reason, because inanimate matter cannot transform itself; it must have a transformer or an external agent acting upon it. Just as ropes and rocks do not turn into buildings, and various metals do not become machines and mechanical equipment except by human action, so matter in general is not the agent in everything, because it is acted upon, not acting.

If it is established that matter must have an active power, and that it cannot move or develop spontaneously without an external agent, and that man is this external agent, then man becomes the possessor of the fundamental value in existence—not as Marxism claims.

Consequently, man and matter, as two created, limited forces acting in fluctuating and deficient ways, indicate a persistent deficiency in them. By their very existence, impotence, and limitation they confirm the existence of unseen powers behind matter and behind man, and above matter and above man.

Matter and man are created; therefore they are deficient, impotent, and perishable, and they need a Creator who existed before them, without needing anyone else—He is Allah, whose existence is proven by His works and His creation.

## Communism Is the Twin of Atheism

The materialist evaluation by Communism of the universe, man, and life made it deny the existence of Allah and deny the other unseen matters such as the soul, life, the *dār* (دار) [abode], reckoning, recompense, the *jinn* (جن), and the *malāʾikah* (ملائكة) [angels]. The following are testimonies to that:

Marx said, “There is no god, and life is matter.”

Lenin said in 1907: “It is not true that Allah is the one who regulates the universe. What is true is that Allah is a mythical idea fabricated by man to justify his impotence. Therefore, everyone who defends the idea of Allah is an ignorant and impotent person.”

In a speech of Lenin at the Russian Congress in 1919: “The upbringing and education of children must aim at inoculating them with communist morals, but these morals are not derived from divine commandments, for we do not believe in Allah.”

Lenin also said, “Every religious idea and every belief in Allah—nay, even mere thinking about Allah—is a baseness hidden in the soul.”

In 1935, the newspaper *Sovetskaya Pravda* published: “Belief in Allah is the legacy of the ancient brutes.”

In 1908 it published: “It is our duty to direct a campaign of correct thought against religion.”

Moscow Radio broadcast on 5 April 1958: “Just as the existence of microbes and different tendencies makes one of them expel the other.”

The newspaper *Turkmenskaya Iskra* published on 1 December of a certain year: “The Islamic religious creed is the obscurantist force that still corrupts minds and the life of peoples, impedes progress, and stands like a barrier in the path of happiness, light, and knowledge. Religious rites are still firmly attached, and religion has not ceased to be the opium of the people for some.”

The newspaper *Bakinsky Rabochy* published on 1 December 1956: “If Allah existed, He would not allow us to abandon religion.”

The newspaper *Krasny Znat* (or *Il’m al-Ahmar*) on 1 March 1931: “It is natural that the struggle between atheism and belief in Allah has not yet ended, and it is necessary to direct the masses toward uprooting the roots of belief in superstitions, the jinn, and gods more deeply than has happened so far.”

In the program of the Sixth International Communist Congress held in Moscow in 1928: “The war against religion—the opium of the people—occupies an important place in the works of the cultural revolution, and this war must continue with determination and in an organized manner.”

A group of Muslim refugees from the Soviet Union related moving stories of torture, starvation, and killing that they had suffered terribly at the hands of the Soviets. They say that the number of Muslims in the Soviet Union dropped over the past thirty years by half or more. They relate that the Soviets violated the sanctity of mosques, executed tens of thousands of Muslims, and sent others to concentration camps in Siberia to eliminate Islam in the Soviet Union.

Nor will Muslims—or their descendants—forget that Islamic conference held in Khokand in 1912, in the early days of Communism, demanding self-rule for Turkestan. Then Communist soldiers attacked the city in a savage, criminal raid, killed thousands of Muslims, demolished their homes, confiscated their properties and wealth, and after that the terrible famine occurred in which an innumerable number of Muslims died.

Russian sources themselves estimated the number of Muslim victims of Soviet rule between 1918 and 1919 at one million Muslims.

## Historical Materialism

Historical materialism is the second principle upon which Marxism rests. It is the view that claims the history of humanity is nothing but a collection of human acts and events that occurred and were driven by purely material motives.

Marx says: “It is not ideas, as is often repeated, that lead the world. Rather, these ideas themselves are connected to economic conditions,” meaning that they depend on matter, which ultimately explains history. Economics, which includes all human efforts to possess and use matter, constitutes the basic structure of human relations, while intellectual doctrines are no more than a superstructure.

Likewise, a translation of the Qur’an into a language other than Arabic is not considered Qur’an.

**Second:** It has been transmitted to us by *tawātur*, meaning that it was transmitted from the Prophet by groups whose number precludes the possibility of collusion upon a lie, and then transmitted from these by others in the same manner, until it reached us in successive generations, preserved in people’s hearts without addition or omission. For Allah, the Exalted, has undertaken to preserve it. He, Mighty and Majestic, said:

> Indeed, it is We who sent down the Qur’an and indeed, We will be its guardian. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥijr 15:9]

**Third:** It is miraculous, in the sense that all created beings are incapable of producing anything like it. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Say, "If mankind and the jinn gathered in order to produce the like of this Qur’an, they could not produce the like of it, even if they were to each other assistants." [Qur’an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:88]

##### Its Rulings

The rulings of the Qur’an are of three kinds:

1. **Rulings relating to creed**, such as faith in Allah, His messengers, and the Last Day, and so on.
2. **Rulings relating to the purification and refinement of souls**, and to the explanation of the noble virtues that one must adorn oneself with, and the vices that one must shun.
3. **Rulings relating to the words and deeds of accountable persons** beyond the two preceding categories. These are the practical rulings that fall within the sphere of *fiqh* (فقه), and they are of two divisions: devotional acts and transactions.

#### 2. The Sunnah

It is the second original source of Islamic legislation. What is meant by it is whatever issued from the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, in word, deed, quality, or tacit approval.

##### Recording the Sunnah

The Sunnah was not officially recorded during the lifetime of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, as the Qur’an was, because the Companions relied on their memories and were occupied with recording the Qur’an during that period. But this does not mean that nothing of the Sunnah was written down; rather, it is established that some Companions did so for their own benefit.

In the first and second centuries *hijrī*, repeated attempts at recording the Sunnah were undertaken. In the third century, a group of eminent scholars took up the task of recording the Sunnah, compiling it, and distinguishing its sound reports from others. At the forefront of these were the two great Imāms, al-Bukhārī and Muslim.

Thus the Sunnah came to have refined, sound compilations, foremost among which were the Six Books: *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī*, *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*, *al-Muwaṭṭaʾ* of Mālik, *al-Jāmiʿ* of al-Tirmidhī, *Sunan Abī Dāwūd*, *Sunan al-Nasāʾī*, and *Sunan Ibn Mājah*.

##### Sciences of Ḥadīth

Among the fruits of the efforts that accomplished the recording of the Sunnah was the establishment of scientific rules and principles for handling *aḥādīth* and classifying them. Of these are:

1. **The science of ḥadīth terminology** (*ʿilm muṣṭalaḥ al-ḥadīth*). This science deals with the foundational rules for accepting a *ḥadīth*, assessing it, and classifying it in light of that as *ṣaḥīḥ* (صحيح), *ḥasan* (حسن), *ḍaʿīf* (ضعيف), or what branches from each of these categories.

2. **The science of jarḥ and taʿdīl** (الجرح والتعديل). It is the science of evaluating men; it investigates the conditions of transmitters, their trustworthiness, reliability, uprightness, and so forth.

##### Evidence for the Authority of the Sunnah and Its Being a Source of Legislation

1. **The explicit statement that the Prophet does not speak from whim.** Allah, the Exalted, said:

> Nor does he speak from [his own] inclination. It is not but a revelation revealed. [Qur’an, An-Najm 53:3–4]

2. **The command to obey the Messenger.** Allah said:

> Say, "Obey Allah and the Messenger." [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:32]

3. **Making obedience to the Messenger obedience to Allah:**

> He who obeys the Messenger has obeyed Allah. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:80]

4. **The command to follow what the Messenger brings us:**

> And whatever the Messenger has given you — take; and what he has forbidden you — refrain from. [Qur’an, Al-Ḥashr 59:7]

5. **The obligation to refer disputed matters to Allah and to His Messenger:**

> And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:59]

6. **The obligation to submit the dispute to the Messenger and to accept his judgment:**

> But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muḥammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:65]

7. **The warning of painful torment for those who oppose the Messenger:**

> So let those beware who dissent from the Prophet's order, lest fitnah strike them or a painful punishment. [Qur’an, An-Nūr 24:63]

8. **The Messenger was given the authority to explain the rulings of the Qur’an**, for it contains general rulings that require elaboration before legal responsibility can be validly imposed. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> And We revealed to you the message that you may make clear to the people what was sent down to them and that they might give thought. [Qur’an, An-Naḥl 16:44]

##### Types of Sunnah with Regard to Its Chain

By its chain is meant its transmitters. In this respect, if the chain is connected to the Prophet, it is of three types—unless the chain is not connected to the Prophet because the *tābiʿī* (تابعي) did not mention the name of the *ṣaḥābī* (صحابي) from whom he narrated the *Sunnah* from the Prophet; in that case it is called a *murassa*l (مرسل) hadith. The three types are:

1. **A *mutawātir* Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by a group whose collusion upon a lie is inconceivable and whose number cannot be counted, then transmitted from them by another group with the same quality until it reached us. Of this type are the practical *sunan* that explain the amounts of *zakāh*, the acts of *ḥajj*, and the form and pillars of *ṣalāh*. These convey certain knowledge.

2. **A *mashhūr* (مشهور) Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by one or two individuals—i.e. a number falling short of *tawātur*—then it became widely known and was transmitted by *tawātur* groups in the era of the *tābiʿīn* and the successors of the *tābiʿīn*.

3. **An *āḥād* (آحاد) Sunnah:** what was narrated from the Prophet by a number that did not reach the level of *tawātur* and did not later become widely known—i.e. it is neither *mutawātir* nor *mashhūr*. This type conveys probable but preponderant knowledge of its sound attribution to the Prophet, and it is obligatory to act upon it.

##### Types of Rulings Brought by the Sunnah

1. **Rulings that agree with and confirm Qur’anic rulings**, such as the *ḥadīth*: “The wealth of a Muslim is not lawful to another Muslim except by his good pleasure.” [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Financial Transactions] It agrees with and confirms the words of Allah, the Exalted:

> O you who have believed, do not consume one another's wealth unjustly but only [in lawful] business by mutual consent. [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:29]

Likewise, the *Sunnah* prohibits disobedience to parents, false testimony, and killing the soul, and the like, just as they are mentioned in the Qur’an.

2. **Rulings that explain and detail what is general in the Qur’an**, such as the *Sunnah* that explains the amounts of *zakāh* and the amount of stolen wealth for which the thief’s hand is cut off.

3. **Rulings that restrict an absolute statement of the Book and specify its general wording.** Some rulings come in the Qur’an in absolute terms, and the *Sunnah* restricts them. For example, the cutting off of the thief’s hand came in absolute terms, but the *Sunnah* restricted it to the wrist, i.e. the hand is cut off from the wrist.

4. **New rulings not mentioned in the Qur’an**, for the *Sunnah* is independent in legislating rulings, and in this it is like the Qur’an. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> Indeed, I have been given the Qur’an and something like it with it. [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Sunnah, Hadith 4604]

Of this type are the prohibition of the blood of domesticated donkeys, the prohibition of eating every predatory animal with a fang and every bird with a talon, judgment by a single witness together with an oath, the obligation of blood-money on the *ʿāqilah* (عاقلة), the inheritance of the grandmother, and the like.

### Second: Subordinate Sources

#### 1. *Ijmāʿ* (الإجماع)

It is, in the terminology of the jurists and the scholars of *uṣūl* (أصول الفقه), the agreement of the *mujtahidīn* (المجتهدين) of the Muslim *ummah* in a given age, after the death of the Prophet, upon a legal ruling.

*Ijmāʿ* is a source of legislation and one of the evidences of rulings. This is established by numerous texts from the Book and the *Sunnah*.

**The basis of *ijmāʿ*:** *Ijmāʿ* must rest upon an evidence, for a statement on legal matters without evidence is error, and the Muslim *ummah* does not agree upon error. The basis of *ijmāʿ* may be a text from the Book of Allah or a *Sunnah* of His Prophet, or it may be analogical. For example, the prohibition of marriage to granddaughters no matter how far down the line is based on the text of the Qur’an:

> Prohibited to you [for marriage] are your mothers, your daughters… [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:23]

And the consensus on the prohibition of pork fat is by analogy to the prohibition of its meat, and so on.

#### 2. *Qiyās* (القياس)

It is, in the terminology of the scholars, the extension of a ruling from an original case, concerning which a text has come, to a new case on account of the equality of the two cases in the effective cause (*ʿillah* (علة)) of the ruling. Thus, when an original case exists concerning which a text has come and we know the *ʿillah* of the ruling, and then a new case occurs concerning which no text has come, the new case takes the ruling of the original case.

**Examples of *qiyās*:**

- The ruling on drinking wine is prohibition, because a text has come to that effect. The *ʿillah* of the ruling is intoxication; therefore, every beverage containing this *ʿillah* is likewise prohibited by analogy to wine.

- Killing the heir to take his inheritance: a text has come concerning this case, and its ruling is the exclusion of the killer from inheritance. The *ʿillah* of the ruling is the heir’s haste to take the inheritance before its proper time, so he is punished by being deprived of it. The killing of a beneficiary named in a will by the testator is a case concerning which no text has come, but it contains the same *ʿillah*—haste to take a thing before its due time. Therefore it takes the same ruling: the beneficiary-killer is deprived of his right in the will, just as the heir-killer is deprived of his right in the inheritance.

#### 3. *Istiḥsān* (الاستحسان)

It is, in the terminology of the scholars, the departure from an obvious analogy to a hidden analogy for an evidence that requires this departure. An example is that donations are not valid from one legally interdicted because of imbecility, and among these donations is the endowment (*waqf* (وقف)). Yet by way of *istiḥsān* an exception is made to this principle, allowing his endowment upon himself for his own benefit. The reason for this is the preservation of his wealth and preventing his means of livelihood from becoming a burden upon others.

#### 4. *Maṣāliḥ Mursalah* (المصالح المرسلة)

It is a jurisprudential source whose authority is established by an inductive survey of the texts and rulings of the *Sharīʿah* in the Book and the *Sunnah*, and by the practice of the jurists among the Companions. This source is broad enough for new events and evolving occurrences; it makes *fiqh* flexible and dynamic, enabling it to embrace the problems of life in every age. The jurists adopted this source and relied upon it.

*Maṣāliḥ mursalah*, then, are interests for which the Lawgiver has not laid down specific rulings to secure them, and for which no particular evidence establishes or negates them. Therefore, every occurrence that contains no text, no *ijmāʿ*, no *qiyās*, and no *istiḥsān*, but contains a benefit for people, permits the *mujtahid* to find the appropriate ruling to secure it—that is, to secure this benefit for the people.

**Examples of *maṣlaḥah mursalah*:**

- The compilation of the Qur’an into a single *muṣḥaf* by Abū Bakr.
- The registration of the *dawāwīn* (الدواوين) by ʿUmar.
- Making artisans liable for the loss of people’s property caused by their hands.
- The right of the ruler to levy taxes upon the wealthy in case of need, and the like.

#### 5. *Sadd al-Dharāʾiʿ* (سد الذرائع)

*Dharāʾiʿ* means means. If a means leads to what is forbidden and corrupt, that means is forbidden, and it is obligatory to block it—that is, to prevent it in order to cut off the material of corrupt means. If, on the other hand, a means leads to something desired in the *Sharīʿah*, that means is likewise desired. Thus *dharāʾiʿ* are blocked and prevented when they lead to corruption, and are required and opened when they lead to benefits.

Because the term *dharāʾiʿ* is most often applied to actions and methods that lead to evil and corruption, it has come to be called *sadd al-dharāʾiʿ*.

**The authority of this principle:**

The principle of blocking means is attested as sound by evidences from the Book, the *Sunnah*, and the practice of the Companions. From the Book is the saying of Allah, the Exalted:

> And do not insult those they invoke other than Allah, lest they insult Allah in enmity without knowledge. [Qur’an, Al-Anʿām 6:108]

And from the *Sunnah* is the Prophet’s prohibition, peace and blessings be upon him, of *iḥtikār* (احتكار) as a means of blocking the way to usury.

Blocking the means is therefore a valid principle and a jurisprudential source from which rulings are drawn. The leading *mujtahid* Imāms adopted it, and the most committed to this source were Imām Mālik and Imām Aḥmad ibn Ḥanbal.

# Characteristics of the Islamic Methodology

#### The Three Hypotheses for the Existence of This Universe

There are three hypotheses for the existence and origin of this universe:

1. That this universe created itself by itself.
2. That it came about by chance.
3. That behind its existence there is a power.

As for the first possibility, no rational person holds it, for existing things do not possess the capacity to act until after they exist. So how could the act of bringing-into-existence proceed from them when they have not yet come to exist? This possibility is rejected instinctively because it collides with the principle of causality — the first principle of reason, which negates the occurrence of any event without a cause, and which makes the existence of a cause for the origin of this universe a decisive matter admitting of no debate. Historians relate that some heretics (*zanādiqa*, زنادقة) requested an appointment with Abū Ḥanīfa (may Allah be pleased with him) to debate him about Allah. When the appointed time came, he delayed. Then he came to them after they had despaired of his coming. They reproached him for being late, so he apologized, saying: "I came to you at the appointed time, but I waited a long while on the bank of the Tigris searching for a boatman to ferry me across, and I found none. So when I despaired, a plank of wood came along of its own accord, and I attached pieces of it together until it became, in my hand, a fine vessel; so I boarded it, crossed the river, and came to you." The heretics all said to Abū Ḥanīfa, "Are you mocking us? Can a plank come along of its own accord and become a boat?" He replied, "This is what you gathered to debate with me about. If you cannot believe that a boat makes itself, how do you want me to believe — indeed, how do you believe in your own minds — that this precise, wondrous universe brought about the changes within it by itself, without a Great Creator?" Thus the heretics were dumbfounded, the decisive proof stood against them, and they accepted Islam at his hand (may Allah be pleased with him). By this, the first possibility is rejected by instinct.

#### The Argument from Chance

As for the second possibility, it is held by the materialists. The truth is that chance does not exist; man resorts to it when he is ignorant of the cause, but once he discovers it he denies chance. The scientist Chris Morrison struck a simple example to refute the principle of chance. He said: "Take ten small wooden pieces of equal size, number them sequentially from 1 to 10, place them in your pocket, and shake it well. Then try to pull them out of your pocket in their order from 1 to 10. The chance of pulling out piece number 1 will be 1 in 10; the chance of pulling out 1 and 2 consecutively will be 1 in 100; and the chance of pulling out 1, 2, and 3 consecutively will be 1 in 1,000, and so on, until the chance of pulling out all of them in order from 1 to 10 becomes 1 in 10 billion." The point of this simple example is to show how enormously the odds pile up against chance. Therefore, life on our earth necessarily requires numerous essential conditions, such that it would be impossible for them to coincide, follow one another, and remain regular by mere chance.

Thus we find that no one who is ignorant of inquiry, or an obstinate who sees the truth and turns away from it, says that chance accounts for the comprehensive, precise order of existence. It is enough to reflect upon some of the signs of existence in a scientific manner for chance and its illusions to vanish and be replaced by rulings explained by their causes.

The following is a selection of examples of the precision of cosmic order, which refutes the claim of chance:

1. If the earth's crust were thicker than it is, it would absorb carbon dioxide and oxygen, and there would be no life.
2. If the atmosphere were lower in height, some of the meteors that burn by the millions every day in the outer air would strike every part of the earth and set ablaze everything flammable.
3. If our sun gave half its present radiation, we would have frozen; and if it increased by half, we would have been ash long ago.
4. If our moon were at a distance of 51,001 miles instead of its present distance, the tide would be so powerful that all lands would be flooded twice a day by sweeping water that would displace the very mountains.
5. If our night were ten times longer than it is now, the hot summer sun would burn our vegetation by day, and by night every plant on earth would freeze.
6. If oxygen were 100% or more in the air instead of 21%, flammable materials would become liable to ignite at the first spark.
7. If the oceans' water were fresh, it would putrefy and life on earth would become impossible; for it is salt that prevents putrefaction and corruption. And were it not that chlorine combines with sodium, there would be no salt, and consequently no life.
8. If the laws of gravity did not exist, from where would atoms and their molecules meet and cohere?
9. If the earth's axis were perpendicular instead of having its present inclination, summer would be eternal and winter perpetual.
10. If the earth were like Mercury, rotating only one face toward the sun, no one would live in the perpetual night or the perpetual day, and there would be no life. By this, too, the claim of chance is rejected.

So the third hypothesis remains — the true hypothesis held by the believers, accepted by minds: that behind this universe there is a Creator.

#### The Human Power of Perception

Someone may say: If we grant the third hypothesis, that behind this universe there is a Creator Who is Allah, does it not follow that we must see Him with our senses? To answer this question, we must explain to the questioner that our senses — which he thinks are the sole path to knowledge of existence and which are the short-range, limited gateways around us in both extent and quality — are limited. From this:

1. Space is full of images we cannot see with our eyes because their vibrations do not match the vibrations of our sight; and it is full of sounds above or below the level of our hearing, which we can only hear by means of special electronic devices; and the earth is full of minerals our senses cannot perceive or distinguish without special devices.

So if our senses are incapable of perceiving the small, sensory things close around us, how could they possibly encompass the great, extraordinary matters beyond nature? Hence man must submit to the existence of things beyond his senses through their effects, for his senses may not help him to touch or see them.

#### Refuting the Doctrine of the Eternity of Existence

Someone may say that existent things are eternal and have no beginning. To discuss this hypothesis we must ask about the variety among these existent things. For it is observed that some of them are inanimate, devoid of life; some are alive, growing and multiplying; and some — namely man — are rational living beings. Is this variety eternal? Or did it arise in existent things after a certain time?

If the variety in the world is eternal, what is the rational justification for it? Why are some existent things rational, thinking, and alive, while others are alive without the capacity for thought and reason, and still others are inanimate, possessing neither life nor thought? The only rational justification is the existence of another power behind this material world that willed this variety for a certain purpose. This leads us to submit to the existence of Allah, glorified and exalted is He.

If the variety is adventitious — that is, all existent things were originally one thing, and then variety arose in them after a period of time — then we must ask: Did this variety arise suddenly, or was it the result of an evolution that took a long time? If the variety in existent things arose in the world suddenly, what is the cause? There is no cause acceptable to reason except that the occurrence of this variety resulted from a will behind this universe, stronger than this world, possessing the power to affect it. This also leads us to submit to the existence of Allah, glorified and exalted is He.

And if this variety arose in the world through evolution over long periods of time, as dialectical materialism claims, then the following is replied to it:

First: The phenomenon of evolution itself requires an acceptable rational explanation. Why did existent things possess the quality of evolution and not be inanimate? Does this question not lead us to submit to the existence of Allah Who granted these existent things the quality of evolution in order to reach a noble goal?

Second: Furthermore, evolution, as observed, is not absolute evolution but evolution constrained within the limits of the original properties of the existent thing. Human history has not known, since the earliest centuries, an evolution that transformed dust into an animal or an animal into a human. Dust may turn to rock, and rock may crumble into dust under certain conditions and factors; and a living worm may come forth from a fruit, because the properties of life are originally present in the fruit. But it is observed that this evolution is confined within the limits of these things and does not go beyond them. It is also observed that man, across thousands of years, has not undergone any evolution whatsoever in his life or in benefiting from his characteristics and qualities.

So if the variety of existent things occurred through evolution, why has this evolution stopped since thousands of years ago? Does this not compel us to acknowledge the existence of a unique rational power that evolved existent things until this species came to be? And if evolution itself needs Allah, why do we not acknowledge, from the outset, that Allah is the One Who created existent things with the variety we see?

It is clear from this that the assertion of the eternity of existent things does not relieve man of the necessity of acknowledging the existence of Allah. And if we add to this that this hypothesis is no more than a conjecture unsupported by any proof, we realize that it is merely an attempt to evade acknowledging the existence of Allah, dictated by man's inability to know the origin and cause of creation.

## Refuting Materialism

The call to Islam must confront materialism at its root, for materialism denies the existence of Allah and reduces life to mere matter. The arguments for Allah's existence are, at the same time, a refutation of materialism. The purpose of proving the existence of Allah is therefore inseparable from the refutation of the materialist conception of life:

1. Refuting the assertion of the materiality of life, and consequently refuting all ideas and schools that branch out from it.
2. Proving that faith in Allah makes obedience to Him and compliance with His commands possible, and affirming the need for guidance.
3. Proving that obedience to Allah and compliance with His command require faith in His prophets and belief in His Books.
4. Proving that Islam is the final message, that it is a *manhaj* of life, and that faith in it requires working for it and *jihād* on its behalf.

In addition, the refutation of the claim that this universe came about by chance — and the demonstration that behind it lies a wise Creator — strikes at the foundation of materialist philosophy. It shows that the order, precision, and purposeful variety in existence cannot be explained by blind material processes, but point instead to a Creator Who originated and governs all things.

## The Afterlife

The brother caller must explain to the one being called, through this topic, the purpose of life as Islam has defined it: that it is, in this life, our path to the Hereafter (*ākhirah*, آخرة); that Allah did not create us in it in vain and without purpose, as the atheists and materialists believe; but rather to test us in what He has given us; and that man is bound by his deed, whether good or evil; and that the Hereafter is the abode of final settlement — either to Paradise or to the Fire. And that this creed, awareness of it, faith in it, and constant reflection upon it are the strong shield and the safeguard against deviation and straying.

Let his simplification of the topic take the following form:

#### The Value of Belief in the Hereafter

Belief in the Hereafter is a fundamental pillar of the Islamic creed (*ʿaqīdah*, عقيدة) and one of the fruits of faith in Allah. It is one of the decisive dividing lines and points of divergence between this creed and materialist creeds.

For Islam, when it establishes that Allah is the Creator and Originator of the universe, and that the universe did not come about by chance or as the result of mere chemical material reactions, has taken with man the first foundational step on the path of knowledge and awareness. From this point all other points of knowledge and awareness branch forth, and consequently behavior and conduct are reflected.

For when man believes in the existence of Allah, it becomes incumbent upon him to believe in His greatness, power, and all His attributes, and that He, glorified is He, is too exalted to create anything in vain. This human being was created by Allah's command to be an agent of reform and cultivation, not a tool of corruption and destruction:

> Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion, and He is over all things competent — [He] who created death and life to test you [as to] which of you is best in deed — and He is the Exalted in Might, the Forgiving. [Qur'an, Al-Mulk 67:1-2]

And at the end of this journey he will encounter the destiny he chose for himself, in complete freedom:

> So as for he who transgressed and preferred the life of the world, then indeed, Hellfire will be [his] refuge. But as for he who feared the position of his Lord and prevented the soul from [unlawful] inclination, then indeed, Paradise will be [his] refuge. [Qur'an, An-Nāziʿāt 79:37-40]

This creed pulls man upward psychologically, always. He does not see this world as everything; rather he sees it as a path to the Hereafter. He sees his deed as a vehicle to the abode of final settlement, and his place in that abode is tied to what his hands have presented and what provision he has taken from his worldly life:

> Whoever should desire the immediate — We hasten for him from it what We will to whom We intend. Then We have made for him Hell, which he will [enter to] burn, censured and banished. But whoever desires the Hereafter and exerts the effort due to it while he is a believer — it is those whose effort is ever appreciated [by Allah]. [Qur'an, Al-Isrāʾ 17:18-19]

This overflowing feeling is bound to play a great role in directing man's life and protecting it from everything that harms it or leads to its ruin. As for those who deny the existence of Allah, they also deny and reject resurrection. Their denial and rejection of resurrection naturally make this world the beginning and end of their thought. And this, without doubt, is a fatal evil.

#### The Value of This World Relative to the Hereafter

The brother must also present the reality of this world and the extent of its value relative to the Hereafter. Its bliss is not worth anything compared to the bliss of the Hereafter, for the bliss of the Hereafter is the true life:

> And this worldly life is not but diversion and amusement. And indeed, the home of the Hereafter — that is the [eternal] life, if only they knew. [Qur'an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:64]

Let him also support this by the *ḥadīth* of the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him):

> "The most fortunate of the people of this world, of those destined for the Fire, will be brought forth on the Day of Resurrection and dipped once in the Fire. Then it will be said: O son of Adam, have you ever seen any good? Has any pleasure ever passed you by? He will say: No, by Allah, O Lord. And the most wretched of people in misery in this world, of the people of Paradise, will be dipped once in Paradise. Then it will be said to him: O son of Adam, have you ever seen any misery? Has any hardship ever befallen you? He will say: No, by Allah, O Lord; no misery has befallen me and I have seen no hardship." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith]

This conception of the reality of this world is bound to make man not attached to it or dependent upon it. Thus his habit in his worldly life will be to do everything that pleases Allah and to take provision for his Hereafter, following in that the testament of the Greatest Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him):

> "Be in this world as a stranger or a wayfarer." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Heart-Softening]

And once man succeeds in rousing his soul from its dust, he is able to push it forward and elevate it through the degrees of human perfection. Thus the essential fruit is realized from the interaction of creed with the soul — namely, noble moral character.

## Divine Decree

In this session the brother must explain to the one being called the creed of Divine Decree (*qaḍāʾ wa-l-qadar*, القضاء والقدر) in a simple, clear style. For the erroneous conception of *qaḍāʾ* and *qadar* has serious repercussions and evil effects in people's lives. Therefore it is the duty of the callers to give it great importance and place it at the beginning of the discussion of creedal topics.

#### The Message of Divine Decree

Among the accusations hurled by the enemies of Islam at the creed of *qadar* is that it was one of the most important causes of the weakness and backwardness of the Muslims, because it paralyzes the faculties and powers and inclines those who believe in it to laziness and complacency, waiting for what the unseen will bring.

#### The Jabriyya: An Erroneous Conception

The belief that Allah, exalted is He, has written upon man all his actions and deeds — that his misguidance, if he is misguided, and his guidance, if he is guided, and his uprightness, if he is upright, and his deviation, if he deviates, are all written upon him from pre-eternity, and he has no power to repel or prevent it — so that he is driven, devoid of will, stripped of thought, carried along by the ship of destinies on a swelling sea, unable to bring any benefit or harm to himself: this determinist (*jabrī*, جبري) belief is contrary, in reality, to the Islamic conception of the creed of *qaḍāʾ* and *qadar*, contradicts the creed of the righteous predecessors (*Salaf al-Ṣāliḥ*, السلف الصالح), and consequently contradicts the simplest rules of Divine justice. For if man were compelled in everything he does, there would be no reason for the existence of reason and perception, and no reason for the existence of revealed laws and statutes — since man would be obliged to do what he does without will or choice?

The truth is that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), when asked about faith, mentioned among its pillars: "And that you believe in the *qadar*, its good and its evil, [that it is] from Allah." This means that both good and evil occur in the universe by measures, scales, laws, and causes ordained by the wisdom of Allah, exalted is He, and that Allah did not create anything except by His will, and that everything that exists in the universe accords with what preceded in His knowledge.

ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib (may Allah be pleased with him) refuted the Jabriyya in the presence of a man who argued by *qadar*, and said to him: "Perhaps you think that the decree is inevitable and the judgment absolute? If that were so, reward and punishment, promise and threat, command and prohibition would be nullified; there would be no rebuke from Allah for a sinner, no praise for a doer of good; the doer of good would not be more deserving of praise than the evildoer, nor the evildoer more deserving of blame than the doer of good. That is the saying of the worshippers of idols, the soldiers of Satan, the false witnesses, the people blind to the truth, the *Qadariyya* of this *ummah*, and its Magians." [Nahj al-Balāgha]

Allah created everything, and placed in every thing specific properties. He placed in fire the property of burning, in water the property of giving life, in the knife the property of cutting, and in wine the property of intoxication. So if man uses fire to harm, he does so by his own will, even though the effectiveness of burning is originally decreed in fire by the will of Allah, exalted is He.

The sphere of Allah's will, exalted is He, thus encompasses the properties of all things in this life. As for the sphere of man's will, it is confined to the scope of disposing of these properties, and this is the locus of moral responsibility (*taklīf*, تكليف).

From this, Allah, exalted is He, placed in the sexual act the property of procreation; so if someone practices it in fornication, the burden of sin is written upon him for that.

Hence we understand that these properties with which Allah distinguished things — and though the susceptibility of some of them to evil is from Allah, exalted is He — Allah did not make this nature in such a way that it compels man to evil. Rather, man has, by what Allah bestowed upon him of a freely choosing intellect, freedom in testing and preferring:

> There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion. The right course has become clear from the wrong. So whoever disbelieves in *ṭāghūt* and believes in Allah has grasped the most trustworthy handhold with no break in it. And Allah is Hearing and Knowing. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:256]

> And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness — he has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]. [Qur'an, Ash-Shams 91:7-10]

> Those who associated with Allah will say, "If Allah had willed, we would not have associated [anything] and neither would our fathers, nor would we have prohibited anything." Likewise did those before deny until they tasted Our punishment. Say, "Do you have any knowledge that you can produce for us? You follow not except assumption, and you are not but falsifying." Say, "With Allah is the far-reaching argument. If He had willed, He would have guided you all." [Qur'an, Al-Anʿām 6:148-149]

#### Guidance and Misguidance

Guidance and misguidance are in the hand of Allah. Some have taken as evidence the verse in which Allah, exalted is He, says:

> Then, is one who has deserved the decree of punishment [to be guided]? Then, can you save one who is in the Fire? [Qur'an, Az-Zumar 39:19]

And His saying:

> Indeed, [O Muhammad], you do not guide whom you like, but Allah guides whom He wills. And He is most knowing of the [rightly] guided. [Qur'an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:56]

The Qur'ān explains these two matters as being based on a prior deservingness of the servants, and it points out their causes with verses such as His saying:

> Indeed, Allah does not guide the wrongdoing people. [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:51]

> Indeed, Allah does not guide he who is a liar and [confirmed] disbeliever. [Qur'an, Az-Zumar 39:3]

> And when they deviated, Allah caused their hearts to deviate. [Qur'an, Aṣ-Ṣaff 61:5]

> Thus does Allah seal over every heart [belonging to] an arrogant tyrant. [Qur'an, Ghāfir 40:35]

> And He misleads not except the defiantly disobedient. [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:26]

> And Allah sends astray the wrongdoers. [Qur'an, Ibrāhīm 14:27]

The possessors of these blameworthy traits do not deserve guidance and are far from the mercy of Allah.

As for those who deserve guidance, their traits are the likes of these:

> And whoever believes in Allah — He will guide his heart. [Qur'an, At-Taghābun 64:11]

> Say, "Indeed, Allah leaves astray whom He wills and guides to Himself whoever turns back [to Him]." [Qur'an, Ar-Raʿd 13:27]

> By which Allah guides those who pursue His pleasure to the ways of peace and brings them out from darknesses into the light, by His permission, and guides them to a straight path. [Qur'an, Al-Māʾidah 5:16]

The Qur'ān, in many of its verses, affirms choice for man, that he is accountable for his deeds, and that the corruption of which he complains in his life and the kinds of evil widespread in his affairs are all results of prior causes that man himself laid down. Allah, exalted is He, says:

> Corruption has appeared throughout the land and sea by [reason of] what the hands of people have earned so He may let them taste part of [the consequence of] what they have done that perhaps they will return [to righteousness]. [Qur'an, Ar-Rūm 30:41]

> And whatever strikes you of disaster — it is for what your hands have earned; but He pardons much. [Qur'an, Ash-Shūrā 42:30]

> Indeed, Allah will not change the condition of a people until they change what is in themselves. [Qur'an, Ar-Raʿd 13:11]

#### Conclusion

From what has preceded we can say that the commands of Allah, exalted is He, in the Qur'ān are of two kinds:

First, creative commands: such as His command to non-rational created beings — "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Abraham" — and "O reassured soul, return to your Lord, well-pleased and pleasing [to Him]." These commands require immediate obedience, and man has no control over them; consequently he is not responsible for them at all.

Thus man's lifespan, form, height, stature, skin color, livelihood, and the other laws upon which human life is established and by which this vast universe runs are creative commands decreed by Allah, exalted is He, to be as they are, and man is not blamed for them.

Second, legislative commands: these are the commands with which man is charged and recompensed for their consequence, good or evil — such as His saying: "Tell the believing men to reduce [some] of their vision and guard their private parts," and His saying: "O you who have believed, do not consume usury, doubled and multiplied," and His saying: "O you who have believed, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire." In this sphere of legislative commands man has the right of preference and choice; indeed he is the one who weighs and chooses. By that he is responsible for his deeds and accountable for them:

> So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it. [Qur'an, Az-Zalzalah 99:7-8]

# Major Outlines of the Islamic Method

In the topics of *sharīʿah* (الشريعة):

- In the worship system
- In the social system
- In the economic system
- In the political system

## The Worship System

It is the system that regulates the relationship between the servant and his Lord, and the acts that draw one near to Allah the Exalted, purifying the soul, elevating it through the stations of devotion and perfection, and realizing the innate balance between the needs of the soul and the needs of the body in human life. It comprises the Five Pillars of Islam. On the authority of ʿUmar (may Allah be pleased with him), the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is none worthy of worship except Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakāh, fasting Ramaḍān, and performing Hajj to the House if one is able.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 8; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 16]

### Prayer (*al-ṣalāh*)

**Its ruling:** Prayer is the pillar of religion and one of the pillars of Islam; it is an individual obligation upon every legally responsible person. Allah the Exalted says:

> “Maintain with care the [obligatory] prayers and [in particular] the middle prayer and stand in devout obedience to Allah.” [Qur’an, An-Nisa 4:103]

**Its wisdom:** Prayer is the *miʿrāj* (معراج) of the believer to Allah, and his spiritual space that illumines his heart, purifies his soul, and disciplines his limbs, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing, and the remembrance of Allah is greater. And Allah knows that which you do.” [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:45]

And in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

> “Tell me, if there were a river at the door of one of you in which he bathed five times a day, would any filth remain on him?” They said, “No filth would remain on him.” He said, “That is the likeness of the five prayers: by them Allah effaces sins.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Times of Prayer, Hadith 528; Sahih Muslim, Book of Purification, Hadith 671]

**The ruling on one who abandons it:** One who deliberately abandons prayer in Islam is a disbeliever according to the imams of the legal schools, and it is obligatory to kill him. According to some others, he is a transgressor if he leaves it deliberately without denial, and he is to be disciplined or imprisoned until he prays. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “The covenant between us and them is the prayer; whoever abandons it has disbelieved.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 82]

And he said:

> “Between a man and disbelief is the abandonment of prayer.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 82]

**Its conditions:** Purification of the body, garment, and place; entry of the prayer time; facing the *qiblah* (قبلة); covering the *ʿawrah* (عورة); and *wuḍūʾ* (وضوء).

**Its pillars:** Intention; the *takbīrat al-iḥrām* (تكبيرة الإحرام); standing for one who is able; recitation of *al-Fātiḥah* (الفاتحة) in every cycle; bowing with tranquility; rising from bowing with tranquility; prostrating with tranquility; sitting between the two prostrations with tranquility; the final *tashahhud* (تشهد); sending blessings upon the Prophet; and the *taslīm* (تسليم).

**Its nullifiers:** The exit of anything from the front or back; deliberate speech; excessive movement; and leaving a pillar from its pillars or a condition from its conditions.

**Its times:** Fajr, Ẓuhr, ʿAṣr, Maghrib, and ʿIshāʾ.

**The *adhān* and *iqāmah*:** These are two *sunnahs* (سنتان) for every obligatory prayer after the time has entered.

**The Friday Prayer (*ṣalāt al-jumuʿah*):** It is an individual obligation upon resident legally responsible persons; it consists of two *rakʿahs* (ركعتان), preceded by two sermons.

**The Prayer of the Traveler:** The traveler may shorten the four-*rakʿah* prayers to two. He may also combine Ẓuhr and ʿAṣr, and Maghrib and ʿIshāʾ. If he intends residence for four days or more, shortening is not permitted for him. If he stays in a place awaiting his need, he may shorten for up to eighteen days. The distance for shortening is forty-eight miles.

**The Funeral Prayer (*ṣalāt al-janāzah*):** It consists of four *takbīrāt* (تكبيرات): *al-Fātiḥah* is recited in the first; blessings upon the Prophet are sent in the second; supplication is made for the deceased in the third; and *taslīm* is given after the fourth.

**The ʿĪd Prayer:** The ʿĪd prayer is two *rakʿahs*: in the first, three additional *takbīrāt* are made after the opening *takbīr* and before recitation; in the second, three additional *takbīrāt* are made after recitation—according to the Ḥanafī school.

### *Wuḍūʾ*

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; washing the face; washing the arms including the elbows; wiping part of the head; washing the feet including the ankles; and sequence.

**Its *sunnahs*:** saying *bismillāh*; washing the palms; rinsing the mouth; inhaling water into the nose; wiping the entire head; wiping the ears; running fingers through the beard; running water between the fingers of the hands and feet; repeating each washing three times except the head; and performing the acts in immediate succession.

**Its nullifiers:** the exit of anything from the two passages; deep sleep; loss of reason; touching the private parts; and touching a strange woman.

### *Ghusl* (ritual bath)

**Its occasions:** penetration of the glans into the vagina; emission of semen through a wet dream or otherwise; cessation of menstrual or postpartum bleeding; and death.

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; causing water to reach the entire body; rubbing; and performing *wuḍūʾ* before *ghusl*.

### *Tayammum* (dry ablution)

**Its conditions:** absence of water; fear of harm from using it; and entry of the prayer time.

**Its obligatory elements:** intention; wiping the face and hands with clean earth.

**Its *sunnahs*:** saying *bismillāh*; shaking off dust; and performing the acts in immediate succession.

**Its nullifiers:** the same things that nullify *wuḍūʾ*; the availability of water; and the disappearance of the excuse.

### Fasting (*al-ṣawm*)

**Its ruling:** Fasting is one of the pillars of Islam and an obligation upon every legally responsible person able to fast. The traveler and the sick may break their fast and must make it up. One unable to fast due to old age or an illness from which recovery is not hoped for may break the fast and must provide *fiḍyah* (فدية) for each day.

Just as nations train and mobilize their sons to endure hardship and struggle in anticipation of needing them on the day of conflict, so too Islam prescribed fasting at one appointed time, including children and adults, men and women. It is training for the greater and perpetual struggle: the struggle of life waged by all, for which all bear responsibility.

**Its definition:** Abstaining from the things that break the fast from the break of dawn until sunset.

**Its nullifiers:** sexual intercourse; deliberate ejaculation; and deliberate vomiting.

**Permissible matters while fasting:** bathing; taking medicine by injection in the vein or muscle; kissing one’s wife; swallowing saliva; tasting food; and sexual relations between sunset and dawn.

**Recommended voluntary fasts:** It is recommended to fast ʿĀshūrāʾ, the tenth of Dhū al-Ḥijjah, three days of every month, Mondays and Thursdays, and six days of Shawwāl.

### *Zakāh*

**Its ruling and wisdom:** It is one of the pillars of Islam, an obligatory act of worship, and also a social and economic necessity, for it fosters solidarity in Muslim society. Allah the Exalted says:

> “Take, [O Muhammad], from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them to increase.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:103]

And the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “Indeed Allah has made obligatory upon the wealthy Muslims in their wealth a portion for the poor among them sufficient for their needs. The poor will not be exhausted by hunger or nakedness except because of what the wealthy do. Beware, Allah will call them to a severe reckoning and punish them with a painful torment.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Zakah, Hadith 1676; Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Zakah, Hadith 609]

**Its thresholds (*niṣāb*):** The threshold of gold is twenty *dīnārs*; the threshold of silver is two hundred *dirhams*; the rate on each is one-fortieth, that is, two and a half percent.

The threshold of camels begins at five, for which one sheep or goat is due.

The threshold of sheep begins at forty, for which one sheep or goat is due.

The threshold of cattle begins at thirty; when this number is reached, a *tabīʿ* or *tabīʿah* (a calf in its second year) is due.

**Zakāh on trade goods:** Trade goods are valued at the end of the lunar year at their price in the local currency; their zakāh is paid in cash at the rate of one-fortieth.

**Zakāh on crops and fruits:** One-tenth is due on what is irrigated by rain, and one-twentieth on what is irrigated by machines. According to most jurists, the threshold for these is five *wasqs*, i.e., 585 kilograms.

**Its beneficiaries:** Zakāh is disbursed to the eight categories specified in the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler—an obligation [imposed] by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:60]

### *Ḥajj* (pilgrimage)

**Its ruling:** It is obligatory once in a lifetime upon every Muslim who is adult, sane, and able, whether male or female. Evidence for its obligation is the words of Allah the Exalted:

> “And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House—for whoever is able to find thereto a way.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:97]

And the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

> “O people, Hajj has been made obligatory upon you, so perform Hajj.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Hajj, Hadith 1337]

**Its wisdom:** It realizes the benefit of the gathering of Muslims and what results from it of consultation on common affairs and issues, devising solutions for existing and emerging problems, and realizing unity and solidarity. In it human equality is realized, for all stand on one plain and in one garment, with no distinction or preference among them. In it the elevation of the spirit is realized, as the soul strips away its earthly attachments and desires, devoting itself to Allah the Exalted and keeping watch over Him. This has an effect in disciplining and purifying the soul from its stains and burdens.

**Its types:** Hajj is performed in three ways:
- *al-Ifrād*: entering *iḥrām* (الإحرام) for Hajj only from the *mīqāt* (الميقات).
- *al-Tamattuʿ*: entering *iḥrām* for Hajj upon ascending to ʿArafāt.
- *al-Qirān*: entering *iḥrām* for Hajj and ʿUmrah together from the spatial *mīqāt*; the *qārin* (القارن) remains in *iḥrām* until he completes the rites of both ʿUmrah and Hajj.

**Its pillars:** intention at *iḥrām*; standing at ʿArafāt; *ṭawāf* (طواف) of the House in completion; *saʿy* (سعي) between Ṣafā and Marwah in completion; and shaving or cutting the hair.

**Its obligations:** *iḥrām* from the *mīqāt*; spending the night at Muzdalifah; spending the nights in Minā during the days of *tashrīq*; and the farewell *ṭawāf*.

**Its *sunnahs*:** the *talbiyah* (تلبية); the arrival *ṭawāf*; combining night and day at ʿArafāt; bathing upon donning the *iḥrām*; and sacrifice.

**Its prohibitions:** sexual intercourse; kissing; masturbation; perfuming; contracting marriage; wearing stitched garments; and hunting game.

## The Social System

The social system in Islam aims to support family cohesion, regulate relations among members of a single family, a single neighborhood, and a single quarter, and realize cooperation among them for the reform and purification of society from everything that corrupts its thought and character. This includes realizing material solidarity among citizens, which strengthens bonds and makes society a cohesive unit in which the words of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) are embodied:

> “The believers in their mutual kindness, compassion, and sympathy are like one body: when one of its limbs complains, the rest of the body responds with sleeplessness and fever.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Beginnings of Creation, Hadith 6011; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2586]

To realize this objective, Islam establishes the following:

1. **The righteous individual is regarded as the cornerstone in building the Islamic society.** Therefore Islam is concerned with his upbringing, building his mind and soul upon the basis of *sharīʿah* rulings and the requirements of *fiṭrah* (فطرة), without excess or neglect. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption].” [Qur’an, Ash-Shams 91:7–10]

2. **The family is regarded as the first cell in the structure of the Islamic state.** The man and woman are its two elements upon which it stands and depends. Therefore Islam legislated many matters, among them:

   - Regulating the reproductive instinct through marriage and considering any other relationship outside it forbidden except with one’s spouse or what one’s right hand possesses. Thus Islam forbade fornication, sodomy, and masturbation. It encouraged young men to marry, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

     > “O young men, whoever among you is able to marry, let him marry; and whoever is not able, then let him fast, for it is a shield for him.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage, Hadith 5065; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1400]

   - Ensuring that virtue is realized through the cooperation of the spouses, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression.” [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:2]

   - Ensuring the freedom of each party to choose their spouse. The Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

     > “Choose carefully for your sperm, for marry those who are suitable and marry them to suitable matches.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1968]

     And he said:

     > “Do not marry off a previously married woman until she is consulted, and do not marry off a virgin until she is asked for permission.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage, Hadith 5136; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1419]

     It is narrated that al-Mughīrah ibn Shuʿbah proposed to a woman, and the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said to him:

     > “Look at her, for that is more likely to create affection between you,” meaning compatibility and harmony.

   - Ensuring the woman’s right to work in what does not conflict with the interest of the family or the limits of *sharīʿah*, does not lead to moral or social harm, and does not burden her beyond her capacity. The women of the Muslims used to participate alongside men in expeditions and wars, and in some trades and commerce. Such was Umm Ḥakīm bint al-Ḥārith, who entered battle between the Romans and the Muslims while still a bride, the scent of her wedding not yet faded. Her husband was martyred before her eyes, yet she neither wept nor wailed; rather, she tightened her garments and killed seven of the enemy at the bridge still known today as “Qanṭarat Umm Ḥakīm.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Military Expeditions, Hadith 4323]

   - Ensuring separation between the society of women and the society of men and preventing mixing except for a need proportionate to the *sharīʿah*, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “And if you ask anything of them, ask them from behind a partition. That is purer for your hearts and their hearts.” [Qur’an, Al-Aḥzāb 33:53]

     And the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

     > “Whenever a man is alone with a woman, Satan is the third among them.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Beginning of Creation, Hadith 5233; Sahih Muslim, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1341]

   - Ensuring the *qiwāmah* (قوامة) of the man over the woman in leading the family and caring for its affairs, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

     > “Men are in charge of women by [right of] what Allah has given one over the other and what they spend [for maintenance] from their wealth.” [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:34]

     And His words:

     > “And they [i.e., women] have rights similar to those [of men] over them in kindness, and men are a degree above them.” [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:228]

   - Ensuring equality in rights and duties.

   - Ensuring ease in dowries and marriage expenses, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

     > “The marriage that brings the most blessing is the one with the least expenditure.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1905]

     And his words:

     > “The best dowry is the easiest.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1908]

3. **Human equality is regarded as the essential characteristic of Muslim society.** All people are equal in the sight of Islam, returning to one origin; there is no distinction between one individual and another, and no superiority of one over another except through the good one brings to himself and society. This is what the Qur’an expressed in the words:

   > “O mankind, indeed We have created you from male and female and made you peoples and tribes that you may know one another. Indeed, the most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous of you. Indeed, Allah is Knowing and Acquainted.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:13]

   And what the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) meant when he said:

   > “O people, your Lord is one, and your father is one; all of you are from Adam, and Adam is from dust. The noblest of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous among you. There is no superiority for an Arab over a non-Arab, nor for a non-Arab over an Arab; nor for a red-skinned person over a black-skinned person, nor for a black-skinned person over a red-skinned person, except by righteousness. Have I conveyed the message? O Allah, bear witness. Let the one who is present convey it to the one who is absent.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Musnad al-Mukththirīn min al-Anṣār, Hadith 23489; al-Mustadrak, Hadith 2348]

4. **Social solidarity is regarded as a sacred religious obligation in Muslim society.** This solidarity is not limited to securing food, clothing, and shelter for the poor and needy; rather, it is comprehensive enough to guarantee the natural human rights of life, freedom, knowledge, dignity, and ownership. The fundamental principle from which this solidarity in Islam proceeds is regarding the members of society as brothers, for Allah the Exalted said:

   > “The believers are but brothers.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥujurāt 49:10]

   They are obliged to fulfill the duties of brotherhood among them, as the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “None of you [truly] believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 13; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 45]

5. **Noble character is regarded as the force that regulates energies and the spirit that spreads goodness, security, and virtue.** The state is the first party responsible for purifying society from corruption and its causes, and it is among its duties to spread virtue and combat vice, until the purpose for which Islam came is realized in society. The Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “I was only sent to perfect noble character.” [Sunan al-Muwaṭṭaʾ, Book of Good Character, Hadith 1614]

   The realization of this comes through concern for instilling *ʿaqīdah* (عقيدة) in souls and applying the rulings of Islam in general. Thus every individual will feel responsible for safeguarding society from grave sins, in fulfillment of the hadith of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Every joint of the son of Adam must give a charity every day the sun rises. It was said: O Messenger of Allah, from where can we give charity every day? He said: The gates of goodness are many: saying *subḥānallāh*, *al-ḥamdulillāh*, *allāhu akbar*, and *lā ilāha illallāh*; enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong; removing harm from the road; giving ear to the deaf; guiding the blind; showing the one who asks for what he needs; carrying with the strength of your leg the one who asks for help in distress; and carrying with the strength of your arm the weak.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Zakah, Hadith 1009]

## The Economic System

The economic system in Islam aims to develop general production and multiply it, so as to ensure the satisfaction of people’s essential needs and enable them to secure their complementary needs without extravagance or waste, narrowing the gap between the different classes in a manner that realizes justice, prosperity, and well-being for all members of society.

To this end, Islam establishes the following:

1. **Human ownership of wealth is accidental and temporary; the entire universe belongs to Allah the Exalted.** Allah the Exalted said:

   > “To Allah belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:189]

   > “And He has subjected to you whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth.” [Qur’an, Al-Jāthiyah 45:13]

   > “And spend out of that over which He has made you trustees.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥadīd 57:7]

2. **Ownership is of three kinds:** individual ownership, public ownership, and state ownership.

3. **Lawful individual ownership is inviolable in the Islamic economic system.** Whatever an individual acquires through lawful means becomes his right, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Whoever reaches something that no other Muslim has reached first, he has the greater right to it.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Division of Spoils, Hadith 3078]

   And his words:

   > “Whoever revives a dead land, it belongs to him.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Division of Spoils, Hadith 3078; Jamiʿ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Division, Hadith 1368]

   To emphasize the sanctity of individual ownership in Islam, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “The wealth, blood, and honor of every Muslim are sacred to every Muslim.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Sanctity of Blood, Hadith 6296; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 45]

   And he said:

   > “Whoever is killed defending his wealth is a martyr.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Blood Money, Hadith 2580]

4. **Public ownership** is permission granted by the Lawgiver for the community to share in benefiting from the thing itself. This includes pastures, forests, firewood collection, mines, oil wells, roads, and water, in accordance with the hadith of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “People are partners in three things: water, pasture, and fire.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Agriculture, Hadith 3477; Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 18005]

5. **State ownership** is what the public interest requires the state to own or what the state consumes for public interests, such as shipbuilding, weapons, aircraft, and the like.

6. **Ensuring the provision of means of work for every individual in the *ummah* (أمة), guaranteeing every person shelter to live in, livelihood that keeps disease at bay, and health, social, and cultural security for him and his children.** Many *aḥādīth* of the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) point to this, among them his words:

   > “If one of you has work but has no home, let him acquire a home; if he has no wife, let him marry; and if he has no mount, let him acquire a mount.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 12481]

7. **Ensuring the worker’s right to receive appropriate wages commensurate with his competence, so that they do not fall below the minimum needed for a moderate standard of living.** Evidence for this is the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Allah says: I will be the opponent of three people on the Day of Resurrection: a man who gave in My Name and then betrayed, a man who sold a free person and consumed his price, and a man who hired a worker and took full advantage of him but did not pay him his wages.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2224]

   And his words:

   > “Your workers are your brothers whom Allah has placed under your authority. So whoever has a brother under his authority, let him feed him from what he eats and clothe him from what he wears.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2356]

   And his words:

   > “Whoever hires a worker, let him pay him his wages.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2443]

   And his words:

   > “Give the hired worker his wages before his sweat dries.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Hiring, Hadith 2443]

8. **Zakāh in all its kinds is the minimum right in wealth**, directed to its specified beneficiaries:

   > “Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:60]

9. **The state has the right to impose just taxes on citizens when need calls for it**, in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him): “Indeed, in wealth there is a right beyond zakāh.” Al-Shāṭibī said: “If we establish an authoritative imam in need of increasing troops to meet the needs of the frontiers and protect a vast kingdom, and the treasury is empty, and the needs of the soldiers rise to what is beyond their means, then a just imam may levy upon the wealthy what he deems sufficient for them—that is, for the soldiers—in the present circumstances.”

10. **The prohibition of every transaction that harms society or is based on usury, monopoly, and fraud.** Therefore Islam forbade many things, among them: selling forbidden items; *gharar* (غرر) sales; price manipulation; monopoly; fraud; giving short measure; usury; and the legal maxim in all of this is: “There shall be no harm nor reciprocal harm.”

11. **Encouragement to spend in avenues of goodness and making mutual solidarity obligatory among citizens.** The Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

    > “The Muslim is the brother of the Muslim; he does not wrong him or desert him. Whoever attends to the need of his brother, Allah will attend to his need; whoever relieves a Muslim of a difficulty, Allah will relieve him of one of the difficulties of the Day of Resurrection; and whoever covers the fault of a Muslim, Allah will cover his fault on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Oppressions, Hadith 2442; Sahih Muslim, Book of Piety and Joining Ties, Hadith 2580]

12. **Affirming the state’s responsibility to protect these foundations**, based on the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

    > “Each of you is a shepherd, and each of you is responsible for his flock.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Administration, Hadith 893; Sahih Muslim, Book of Oaths, Hadith 1829]

## The Political System

The political outlook of Islam is summarized in the following points:

1. **The Islamic *ummah* is one.** The Islamic lands existing in the world are regarded as if they were one country, subject to one leadership, in accordance with the words of Allah the Exalted:

   > “Indeed this *ummah* of yours is one *ummah*, and I am your Lord, so worship Me.” [Qur’an, Al-Anbiyāʾ 21:92]

2. **The Islamic *ʿaqīdah* is the foundation of the state**, so that all its affairs proceed from it.

3. **Carrying the Islamic *daʿwah* (الدعوة) is the essential work of the state**, in fulfillment of the words of Allah the Exalted:

   > “We have already sent Our messengers with clear evidences and sent down with them the Scripture and the balance that the people may maintain [their affairs] in justice. And We sent down iron, wherein is great military might and benefits for the people.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥadīd 57:25]

   And His words:

   > “[And they are] those who, if We give them authority in the land, establish prayer and give zakāh and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. And to Allah belongs the outcome of [all] matters.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:41]

   And His words:

   > “You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind. You enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110]

4. **Sovereignty in the Islamic state belongs to Allah**, and the Book and the *Sunnah* are the only authoritative proofs for *sharīʿah* rulings. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “And in anything over which you disagree—its ruling is [to be referred] to Allah. [Say], ‘That is Allah, my Lord; upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn back.’” [Qur’an, Ash-Shūrā 42:10]

   And His words:

   > “Indeed, the ruling is only for Allah.” [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:40]

   And the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “I have left among you two things: you will never go astray so long as you hold fast to them: the Book of Allah and my *Sunnah*.” [Muwatta’ Imam Malik, Book of the Decree, Hadith 1655]

   It is narrated that the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him) asked Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān when he appointed him over Yemen: “By what will you judge, O Ḥudhayfah, if a case is presented to you?” Ḥudhayfah said: “By the Book of Allah.” He said: “And if you do not find it?” He said: “By the *Sunnah* of the Messenger of Allah.” He said: “And if you do not find it?” He said: “I shall exert my own judgment and spare no effort.” Thereupon the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) patted him on the shoulder and said: “Praise be to Allah.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of the Qādī, Hadith 3585; al-Mustadrak, Hadith 3585]

5. **Consultation (*shūrā*, الشورى) in the Islamic state is a right of all Muslims over the head of state**, and he is required to consult them and not act on his own opinion alone. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. So pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter. And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him].” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:159]

   And He says:

   > “And those who have responded to their Lord and established prayer and whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves, and from what We have provided them, they spend.” [Qur’an, Ash-Shūrā 42:38]

   And the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “Whoever consults others is never disappointed, and whoever seeks counsel is never misguided.” [Musnad Aḥmad, Hadith 15308]

   And he said:

   > “The one consulted is trusted.” [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Seeking Permission, Hadith 2650]

   And he said:

   > “No people ever consult one another except that they are guided to the best course for their affair.” As for the result of consultation, it is not binding—and Allah knows best.

6. **The ruler in the Islamic state is responsible before Allah and before the people.** He is the *imām* (الإمام) charged with establishing the command of Allah, and the *ummah* is required to listen to him and obey him so long as he does not command disobedience. Allah the Exalted says:

   > “O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result.” [Qur’an, An-Nisāʾ 4:59]

   And the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

   > “Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah, and whoever disobeys me has disobeyed Allah; whoever obeys the commander appointed over him has obeyed me, and whoever disobeys him has disobeyed me.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Jihad, Hadith 2955; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1833]

7. **Counseling the rulers is one of the rights of Muslims and a collective obligation upon them.** This is expressed in the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Hearing and obedience are due in what one likes and dislikes, unless one is commanded with disobedience. If he is commanded with disobedience, then there is no hearing and no obedience.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Obedience, Hadith 7137; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1839]

   And his words:

   > “Religion is sincere counsel. We said: To whom? He said: To Allah, His Book, His Messenger, the leaders of the Muslims, and their common people.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 55]

   And his words:

   > “If you see my *ummah* afraid to say to the oppressor, ‘You are an oppressor,’ then it has been stripped of blessings.” And his words: “The best *jihād* is a word of truth spoken to a tyrannical ruler.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Trials, Hadith 4344; Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Trials, Hadith 4012]

   And his words:

   > “There is no obedience to the created in disobedience to the Creator.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of the Beginning of Creation, Hadith 6697; Sahih Muslim, Book of Obedience, Hadith 1840]

8. **Non-Muslim communities enjoy the public rights enjoyed by Muslims and bear the same duties as Muslims.** They have autonomy in personal status and some special matters. The legal maxim in this regard is: “They have what we have, and upon them is what is upon us,” in accordance with the words of the Prophet (peace and blessings be upon him):

   > “Whoever harms a *dhimmī* (ذمي) has harmed me, and whoever harms me has quarreled with me on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Trials, Hadith 2834]

   And his words:

   > “Beware, whoever wrongs a person under covenant, or cheats him, or burdens him beyond his capacity, or takes something from him without his consent, I will contend for him on the Day of Resurrection.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Jihad, Hadith 3052]

   ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (may Allah be pleased with him) narrated from Ibrāhīm (may Allah have mercy on them both) that a Muslim man killed a man from the People of the Covenant. When that was raised to the Messenger of Allah (peace and blessings be upon him), he said: “I am the one most entitled to fulfill my covenant,” and then he ordered the Muslim to be killed.

## Section 4: The Necessity of Organizational Movement

# The Necessity of Organizational Movement

## Fourth Investigation: The Necessity of Organizational Movement and the Obligation of Working Within It

In this section the caller should address the subject of working to resume Islamic life and establish Islamic rule, affirming that it is a sharʿī obligation. He should make clear that working to resume Islamic life and establish Islamic rule necessitates the existence of an activist organization, or joining an Islamic activist organization.

The caller may approach this subject through the following points:

### The Messenger’s Work Was Activist, Not Individual

One of the things that confirms the necessity of establishing an activist organization for Islam, as the path to resuming Islamic life and establishing Islamic rule, is the method adopted by the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) in his preparation for establishing the Islamic state.

For the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) did not adopt the method of individual work. Rather, from the very first day he was eager to establish an activist organization, choosing its elements with discernment and selecting its members carefully, so that it would be the driving force of the Islamic movement that, in a short time, filled the eye, hearing, and sight of the world. (See the Prophetic biography in the Meccan period.)

### Collective Work Is a Sharʿī Obligation

It should also be said that collective work for Islam is a sharʿī obligation. This is because working to resume Islamic life is a sharʿī obligation, and this obligation is realized only through the establishment of a collective organization. Thus collective effort in working for Islam likewise becomes a sharʿī obligation.

Many texts in the Qur’an and the Sunnah call to cooperation, unity, and joining the ranks.

In the Noble Qur’an, Allah the Exalted says:

> And let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong, and those will be the successful. [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:104]

And He says:

> You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind. You enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah. [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110]

And He says:

> And it is not for the believers to go forth [to battle] all at once. For there should separate from every division of them a group [remaining] to obtain understanding in the religion and warn their people when they return to them that they might be cautious. [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:122]

And He says:

> [Allah] said, “We will strengthen your arm through your brother and grant you both supremacy so they will not reach you. [It will be] through Our signs; you and those who follow you will be the predominant.” [Qur’an, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:35]

And He says:

> And cooperate in righteousness and piety, but do not cooperate in sin and aggression. [Qur’an, Al-Māʾidah 5:2]

In the noble hadith, the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) said:

> “Hold fast to the *jamāʿah* (جماعة), for the *jamāʿah* is mercy and division is punishment.”

And he said:

> “Whoever separates is not of us. The hand of Allah is with the *jamāʿah*, and the wolf eats only the sheep that strays from the flock.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Government, Hadith 1846]

And he said:

> “Keep to the *jamāʿah* of the Muslims and their *imām*." It was narrated by al-Ṭabarānī in *al-Muʿjam al-Kabīr*.

Ḥudhayfah said: “What if they have no *jamāʿah* and no *imām*?” He said: “Then keep away from all those factions, even if you must bite on the root of a tree until death overtakes you while you are in that state.”

And he said:

> “Whoever dies while separate from the *jamāʿah* dies a death of *jāhiliyyah* (جاهلية).” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Government, Hadith 1848]

### Organization, Pledge of Allegiance, and Leadership

Another point that may be considered evidence for the necessity of the activist organization for Islam is that every action, in the judgment of Islam, requires organization; and every organization, in turn, must have at its head a leader who is obeyed in what is right. Hence numerous sharʿī texts require appointing an *amīr* (أمير) or leader under whose judgment everyone submits, in order to preserve unity of word, order of ranks, and discipline. Calling for the appointment of leaders clearly implies the existence of an organization and a group, for there is no justification for having a leader without a group or a following.

This is what the Messenger (peace and blessings be upon him) indicated when he said:

> “When three of you set out, appoint one of you as *amīr*." [Sunan Abī Dāwūd, Book of Travel, Hadith 2608]

And he said:

> “Whoever throws off the hand of obedience will meet Allah on the Day of Resurrection without any proof for himself, and whoever dies while having a pledge of allegiance around his neck dies a death of *jāhiliyyah*." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Government, Hadith 1847]

### The Immense Goals of Islam Require an Activist Organization

The caller should also make clear to the one being called that the path to resuming Islamic life and establishing the Islamic state is not realized merely by preaching and guidance, or by moving the tongue in speech and the pen in writing. Rather, this requires preparing all the scientific, material, technical, and human prerequisites and requirements demanded by this immense goal.

Islam is a call of demolition and construction: demolition of the entire *jāhili* society and the erection of the Islamic society in its place. This process of demolition requires burdens that individuals are incapable of bearing. Indeed, nothing can rise to it, despite strenuous effort, except an activist organization equal to the anticipated struggle.

Furthermore, the path of Islamic work is strewn with thorns and surrounded by dangers. The obstacles that block the way are many, the challenges facing Islam and its people are grave, and the problems left behind by materialist civilizations and philosophies demand colossal efforts on every front.

This makes the existence of an activist organization imperative, just as it makes it incumbent upon everyone who believes in Islam as a *manhaj* (منهج) to be part of the nucleus of this organization, its vanguard, and among those who call to it and work to support it, develop it, and expand it.
