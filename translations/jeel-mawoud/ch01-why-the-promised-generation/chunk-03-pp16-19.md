**Leading the Ummah in Hardship (Qiyādat al-Ummah fī al-Ḍarrāʾ):**

--- PAGE 16 ---

To the extent of the effort that the sons of the promised generation will exert in awakening the ummah, breathing life into it, and properly directing its sentiment toward real change and reconciliation with Allah — to that extent will come the support and backing (al-madad wa al-taʾyīd) from Allah, Mighty and Majestic. It will inevitably come, so that the first glimmers of dawn begin to rise, and the sun of Islam shines anew in various parts of the ummah.

So what will happen then?

History tells us that as soon as a ray of light appears, and the beginning of the giant's awakening and emergence from the darkness in which he was placed is announced, all the disbelieving forces surrounding our ummah will unite against us, and will work to extinguish this light, abort this hope, and eliminate the Islamic project.

**There will be a comprehensive boycott (muqāṭaʿah shāmilah) from all sides, and pressure will increase wherever the banner of Islam is raised.**

The pressure will be severe, seeking to create division (tafarruq) within the ummah, and working to subjugate it. It is expected that this pressure will cause distress and grumbling (ḍīq wa dhamīr) among the masses, for even if they have declared their support for Islam and their readiness to sacrifice for it, they may not be able to bear the consequences of the boycott — such as being deprived of some of their basic needs, let alone

--- PAGE 17 ---

their [needs for] comfort and perfection. Here comes the role of the promised generation, which will work diligently to provide for people's needs, breathe the spirit of hope into their souls, work to steady them, raise their morale, and bind them to Allah, Mighty and Majestic.

This means that these devoted ones (al-khullaṣ) will not sleep in these circumstances except after the people have slept, and will not eat until they are assured that food has reached everyone.

**A dangerous role awaits this generation at the inception of the Islamic state — if it does not rise to it, everything will be lost, and matters will return to worse than they are, and Allah forbid.**

**With the First Generation (Maʿa al-Jīl al-Awwal):**

These stages, these difficult conditions, were experienced by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ and those with him of the first generation — the generation of the foremost predecessors from the Muhājirūn and Anṣār, who served as the pillars upon which the Muslim community in Madīnah was built. This community was subjected, at the beginning of its formation, to severe pressure, siege, boycott, threats, and wars.

Abū al-ʿĀliyyah said in his tafsīr of His saying, Exalted be He:

> ﴿وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنْكُمْ وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ كَمَا اسْتَخْلَفَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ وَلَيُمَكِّنَنَّ لَهُمْ دِينَهُمُ الَّذِي ارْتَضَى لَهُمْ وَلَيُبَدِّلَنَّهُمْ مِنْ بَعْدِ خَوْفِهِمْ أَمْنًا يَعْبُدُونَنِي لَا يُشْرِكُونَ بِي شَيْئًا وَمَنْ كَفَرَ بَعْدَ ذَلِكَ فَأُولَئِكَ هُمُ الْفَاسِقُونَ﴾

*Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession [to authority] upon the earth, just as He granted it to those before them, and that He will surely establish for them their religion which He has preferred for them, and that He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security — [as] they worship Me, not associating anything with Me. And whoever disbelieves after that — then it is those who are the defiantly disobedient.* (al-Nūr 24:55)

He said: The Prophet ﷺ and his companions were in Mecca for about ten years, calling to Allah alone, and to the worship of Him alone, with no partner — secretly, in fear, not commanded to fight, until they were commanded to emigrate to Madīnah. They did so, and then Allah commanded them to fight. They were in fear — they would spend the night in weapons and rise in the morning in weapons. They bore this, by Allah's will. Then a man from among the Companions said:

"O Messenger of Allah... will we remain in fear like this forever?! Will a day not come upon us when we can be secure and put down our weapons?!"

The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "You will not have to wait long before a man among you will sit in the great gathering, leaning on his couch, with no weapon on him." And Allah revealed this verse, and Allah established His Prophet over the Arabian Peninsula, and they became secure and put down their weapons.

And al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib said: "This verse was revealed while we were in severe fear."⁽¹⁾

**Why Hardship? (Limādhā al-Ḍarrāʾ?):**

--- PAGE 19 ---

So there is a harsh period through which the ummah will pass, especially in the places where the banner of Islam will be raised.

If you say: But why will the rising ummah pass through this period? And why will it be left to this tremendous tribulation (fitnah) while it stands alone before the entire world?

The answer would be in His saying, Exalted be He:

> ﴿أَحَسِبَ النَّاسُ أَنْ يُتْرَكُوا أَنْ يَقُولُوا آمَنَّا وَهُمْ لَا يُفْتَنُونَ وَلَقَدْ فَتَنَّا الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِهِمْ فَلَيَعْلَمَنَّ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ صَدَقُوا وَلَيَعْلَمَنَّ الْكَاذِبِينَ﴾

*Do the people think that they will be left to say, "We believe," and they will not be tried? And We have certainly tested those before them, so Allah will surely know those who are truthful, and He will surely know those who are liars.* (al-ʿAnkabūt 29:1–3)

This period serves as a test (imtiḥān) of the ummah's sincerity in its love for Allah, its attachment to Him, and its reliance upon Him — and the extent of its readiness to sacrifice for His sake. It is the period of the final crucible (makhāḍ) for the birth of the young, strong ummah.

Among its benefits, also, is that it severs the attachment of hearts to anything other than Allah, and strips them for Him alone, Glorified be He. When material means are cut off, patience is exhausted, and calamities intensify, the ummah finds nothing before it except Allah, Mighty and Majestic — so it rushes to Him and calls upon Him with the call of the desperate, overwhelmed by drowning. Then relief comes, and victory (naṣr) and establishment (tamkīn) arrive:

> ﴿أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَنْ تَدْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ وَلَمَّا يَأْتِكُمْ مَثَلُ الَّذِينَ خَلَوْا مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ مَسَّتْهُمُ الْبَأْسَاءُ وَالضَّرَّاءُ وَزُلْزِلُوا حَتَّى يَقُولَ الرَّسُولُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مَعَهُ مَتَى نَصْرُ اللَّهِ أَلَا إِنَّ نَصْرَ اللَّهِ قَرِيبٌ﴾

*Or do you think that you will enter Paradise while such [trial] has not yet come to you as came to those who passed before you? They were touched by misery and hardship and were shaken until [even] the Messenger and those who believed with him said, "When is the help of Allah?" Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near.* (al-Baqarah 2:214)

**Translator's notes for Chunk 3:**

1. **Section headings — four paired concepts:** Pages 16–19 contain two formal section headings that are part of a four-part structure spanning the whole chapter:
   - Qiyādat al-Ummah fī al-Ḍarrāʾ (Leading the Ummah in Hardship) — page 16
   - Limādhā al-Ḍarrāʾ (Why Hardship?) — page 18
   - Qiyādat al-Ummah fī al-Sarāʾ (Leading the Ummah in Prosperity) — page 19 (content on page 20+)
   The pairing of ḍarrāʾ (hardship) and sarāʾ (prosperity) is a Quranic contrast rooted in verses like 2:214 and 24:55.

2. **24:55 — the central promise verse:** This is the pivotal Quranic verse of the entire chapter. Abū al-ʿĀliyyah's tafsīr (narrated via Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr 3/283) provides the historical context: the Ṣaḥābah lived in fear for ~10 years in Mecca, then after the Hijrah were commanded to fight, sleeping and waking with weapons — until a companion asked if this fear would ever end. The Prophet ﷺ gave glad tidings, and 24:55 was revealed. This is the verse that gives the book its title: the "promised generation" (al-jīl al-mawʿūd) is the generation to whom this divine promise (waʿd) applies.

3. **"The giant's awakening" (استيقاظ المارد):** Mārid (مارد) means a rebellious giant or a formidable force. Al-Hilali uses it metaphorically for the Muslim ummah — a sleeping power whose awakening terrifies its enemies. The metaphor implies latent strength, not a threat to be dismissed.

4. **"Comprehensive boycott" (مقاطعة شاملة):** Bolded in the original. Al-Hilali predicts that when the Islamic awakening begins, enemies will impose a total economic, political, and social blockade — mirroring the boycott of the Muslims in Mecca by Quraysh.

5. **"The devoted ones" (الخُلَّص):** Al-khullaṣ refers to those who are pure, sincere, and dedicated — those who have given themselves entirely to the cause. The term implies selflessness: they eat last and sleep last.

6. **"The final crucible" (المخاض الأخير):** Makhāḍ literally means the throes/pangs of childbirth. The metaphor is deliberate — the hardship period is not merely a test but a birth process. The strong generation cannot emerge without it, just as a child cannot be born without labour pains.

7. **"The call of the desperate, overwhelmed by drowning" (دعاء المضطر المشرف على الغرق):** A Quranic allusion to 27:62 ("Is He [not best] who responds to the desperate one when he calls upon Him...?"). Al-Hilali's point: only when all material means are exhausted does the ummah turn to Allah with complete sincerity — and this is precisely when relief comes.

8. **The hadith of the companion's question:** The companion is not named in al-Hilali's text, but Ibn Kathīr's tafsīr identifies the context. The Prophet's ﷺ response — "a man among you will sit in the great gathering (al-majlis al-ʿaẓīm) leaning on his couch (muḥtabiyan) with no weapon on him" — is a vivid image of security after fear. Grade not specified in the source.

9. **Al-Barāʾ ibn ʿĀzib's testimony:** "This verse was revealed while we were in severe fear" — a direct companion testimony confirming the historical context of 24:55. Source: Tafsīr Ibn Kathīr 3/283, Maktabat al-ʿAbīkān, Riyadh. Grade not specified in the source.

10. **Key Arabic terms preserved:** muqāṭaʿah shāmilah (comprehensive boycott), al-khullaṣ (the devoted ones), makhāḍ (crucible/birth-pangs), imtiḥān (test), ḍarrāʾ (hardship), sarāʾ (prosperity), istiqlāl (independence — implied), ḥikmah (wisdom), daʿwah (calling to Islam).
