# Chapter Three: How Are the Attributes of the Promised Generation Achieved?

## Page 98

When one reflects deeply on the ten previous attributes that form the minimum requirement for what the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment (al-Jeel al-Maw'ud bi al-nasr wa al-tamkeen) ought to be, one finds that the realization of these attributes is an extremely difficult matter. If one of us undertakes some of them for a period of time, he will quickly revert to his former condition. And reality is the best proof of that, for many of us wish to be detached from the world (zahid), desirous of what is with Allah, eagerly striving for Paradise, sincere to Allah (mukhlis lillah), fearful of Him, loving Him… yet he is unable to do so.

We read reports of the righteous and are moved by them, and we dream of being like them, but we are unable to turn the dream into reality. Why?!

Because we want to leap directly to the fruit without beginning the matter from its beginning. Since the stage of fruit-bearing is the last of the stages of cultivation, preceded by many stages of care and attention, likewise the ten previous attributes cannot appear except through work, construction, and inner change that escalates gradually until the signs appear and the fruits ripen.

For example: a person cannot undertake the deeds of those sincere to Allah (al-mukhlisin lillah), Exalted and Majestic is He, and persist in that for a period of time, for sincerity (al-ikhlas) is nothing but one form of a servant's interaction with his Lord; and it must arise from within him naturally. The same applies to love, fear, hope, glorification, and so on.

## Page 99

### Treatment is Proportionate to Knowledge (al-mu'amlah bi qadr al-ma'rifah)

If you ask: how can a servant interact with his Lord in these ways and persist in that throughout his life?!

The answer is that it is commonly known among people that the degree of a person's treatment of another is proportionate to the extent of his knowledge of that other. If, for example, a person were seated next to another on a train traveling to a distant city, and conversation began between the two individuals… at the beginning of the conversation there would be much reserve between them, but with continued acquaintance and the exchanging of conversation, the awkwardness would fade and the degree of each one's treatment of the other would improve. This is one aspect.

On the other hand, if one of them discovered that the other held a prestigious position, his view of him would change from what it had been at the beginning, and that would undoubtedly be reflected in his manner of dealing with him.

### Ignorance of Allah (al-jahl billah)

If we look at our treatment of and relationship with Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, we will find it a cold treatment and a limited relationship. What governs and influences it is the degree of our knowledge of Him, Glorified is He. Were people to know the true magnitude of their Lord, they would fear Him and be upright in obedience to Him: "They did not appraise Allah with true appraisal." (Quran, al-An'am 6:91)

And as the servants' ignorance of Allah increases, their false perception of Him—Glorified is He—increases, and that is reflected in the form of interaction between them and Him.

## Page 100

Consider, for example, His Exalted saying: "The foolish among the people will say, 'What has turned them away from their qiblah which they used to face?' Say, 'To Allah belongs the east and the west. He guides whom He wills to a straight path.'" (Quran, al-Baqarah 2:142)

The Quran called those who ask this question "the foolish" (al-sufaha') because they do not know Allah, and they do not know that to Him belongs the dominion of the heavens and the earth, that He does what He wills and rules as He pleases.

Another example is His saying: "And the Day the enemies of Allah are gathered to the Fire, and they will be driven in rows. Until, when they reach it, their hearing, their sight, and their skins will testify against them of what they used to do. And they will say to their skins, 'Why have you testified against us?' They will say, 'Allah, who made all things speak, made us speak, and He created you the first time, and to Him you are returned. And you were not covering yourselves, lest your hearing testify against you or your sight or your skins, but you assumed that Allah does not know much of what you do. And that was your assumption which you assumed about your Lord. It has brought you to ruin, and you have become among the losers.'" (Quran, Fussilat 41:19–23)

This limited and deficient perception of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, caused them to stray from His straight path, bringing them to the Fire—and refuge is sought in Allah.

## Page 101

### With Musa, peace be upon him

Reflect with me on this incident that occurred with Musa, peace be upon him: "And when Musa arrived at Our appointed time and his Lord spoke to him, he said, 'My Lord, show me [Yourself] that I may look at You.' [Allah] said, 'You will not see Me, but look at the mountain; if it should remain in place, then you will see Me.' But when his Lord appeared to the mountain, He rendered it level, and Musa fell unconscious. And when he awoke, he said, 'Exalted are You! I have repented to You, and I am the first of the believers.'" (Quran, al-A'raf 7:143)

In this incident, Musa—peace be upon him—asked his Lord to see Him. Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, informed him that he could not see Him and set for him—Glorified and Exalted is He—a proof of that: if He were to manifest Himself to the mountain and the mountain were to remain in its place and nothing happened to it, then Musa would be able to see Him. Then Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, manifested Himself to the mountain, and the mountain was crushed and pulverized. What happened to Musa, peace be upon him?!

He fell unconscious from the terrifying sight he witnessed. Then when he awoke, his knowledge of the greatness and majesty of his Lord had increased, and that knowledge was reflected in his speech and his treatment of his Lord. When he regained consciousness, he said: "Exalted are You! I have repented to You, and I am the first of the believers."

## Page 102

Musa, peace be upon him, had repented to Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, from falling short in His right, Glorified is He, from failing to glorify, venerate, and sanctify Him as He deserves, and from asking to see Him. This repentance was the result of the knowledge that increased in him after he saw the effects of Allah's greatness and glory, Exalted and Majestic is He.

Therefore, treating Allah with sincerity (al-ikhlas), love (al-mahabbah), fear (al-khawf), awe (al-mahabah), glorification (al-ta'zim), piety (al-taqwa), and surrender (al-istislam)—all of that is the product of the strength of knowledge of Him, Glorified is He.

This means that the first axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment should arise is knowledge of Allah (ma'rifat Allah), Exalted and Majestic is He.

### The Required Knowledge (al-ma'rifah al-matlabah)

Yes, treatment is proportionate to knowledge; but the knowledge whose presence is required in order to effect change in one's treatment of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, must be strong, firmly rooted, deep, and continuous.

What is required is knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, that takes hold of a person's certainty and inner mind, affects his emotions, and becomes an integral part of his faith (iman).

### Knowledge and Faith (al-ma'rifah wa al-iman)

As we know, the source that directs human actions is the heart (al-qalb), and the heart is the gathering place of emotions: love and hatred, desire and dread, joy and pleasure, and so on.

## Page 103

### These Emotions Are Tugged by Two Forces: Faith (iman) and Desire (hawa)

Faith in what the mind contains of realities, and desire for what the soul inclines to and wants… and according to the strength of one of them at the moment of decision, the action will be on its side.

The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "No servant commits adultery while he is a believer; nor does he steal while he is a believer; nor does he drink wine while he is a believer; nor does he kill while he is a believer."[1]

The moments of adultery, theft, and drinking wine reflected the victory of desire (hawa) over faith (iman).

This means that faith in Allah must increase in the heart so that it may bear righteous deeds through the limbs.

And increasing faith in Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, means increasing the orientation of emotions toward Him, Glorified is He—that is, increasing the orientation of love, dread, awe, and so on.

And as emotions change and turn toward Allah, the servant's inner treatment of his Lord improves, and then that is easily reflected in his outward limbs. Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, said: "That [is so]. And whoever honors the symbols of Allah—indeed, it is from the piety of hearts." (Quran, al-Hajj 22:32)

And the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "O Allah, apportion to us fear of You that prevents us from disobeying You."[2]

## Page 104

Therefore, the degree of leaving sins is according to the degree of dread… and this dread is nothing but an inner treatment on the servant's part toward his Lord that increases and decreases according to the strength of knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He.

### Thought and Emotion

In order for knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, to affect the emotions and become part of the individual's faith, it must be deep and effective; it must address both thought and emotion, and it must be continuous so that the emotions may remain directed toward Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He.

Thus, faith in Allah means: knowledge of Allah that affects the emotions, causing the heart to direct this portion of its emotions toward Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He.

As for increasing faith, it means increasing the orientation of emotions toward Him, Glorified is He; and completing faith means the total orientation of emotions toward Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He. The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Whoever loves for Allah, hates for Allah, gives for Allah, and withholds for Allah has completed faith."[3]

From here appears the value of the presence of both mind and heart together in the righteous deeds performed by the servant in order to increase his faith; and according to the degree of movement of the inner emotions along with obedience, the resulting increase in faith is determined.

## Page 105

This means that the obedience which increases faith is the obedience during which the heart moves; otherwise, however much the servant performs of outward acts of obedience, if his heart does not move with them, the effect resulting from them in increasing faith will be weak, and consequently they will not have a positive effect on the conduct of their doer.

In summary, the first axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment arise is knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He. This knowledge must be deep and firmly rooted, increasing faith by it, affecting the emotions, turning them toward Allah; then the inner treatment of Him, Glorified is He, improves, which in turn yields deeds and manifestations that reflect the improvement of this treatment.

### Knowledge of the Self (ma'rifat al-nafs)

And just as the principle "treatment is proportionate to knowledge" applies to our relationship with Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, and the manner of our treatment of Him, Glorified is He, it also applies to our view of ourselves and the way we deal with them. What is observed in some people is that they deal with themselves with self-admiration and contentment, sensing their superiority and distinction over others… This erroneous treatment of the self arises from ignorance of its true nature.

## Page 106

If a person were to know the reality of his origin, the extent of his weakness, his need for his Lord, and the severity of his poverty toward Him… and if he were to know all this with knowledge that becomes entrenched in his certainty and intertwined with his faith, his caution against his own self and his fear of it would intensify, and his sense of need for Allah would intensify.

This means that true knowledge of the self leads to increased sincerity to Allah (al-ikhlas lillah), Exalted and Majestic is He, sincere reliance (al-tawakkul) upon Him, and also produces the attribute of humility (al-tawadu'). For the root of humility is the servant's belittlement of himself and viewing it with the eye of deficiency, and viewing others as better than himself.

From here it becomes clear to us that knowledge of the self is the second axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment arise. This axis is undoubtedly closely connected to the axis of knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, for the more a servant's knowledge of his Lord increases, the smaller his self becomes in his sight; and the more he forgets his Lord, the more he forgets the danger his self poses to him, and consequently the more he inclines toward it. This is confirmed by His Exalted saying: "And be not like those who forgot Allah, so He made them forget themselves." (Quran, al-Hashr 59:19)

### Knowledge of the World (ma'rifat al-dunya)

What is it that makes us covet the world (al-dunya) and think so much about our future? What is the reason for our greed for wealth, our joy at its increase, and our grief at its decrease? What is it that makes us stretch our eyes toward those who have been favored over us in provision, wishing we were like them?

## Page 107

It is ignorance of the world and its reality, and that it is merely a stopping place where we rest like a traveler rests, a stage among the stages of the journey back to Allah.

It is also ignorance of its nature, and of the reality of its adornment and finery.

This ignorance has made us deal with it in a wrong way: instead of shunning it and not inclining toward it, we have loved it and been eager for it.

Imagine that someone has invited you and your family to spend the summer vacation in his country. You resolve to travel, but you want some information about that country. You research it and ask those familiar with it, and you find that its weather in summer is extremely hot, electricity is constantly cut off, summer diseases spread there frequently, humidity is very high, and thieves and highwaymen fill its roads… What would your reaction be after hearing this news?!

Would you travel to that country, or would your desire turn away from it?!

Undoubtedly you would lose interest in going there, and your desire would turn away from it completely as a result of your knowledge of its reality.

Likewise is the world: the main reason for our attachment to it is a result of our ignorance of its reality.

## Page 108

Yes, we hear about some of its attributes from time to time and we wish to renounce it, but we are unable, because knowledge must be deep and firmly rooted, entering a person's certainty and affecting his faith, so that it may have a positive and continuous effect on his dealing with it through renunciation (al-zuhd) and turning away from it.

Thus, knowledge of the world is the third axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation arise, especially the attribute of renunciation (al-zuhd) and similar attributes.

### Faith in the Hereafter (al-iman bi al-akhirah)

On the opposite side of our treatment of the world, we deal with the Hereafter (al-akhirah). Despite the Hereafter being the abode of settlement, and despite Paradise containing forms of bliss that the limited human mind cannot comprehend, and despite the Fire containing forms of punishment that no human can bear, we do not deal with this reality as we should. We are not seen eagerly striving for Paradise; rather, we are indifferent to it and to everything that draws near to it. Nor are we seen fearful and trembling of the Fire, or acting to flee from it; and this is undoubtedly a result of ignorance of their reality.

## Page 109

Indeed, clarity of vision regarding Paradise and the absence of its reality from the mind of the Muslim has a great role in spurring him toward it, and that is reflected in his conduct: hastening to good deeds, increasing in worship, striving in the path of Allah, and being patient over the hardships of the path.

This means that knowledge of the Hereafter and faith in it form the fourth axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment arise.

### The Four Knowledges (al-ma'arif al-arba')

Thus, there are four knowledges that form the fundamental starting points for most of the attributes of the Generation of Empowerment (Jeel al-Tamkeen): knowledge of Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He; knowledge of the self; knowledge of the world; and knowledge of the Hereafter. Taking into consideration that the required knowledge ought to be deep and firmly rooted in a person's certainty and inner mind, affecting his emotions so that it becomes an integral part of his faith, driving him to correct interaction with Allah, with the self, with the world, and with the Hereafter; and this yields sincerity to Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, love for Him, fear of Him, increase in worship, humility, renunciation of the world, jihad, sacrifice, patience, steadfastness, obedience, devotion, and reliance upon Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He.

### Wisdom as the Fruit of Understanding (al-hikmah thamarat al-fahm)

## Page 110

As for the attribute of balance and equilibrium (al-i'tidal wa al-tawazun), which is the tenth attribute, it is the fruit of correct and comprehensive understanding of the religion, of its levels and rulings; and it is also the fruit of knowing the fiqh of priorities, the fiqh of balances, the fiqh of reality, the fiqh of objectives, and the fiqh of differences of opinion. Taking into consideration the importance of this axis—the axis of understanding (fiqh)—as a restraint that prevents the deeds from swerving toward extremes that strong faith in Allah and the Last Day, and strong caution against the world and the self, might push one toward.

It is expected that these four knowledges, when they take hold of the servant, may push him to renounce the world or to be harsh with the self and deprive it of some of its lawful pleasures; and they may push him to be severe in matters in which severity is not appropriate, or lenient in matters in which leniency is not appropriate.

From here, wisdom (al-hikmah), balance (al-i'tidal), equilibrium (al-tawazun), and the middle course (al-wasatiyyah) became wholesome fruits of correct understanding of the religion.

This means that correct understanding of Islam is the fifth axis from which the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment arise.

### Brotherhood (al-ukhuwwah)

## Page 111

The attribute of brotherhood, bonding, and loyalty to the believers remains… This attribute is nothing but one of the fruits of faith in Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, and a necessary consequence of it; and the more faith in Allah takes hold of the heart, the more love for Allah increases.

Reflect with me on the state of the Ansar when faith took possession of their hearts until they reached the level of which Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, said: "And those who settled in the home [of Madinah] and accepted faith before them." (Quran, al-Hashr 59:9)

It is as if they entered faith with their whole being, so it mixed with their flesh and blood in addition to its taking hold of their hearts. What did this wondrous level of faith produce?

It produced a love for the sake of Allah toward the Muhajirun, the effects of which appeared in deeds unprecedented among human beings: "And those who settled in the home [of Madinah] and accepted faith before them love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what they [the emigrants] were given, but give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation. And whoever is protected from the stinginess of his soul—it is those who will be the successful." (Quran, al-Hashr 59:9)

And what is amazing is that they did all that despite poverty and need, not wealth and ease.

### Means of Forming the Axes

## Page 112

What has become clear to us from the foregoing is that the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment are nothing but natural fruits of five knowledges: knowledge of Allah, knowledge of the self, knowledge of the world, knowledge of the Hereafter, and knowledge of the foundations of correct understanding of the religion.

These knowledges must become firmly rooted in the Muslim's certainty, affect his emotions, and draw his faith, so that they may yield the attributes that Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, desires from His servants in a natural and continuous manner.

If the matter is thus, the question remains: How can we form these knowledges in the required manner? The discussion in the following pages revolves around answering this question.
