# Introduction

Praise be to Allah, and peace and blessings be upon His Messenger. To proceed:

This book is divided into two parts. The first part deals with the attributes that must be present in the Muslim—every Muslim—so that he may thereby be a Muslim. It explains the conditions that must be present in everyone who belongs to this religion.

For many people are Muslims by inheritance, or Muslims because they were born to Muslim parents. These and those like them do not, in reality, realize the meaning of their belonging to Islam, nor do they know the requirements of this belonging. That is why you see them in one valley and Islam in another.

The purpose of the first part of this book is to answer all these questions and to clarify what Islam requires of the Muslim so that his belonging to Islam may be sound and real, and consequently so that he may be truly a Muslim:

> He has chosen you and has not placed upon you in the religion any difficulty. [It is] the religion of your father, Abraham. He named you "Muslims" before and in this [revelation] that the Messenger may be a witness over you and you may be witnesses over the people. So establish prayer (*salah*, الصلاة) and give *zakah* (الزكاة) and hold fast to Allah. He is your protector; and excellent is the Protector, and excellent is the Helper. [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:78]

As for the second part of the book, it explains the obligation of working for Islam and belonging to the Islamic movement (*al-harakah al-Islamiyyah*, الحركة الإسلامية), as it deals with the characteristics of the Islamic movement, its goals, its means, its philosophy, its way of working, and the attributes that must be present in those who belong to it.

## The Ummah's Crisis

And among what is worth mentioning at this point is that all the events taking place in the Islamic world in general and in the Arab region in particular confirm a great truth: that the *Ummah* (الأمة) is experiencing a fatal void in all aspects of its life.

The Ummah passed through the past period in harsh circumstances. Many systems and principles fell during it, and many movements and leaderships were exposed. When all these were placed on the front line of confrontation with the various challenges of the age, they fell because they did not possess, in the first place, the factors of survival and continuity.

They fell because they were empty, glittering slogans, having no value and no content.

They fell because they were not authentic; they did not express the personality of this Ummah. They were foreign, artificial, imported—as we import shoes and socks. And these did not last long. They were soon uncovered; soon their evils appeared.

For that reason the masses rejected them, because they were alien to them, not compatible with their principles and beliefs. They are like a kidney or a heart that is transplanted into the human body: if the body accepts them, it is for a short while full of suffering and pain, then the body soon wastes away and dies.

This is what actually happened to the Islamic Ummah when it woke to its feeble, sick self. It did not think about what it was doing. Rather, it hastened to import whatever it thought were sound principles and man-made systems—systems that carried within them factors of destruction and ruin, factors of chaos and corruption, factors of loss and wandering.

As for the first operation, it was planting Western civilization and capitalist thought into the entity of this Ummah. The result was a cancerous growth that appeared in every aspect of its social, economic, and political life. A cancerous growth that worked to distort its personality, to confuse its thoughts, to corrupt its morals—and finally to prepare it to receive the first defeat, the defeat of 1948.

And despite the fierceness of the experience and the severity of the catastrophe, the Ummah remained captive to its loss and wandering, drawn by its emotions, taken by false slogans and deceptive appearances. This is what made it, for the second, third, and fourth time, drink from the same cup of defeat one after another. Its progressive-revolutionary-socialist systems did not avail it at all; indeed, its intimate alliances did not shield it from harm.

If the defeat of 1948 was the harvest of Western imperialist humiliation, then the defeat of 1967 is the harvest of leftist proletarian dependency.

And the Ummah emerged from these events and experiences laden with worries, saturated with wounds. Its hopes in those on whom it had pinned its hopes were disappointed, and its trust in the leaders and commanders around it was shaken.

So did the Ummah wake up after that, do you think? Did it recover from the shock and the severity of the experience? Did it realize that the international powers—all the international powers—are conspiring against it?

It is indeed the utmost misguidance for the Ummah to be swept away by this current, [as if] it must be either communist or capitalist.

## Islam: The Only Filling of the Void

Rather, the Islamic Ummah must realize that it has an independent, distinctive personality: a personality that is neither rightist nor leftist, an authentic personality that derives its characteristics and features from Islam—the religion of the *fitrah* (الفطرة, the innate nature) and the message of the *fitrah*. And by virtue of this distinction and authenticity, it can assume a place of intellectual and political leadership in the world.

It must realize that this great void that it is living through is not filled by the projects of the American White House, nor by the projects of the Soviet Kremlin; not by the ideas of Marx and Lenin, nor by the principles of Guevara and Ho Chi Minh.

Indeed, this void is filled by nothing except Islam: as creed and system, as morals and legislation, as all of that. Therefore, it places the Islamic movement before a historic, fateful responsibility—a responsibility that requires an act of faith and a will that endures. Are the callers to Islam aware of their responsibility?

## The Subjects of This Book

The first part of this book, entitled *Mādhā Yaʿnī Intimāʾī lil-Islām* (What My Belonging to Islam Means), deals with the most important attributes that must be present in a person for him to be truly a Muslim.

For belonging to Islam is not belonging by inheritance, nor belonging by identity, nor is it belonging by outward appearance. Rather, it is belonging to Islam, commitment to Islam, and adaptation to Islam in all aspects of life.

And in what follows we will briefly explain the most prominent attributes that should be present in the Muslim so that his belonging to this religion may be a sound, truthful belonging:

> He named you "Muslims" before and in this [revelation] that the Messenger may be a witness over you and you may be witnesses over the people. [Qur'an, Al-Hajj 22:78]

1. That I be a Muslim in my *ʿaqeedah* (عقيدتي, creed)
2. That I be a Muslim in my *ʿibādah* (عبادتي, worship)
3. That I be a Muslim in my public life
4. That I be a Muslim in my family and home
5. That I overcome my self
6. That I be confident that the future belongs to Islam

That I be a Muslim.
# Chapter 1: Being a Muslim in My Belief

The first condition among the conditions of belonging to Islam and affiliating with this din (religion) is that the Muslim's 'aqeedah (creed) be sound and correct, consistent with the first Muslims, our righteous salaf (predecessors), and the imams (leaders) of religion who are testified to for their goodness, righteousness, taqwa (piety), and correct understanding of Allah's religion, Exalted and Mighty is He.

And in order for me to be a Muslim in my 'aqeedah, that necessitates from me the following:

1. That I believe that the Creator of the universe is a God who is Wise, All-Powerful, All-Knowing, and Self-Subsisting — by the evidence that this universe is characterized by excellence, precision, and harmony, and by the dependence of some of its parts upon others such that it would be impossible for it to remain and continue without the support of this Exalted, All-Powerful God. "Had there been within them gods besides Allah, they both would have been ruined. So exalted is Allah, Lord of the Throne, above what they describe." [Qur'an, Al-Anbya 21:22]

2. That I believe that the Creator, Exalted is His majesty, did not create this universe in vain or purposelessly, for it is not possible that One described with perfection be playful in what He creates. And it is impossible to understand Allah's purpose in this creation in detail except through a messenger from Him and revelation. "Then did you think that We created you uselessly and that to Us you would not be returned? So exalted is Allah, the Sovereign, the Truth; there is no deity except Him, Lord of the Noble Throne." [Qur'an, Al-Mu'minun 23:115-116]

3. That I believe that Allah, Glorified is He, sent the messengers and revealed the Books to acquaint people with Him, with the purpose of their creation, their Originator, and their final return. And the last of those noble messengers was Muhammad, peace and blessings be upon him, whom Allah supported with the eternal miracle of the Noble Qur'an. "And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying], 'Worship Allah and avoid Taghut.'" [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:36]

4. That I believe that the goal of human existence is to know Allah, Exalted and Mighty is He, as He has described Himself, and to obey and worship Him. "And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. I want from them no provision, nor do I want them to feed Me. Indeed, it is Allah who is the [continual] Provider, the firm possessor of strength." [Qur'an, Adh-Dhariyat 51:56-58]

5. That I believe that the reward of the believing, obedient person is Paradise, and that the reward of the disbelieving, disobedient person is the Fire. "A group [of you] will be in Paradise and a group in the Blaze." [Qur'an, Ash-Shura 42:7]

6. That I believe that man earns good and evil by his choice and will, but he is not guided to good except by Allah's enabling and support; and he does not commit evil under compulsion from Allah, but rather within the framework of His permission and will. "And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]." "Every soul, for what it has earned, will be retained." [Qur'an, Ash-Shams 91:7-10; Al-Muddaththir 74:38]

7. That I believe that legislation belongs to Allah alone and no one else may encroach upon it; and that it is possible for the Muslim scholar to perform ijtihad (independent juristic reasoning) in deriving rulings within the framework of what Allah has legislated. "And in anything over which you disagree, its ruling is [to be referred] to Allah, [saying], 'That is Allah, my Lord; upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn back.'" [Qur'an, Ash-Shura 42:10]

8. That I acquaint myself with Allah through names and attributes befitting His majesty. On the authority of Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "Allah has ninety-nine names, one hundred less one. Whoever memorizes them will enter Paradise; and He is Witr (One) and loves the Witr." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Tawhid, Hadith 2736; Sahih Muslim, Book of Remembrance, Hadith 2677]

9. That I reflect upon Allah's creation and not upon His essence, in compliance with the words of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: "Contemplate the creation of Allah, but do not contemplate Allah, for you cannot comprehend His greatness." [Narrated by Abu Nu'aym in Hilyat al-Awliya; the narration is weak (da'"'if)]

10. As for His attributes, Exalted is He, many verses of the Noble Qur'an have pointed to them, and to what the requirements of uluhiyyah (divinity) entail. Thus there are verses that point to the existence of Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He; verses that point to His attributes of baqa' (eternal continuance) and qidam (pre-eternity); verses that point to Allah's being unlike the hawadith (temporal events) among His creation, and His being transcendent above child, parent, like, or equal; verses that point to Allah's self-subsistence and His independence from His creation despite their need of Him; verses that point to His wahdaniyyah (uniqueness) in His essence, attributes, actions, and disposals; verses that point to Allah's qudrah (power) and the manifestations of His greatness; verses that point to the vastness of Allah's knowledge and His encompassing of all things; verses that point to Allah's iradah (will) being above every will and desire; verses that point to Allah's being attributed with perfect hayat (life); and verses upon verses that point to perfect attributes of Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He, that are inexhaustible and whose reality human minds cannot grasp. Glory be to Him; we cannot encompass praise of Him as He has praised Himself. (See the treatise *Al-'Aqa'id* by the martyred Imam, and the treatise *Al-Mabadi' al-Kulliyyah lil-I'tiqad* by Abd al-Haqq.)

11. That I believe that the opinion of the salaf is most worthy of being followed, putting an end to the matter of ta'wil (figurative interpretation) and ta'til (denial of attributes), and entrusting knowledge of these meanings to Allah, Blessed and Exalted is He. And the ta'wilat of the salaf do not necessitate ruling them as disbelievers or transgressors, nor does this long dispute between them and others stretch across old and new times.

12. That I worship Allah and do not associate anything with Him, in response to Allah's da'wah (call) throughout the messages and the messengers, peace be upon them, by which He called them to worship Him alone and not to submit to other than Him. "And We certainly sent into every nation a messenger, [saying], 'Worship Allah and avoid Taghut.'" [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:36]

13. That I fear Him and do not fear anyone else, and that my fear of Him be a motive for me to keep away from His displeasure and His prohibitions. "Whoever obeys Allah and His Messenger and fears Allah and is cautious of Him — it is those who are the successful." "Indeed, those who fear their Lord unseen will have forgiveness and great reward." [Qur'an, An-Nur 24:52; Al-Mulk 67:12]

14. And that I remember Him and persist in His dhikr (remembrance), and that it be practical, intellectual, and verbal dhikr. For the dhikr of Allah, Exalted is He, is the closest remedy for the soul and the most potent weapon against the vicissitudes of time, the worries of life and its calamities — and this is what humanity needs today. Allah, Exalted is He, spoke the truth when He said: "Those who have believed and whose hearts are assured by the remembrance of Allah. Unquestionably, by the remembrance of Allah hearts are assured." "And whoever is blinded from remembrance of the Most Merciful — We appoint for him a devil, and he is to him a companion. And indeed, they [i.e., the devils] avert them from the way while they think that they are [rightly] guided." [Qur'an, Ar-Ra'd 13:28; Az-Zukhruf 43:36-37]. Dr. Brill acknowledged this when he said, "The truly religious person never suffers from any psychological illness," and the psychologist Dale Carnegie said, "Psychiatrists realize that strong faith and clinging to religion are capable of overcoming anxiety and nervous tension and curing diseases."

15. That I love Allah with a love that makes my heart enamored with His majesty, attached to Him, motivating me always to seek increase in good and to sacrifice and strive in jihad (struggle in His cause) forever, with nothing of worldly debris or bonds of kinship preventing me from that, in compliance with His saying: "Say, [O Muhammad], 'If your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your wives, your clans, the wealth that you have gained, commerce you fear may slacken, and dwellings you love are more beloved to you than Allah and His Messenger and jihad in His cause, then wait until Allah executes His command. And Allah does not guide the defiantly disobedient people.'" [Qur'an, At-Tawbah 9:24]. And hoping for the sweetness of iman (faith) to which the Greatest Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed when he said: "There are three qualities, whoever has them will taste the sweetness of faith: that Allah and His Messenger are more beloved to him than anything else; that he loves a person only for the sake of Allah; and that he hates to return to disbelief as much as he would hate to be thrown into the Fire." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 16; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 43]

16. That I place my tawakkul (trust) in Allah in every matter and rely upon Him in every affair. This instills in my soul strength and spiritual energy by which I make difficulties easy. "And will provide for him from where he does not expect. And whoever relies upon Allah — then He is sufficient for him. Indeed, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has already set for everything a [decreed] extent." [Qur'an, At-Talaq 65:2-3]. And among the most magnificent counsels given to us by the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, is: "Be mindful of Allah, and He will be mindful of you. Be mindful of Allah, and you will find Him before you. If you ask, ask of Allah; if you seek help, seek help from Allah. And know that if the entire nation were to gather to benefit you with something, they would not benefit you except with what Allah has already written for you; and if they were to gather to harm you with something, they would not harm you except with what Allah has already written against you. The pens have been lifted and the pages have dried." [Jami' at-Tirmidhi, Book of Supplications, Hadith 2516]

17. That I thank Allah, Exalted is He, for His blessings that cannot be counted, His favor, and His mercy that cannot be comprehended. For shukr (gratitude) is among the characteristics of good manners toward the One who bestowed favors, did good, and showed grace. "And Allah has extracted you from the wombs of your mothers not knowing a thing, and He made for you hearing and vision and hearts [i.e., intellect] that perhaps you would be grateful." "And a sign for them is the dead earth. We have brought it to life and brought forth from it grain, and from it they eat. And We placed therein gardens of palm trees and grapevines and caused to burst forth therefrom some springs — that they may eat of His fruit. And their hands have not produced it, so will they not be grateful?" [Qur'an, An-Nahl 16:78; Ya-Sin 36:33-35]. Allah, Exalted is He, promised the grateful increase in blessings, just as He promised the people of ingratitude and denial increase in regret. "And [remember] when your Lord proclaimed, 'If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]; but if you deny, indeed, My punishment is severe.'" [Qur'an, Ibrahim 14:7]

18. That I seek Allah's forgiveness and persist in istighfar (seeking forgiveness). For istighfar is an expiation for sin, a renewer of taubah (repentance) and iman, and a source of comfort and tranquility. "And whoever does a wrong or wrongs himself but then seeks forgiveness of Allah will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful." "And those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves [by transgression], remember Allah and seek forgiveness for their sins — and who can forgive sins except Allah? — and [who] do not persist in what they have done while they know. Those — their reward is forgiveness from their Lord and gardens beneath which rivers flow, wherein they will abide eternally; and excellent is the reward of the [righteous] workers." [Qur'an, An-Nisa 4:110; Ali 'Imran 3:135-136]

19. That I observe Allah, Exalted is He, in my secret and open life, conscious of Allah's saying: "Have you not considered that Allah knows what is in the heavens and what is on the earth? There is in no private conversation three but that He is the fourth of them, nor are there five but that He is the sixth of them — and no less than that and no more except that He is with them [in knowledge] wherever they are. Then He will inform them of what they did, on the Day of Resurrection. Indeed Allah is, of all things, Knowing." [Qur'an, Al-Mujadila 58:7]
# Chapter 2: Being a Muslim in My Worship

Worship (*‘ibādah*) in Islam is the fountainhead of submission and the summit of feeling the greatness of the One worshipped. It is the ascending path of connection between the creature and the Creator. It also has profound effects in one’s dealings with Allah’s creation. In this regard the pillars of Islam — prayer, fasting, zakah, and Hajj — and all works by which a person seeks the face of Allah and follows His law, are equal. The logic of Islam insists that life in its entirety be worship and obedience. This is the meaning of His, the Exalted, saying: *“And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. I want from them no provision, nor do I want them to feed Me. Indeed, it is Allah who is the Provider, the Firm Possessor of Strength.”* [Qur’an, Adh-Dhariyat 51:56–58] And His saying: *“Say, indeed my prayer, my sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds.”* [Qur’an, Al-An‘am 6:162]

So that I may be a Muslim in my worship, this obliges me to the following:

## 1. That My Worship Be Alive, Connected to the Worshipped

This is the level of excellence (*iḥsān*) in worship. The Messenger of Allah ﷺ was asked about *iḥsān* and said: *“That you worship Allah as though you see Him; and if you do not see Him, then indeed He sees you.”* [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 2, Hadith 47; Sahih Muslim, Book 1, Hadith 1]

## 2. That It Be a Worship of Humility

I should feel in it the warmth of closeness and the sweetness of devotion. ‘Ā’ishah, may Allah be pleased with her, said: *“The Messenger of Allah ﷺ would be joking with us and we with him. Then when prayer came, it was as if he did not know us and we did not know him.”* [Sahih Muslim, Book 4, Hadith 862]

To this the Messenger ﷺ pointed with his saying: *“How many a fasting person has nothing from his fast but hunger and thirst.”* [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book 7, Hadith 1687; authenticated by al-Albani] And his saying: *“How many a praying person has nothing from his prayer but fatigue and exertion.”* [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book 5, Hadith 1437; authenticated by al-Albani]

## 3. That I Be Present of Heart in My Worship

Removed from the occupations of the world around me and its anxieties. To this the Messenger ﷺ pointed with his saying: *“Allah does not look at a prayer in which a person does not bring along his heart with his body.”* [Sunan an-Nasa’i, Book 10, Hadith 1269] And it was said: *“Prayer is of the Hereafter; so when you enter it, you have left the world.”*

It is reported from al-Ḥasan [al-Baṣrī] that he said: *“Every prayer in which the heart is not present is quicker to punishment.”*

## 4. That in Worship I Be Insatiable in Hope and Unsated in Thirst

I draw near to Allah through the supererogatory acts (*nawāfil*) in response to the words of Allah, the Exalted, in the sacred hadith (*ḥadīth qudsī*): *“Whosoever shows enmity to a friend of Mine, I declare war upon him. My servant does not draw near to Me with anything more beloved to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him. And My servant continues to draw near to Me with supererogatory acts until I love him. When I love him, I am his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes, and his foot with which he walks. If he asks of Me, I will surely give him; and if he seeks refuge in Me, I will surely protect him. I do not hesitate regarding anything I am to do as I hesitate to take the soul of a believer who hates death, for I dislike displeasing him.”* [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 81, Hadith 6502]

## 5. That I Be Eager for the Night Vigil (*Qiyām al-Layl*)

And that I train my soul upon it until it grows accustomed to it. For the night vigil is among the strongest generators of faith. And Allah, the Exalted, spoke truly when He said: *“Indeed, the hours of the night are more effective for concurrence [of heart and tongue] and more suitable for words.”* [Qur’an, Al-Muzzammil 73:6] And He described His believing servants by His saying: *“They used to sleep but little of the night, and in the hours before dawn they would ask forgiveness.”* [Qur’an, Adh-Dhariyat 51:17–18] And *“Their sides part [i.e., they arise] from [their] beds; they supplicate their Lord in fear and aspiration, and from what We have provided them, they spend.”* [Qur’an, As-Sajdah 32:16]

## 6. That I Have Sessions and Contemplations with the Noble Qur’an

Especially at dawn, for He, the Exalted, said: *“Indeed, the Qur’an of dawn is ever witnessed.”* [Qur’an, Al-Isrā’ 17:78] I recite it with reflection, thought, humility, and sorrow, for the Messenger ﷺ said: *“Indeed, this Qur’an was sent down with sorrow; so when you recite it, show sorrow.”* [Reported by Abū Ya‘lā and Abū Nu‘aym in *al-Ḥilyah*] And also because I must remember the saying of Allah, the Exalted: *“If We had sent down this Qur’an upon a mountain, you would have seen it humble itself, splitting from fear of Allah.”* [Qur’an, Al-Ḥashr 59:21] And the saying of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: *“No one truly believes in the Qur’an who considers lawful what it has declared unlawful.”* [Sunan al-Bayhaqī, Shu‘ab al-Īmān] And his saying: *“The best act of worship of my Ummah is the recitation of the Qur’an.”* [Reported by al-Ḥākim]

And in the hadith of ‘Abdullah ibn Mas‘ūd, may Allah be pleased with him, from the Prophet ﷺ who said: *“Indeed, this Qur’an is the banquet of Allah; so accept His banquet as much as you are able. This Qur’an is a rope whose one end is in the hand of Allah and the other end is in your hands; so hold fast to it, for you will not go astray nor be destroyed. And it will not wear out from much recitation. Recite it, for Allah will reward you for every letter with ten good deeds. I do not say that Alif Lām Mīm is one letter, but rather alif is a letter, lām is a letter, and mīm is a letter.”* [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book 9, Hadith 2910; authenticated by al-Albani]

And in his counsel to Ibn ‘Umar, may Allah be pleased with them both: *“Hold fast to the recitation of the Qur’an, for it is a light for you on earth and a treasure for you in heaven.”* [Reported by al-Ḥākim]

The following are some of the supererogatory acts that are recommended to persevere in and to do much of: the night vigil, the *Ḍuḥā* prayer, the *Tarāwīḥ* prayer, fasting — especially the day of ‘Arafah for non-pilgrims, the day of ‘Āshūrā’, six days of Shawwāl, the white days [the 13th, 14th, and 15th] of every month, and *i‘tikāf* (spiritual retreat).

## 7. That Supplication (*Du‘ā’*) Be My Ascent to Allah in Every Matter of Mine

For supplication is the essence of worship. And I should be eager for the reported supplications. Allah spoke truly when He said: *“Call upon Me; I will respond to you.”* [Qur’an, Ghāfir 40:60]

Among these reported supplications are:

- **At sleep:** *“In Your name, my Lord, I lie down, and in Your name I rise. If You take my soul, forgive it; and if You release it, protect it as You protect Your righteous servants.”* [Sahih Muslim, Book 48, Hadith 2713]
- **Upon waking:** *“All praise is for Allah who gave us life after He caused us to die, and to Him is the return.”* [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book 80, Hadith 6324]
- **When putting on and taking off a garment:** *“O Allah, I ask You for the good of it and the good for which it was made, and I seek refuge in You from the evil of it and the evil for which it was made.”* [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book 5, Hadith 3455]
- **When leaving and entering the home:** *“In the name of Allah, I place my trust in Allah, and there is no power nor strength except with Allah.”* [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book 6, Hadith 3518; Sunan Abu Dawud, Book 39, Hadith 5095]
- **When going to the mosque:** *“O Allah, place light in my heart, light in my sight, light in my hearing, light on my right, light on my left, light above me, light below me, light in front of me, light behind me, and grant me light.”* [Sahih Muslim, Book 4, Hadith 763]

- **At the close of prayer:** *“Whoever glorifies Allah thirty-three times after every prayer, and praises Allah thirty-three times, and magnifies Allah thirty-three times, and completes the hundred by saying: ‘There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah alone, without partner; to Him belongs sovereignty and to Him is all praise, and He is over all things competent’ — his sins will be forgiven, even if they are like the foam of the sea.”* [Sahih Muslim, Book 5, Hadith 597]

# Chapter 3: Being a Muslim in My Character

## Noble Character Is the Core of Islam’s Message

Noble character (*khuluq*) is the fundamental objective of the message of Islam, as the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, expressed in his hadith: “I was only sent to perfect noble character.”[^1] This is confirmed by the Noble Qur’an: “Those whom We have given authority in the land establish prayer, give zakah, enjoin what is right, and forbid what is wrong. And to Allah belongs the outcome of [all] matters” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Hajj 22:41], and the verse: “Righteousness is not that you turn your faces toward the east or the west, but [true] righteousness is [in] one who believes in Allah, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets and gives wealth, in spite of love for it, to relatives, orphans, the needy, the traveler, those who ask [for help], and for freeing slaves; [and who] establishes prayer and gives zakah; [those who] fulfill their promise when they promise; and [those who] are patient in poverty and hardship and during battle. Those are the ones who have been true, and it is those who are the righteous” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:177].

Noble character is the proof of faith and its fruit. Faith without character has no value. To this meaning the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed when he said: “Faith is not expressed through one’s desires, but rather it is what settles in the heart and is confirmed by action.”[^2]

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, was asked, “What is good fortune?” He said, “Good character.” And he was asked, “What is bad fortune?” He said, “Bad character.”[^3]

Character is the heaviest thing in the servant’s scale on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever has corrupt character and evil deeds will not be hastened [to Paradise] by his lineage. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “There is nothing heavier in the scale of the servant on the Day of Resurrection than good character.”[^4]

Noble character is the outcome of the acts of worship in Islam. Without that, they remain mere rites and movements devoid of value or benefit. Regarding prayer, Allah the Exalted says: “Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing” [Qur’an, Surah Al-‘Ankabut 29:45], and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever is not prevented by his prayer from immorality and wrongdoing, his prayer will only increase him in distance from Allah.”[^5] Regarding fasting, he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “When one of you is fasting, let him not utter obscene speech or raise his voice. If anyone abuses him or fights him, let him say: ‘Indeed, I am fasting.’”[^6] Regarding Hajj, Allah the Exalted says: “Hajj is [during] well-known months, so whoever has made Hajj obligatory upon himself therein [by entering the state of ihram], there is [to be for him no] sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing during Hajj” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:197], and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever performs Hajj and does not have sexual relations or commit disobedience will return [free from sin] as the day his mother bore him.”[^7]

## Traits of the Muslim

Among the most important moral traits that a person should possess in order to be a Muslim in character are the following:

### 1. Cautious Avoidance of Suspicious Matters (*al-tawarra‘ ‘an al-shubuhāt*)

That a person avoid the forbidden and guard against doubtful matters, in compliance with the statement of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: “The lawful is clear and the unlawful is clear, and between them are doubtful matters which many people do not know. Whoever guards against the doubtful matters has safeguarded his religion and his honor. And whoever falls into the doubtful matters falls into the unlawful, like the shepherd who grazes his flock around a sanctuary, nearly grazing within it. Beware, for every king has a sanctuary, and beware, the sanctuary of Allah is His prohibitions. Beware, in the body there is a piece of flesh: if it is sound the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt the whole body is corrupt. Indeed, it is the heart.”[^8]

He also said: “The servant will not attain the status of the pious until he abandons what is permissible out of caution against what is doubtful.”[^9]

### 2. Lowering the Gaze (*ghad al-baṣar*)

That he lower his gaze from what Allah has forbidden, for looking sows desire and gradually draws its owner into falling into sin and disobedience. The Noble Qur’an has warned against superfluous looking, for Allah the Exalted says: “Tell the believing men to lower their gaze” [Qur’an, Surah An-Nur 24:30]. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “The glance is a poisoned arrow of Iblis,” and he said: “Lower your gazes and guard your private parts, or Allah will brand your faces.”[^10]

### 3. Guarding the Tongue (*ṣawn al-lisān*)

That he guard his tongue from superfluous speech, backbiting, and tale-bearing. Imam al-Nawawi said: “Know that it is incumbent upon every person to guard his tongue from all speech, except speech in which there is manifest benefit. When speech and silence are equal in benefit, the sunnah is to remain silent, for permissible speech often leads to forbidden or disliked speech, which is common in practice. And there is nothing like safety.”[^11]

Many hadiths have come from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, explaining what the tongue brings upon its owner of evil and affliction, such as his saying: “Are people dragged on their faces into Hellfire except because of the harvests of their tongues?”[^12] and his saying: “The believer is not one who slanders, curses, or speaks obscenely and foully.”[^13] And he said: “Whoever speaks much, his slips increase; whoever slips much, his sins increase; and whoever has many sins, the Fire becomes more deserving of him.”[^14]

### 4. Modesty (*al-ḥayā’*)

That he be modest in all his states, without that preventing him from having courage in matters of truth. Among the manifestations of modesty are: not interfering in the affairs of others, lowering the gaze, being humble, not raising the voice, contentment, and similar qualities. It has been narrated about the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he was more modest than a young woman in her seclusion. He used to say: “Faith has seventy-odd or sixty-odd branches. The best of them is saying: ‘There is no deity worthy of worship except Allah,’ and the least of them is removing harm from the path. And modesty is a branch of faith.”[^15] The scholars said concerning modesty: “Its reality is a quality that prompts one to abandon abominations, and it is a part of vigilance against shortcomings in the rights of the One [Allah] who sees.”

### 5. Forbearance and Patience (*al-ḥilm wa al-ṣabr*)

Among the most prominent qualities that must be present in the Muslim are patience and forbearance, for working for Islam is filled with hardships, and the path of *da‘wah* (دعوة) is surrounded by difficulties. Harm, persecution, accusation, insult, and mockery are all obstacles hurled in the path of workers in order to depress their morale, paralyze their movement, and turn them away from calling to Allah.

Hence it becomes clear that the task of the brother who calls [to Allah] is among the most difficult tasks. He must carry the message to all people, with their different temperaments, minds, and natures; to the ignorant and the learned, to the rational and the emotional, to the flexible and the rigid, to the calm and the excitable. Then, consequently, he must address people according to their understanding, encompass all of them, and try to enter into all their souls — and this alone requires an enormous capacity for patience, endurance, and forbearance.

For this reason, Qur’anic and Prophetic guidance overflows with encouragement to adorn oneself with patience, forbearance, and deliberateness.

From the Qur’anic guidance:

- “And whoever is patient and forgives — indeed, that is of the matters [requiring] determination” [Qur’an, Surah Ash-Shura 42:43].
- “So pardon them with gracious pardon” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Hijr 15:85].
- “Indeed, the patient will be given their reward without account” [Qur’an, Surah Az-Zumar 39:10].
- “And let them pardon and overlook. Would you not like that Allah should forgive you?” [Qur’an, Surah An-Nur 24:22].
- “And when the ignorant address them [harshly], they say [words of] peace” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Furqan 25:63].

From the Prophetic guidance:

- “The servant may attain by forbearance the rank of one who fasts and prays [at night].”
- “Shall I not tell you of that by which Allah elevates your houses and raises your ranks?” They said, “Yes, O Messenger of Allah.” He said: “Forgiving one who has wronged you, giving to one who has deprived you, maintaining ties with one who has cut you off, and being forbearing toward one who has been foolish toward you.”
- The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “When Allah gathers the creation, a caller will call out: ‘Where are the people of virtue?’ A group of people will rise, few in number, and they will hasten toward Paradise. The angels will meet them and say: ‘What is your virtue?’ They will say: ‘When we were wronged, we were patient; and when we were treated badly, we were forbearing.’ It will be said to them: ‘Enter Paradise; excellent is the reward of the workers.’”

From the Prophetic applications:

- On the Day of Hunayn, a man said, “By Allah, this distribution is not for the sake of Allah’s pleasure.” The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, became angry and said: “May Allah have mercy on Moses; he was harmed with more than this, yet he was patient.”
- Anas reported: The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, entered the mosque one day wearing a thick Najrani cloak with a coarse hem. A Bedouin came up behind him and pulled his cloak so roughly that the hem left a mark on the Messenger’s neck. Then the man said: “O Muhammad, give me from the wealth of Allah that is with you.” The Messenger of Allah turned to him and smiled, then ordered that he be given something.
- Abu Hurayrah reported that a Bedouin said to the Messenger, “O Muhammad, load me on two camels, for you are not loading me from your wealth nor your father’s wealth,” and he tugged his cloak when he caught up to him, so that his neck reddened. The Messenger of Allah ordered that he be given a load of barley and a load of dates.
- Al-Tabarani reported: A woman used to speak to men indecently. She passed by the Prophet while he was sitting on the ground eating *tharīd* (ثريد). She said, “Look at him, sitting like a slave and eating like a slave.”
- Abu Hurayrah reported that a man said, “O Messenger of Allah, I have relatives with whom I maintain ties, yet they cut me off; I treat them well, yet they treat me badly; I am forbearing with them, yet they are ignorant toward me.” He said, “If you are as you say, it is as if you are feeding them hot ashes, and you will continue to have a supporter from Allah over them as long as you remain so.”
- [Also relevant is] the statement of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, to ‘Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb when he almost struck the neck of a Jew who came to demand from the Messenger a debt owed to him and said, “You are a people of *Muḍar*, a people of delay.” The Messenger said: “You should have demanded payment kindly, and you should have paid kindly, O ‘Umar.”

It is reported that Jesus, peace be upon him, used to travel from village to village calling to Allah, accompanied by his disciples. He would speak good to people, but they would respond with evil, insulting and abusing him. His disciples wondered at his forbearance and asked him the secret of that. He said: “Every person spends from what he has.”

All these examples and others confirm the necessity of the callers [to Allah] being adorned with forbearance, patience, pardon — especially when the harm comes from relatives, friends, beloved ones, companions, and brothers. For that engenders love and affection, removes division and discord, and suffices in preserving the pleasure of Allah, the Exalted and Majestic.

### 6. Truthfulness (*al-ṣidq*)

That he be truthful, not lying. He follows the truth even against himself, without fearing in Allah the blame of any blamer. Lying is among the ugliest qualities and the most despicable; it is an entry to many Satanic pitfalls. Guarding against the causes of lying gives the soul an immunity that protects it from Satan’s whisperings and suggestions, and it remains upon its purity, cleanliness, and nobility. Lying destroys the soul and humiliates the human being. For this reason Islam forbade lying and considered it among the accursed afflictions. The Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Indeed, truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man may continue to be truthful until he is written with Allah as a truthful one. Indeed, lying leads to wickedness, and wickedness leads to the Fire. A man may continue to lie until he is written with Allah as a liar.”[^16]

### 7. Humility (*al-tawāḍu‘*)

That he be humble, especially among his Muslim brothers, making no distinction between rich and poor in that. The Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, used to seek refuge with Allah from the puffing up of arrogance. He said: “No one will enter Paradise in whose heart is a speck of arrogance.”[^17] And he informed about his Lord, saying: “Might is My garment and pride is My cloak; whoever contends with Me regarding one of them, I will punish him.”[^18]

### 8. Avoiding Suspicion, Backbiting, and Seeking Out the Faults of Muslims

This is in compliance with the saying of Allah the Exalted: “O you who have believed, avoid much [negative] assumption. Indeed, some assumption is sin. And do not spy or backbite each other. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother when dead? You would detest it. And fear Allah; indeed, Allah is Accepting of repentance and Merciful” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Ḥujurāt 49:12], and “Indeed, those who abuse believing men and believing women undeservedly have borne upon themselves a slander and manifest sin” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Aḥzāb 33:58], and in compliance with the statement of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: “O you who have believed with your tongues while faith has not yet entered your hearts: do not backbite Muslims and do not seek out their faults, for whoever seeks out the fault of his Muslim brother, Allah will expose him even in the depth of his house.”[^19]

### 9. Generosity and Munificence (*al-jūd wa al-karam*)

That he be generous and munificent, giving himself and his wealth in the path of Allah. Among the most revealing things of the stinginess of souls is dealing with them through dinars and dirhams. How many positions and concerns collapse when touched by the spark of material calculation! In the Noble Qur’an are dozens of verses in which traits of faith are joined with the trait of spending: “And from what We have provided them, they spend” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:3], and “And whatever good you spend is for yourselves, and you do not spend except seeking the countenance of Allah. And whatever you spend of good — it will be fully repaid to you, and you will not be wronged” [Qur’an, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:272]. And let the withholding miser hear the statement of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: “There is no day on which the servants wake but that two angels descend. One of them says: ‘O Allah, give the spender a substitute,’ and the other says: ‘O Allah, give the miser ruin.’”[^20]

## Conclusion

Finally, the Muslim must be a good example among people and a practical translator of the message of Islam and its etiquette in his food, drink, clothing, speech, greeting, travel, presence, and in all his movements and stillness.

# Chapter 4: Being a Muslim in My Family and Home

My belonging to Islam must make me a bearer of a message in life—nay, it must make my life, all of my life, directed in accordance with this message. If my belonging to Islam obliges me to be a Muslim in myself—in *‘aqeedah* (عَقِيدَة), worship, and morals—then it also obliges me to work to make the society in which I live Muslim around me. That is because among the effects that Islam instills and pours into the human soul, if it has *iman* (إيمان) and does well, are concern for others, calling them, advising them, and protective jealousy (*ghayrah*, غَيْرَة) over them, in fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever spends the night while not concerned with the affairs of the Muslims is not one of them.” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Faith] From here is laid upon me a new responsibility: the responsibility of establishing the Muslim society and the responsibility of carrying Islam to society.

And the first step in this field—the first, nearest step—is that my home be Muslim: that I carry the message of Islam to my small society, to my family, to my wife, and to my children; then to the nearest and nearest of kin. This is what the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, adhered to at the beginning of his call: “So do not invoke with Allah another deity, lest you be among the punished. And warn, [O Muhammad], your closest kindred, and lower your wing to those who follow you of the believers.” [Qur’an, Ash-Shu‘ara 26:213-215]

And from here, the first duty entrusted to the Muslim immediately after *iman* (faith) is his responsibility toward his family, his home, and his children—by the word of Allah, the Exalted: “O you who have believed, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones, over which are [appointed] angels, harsh and severe; they do not disobey Allah in what He commands them but do what they are commanded.” [Qur’an, At-Tahrim 66:6]

## The Responsibility of Marriage

Indeed, in order for Islam to help me succeed in establishing the Muslim home, it guided me to the way and pointed to a set of factors and causes that facilitate my task and achieve my goal, among them:

- That my marriage be for Allah—that is, for establishing the Muslim home, for producing righteous offspring (*dhurriyyah*, ذُرِّيَّة) who undertake the bearing of the trust (*amanah*, أَمَانَة) and through whom guidance continues to be handed down, “offspring, some of them from others.” [Qur’an, Aal Imran 3:34]

- That among the objectives of my marriage be that I lower my gaze, guard my chastity, and fear Allah, my Lord. In this regard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Three for whom Allah is bound to help: the *mujahid* (مُجَاهِد) in the path of Allah, the *mukatab* (مُكَاتَب) who desires to fulfill his contract, and the one who marries seeking chastity (*‘iffah*, عِفَّة).” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Marriage] And he said: “Whoever marries has completed half of his religion, so let him fear Allah regarding the remaining half.” [Al-Tabarani, Al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat]

- That I make a good choice in my wife, the partner of my life and companion of my path, because the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Choose carefully for your sperm (*nutfah*, نُطْفَة), for indeed offspring is inherited.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage] And in another narration: “Marry the compatible ones (*al-akfa’*, الأَكْفَاء) and give your daughters in marriage to the compatible ones.” [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Marriage]

- That I choose a woman of *khuluq* (character, خُلُق) and *din* (religion, دِين), even if she be below others in wealth and beauty. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Do not marry women for their beauty, for their beauty may fade; and do not marry them for their wealth, for their wealth may make them rebellious; but marry them for their religion. And a black slave woman (*amah*, أَمَة) with religion is better.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage]

- That I beware of opposing Allah’s command in this and guard against His anger and vengeance. For the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever marries a woman for her wealth, Allah will increase him only in humiliation; and whoever marries her for her beauty, Allah will increase him only in poverty; and whoever marries her for her lineage, Allah will increase him only in baseness. And whoever marries a woman intending only to lower his gaze, guard his chastity, or maintain ties of kinship (*rahim*, رَحِم), Allah will bless him through her and bless her through him.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage]

## Responsibility after Marriage

Indeed, my good choice of a wife does not exempt me from continuing my responsibility toward her after marriage; rather, the greater responsibility begins from the first moment of my marriage. From here are laid upon me a number of consequences, among them:

- That I treat her well and honor my conduct toward her, in order to realize trust between her and me, in fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “The best of you is the one who is best to his family, and I am the best of you to my family.” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1162] And his statement: “Indeed the best of you in character are the kindest of you to their families.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage, Hadith 1978]

- That my relationship with her not be limited to the relationship of bed and desire; rather, before this and above it there must be realized between us intellectual, spiritual, and emotional harmony. We read together, perform some acts of worship together, manage the affairs of the home together, and then we have some opportunities for play and amusement. In the sphere of worship, Allah the Exalted says: “And enjoin prayer upon your family [and people] and be steadfast therein.” [Qur’an, Taha 20:132] And [He says about Ismail]: “And he used to enjoin on his people prayer and zakah and was to his Lord pleasing.” [Qur’an, Maryam 19:55] In the sphere of play and recreation, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to race with Aisha, may Allah be pleased with her, by running. [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Marriage] And in the sphere of domestic cooperation, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, used to take up many burdens, among them mending the sandal. [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Good Manners]

- That my relationship with my wife—what has been mentioned and what has not—acquire the character of legality, so that it is not at the expense of Islam or in what Allah has forbidden. For it is reported from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, that he said: “Indeed, there is no one who obeys a woman in disobedience to Allah except that Allah casts him into the Fire.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Marriage] And his statement: “Allah will not confront a servant with a sin greater than the ignorance of his family.” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Wills] And he said: “Wretched be the slave of luxury.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Riqaq]

## My Responsibility with Her in Raising the Children

In reality, success in marriage—in choosing a righteous woman and in the fusion of the two spouses in the crucible of Islam—helps to a great extent in achieving the desired Islamic upbringing (*tarbiyyah*, تَرْبِيَّة) of the children. As for failure in achieving the Islamic marriage and a poor choice of the woman, that leads to ruinous consequences and heralds widespread evil for the whole home. Any contradiction that occurs in the life of the spouses will inevitably, directly, and quickly reflect upon the upbringing of the children and upon their souls; and consequently they will bear many complexes and deviations. Therefore, the first factor in achieving the Islamic upbringing of the children is achieving the Islam-ness of marriage, as we mentioned earlier.

The truth is that the hoped-for fruit of establishing the Muslim home is producing righteous offspring. [As the believers pray:] “Our Lord, grant us from among our wives and offspring comfort to our eyes and make us an example for the righteous.” [Qur’an, Al-Furqan 25:74] The child is born on the *fitrah* (الفِطْرَة); if sound upbringing is provided for him, he becomes righteous, and if he grows up between contradictory or deviant parents, he becomes accordingly a fulfillment of the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Every child is born on the *fitrah*; then his parents make him a Jew, a Christian, or a Magian.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Beginning of Creation, Hadith 1292]

For this reason Islam emphasized and insisted on the good upbringing of children, and on providing all the causes, supports, atmospheres, and climates that realize good upbringing. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “For a man to discipline his child is better for him than giving a *sā‘* (صاع) in charity,” [Sunan Ibn Majah, Book of Adab, Hadith 3668] and he said: “No father has given his child a gift better than good character (*adab*, أَدَب),” [Jami‘ at-Tirmidhi, Book of Knowledge] and he said: “Honor your children and refine their manners,” [Al-Tabarani, Al-Mu‘jam al-Awsat] and he said: “When the son of Adam dies his deeds are cut off except for three: an ongoing charity (*sadaqah jariyah*, صَدَقَةٌ جَارِيَةٌ), knowledge benefited from, or a righteous child who supplicates for him.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Wills, Hadith 1631]
# Chapter 5: Overcoming My Ego

## That I Triumph over My Self

Man is in conflict with his *nafs* (نفس) — his self — until he triumphs over it, or it triumphs over him, or the conflict remains ongoing, the battle ebbing and flowing, until death overtakes him while he is still in that state: *"And [by] the soul and He who proportioned it, and inspired it [with discernment of] its wickedness and its righteousness. He has succeeded who purifies it, and he has failed who instills it [with corruption]."* [Qur'an, Surah Ash-Shams 91:7-10].

To this meaning the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, alluded when he said: *"Trials are presented to hearts like a reed mat, straw by straw. As for any heart that absorbs them, a black spot is dotted in it; and as for any heart that rejects them, a white spot is dotted in it, until hearts become one of two kinds: a white heart like a smooth stone, harmed by no trial; and another black and inverted, recognizing no good and rejecting no evil."* [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 144].

### Three Categories of People in the Battle of the Self

People, in the battle of the *nafs*, are of three categories.

**First**, a category whose desires have triumphed over them, so they inclined to the earth and settled into worldly life. These are the disbelievers and those who followed their path of those whom Allah forsook and so their own selves caused them to forget Him. Allah the Exalted describes them in His Book: *"Have you seen he who has taken as his god his [own] desire, and Allah has sent him astray due to knowledge and has set a seal upon his hearing and his heart and put over his vision a veil? So who will guide him after Allah? Then will you not be reminded?"* [Qur'an, Surah Al-Jathiyah 45:23].

**Second**, a category who struggle against their selves and wrestle with their desires. They triumph at times and are defeated at others. They err and then repent; they disobey Allah and then regret and seek forgiveness: *"And those who, when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves [by transgression], remember Allah and seek forgiveness for their sins — and who can forgive sins except Allah? — and [who] do not persist in what they have done while they know."* [Qur'an, Surah Aal-E-Imran 3:135]. To these the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, alluded when he said: *"Every son of Adam is a sinner, and the best of sinners are those who repent."* [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Repentance, Hadith 2499].

It is narrated in this regard from Wahb ibn Munabbih that he said: Iblis met Yahya ibn Zakariyya, peace be upon them both. Yahya said to him: Tell me about the dispositions of the son of Adam in your view. Iblis said: They are of three kinds. A kind among them are like guarded kings; we have no power over them at all. And a kind who are in our hands like a ball in the hands of your children, and sometimes they suffice themselves against us. The third kind are the most difficult of the categories upon us: we pounce upon one of them until we attain what we need from him, then he rushes to *istighfar* (استغفار) — seeking forgiveness — and spoils by it what we had attained from him. So neither do we despair of him, nor do we attain our need from him.

### Supports of Victory in the Battle of the Self

#### The Heart

That the *qalb* (قلب) — the heart — be alive, tender, pure, firm, and radiant, according to the statement of Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah be pleased with him: *"Indeed, Allah has vessels upon His earth, and the vessels of Allah are the hearts. The most beloved of them to Him are the most tender, the purest, and the firmest."* Then he explained, saying: *"The firmest in religion, the purest in certainty, and the tenderest toward brothers."* And his statement: *"The heart of the believer is bare, with a shining lamp within it; and the heart of the disbeliever is black and inverted."* [Recorded by Ahmad and al-Tabarani].

The Noble Qur'an depicts the hearts of the believers, saying: *"The believers are those whose hearts tremble when Allah is mentioned, and when His verses are recited to them, they increase them in faith."* [Qur'an, Surah Al-Anfal 8:2]. And it depicts the hearts of the disbelievers, saying: *"So indeed, it is not the eyes that are blind, but blind are the hearts which are within the breasts."* [Qur'an, Surah Al-Hajj 22:46]. And He says: *"Then do they not reflect upon the Qur'an, or are there locks upon [their] hearts?"* [Qur'an, Surah Muhammad 47:24].

#### The Intellect

That the *aql* (عقل) — the intellect — be discerning, perceptive, discriminating, and guided by the sciences through which nearness to Allah is attained and His greatness and power are realized. It is the basis of the statement of the Exalted: *"Only those fear Allah, from among His servants, who have knowledge."* [Qur'an, Surah Fatir 35:28].

The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, pointed to the value of this blessing when he said: *"Allah has not created any creature more noble to Him than the intellect."* And his statement to Ali ibn Abi Talib, may Allah be pleased with him: *"When people draw near to Allah through various acts of righteousness, draw near to Him through your intellect."* And his statement: *"A man has not acquired anything like the excellence of an intellect that guides its possessor to guidance and turns him away from misguidance."* [Recorded by Ibn Hibban and others].

For this reason Islam drove people toward knowledge and understanding, and toward deep comprehension of religion, so that the intellect may take from causes what helps it to distinguish between good and evil, right and wrong, lawful and unlawful. The Messenger of Allah said: *"Whoever Allah intends good for, He grants him understanding of the religion."* [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Knowledge, Hadith 71]. And he said: *"The virtue of the scholar over the worshipper is like my virtue over the least of my companions."* [Sunan at-Tirmidhi, Book of Knowledge, Hadith 2685]. All of this is because of the value and effect of knowledge in deepening faith in souls and acquainting man with the realities of this universe.

Thus the believer's intellect is an aware worker that distinguishes between good and evil, lawful and unlawful, right and wrong, because in it he looks by the light of Allah from behind a thin veil: *"And he for whom Allah has not appointed light, for him there is no light."* [Qur'an, Surah An-Nur 24:40].

Nothing extinguishes the light of the intellect except sins, persistence in them, open commission of them, and failure to repent from them, according to the statement of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: *"Whoever commits a sin and parts with his intellect, it never returns to him."* And his statement: *"Were it not that the devils hover over the hearts of the sons of Adam, they would behold the dominion of the heavens and the earth."* [Recorded by Ahmad and others].

It is narrated from Anas ibn Malik, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: *"When I entered upon Uthman, may Allah be pleased with him, I had met a woman on my way and looked at her askance and contemplated her beauty. Uthman said when I entered: One of you enters while the trace of fornication is upon his eyes. Do you not know that the fornication of the eyes is looking? Repent, or I shall punish you. I said: Was this revealed after the Prophet? He replied: No, but it is insight, evidence, and true perspicacity."* [Recorded by Abu Nu'aym in *Hilyat al-Awliya*].

### Manifestations of Psychological Defeat

When man's heart dies or hardens, and when his intellect is extinguished or deviates — indeed, when he is defeated in his battle with *Shaytan* (الشيطان) — the entryways of evil into his self multiply, especially since *Shaytan* courses through the son of Adam like the coursing of blood.

When his resistance and psychological immunity are shattered, *Shaytan* becomes his constant companion: *"Satan has overcome them and made them forget the remembrance of Allah."* [Qur'an, Surah Al-Mujadilah 58:19]. To this meaning the noble verse alludes: *"[Iblis] said, 'Because You have put me in error, I will surely sit in wait for them on Your straight path. Then I will come to them from before them and from behind them and on their right and on their left.'"* [Qur'an, Surah Al-A'raf 7:16-17].

Moreover, the most dangerous disease that afflicts the defeated is *waswasah* (وسوسة) — insinuation: *Shaytan* whispers to them in every matter of their lives to turn them away from the path of Allah. In this regard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: *"Indeed, Satan lies in wait for the son of Adam at the paths. So he lay in wait for him at the path of Islam and said: Will you submit and leave your religion and the religion of your forefathers? So he disobeyed him and submitted. Then he lay in wait for him at the path of *hijrah* (الهجرة) and said: Will you emigrate? Will you leave your land and your sky? So he disobeyed him and emigrated. Then he lay in wait for him at the path of *jihad* (الجهاد) and said: Will you strive? It is but the waste of life and wealth: you will fight and be killed, your wives will be married by others, and your wealth will be divided. So he disobeyed him and strove. Then the Messenger of Allah said: Whoever does that and dies, it is incumbent upon Allah to admit him to Paradise."* [Sunan an-Nasa'i, Book of Jihad, Hadith 3097]. How good it would be if the reader-brother would review the story of *Shaytan* and the monk of the Children of Israel in the exegesis of the statement of the Exalted: *"Like Satan when he said to man, 'Disbelieve.' But when he disbelieved, he said, 'Indeed, I am disassociated from you. Indeed, I fear Allah, Lord of the worlds.'"* [Qur'an, Surah Al-Hashr 59:16].

### Means of Protection from Satan's Entryways

Indeed, in order to help man confront the satanic challenges and the diabolical suggestions of *Iblis* (إبليس), Islam directed him to many things that help him stand firm in the battle and enable him to overcome the most hostile of his enemies.

One of the righteous summarized them when he said: *"I looked and reflected: from which door does Satan come to man? I found that he comes through ten doors:

- **First:** greed and bad assumption — so I confronted him with trust and contentment.
- **Second:** love of life and long hopes — so I confronted him with fear of the sudden surprises of death.
- **Third:** love of blessing and fear of bad reckoning — so I confronted him with gratitude and contentment.
- **Fourth:** self-admiration (*al-ujb*) — so I confronted him with humility and fear of the consequence.
- **Fifth:** belittling people and showing them little respect — so I confronted him with recognizing their rights and sanctity.
- **Sixth:** envy — so I confronted him with contentment and satisfaction with Allah's distribution among His creation.
- **Seventh:** showing off and people's praise — so I confronted him with sincerity.
- **Eighth:** stinginess — so I confronted him with the perishing of what is in creatures' hands and the permanence of what is with the Creator.
- **Ninth:** arrogance — so I confronted him with contentment."*

Among the directives that Islam emphasized as a way to ward off the arrows of *Iblis* and his schemes is *dhikr* (ذكر) — the remembrance of Allah — at the beginning of every action. Abu Hurayrah narrated in this regard the following: *"The devil of the believer met the devil of the disbeliever. The devil of the disbeliever was fat, fleshy, and powerful, while the devil of the believer was emaciated, disheveled, dusty, and humiliated. The devil of the disbeliever said to the devil of the believer: Why are you so emaciated? He said: I am with a man who, when he eats, mentions the name of Allah, so I remain hungry; and when he drinks, mentions the name of Allah, so I remain thirsty; and when he puts on clothing, mentions the name of Allah, so I remain naked; and when he anoints himself, mentions the name of Allah, so I remain disheveled. The devil of the disbeliever said: And I am with a man who does none of that, so I share with him in his food, drink, and clothing."* [Musnad Ahmad].

Among the means of protection is avoiding satiety and overeating, even if the food is lawful and pure, according to the statement of the Exalted: *"Eat and drink, but be not excessive."* [Qur'an, Surah Al-A'raf 7:31]. And His statement, exalted be He: *"Indeed, Satan runs through the son of Adam like the running of blood, so narrow his passageways with hunger."* [Musnad Ahmad].

Among them also are recitation of the Qur'an, remembrance of Allah, and *istighfar* — seeking forgiveness — according to his statement: *"Indeed, Satan places the tip of his snout on the heart of the son of Adam. When he remembers Allah, he withdraws; and when he forgets Allah, he devours his heart."* [Ibn Abi al-Dunya, *al-Mujabbin*].

Among them is dispelling haste and deliberating over matters, according to his statement: *"Haste is from Satan and deliberateness is from Allah."* [Sunan Abu Dawud, Book of Behavior, Hadith 4606].

The space is too narrow to mention all the causes, works, and counsels that Islam enjoined for protection against the ambushes and schemes of *Shaytan*. And Allah the Exalted spoke truly when He said: *"Indeed, those who fear Allah — when an impulse touches them from Satan, they remember [Him]; and suddenly they have insight."* [Qur'an, Surah Al-A'raf 7:201].
# Chapter 6: Certainty That the Future Belongs to Islam

## To Be a Believer That the Future Belongs to Islam

That my faith in Islam should reach the level of certainty that the future belongs to this religion. For Islam's being from Allah makes it the most worthy and most capable of organizing the affairs of life, leading the caravan of humanity, and guiding it. It is the unique manhaj (المنهج)—suited to the needs of fitrah (الفطرة) and to harmonizing between the psychological and material requirements of man. "Does He not know who He created, while He is the Subtle, the Acquainted?" [Qur'an, Surah Al-Mulk 67:14].

For the rabbaniyyah (ربانية)—the divinity—of the Islamic manhaj is the characteristic that gives it superiority over all man-made methodologies, and distinguishes it with the qualities of permanence and giving in every time and place, and at every level.

And the universality of the Islamic manhaj embodies the humanistic character within it: the character of openness and the ability to bear the responsibility of this openness; the character that makes it transcend all regional, racial, national, ethnic, and linguistic considerations; the character that derives its openness, comprehensiveness, and humanity from its rabbaniyyah (ربانية).

And the flexibility of the Islamic manhaj is the characteristic that grants it the capacity to absorb the renewed, varied, and multiple problems of life—the characteristic that leaves room for ijtihad (الاجتهاد) in deriving rulings in matters where there is no explicit text, through qiyas (القياس), consideration of al-maslahah al-mursalah (المصلحة المرسلة), istihsan (الاستحسان), and other shar'i (الشرعي) evidences.

And the comprehensiveness of the Islamic manhaj is the characteristic that distinguishes it from other earthly methodologies and man-made systems with limited objectives. For the Islamic manhaj is the manhaj of the All-Knowing, the All-Aware, who knows the affairs of people, what they need, what is right for them, what harms and benefits them, and what makes them happy and miserable. Therefore, Islam is the manhaj capable of satisfying the individual and collective, legislative and guidance-oriented, internal and external needs of human life. "And [say, 'Ours is] the religion of Allah. And who is better than Allah in [ordaining] religion? And we are worshippers of Him." [Qur'an, Surah Al-Baqarah 2:138].

## To Be a Believer in the Inadequacy of Man-Made Methodologies

And I must realize the extent of the confusion and failure suffered by man-made systems throughout the world—whether capitalist, democratic, and liberal, or socialist and communist—because of their man-made nature, limitations, inability, inadequacy, and temporality.

On the social level: the systems (capitalist and socialist) failed to secure happiness, tranquility, and stability for man; rather, they caused his misery and wretchedness. The familial and social ties were loosened; morals disintegrated; values and virtues disappeared; and tension and agitation replaced tranquility and stability. Selfishness, self-preference, and love of self replaced cooperation, preferring others, love of others, and self-dedication to serving them.

On the economic level: the systems (capitalist and socialist) were unable to create the paradise we dream of and achieve the sufficiency and justice they claim to call for. Under both systems, there were problems of class warfare, colonial injustice, human exploitation, monopoly, poverty, unemployment, and endless daily problems.

On the political level: the systems (democratic and military, republican and monarchic, presidential and parliamentary) bear responsibility for the rottenness and deviation that has afflicted political life at every level. For exploitation, favoritism, bribery, and domination—not to mention civil strife, massacres, revolutions, coups, purges, assassinations, and the like—have become the characteristic of all these systems.

On the military level: all these systems bear responsibility for neglecting the issues of the oppressed Islamic peoples, such as the issues of Kashmir, Abyssinia, Eritrea, the Philippines, and others, and the issue of Palestine in particular—not to mention trafficking in and exploiting it for nearly a quarter of a century, and falling short of the psychological and military preparation that enables the Ummah (الأمة) to confront colonialism in whatever form it takes and to repel Israel.
# Part Two: Introduction

Belonging to Islam is the foundation. And belonging to the movement is an inseparable part of true belonging to this religion.

And Allah is the Granter of success, and in Him we seek help.

— The Author# Chapter 7: Living for Islam

## That I Should Live for Islam

If my belonging to Islam obliges me to live Islam as a creed, worship, and moral character — to live it within myself, in my home, and among my family — then it likewise obliges me to live *for it*; that is, to orient my entire life toward it, and to dedicate all my energies and capabilities to it.

People in this world are of three kinds. Which of these kinds am I?

## A Kind That Lives for This World

They are the materialists — in belief or in practice. The Noble Qur’an called them the *al-dahrīyyūn* (the temporalists) and said concerning them:

> And they say, "There is none but our worldly life, and we will not be resurrected." [Qur’an, Al-An‘ām 6:29]

and:

> And they say, "There is none but our worldly life: we die and live, and nothing destroys us but time." And they have of that no knowledge; they are only assuming. [Qur’an, Al-Jāthiyah 45:24]

The modern communists, and the secularists and existentialists who follow their path, proceed from this very doctrine. Lenin commented on the statement of a certain philosopher: "The world was created by no God or man, and has been, is, and will forever be a living flame, igniting and extinguishing according to laws."

When a person disbelieves in a life after this life in which he will be recompensed for what his hands have earned, this world becomes his greatest concern and the limit of his knowledge. He lives for it, rushes after it, and drowns in its passions and pleasures without reckoning.

## A Kind Lost Between Two Matters

They are the generality of people whose beliefs have become confused and whose striving in the worldly life has gone astray, while they imagine that they are doing well. Even if they believe in Allah and the Last Day, these beliefs of theirs are merely formal, wholly detached from their reality — a reality of mere spiritual rituals. The poet’s words ring true for such people:

> We patch our worldliness by tearing our religion,
> so neither our religion remains nor what we patch.

Imām al-Shahīd Ḥasan al-Bannā says in his epistle *"To What Do We Call People?"*:

"The Qur’an has defined the goals of life and the purposes of people in it. It states that some people’s concern in life is only eating and enjoying themselves. The Exalted says:

> As for those who disbelieve, they enjoy themselves and eat as cattle eat, and the Fire will be their residence. [Qur’an, Muḥammad 47:12]

It states that another people’s concern is adornment and perishing display. The Exalted says:

> Beautified for people is the love of that which they desire — of women and sons, heaped-up sums of gold and silver, fine branded horses, and cattle and tilled land. That is the enjoyment of worldly life, but with Allah is the best return. [Qur’an, Āl ‘Imrān 3:14]

It states that yet another people’s concern in life is kindling strife and reviving evils and corruptions. These are those concerning whom Allah said:

> And among people is he whose speech pleases you in the worldly life, and he calls Allah to witness as to what is in his heart, yet he is the fiercest of opponents. And when he turns away, he strives upon the earth to cause corruption in it and destroy crops and lineage. And Allah does not like corruption. [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:204-205]"

These are some of the purposes of people in life. Allah absolves the believers of them, exonerates them from them, and assigns them a higher mission, laying upon their shoulders a nobler duty. That duty is the guidance of humankind to the truth, the direction of all people to goodness, and the illumination of the whole world with the sun of Islam.

## A Kind That Considers This World a Field for the Hereafter

These are the true believers, who understand the reality of this life as they understand the value of this world compared to the Hereafter:

> And what is the worldly life except amusement and distraction? But the Home of the Hereafter is best for those who fear Allah. Then will you not reason? [Qur’an, Al-An‘ām 6:32]

So it does not distract or preoccupy them from fulfilling the purpose for which they were created:

> And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. [Qur’an, Al-Dhāriyāt 51:56]

Those who truly belong to Islam regard the world as an arena of competition in obedience to Allah and His pleasure. Their entire lives — their knowledge, their commerce, their wealth, their emotions, their time, their minds — are enlisted in this path. This is in contrast to the materialists, who enlist everything for the sake of desires and passions.

One of the things that confirms this is that the whole direction of modern civilization and the ingenuity of inventors aims at nothing but securing the greatest possible share of sensory pleasure and comfort for man. It contains nothing aimed at building this universe with goodness, security, and peace. The automobile, the airplane, the stove, the refrigerator, ornaments, juicers, refrigerators, vacuum cleaners, tools of adornment, furniture, clothing, entertainment and amusement — indeed hundreds, nay thousands, of instruments produced by factories across the earth are for the sensory pleasure and comfort of this human being.

Islam is not averse to research, inquiry, invention, and production — but first: to the extent that it does not harm man; and second: in a manner that realizes goodness and spreads righteousness in society, and observes the legitimacy and morality of their use and benefit.

Imām al-Shahīd Ḥasan al-Bannā pointed to this meaning in his epistle *"To What Do We Call People?"*:

"I ask you, my dear one: Have the Muslims understood from their Lord’s Book this meaning, so that their souls are elevated, their spirits are refined, they are freed from the bondage of matter, purified from the pleasure of passions and desires, raised above trivial matters and base purposes, and turn their faces toward Allah, the Originator of the heavens and the earth, as *ḥunafā’* (upright monotheists), exalting the word of Allah, striving in His cause, spreading His religion, and defending the precincts of His law? Or are they captives of desires, slaves of passions and ambitions, their only concern a soft morsel, a luxurious vehicle, a beautiful suit, a comfortable sleep, a charming woman, a false appearance, and a hollow title? They are content with wishes and live by their fancies; they plunge into the seas of false claims but never cross them."

And the Messenger of Allah ﷺ was truthful when he said:

> Wretched is the servant of the dinar; wretched is the servant of the dirham; wretched is the servant of the striped cloak. [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Riqāq, Hadith 2887]

## How Do I Live for Islam?

For my life to be directed toward the path of Islam and for the sake of Islam, I must realize a number of matters and adhere to them. Among them are:

### 1. Realizing the Purpose of Life

> And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me. [Qur’an, Al-Dhāriyāt 51:56]

and:

> It is He who created the heavens and earth in six days, and His Throne had been upon water, that He might test you as to which of you is best in deed. [Qur’an, Hūd 11:7]

### 2. Realizing the Value of This World Compared to the Hereafter

> Are you satisfied with the life of this world rather than the Hereafter? But what is the enjoyment of the life of this world compared with the Hereafter except a little. [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:38]

and:

> That is the enjoyment of the worldly life, but what is with Allah is better and more lasting. [Qur’an, Al-Kahf 18:46]

It is narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ once stood over a garbage heap and called to his Companions, saying: "Come to the world!" Then he grabbed a worn-out rag and a gnawed bone and said: "This is the world."

It is also narrated that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ once saw a dead sheep whose owners had discarded it. He turned to his Companions and said: "Do you see how little its owners valued it? By Allah, the world is of less value to Allah than this is to its owners. If the world were worth the wing of a mosquito to Allah, He would not have given a disbeliever a sip of water from it."

He also said:

> The Hour has drawn near, and people only increase in greed for this world, and only increase in distance from Allah.

And he said:

> The world is the prison of the believer and the paradise of the disbeliever. [Sahih Muslim, Book of Zuhd, Hadith 2956]

### 3. Realizing the Inevitability of Death and Taking Lesson From It

> Everyone upon the earth will perish, and there will remain the Face of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honor. [Qur’an, Al-Raḥmān 55:26-27]

and:

> Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your full compensation on the Day of Resurrection. So he who is drawn away from the Fire and admitted to Paradise has attained success. And what is the life of this world except the enjoyment of delusion. [Qur’an, Āl ‘Imrān 3:185]

It is narrated from the Messenger of Allah ﷺ that he said:

> Remember often the destroyer of pleasures and the disperser of assemblies. [Sunan al-Tirmidhī, Book of Asceticism, Hadith 2307; graded ṣaḥīḥ]

Abū Dharr al-Ghifārī, may Allah be pleased with him, said: "O Messenger of Allah, what were the scrolls of Mūsā?" He said: "They were all lessons. I wonder at one who is certain of death yet rejoices; I wonder at one who is certain of the Fire yet laughs; I wonder at one who is certain of destiny yet worries; I wonder at one who sees the world and its turnover with its people yet feels secure in it; and I wonder at one who is certain of the reckoning tomorrow yet does not act." [Al-Mu‘jam al-Awsaṭ]

It is narrated from Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said: "We went out with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ for a funeral. He sat by one of the graves and said: ‘Every day this grave calls out to me with a clear voice: O son of Adam, have you forgotten me? Do you not know that I am the abode of solitude, the abode of estrangement, the abode of loneliness, the abode of worms, and the abode of constriction — except for whomever Allah enlarges it for?’ Then he said: ‘The grave is either a garden from the gardens of Paradise or a pit from the pits of Hell.’" [Al-Tirmidhī]

### 4. Realizing the Reality of Islam

This is done through *tafaqquh* (deep understanding), learning, and knowing its principles, rulings, lawful and unlawful matters:

# Chapter 8: Believing in the Obligation to Work for Islam

Working for Islam — in order to create the personality that embodies it as creed and as moral character, to create the society that adheres to it in thought and behavior, to create the state that applies it as *shari'ah* (الشريعة), methodology, and constitution, and carries it as a guiding call to establish truth and justice in all worlds — this work, and all that it needs, is connected to it, branches from it, and requires it, is an Islamic *shar'i* (شرعي) obligation. It does not fall until there arises the authority that undertakes this responsibility and oversees the affairs of Muslims. And as long as this authority is non-existent, every shortcoming in this regard is nothing but the failure to promptly rise to the burdens of working for Islam.

And among what confirms the obligation of working for Islam is that it is a charged duty and not voluntary: the certainty of its obligation stems from several aspects.

## First: Its Obligation as a Principle

Working for Islam is an obligation of principle, because it is the basis of Allah's charge to all human beings — to the prophets and messengers first, then to all people until Allah inherits the earth and all that is upon it. By the evidence of His saying:

> "By time, indeed mankind is in loss, except for those who have believed and done righteous deeds and advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience." [Qur'an, Al-'Asr 103:1-3]

And His saying:

> "O Messenger, announce that which has been revealed to you from your Lord, and if you do not, then you have not conveyed His message." [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:67]

And His saying:

> "Indeed, those who conceal what We sent down of clear evidences and guidance after We made it clear for the people in the Scripture — those are cursed by Allah and cursed by those who curse." [Qur'an, Al-Baqarah 2:159]

And the pure Sunnah is replete with what was narrated from the Messenger ﷺ of hadiths that encourage calling to the truth and combating falsehood. Among them is his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> "Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he is unable, then with his tongue; if he is unable, then with his heart — and that is the weakest of faith. And there is not beyond that [even] a mustard seed of faith." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 49]

And his saying:

> "O people, indeed Allah says to you: Command the good and forbid the evil before you ask Me for victory, and I will not help you." [Sunan at-Tirmidhi]

And his saying:

> "If you see my ummah fearing to say to the wrongdoer: 'O wrongdoer,' then it is as if it has departed from them." [Sunan at-Tirmidhi]

## Second: Its Obligation as a Legal Ruling

Working for Islam is an obligation as a legal ruling, because the suspension of Allah's rule on earth, and the dominance of man-made systems and legislations over human societies, imposes upon Muslims the work of establishing the Islamic society, resuming Islamic life, and making people's servitude to Allah pervade their beliefs, morals, and systems. By the evidence of His saying:

> "But no, by your Lord, they will not [truly] believe until they make you, [O Muhammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged and submit in [full, willing] submission." [Qur'an, An-Nisa 4:65]

And His saying:

> "And in anything over which you disagree — its ruling is [to be referred] to Allah. [Say], 'That is Allah, my Lord; upon Him I have relied, and to Him I turn back.'" [Qur'an, Ash-Shuraa 42:10]

And His saying:

> "And whoever does not judge by what Allah has revealed — those are the disbelievers," and the verse about the wrongdoers, and the verse about the defiantly disobedient. [Qur'an, Al-Ma'idah 5:44-48]

So if realizing the Islamic society and ruling by what Allah has revealed is obligatory in itself, then the work to establish it and unify it becomes obligatory as a legal ruling, by the evidence of the legal maxim: "Whatever is indispensable for the discharge of an obligatory duty is itself obligatory."

Indeed, most — if not all — of the Islamic lands are governed by man-made systems that are a mixture of Roman, Greek, French, and other legislations. And the prevailing economic systems in these lands are capitalism and socialism, which makes working to demolish these *jahiliyyah* (الجاهلية) entities and resume Islamic life a *fard 'ayn* (فرض العين) — an individual obligation — upon every Muslim, until leadership and guardianship return to Islam.

Furthermore, many of the legal obligations depend for their execution and practice upon the establishment of a *khilāfah* (الخلافة) or an *imām* (الإمام), and this in turn is tied to the existence of an Islamic authority. Thus all the legislations related to the Islamic social, economic, and political systems, all the legislations related to governance, punishments, peace, war, *jihad* (الجهاد), reconciliation, treaties, and social and economic transactions — these and other aspects of Islamic legislation cannot be implemented except through a state based on the foundation of Islam.

## Third: Its Obligation by Necessity

Working for Islam is, likewise, an obligation by necessity in order to confront the challenges of the age and the conspiracies of the enemies of Islam, and to stop the materialistic waves and atheistic advances that threaten the Islamic existence with eradication and disappearance.

Indeed, a penetrating look at the conditions of the lands of the Islamic world confirms the necessity of an Islamic struggle — indeed, it makes undertaking it imperative. For there are lands that complain of the control of non-Muslims over them and their rule therein, such as Palestine, Kashmir, Eritrea, Cyprus, Bukhara, Samarkand, and others. And there are lands that complain of the domination of sectarian minorities filled with hatred, ruling over the necks of Muslims by force of iron and fire. And there are other parts of the Islamic world that complain of the domination of leftist or rightist parties over them, just as other parts experience bloody and terrible conflicts due to the clash between the forces of right and left over power.

In addition to all of this and that, the lands of the Islamic world all experience a state of loss… a state of chaos… political chaos, social chaos, economic chaos. They experience a terrifying deterioration in morals and values as well as in ideas and beliefs. All of that, and more, confirms the obligation of work, if only by necessity, to confront these challenges that Islam faces — whether from colonialism and international forces from without, or from its agents, hirelings, and collaborators with it from within:

> "And fight them until there is no *fitnah* (الفتنة) and [until] the religion, all of it, is for Allah." [Qur'an, Al-Anfal 8:39]

# Chapter 9: The Islamic Movement — Its Mission and Characteristics

## The Foundational Basis

The foundational basis on which Islam rests throughout human history is the foundation of **"Lā ilāha illa Allāh"** (لا إله إلا الله — There is no god but Allah) — that is, to single out Allah, glorified be He, in divinity and sovereignty, which entails belief of it in the conscience, worship of it in the rites, and law of it in the reality of life.

The Islamic movement believes that Islam is a fundamental force for the revival of the Muslims, the salvation of humankind, and the liberation of the oppressed from tyrants and oppressors, until there is no more tribulation (*fitnah*) and the religion is entirely for Allah.

The Islamic movement regards the exposure of the capitalist, socialist, communist, and other man-made systems as disbelief in the great Islam, denial of Allah, Lord of the Worlds, walking without guidance, confusion without benefit, wasting of efforts, and squandering of time: **"And whoever desires other than Islam as religion — never will it be accepted from him, and he, in the Hereafter, will be among the losers"** [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:85].

## The Mission of the Islamic Movement in General

Imam Ḥasan al-Bannā, may Allah be pleased with him and grant him pleasure, summarized the mission of the Islamic movement when he said:

> Our mission, in general, is to stand against this overwhelming tide of material civilization and the civilization of pleasures and lusts that has swept away the Muslim peoples, distanced them from the leadership of the Prophet and the guidance of the Qur'an, deprived the masses of the lights of guidance, and delayed their progress for long years — until it recedes from our lands and our people are healed of it. And we will not stop at this limit; rather, we will pursue it in its own lands and invade it in its very stronghold, until the entire world proclaims the name of the Prophet and the entire world is convinced of the teachings of the Qur'an, and the spreading shade of Islam covers the earth. Then what the Muslim longs for will be realized: there will be no more tribulation, and the religion will be entirely for Allah. **"And to Allah belongs the command before and after. And that day the believers will rejoice in the victory of Allah. He gives victory to whom He wills, and He is the Exalted in Might, the Merciful"** [Qur'an, al-Rūm 30:4-5].

This is our mission in general. As for some of its details, it is that the following be established in the Ummah:

1. **An internal system of government** realized through the words of Allah, blessed and exalted: **"And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their inclinations; and beware of them, lest they tempt you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you"** [Qur'an, al-Māʾidah 5:49].

2. **A system of international relations** realized through the words of the Noble Qur'an: **"And thus We have made you a middle nation that you will be witnesses over the people, and the Messenger will be a witness over you"** [Qur'an, al-Baqarah 2:143].

3. **A practical system of judiciary** derived from the noble verse: **"But no, by your Lord, they will not truly believe until they make you, [O Muhammad], judge concerning that over which they dispute among themselves and then find within themselves no discomfort from what you have judged, and submit in full, willing submission"** [Qur'an, al-Nisāʾ 4:65].

4. **A system of defense and mobilization** that achieves the objective of the general call to arms: **"Go forth, whether light or heavy, and strive with your properties and your lives in the cause of Allah"** [Qur'an, al-Tawbah 9:41].

5. **An economic system**, independent for wealth and money, state and individuals, based on His words: **"And do not give the weak-minded your property, which Allah has made a means of support for you"** [Qur'an, al-Nisāʾ 4:5].

6. **A system of culture and learning** that dispels ignorance and darkness and corresponds to the call of revelation, beginning with the first verse of the Book of Allah: **"Read in the name of your Lord who created"** [Qur'an, al-ʿAlaq 96:1].

7. **A system for the family and the home** that raises the Muslim boy, the Muslim girl, and the Muslim man, and realizes His words: **"O you who have believed, protect yourselves and your families from a Fire whose fuel is people and stones"** [Qur'an, al-Taḥrīm 66:6].

8. **A system for the individual** in his personal behavior, so that he attains the flourishing meant by His words: **"He has succeeded who purifies it"** [Qur'an, al-Shams 91:9].

9. **A general spirit** that prevails over every individual in the Ummah, ruler or ruled, based on the words of Allah, exalted: **"But seek, through that which Allah has given you, the home of the Hereafter; and [yet], do not forget your share of the world. And do good as Allah has done good to you. And desire not corruption in the land"** [Qur'an, al-Qaṣaṣ 28:77].

10. **We want the Muslim individual, the Muslim home, the Muslim people, the Muslim government, and the Muslim state** that leads the Islamic states, unites the dispersed Muslims, restores their glory, returns to them their lost lands, their usurped homelands, and their occupied countries, then carries them the burden of *jihād* and the banner of the call to Allah until the world prospers through the teachings of Islam.

## Its Foundational Characteristics

The most prominent of the characteristics of the Islamic movement are the following:

### 1. It is Divine (*Rabbaniyyah*)

It derives its conception, rulings, ethics, traditions, and ideas from the religion of Allah, the Eternal, and His all-encompassing message. It is a call to Allah, not to the prestige of those working in it or standing over it: **"Indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds"** [Qur'an, al-Anʿām 6:162]. Thus it is not a partisan organization like modern political parties, nor is it a personality-driven organization aiming to achieve some personal gains and ambitions like well-known personality cults. Its fundamental principle is that the great in it, before the small, and the leader in it, before the follower, submit to the limits Allah has prescribed.

### 2. It is Indigenous

That is, it springs from the reality of the Islamic society itself, not imported or inspired from East or West like other organizations and trends. Our call is a call to the religion, and the religion is one of the strongest characteristics of this Ummah; it has been known by it, and it has been known by the religion, throughout long periods of time. **"And nothing will restore the latter part of this Ummah but that which restored its first part."**

### 3. It is Progressive

It carries, in its creedal and legislative content, and in its conception of the universe, man, and life, what is earlier and more capable of solving the problems of man and life than the incapable, deficient human legislations. However, the progressiveness of Islam is not — as those falsely called progressives want or understand — a breaking free from moral values and noble manners. Rather, it is progress in elevating people's feelings, thoughts, and morals. It is progress in the field of directed science and the discovery of the horizons of this great universe, not for the sake of registering a scientific precedence but solely for the sake of increasing recognition of the majesty, beauty, power, and greatness of Allah, Lord of the Worlds.

The progressiveness of Islam commands benefiting from every innovation and means produced by minds, provided that they are employed within the general framework of goodness. This is the meaning of His saying: **"Wisdom is the lost property of the believer; wherever he finds it, he is most deserving of it."**

### 4. It is Comprehensive (*Shāmilah*)

It is a call that is not restricted to reforming one aspect of life to the exclusion of others; its Islam-ness means its universality and inclusiveness. It is a *Salafī* call in that it calls to return Islam to its spring; it is a *Sunnī* method in that it works to revive the pure Sunnah in individuals and in society; it is a divine bond in that it realizes that the basis of goodness is the purification of the soul, the rectification of the heart, and the good connection with Allah; and it is a political movement that attends to the affairs of the Ummah with Islam at every level.

### 5. Distance from the Locations of Juristic Disagreement

It believes that disagreement over subsidiary matters is inevitable; therefore, it calls the Muslims to gather around the fundamentals and foundations of Islam.

> We are not a political party, although politics based on the foundations of Islam is at the very core of our idea. We are not a charitable reformist association, although doing good and reform are among our greatest objectives. We are not a sports club, although physical and spiritual training are among our most important means. We are none of these formations, for all of them are created for a limited, local purpose for a limited time, and perhaps nothing motivates their composition but the desire to form a body and adorn oneself with administrative titles in it. But we are an idea and a creed, a system and a method, not limited by place, restricted by race, stopped by a geographical barrier, or ended by a matter until Allah inherits the earth and those upon it. That is because it is the system of the Lord of the Worlds and the method of His trustworthy Messenger.

O people, we are, without boast, the followers of the Companions of the Messenger of Allah, the carriers of his banner after him, the raisers of his standard as they raised it, the spreaders of his banner as they spread it, the preservers of his Qur'an as they preserved it, the proclaimers of his call as they proclaimed it, and a mercy to the worlds: **"And you will surely know [the truth of] its news after a time"** [Qur'an, Ṣād 38:88].

## Its Movement Characteristics

### 1. Distance from the Domination of Rulers and Politicians

Although it is natural that among its members there be rulers and politicians, this characteristic preserves the Islamic movement from exploitation and trafficking and keeps upon it the quality of disinterestedness, truthfulness, and sincerity.

### 2. Gradualism in Steps

Because it realizes that its path is difficult and long, its goals are huge and great, and that gradualism in steps and giving each step its due will bring the group to what it seeks and desires. Imam al-Shahīd Ḥasan al-Bannā defined three stages for the call: the stage of introduction, the stage of formation, and the stage of execution.

In the message of teachings of Imam al-Shahīd it says:

- **The stage of introduction:** By spreading the general idea among the people; its means are preaching, guidance, establishing beneficial institutions, and other practical means.

- **The stage of formation:** By selecting the sound elements capable of bearing the burdens of *jihād* and joining them to one another. The system of the call in this stage is purely spiritual from the spiritual side, and purely military from the practical side. The call in this stage is not joined by one who has not truly prepared himself to bear the burdens of long-term *jihād* with its many consequences. The first sign of this readiness is "the perfection of obedience."

- **The stage of execution:** The call in this stage is *jihād* without leniency, continuous work to reach the goal, and trial and test that none but the truthful can endure. Success in this phase is not guaranteed except by "the perfection of obedience."

### 3. Preferring Action and Activity over Propaganda and Media

The Islamic movement is driven to this by several matters: what has come in Islam specifically concerning this aspect; caution that these works not be tainted by the blemish of showing off, which would end in ruin and corruption; the Islamic nature's aversion to relying on inflated propaganda and sensationalism behind which there is no action; the refusal to expend effort and time except in what is fruitful and constructive; and what the security of the movement and the security of its individuals impose, in fulfillment of the saying of the Greatest Messenger: **"Seek help in fulfilling your needs through concealment."**

### 4. Long-Term Policy (*Siyāsat al-Nafs al-Ṭawīlah*)

The enormity of the burden and the weight of the consequences placed upon the shoulders of those working in the Islamic field make it certain that the path is long, the work is hard, and *jihād* is bitter. Those who walk this path must prepare themselves to face every hardship and difficulty, and every sacrifice and self-offering: **"Do the people think that they will be left to say, 'We believe,' and they will not be tried? But We have certainly tried those before them, and Allah will surely make evident those who are truthful, and He will surely make evident the liars"** [Qur'an, al-ʿAnkabūt 29:2-3].

Those belonging to the Islamic movement must adopt the policy of the "long breath," so that the call to Allah is with insight and seeking His pleasure. They must not rush the path or hasten the fruit before it ripens. This will not be achieved unless they understand that Islamic rule is a means to a higher end. If it is realized at their hands, they praise Allah; if it is not realized, there is no despair, no despondency, no retreat, and no fear. Thus they will have delivered the trust and performed the duty of the call: **"And victory is not but from Allah"** [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:126]; **"And you do not will except that Allah wills"** [Qur'an, al-Takwīr 81:29].

Someone may say: But why do trivial, servile parties reach power and leap to authority here and there in a shorter time and by an easier method? The answer is that the two paths are different, and the Islamic movement will only arrive by a distinct, clean path. Had it been willing to be a vehicle for this power or that, for this colonialism or that, it would have arrived as many others have arrived: **"So continue to remind, [for indeed, the reminder benefits]. And fear none but Me"** [Qur'an, Hūd 11:112].

### 5. Openness of Work and Secrecy of Organization

Work for Islam cannot be one of the secrets carried out by preachers behind veils and curtains, fearing that people will snatch them away, and philosophizing that as a benefit and wisdom. The voice of Islam must rise, the word of truth must be spoken, and the one silent about truth is a mute devil: **"Whoever among you sees an evil, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; if he cannot, then with his heart — and that is the weakest of faith"** [Sahih Muslim, Book on Faith, Hadith 49].

The caller must speak the word of truth in his place where he works and in his environment where he lives, by the good word he is able to speak.

But this does not mean that the movement should reveal all its plans and organizations; there is no benefit in that at all. Rather, that is counted as ignorance of Islam and exposure of the movement and its individuals to the scheming, treachery, and harm of enemies. The rule that the movement likes to adopt in this field is "openness of work and secrecy of organization," acting on the saying of the Messenger: **"Seek help in fulfilling your needs through concealment,"** and his saying: **"War is deception."**

### 6. Spiritual, Not Physical, Isolation

Much has been said recently: "There must be a vanguard that carries this resolve and proceeds along the path, proceeding in the midst of the ignorant society that has extended its tentacles throughout the earth, proceeding while practicing a kind of isolation on one side and a kind of connection on the other with the surrounding ignorance."

The concept of isolation here meant by the Shahīd is the isolation of feeling from being soiled by the filth of ignorance, the isolation of the soul and the elevation of its faith while it proceeds in the "midst of ignorance," exposing falsehood, challenging falsehood, struggling and striving without fearing in Allah the blame of any blamer.

Here, isolation means distinction — distinction of the believing group from the disbelieving group, distinction by thought and conception, by morals and behavior, by feelings and sentiments. This is what Islam called to, and the Messenger of Islam expressed in many of his sayings, among them: **"Be like the olive among the trees,"** and **"Let none of you be a mere follower who says, 'I am with the people: if they do good, I do good, and if they do evil, I do evil.' Rather, habituate yourselves: if the people do good, do good with them, and if they do evil, avoid their evil."**

As for work, movement, contact, and call, there is no room in them for isolation or withdrawal; otherwise work, movement, contact, and call would be paralyzed.

Those working for Islam today are before categories of people with whom it is not fitting to deal in one manner. Among people are those who are Muslims with an individual commitment to its rulings; their failure to join the Islamic movement does not make them equal to the disbelievers. And among people are those who are Muslims proud of Islam despite their ignorance of it and their failure to commit to its rulings; these represent the great mass of Muslims today, and they cannot be placed on the same level as the disbelievers. And among people are those who are enemies of Islam, waging war against Allah and His Messenger, whether as individuals, parties, or rulers.

The stances of the callers toward these must vary according to the situation, according to their openness or turning away, and according to their loyalty or hostility. Some of them require care and guidance; some require awareness and education; and some will respond to nothing but force: **"And the final remedy is the cauterization."** This requires that the callers maintain a permanent and continuous connection with society, because it is the field of work and the arena of *jihād* — but a distinct connection, a connection of influence, not of being influenced; of purification, not of defilement. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truth when he said: **"Patience of one of you for an hour in a post of *jihād* is better than the worship of one of you for sixty years."**

### 7. The End Does Not Justify the Means

The germ of corruption of the non-Islamic trends and organizations is "benefit," or in other words, their "utilitarianism," which often transgresses the limits of the principles and theories they raise and proclaim. How many ideological movements have deviated from their intellectual frameworks at the first practical experience — whether at the level of government or even in parliamentary representation!

In truth, these trends possess nothing in themselves or in those who ascribe to them of that creedal depth that could protect them from the calamities of deviation, change, and hypocrisy, which are rife in the political and intellectual conflict, and apparent in the conduct of traditional politicians and doctrinal leaders alike. Thus the interest of the people becomes the interest of the party; the interest of the party becomes the personal interest of the party leaders; the issues of the Ummah become winning cards in the market of trafficking, consumption, and bargaining; enemies of yesterday become friends of today; and friends of today become enemies of tomorrow — until nothing remains of the ideological character of these trends but false, counterfeit names and slogans.

This is what passes on the stage of the non-Islamic forces and trends; and this explains and uncovers the secret of the hidden penetration into them, their spread, and their rapid arrival to power: they have no creedal or moral commitments in the true sense; they have no clear, firm stances; rather, there are strategies seizing one another by the neck, racing breathlessly to exploit every opportunity, take advantage of every circumstance, and trade in every issue, no matter how heavy the price, even if the sacrifice is the issue itself or the entire people.

As for what passes, or should pass, on the Islamic stage, it is completely different from what passes there and there. The Islamic movement does not distinguish between near and far in terms of the necessity of its commitment to its creed, morality, and ethics. Those working in the Islamic field do not work for themselves or according to what their selves dictate; they are bound by creedal and moral limits and commitments from which they may not deviate or turn.

- **First:** Work for truth is not permitted to use falsehood to reach its ends and objectives, even if that falsehood is no more than a word or slogan: **"And say, 'The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills — let him believe, and whoever wills — let him disbelieve'"** [Qur'an, al-Kahf 18:29].

- **Second:** Truth is an indivisible whole; giving up part of truth is giving up truth entirely. There is nothing after truth but falsehood, and nothing after guidance but misguidance. Thus Quraysh offered the Messenger generous offers in exchange for some creedal concessions from him, and he responded to them by the words of his Lord: **"Say, O disbelievers, I do not worship what you worship. Nor are you worshippers of what I worship. Nor will I be a worshipper of what you worship. Nor will you be worshippers of what I worship. For you is your religion, and for me is my religion"** [Qur'an, al-Kāfirūn 109:1-6].

- **Third:** Truth must be worked for with strength, and sacrificed for with everything, without bargaining over it due to enticement or intimidation. The magicians of Pharaoh, when the truth became clear to them, rebelled against every threat and exalted their faith above every interest, saying to Pharaoh: **"Decree whatever you are to decree. You can only decree for this worldly life. Indeed, we have believed in our Lord that He may forgive us our sins and what you compelled us to do of magic. And Allah is better and more enduring"** [Qur'an, Tā Hā 20:72-73]. And the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, when he was offered kingdom, glory, and wealth in exchange for abandoning his call, rose up saying: **"By Allah, if they were to place the sun in my right hand and the moon in my left hand on condition that I leave this matter, I would not leave it until Allah makes it manifest or I perish in it."**

- **Fourth:** The people of truth must be aware, cautious, holding fast to the rope of Allah all together and guided by His guidance, so that stances do not lure them or impose upon them solutions incompatible with their creedal and moral starting points: **"And judge between them by what Allah has revealed, and do not follow their inclinations; and beware of them, lest they tempt you away from some of what Allah has revealed to you. And if they turn away — then know that Allah only intends to afflict them because of some of their sins. And indeed, many among the people are defiantly disobedient"** [Qur'an, al-Māʾidah 5:49-50].

The Prophetic biography tells us that when the king of Ghassān tried to lure Kaʿb ibn Mālik — one of the three who stayed behind from the expedition of Tabūk and remained in Madīnah without going out with the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, for the Messenger had boycotted these three and no Muslim would speak to them — and wrote him a letter saying, "It has reached me that your companion has shunned you" — and the Messenger of Allah had cut off these three — "and Allah has not placed you in a house of humiliation or loss; so join us," Kaʿb, when he read the letter, said: "This too is part of the trial. It has reached him what I have fallen into, meaning staying behind — that a man from the people of polythesis desires me." Then he went to an oven and burned the letter in it.

- **Fifth,** and finally: It is the truth prescribed by the Sharīʿah that must be followed, not the people, even if all of them follow their desires and opinions. Islam rejects flattering the masses in what contradicts the Sharīʿah of Allah, no matter how slight. This is the logic of Islam against hypocrisy and transgression of the limits of Allah: **"Whoever seeks the pleasure of the people at the cost of Allah's anger, Allah will increase him only in humiliation. And whoever seeks the pleasure of Allah at the cost of the people's anger, Allah will increase him only in honor"** [Sahih al-Tirmidhi, Book on Asceticism, Hadith 2414; declared *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ* by al-Albānī]. And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, spoke truth when he said: **"Let none of you be a mere follower who says, 'I am with the people: if they do good, I do good, and if they do evil, I do evil.' Rather, habituate yourselves. If the people do good, do good with them, and if they do evil, avoid their evil."**

## The Equipment of the Islamic Movement

Imam al-Shahīd points to the equipment of the Islamic movement, saying: **"Our equipment is the equipment of our predecessors before us, and the weapon by which they conquered the world we will conquer the world anew."**

1. **They believed in the most sublime, strongest, holiest, and most eternal faith:** in Allah, His victory, and His support: **"If Allah should aid you, no one can overcome you"** [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:160].

2. **In the method, its excellence, and its suitability:** **"There has come to you from Allah a light and a clear Book by which Allah guides those who pursue His pleasure to the ways of peace"** [Qur'an, al-Māʾidah 5:15-16].

3. **In brotherhood, its depth, sacredness, and majesty:** **"Indeed, the believers are brothers"** [Qur'an, al-Ḥujurāt 49:10].

4. **In reward, its grandeur, magnificence, and abundance:** **"That is because they are not afflicted by thirst or fatigue or hunger in the cause of Allah, nor do they tread on any ground that enrages the disbelievers, nor do they inflict upon an enemy any infliction but that it is registered for them as a righteous deed. Indeed, Allah does not allow the reward of the doers of good to be lost"** [Qur'an, al-Tawbah 9:120].

5. **And in themselves:** They are the group Allah has chosen to save the worlds, and He has written for them virtue thereby, so they were **"the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind"** [Qur'an, Āl ʿImrān 3:110].

They heard the caller calling to faith, so they believed; and if it were not that Allah had made this faith beloved to us and adorned it in our hearts as He made it beloved to them and adorned it in their hearts before — then faith is our first equipment.

## *Jihād* Is Also of Our Equipment

And they knew with the truest knowledge and the deepest fiqh that their call would not prevail except by *jihād*, sacrifice, self-offering, and steadfastness. So they advanced their souls, gave their lives, and strove in Allah with a *jihād* worthy of Him.

They heard the Caller of the Most Merciful calling them: **"Say, [O Muhammad], 'If your fathers, your sons, your brothers, your wives, your clans, the wealth you have obtained, the trade you fear will decline, and the dwellings you delight in are more beloved to you than Allah and His Messenger and *jihād* in His cause, then wait until Allah executes His command. And Allah does not guide the defiantly disobedient people'"** [Qur'an, al-Tawbah 9:24]. Another gave all his wealth, saying: **"I have left for my family Allah and His Messenger."**

Another risked himself while the sword was at his neck:

> I do not care, when I am killed as a Muslim,  
> On whichever side my fall is for Allah.

Thus they were: truthful *jihād*, great sacrifice, and abundant self-offering. And thus we strive to be.

## Why the Islamic Movement?

My belonging to the Islamic movement obliges me to know why I am in the Islamic movement and not in something else. What made me here and not there or yonder? Is it chance — and chance is rejected in the logic of the Sharīʿah — or is it the result of research, investigation, and scrutiny?

I must acquaint myself with the other methods of Islamic work so that my belonging to the Islamic movement may be an aware, rational belonging, not a random or whimsical one.

I must realize, when I belong to the Islamic movement, that the other methods of Islamic work and its different organizations do not represent the authentic Islamic line. Not one of them is committed to the method of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, with complete commitment, although some differ from others greatly in the degree of commitment and in its partiality or totality.

The fact is that whoever looks at the corners of the Islamic world sees a number of Islamic faces and facets.

There are bodies with a purely spiritual orientation, concerned with spiritual training and having completely dropped from their account the other aspects of Islamic concerns — intellectual, political, *jihādī*, organizational, planning, and so on — which has made them distant from the reality of life, ignorant of much of what is happening around them of events and occurrences, incapable of understanding the circumstances they live in, let alone affecting them and changing them.

And there are bodies with a cultural orientation that arose originally as an emotional reaction or as an equivalent competition to Christian or Jewish organizations, such as Muslim youth groups. This has made these bodies prisoner to the traditional framework from which they started and by whose pressure and influence they were formed. Indeed, this type of Islamic face often did harm to Islam because of its closeness to systems and ideologies known for their hostility to Islam and its people — and this is what the Sharīʿah rejects and the distinctive nature of Islam refuses.

And there are associations with a charitable orientation that arose under the pressure of the need to help the destitute, prepare doctors for the sick and the needy, orphanages, and the like. These associations, although they carry out good deeds, have limited themselves to that and have failed to represent the comprehensive Islamic work.

And there are Islamic parties with a purely political orientation, pursuing a type of work they do not transcend or exceed: maneuvering in the name of Islam, raising Islamic slogans, but without a creedal depth that they commit to as an organization and as individuals. These parties do not hesitate at times to violate a fundamental of Islam or abandon a principle of its principles under the pretext of flexibility and openness, and under the claim of achieving the interest of the Muslims — such as participating in power under secular, disbelieving systems, or raising side and partial issues and emptying effort into them and busying with them away from the fundamental issues that Islam brought.

These bodies, faces, and parties, and those like them, do not practice the authentic, integrated Islamic work — the work that can achieve the great transition from ignorance to Islam; the work that can save the Islamic world from the dominion of the dissolute Western civilization and the dominion of disbelief and *ṭāghūt*; the work that aims at forming the Muslim individual, the Muslim home, the Muslim society, and the Muslim state that rules by what Allah has revealed.

Therefore, the works of all these categories remain truncated and deformed, not to mention that some of them have caused great offenses to Islam in the name of Islam. And the partiality and deficiency of the work of these categories may give the impression that Islam is like that — and exalted is Allah above that greatly — or may lessen the gravity of the man-made systems and assuage the Muslims' fervor against them, helping them to continue and remain.

The Islamic work that is based on **total change** and prepares for total change, and that carries Islam as a whole to realize it as a whole — even if it falters at times and stumbles in some stages of work — is the sound work that represents the authentic line, whatever its external names and labels may be, because it meets on unity of understanding, unity of methods, unity of path, and unity of goal, however far the lands and distant the homelands.

Imam al-Shahīd Ḥasan al-Bannā, may Allah's pleasure and mercy be upon him, expressed the qualities of this group of workers most clearly when he said: **"O Brothers, you are neither a charitable association, nor a political party, nor a local body for limited purposes. Rather, you are a new spirit coursing through the heart of this Ummah, reviving it with the Qur'an; a new light rising, dispelling the darkness of matter by knowledge of Allah; and a resounding voice proclaiming the call of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him."** *(From the collected letters of Imam al-Shahīd)*.

## The Dimensions of My Belonging to the Islamic Movement

### The Creedsal Belonging

My belonging to the Islamic movement is not achieved by submitting a membership application and registering a name only, nor by attending the gatherings, centers, and meetings of the movement only. Rather, this belonging must be a creedal belonging, a commitment of the heart and conscience before it is a commitment of the tongue and limbs.

# Chapter 10: Understanding the Methods of Islamic Work

Dimensions that transcend formal boundaries and outward appearances…
Dimensions that affirm doctrinal depth and the strength of the intellectual and organizational bond…

## The Ideological Dimension of Belonging

The first dimension we must recognize in our belonging to the Islamic movement (*al-harakah al-Islamiyyah*) is the ideological dimension. For the movement rejects the familiar *personal* belonging found in leadership-based blocs, which is considered the seed of their rise and fall.

Belonging to the Islamic movement is, first and foremost, belonging to this religion; it is compliance with Allah’s command and hope for His mercy and pleasure. This is what keeps belonging far from being affected by the death of individuals, their departure, or their absence from the arena of *da’wah* for one reason or another, because it makes the bond of individuals with Allah and their gathering upon Him—exalted is He—the very secret of this *da’wah*’s permanence, survival, and continuity: “Whoever used to worship Muḥammad, then Muḥammad is dead; but whoever used to worship Allah, then Allah is Alive and does not die.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Funerals, Hadith 1241]

“Indeed, those who pledge allegiance to you, [O Muḥammad], are actually pledging allegiance to Allah. The hand of Allah is over their hands. So whoever breaks his pledge only breaks it against himself, and whoever fulfills what Allah has pledged him to, He will give him a great reward.” [Qur’an, Al-Fath 48:10]

## Rejecting Spontaneous or Emotional Belonging

The Islamic movement likewise rejects spontaneous or emotional belonging, because Islam is a *manhaj* (way of life) built on defined concepts of the universe, man, and life; and because working for Islam aims to realize this *manhaj* in society—to realize it with awareness, depth, and objectivity.

Therefore, doctrinal and activist awareness and practical commitment are among the prerequisites of this belonging. Indeed, steadfastness in *da’wah* and its work, remaining on the Islamic path, and achieving productivity in it require correct understanding and sound awareness of the dimensions of belonging. Most of those who fall along the way are those who rushed through the journey quickly—people who proceeded spontaneously and were swept along by emotion under some circumstance, without grasping the dimensions of the path.

## Rejecting Self-Interested Belonging

The Islamic movement also rejects self-interested belonging—that is, the belonging people use as a means to achieve some of their personal aims and interests, and as a pretext to reach private material or social gains.

For belonging to the Islamic movement means mobilizing the individual’s energy in service of the group; it means subordinating his interests to the interest of Islam, and not the reverse. This is what connects action with *niyyah* (intention); and belonging is the framework of action, so it must be purified, clarified, and cleansed: “Actions are judged only by intentions, and every person will have only what he intended. So whoever emigrated (*hijrah*) for Allah and His Messenger, his emigration is for Allah and His Messenger; and whoever emigrated for a worldly gain he would obtain or a woman he would marry, then…” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Revelation, Hadith 1; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 1907]
# Chapter 11: Understanding the Dimensions of Belonging to the Islamic Movement

## The Destiny Dimension of Belonging

The other dimension that must be realized in belonging to the Islamic movement (*al-ḥarakah al-Islāmiyyah*) is the destiny dimension. That is, belonging must be a destiny in which the fate of the one who belongs is tied to the fate of the cause, whatever the circumstances may be.

Thus it should not be a stage-bound belonging that ends when the stage ends, nor a circumstance-bound belonging that ends when the circumstance passes.

Should it be belonging in the stage of youth and bachelorhood, then withdrawal in the stage of manhood and marriage?

Should it be belonging in poverty and hardship, then flight in wealth and ease?

Should it be belonging in comfort, then retreat in affliction?

Rather, belonging to the movement must be a permanent belonging, with no breaking away from it, no retreat from it, and no fleeing from it, until the one who belongs meets his Lord while still upon that path. This was the way of our righteous predecessors; indeed, this is what many of Allah’s clear verses affirmed. Among them is His statement—exalted is He:

> “And among the people is he who says, ‘We believe in Allah,’ but when he is harmed for [the sake of] Allah, he considers the trial [i.e., harm] of the people as if it were the punishment of Allah. But if victory comes from your Lord, he will surely say, ‘Indeed, we were with you.’ Is not Allah most knowing of what is within the chests of all creatures? And Allah will surely make evident those who believed, and He will surely make evident the hypocrites.” [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:10–11]

And His statement:

> “And among the people is he who worships Allah on an edge. If good befalls him, he is content with it; but if trial befalls him, he turns back upon his face. He loses this world and the Hereafter. That is what is the manifest loss.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:11]

And His statement:

> “And how many prophets fought [in the cause of Allah], and with them were many rabbis [devoted scholars]. But they never lost assurance due to what afflicted them in the cause of Allah, nor did they weaken or submit. And Allah loves the steadfast. And their words were not but that they said, ‘Our Lord, forgive us our sins and the excess [committed] in our affairs and make firm our feet and give us victory over the disbelieving people.’ So Allah gave them the reward of this world and the excellent reward of the Hereafter. And Allah loves the doers of good.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:146–148]

Indeed, the call to truth is a call of commitments; a call of faith and action; a call of *jihād*, steadfastness (*ribāṭ*), and perseverance; a call of sacrifice, giving, and selfless dedication. And it is in the nature of the call to truth that its people be exposed to the challenges, aggression, schemes, and treachery of the people of falsehood:

> “And We will surely test you until We make evident those who strive among you and the patient, and We will test your affairs.” [Qur’an, Muḥammad 47:31]

Those who belong to the Islamic movement must be the firmest of people in their morals, dealings, and feelings. They must uphold their principles and loyalty whatever the conditions and circumstances may be. Indeed, they must move from the narrowness of their own egos and subjectivity to the subjectivity of the *daʿwah* and its interest. These qualities, and others like them, cannot shine forth in the Islamic personality nor be characteristic of the workers except after a long process of refinement through struggle and hardship:

> “As for the foam, it vanishes as scum; but as for what benefits the people, it remains upon the earth.” [Qur’an, Al-Raʿd 13:17]

From this angle, trial becomes a gift from Allah by which He distinguishes the good, purifies, and selects those worthy of being made successors (*istikhlāf*) on the earth and bearers of the tremendous trust (*amānah*) which the heavens, the earth, and the mountains refused to bear, and they feared it:

> “If a wound should touch you, a similar wound has already touched the [disbelieving] people. And these days [of varying conditions] We alternate among the people so that Allah may make evident those who believe and [may] take to Himself from among you martyrs—and Allah does not like the wrongdoers—and that Allah may purify the believers [through trials] and destroy the disbelievers.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:140–141]

The trials of the callers in the *daʿwah* are of two fundamental types, from which branch the various kinds of tribulations and tests:

**First:** The callers are exposed to various forms of allurement and temptation—such as wealth, prestige, authority, and position—in exchange for giving up some or all of their principles:

> “Beautified for people is the love of that which they desire—of women, sons, heaps of gold and silver, fine branded horses, cattle, and tilled land. That is the enjoyment of worldly life, but Allah has with Him the best return. Say, ‘Shall I inform you of something better than that? For those who feared Allah will be gardens in the presence of their Lord, beneath which rivers flow, wherein they will abide eternally, and purified spouses, and acceptance from Allah. And Allah is Seeing of [His] servants.’” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:14–15]

**Second:** The callers are exposed to kinds of terror, intimidation, persecution, and torture. They are beaten, imprisoned, killed, or fought in their livelihoods. Their duty here is patience and steadfastness until Allah decrees His command:

> “Those to whom the people said, ‘Indeed, the people have gathered against you, so fear them.’ But it [merely] increased them in faith, and they said, ‘Sufficient for us is Allah, and [He is] the best Disposer of affairs.’ So they returned with favor from Allah and bounty, no harm having touched them. And they pursued the pleasure of Allah, and Allah is the possessor of great bounty.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:173–174]

This is what makes trial in the life of the *daʿwah* one of the elements of refinement, and a means of promotion and relegation. In this, it is like fire that removes its dross, just as the bellows remove the dross of iron. Allah—exalted is He—spoke the truth when He said:

> “And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits, but give good tidings to the patient.” [Qur’an, Al-Baqarah 2:155]

The martyr-Imam wrote in his epistle *Bayna al-Amsi wa al-Yawm* (Between Yesterday and Today), under the heading “The Obstacles in Our Path”:

Only at that time will you have begun to walk the path of the people of [divine] calls. As for now, you are still unknown, still preparing the ground for the *daʿwah* and getting ready for the struggle and *jihād* it requires.

You will find among the outwardly religious and among the official scholars those who marvel at your understanding of Islam and denounce your *jihād* in its cause. Presidents, leaders, possessors of wealth and authority will harbor hatred against you. Every government, without exception, will stand in your face, and every government will try to limit your activity and place stumbling blocks in your path.
>
> The usurpers will tremble at your rising and at the extinguishing of the light of your *daʿwah*, and they will seek help in that from weak governments, weak morals, and hands extended to them with requests while extended to you with harm and aggression.
>
> All of them will stir around your *daʿwah* the dust of doubts and the darkness of accusations. They will try to attach every deficiency to it and to present it to the people in the ugliest image, relying on their power and authority, boasting in their wealth and influence:

> “They want to extinguish the light of Allah with their mouths, but Allah will perfect His light, although the disbelievers dislike it.” [Qur’an, Al-Ṣaf 61:8]
>
> Undoubtedly, you will thereby enter the stage of trial and testing. You will be imprisoned, detained, deported, scattered, your interests confiscated, your work suspended, your homes searched—and the duration of this test may be long:
>
> “Do the people think that they will be left to say, ‘We believe,’ and they will not be tried?” [Qur’an, Al-ʿAnkabūt 29:2]
>
> But Allah has promised you, after all of that, victory for the *mujāhidīn* and reward for the doers of good:
>
> “O you who have believed, shall I guide you to a transaction that will save you from a painful punishment?” [Qur’an, Al-Ṣaf 61:10]
>
> “Then We supported those who believed against their enemy, and they became dominant.” [Qur’an, Al-Ṣaf 61:14]
>
> So are you resolved to be the helpers of Allah?

## Sixth: Recognizing the Pillars of Islamic Work

- Clarity of goal
- Clarity of path
- A transformative nature
- A comprehensive nature
- A global nature
- The way of the Messenger ﷺ
- The place of material power in the strategy of the movement

What does my belonging to Islam mean?

It means that I am aware of the foundations of Islamic work.

There are three foundations for the success of Islamic work that the workers for Islam must recognize:

1. Clarity of the goal
2. Clarity of the path
3. Commitment to both

### First: Clarity of the Goal

Clarity of the goal of Islamic work saves the workers much effort, and consequently preserves that effort from being consumed and wasted on peripheral issues and side battles that have no connection, near or far, with the fundamental goal that Islamic work targets and toward which all energies and resources should be directed.

Through our study of the Islamic *manhaj* (methodology), through our contemplation of the Book of Allah—exalted is He—and the Sunnah of His Messenger, and through the practical, applied experiences in the various fields of Islamic history, it becomes certain to us that the loftiest objective of Islam’s message, and the authentic goal of this religion, is: to make people worship their Lord, as individuals and societies; to worship Him in the mosque during prayer as in the marketplace during buying and selling; to worship Him in fasting as in judgment; in supplication as in adjudication, equally so:

> “And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me.” [Qur’an, Al-Dhāriyāt 51:56]

More frankly: making people worship Allah means that their loyalty (*walāʾ*) to Him must be in every matter of their lives—not only in matters of religion, but in matters of religion and worldly life. For there is no separation or split in the Islamic concept between matters of religion and matters of worldly life. This is what explains Islam’s rejection of secularization (*al-ʿilmāniyyah*), which is based on separating religion from the state, on “what is Caesar’s is Caesar’s and what is Allah’s is Allah’s,” and on “religion is for Allah and the homeland is for all.”

Thus, making people worship Allah, in the Islamic concept, necessarily means negating every human methodology whose function is to make people serve false deities (*ṭāghūt*). These methodologies are negated, first, because they represent a flagrant assault upon Allah’s right to servitude and sovereignty:

> “Indeed, judgement is only for Allah.” [Qur’an, Yūsuf 12:40]

And second, because they are, in their essence, incapable, by their very potential, of realizing the humanity of man in his struggle for self-actualization:

> “Then is He who creates like one who does not create? Will you not be reminded?” [Qur’an, Al-Naḥl 16:17]

### Second: Clarity of the Path

We must also realize that working for Islam means working to apply Allah’s *sharīʿah* on earth. It means establishing the rulings of this *sharīʿah* in place of the laws of humans and false deities. Allah’s *manhaj* and His *sharīʿah* in it are sufficient for it—sufficient in creed, in morality altogether, in legislation in its breadth, depth, and flexibility.

Therefore, coexistence with *jāhiliyyah* is not a goal and a destiny in itself, except within the limits required by the process of overturning it, in terms of strength and resources. For the goal is the realization of this substitution; every work that does not assist in realizing it, or contribute to reaching it, is a diversion from what is more important:

> “So to that [religion of Allah] invite, [O Muḥammad], and remain on a right course as you are commanded, and do not follow their inclinations.” [Qur’an, Al-Shūrā 42:15]

As for the path of working for Islam—which is the subject of disagreement among workers—it can be determined in light of the goal for which Islam came, and through the Prophetic course adopted by the Messenger of Allah ﷺ to realize that goal.

The goal of the Islamic *manhaj* determines the nature and dimensions of the path, and thereby outlines the essential landmarks of the plan that the Islamic movement must adopt in every time and place.

This means, consequently, that the path of working for Islam must be subject to fixed rules and principles dictated by the fundamental goal of the work, and confirmed by the practical application in the biography of the Greatest Messenger ﷺ.

If the goal of a certain methodology is scientific, then the nature of the path will, consequently, be scientific and cultural, in harmony with that goal. Likewise, if the goal is educational, economic, military, athletic, or charitable, the nature of the path will be consistent with that same goal.

If the goal of the Islamic *manhaj*—as is known—is to make people worship Allah in their conduct, dealings, systems, legislation, and in every aspect of their lives, then that means replacing the positivistic systems with the Islamic *manhaj*. It means replacing one state of affairs with another. It means negating the foundations and pillars upon which society stands, and negating the civilization it adopts and relies upon, so that the process of building upon the foundations and pillars of Islam may begin thereafter.

### A Transformative Nature

That necessitates that the nature of work for Islam be transformative, not merely reformist.

Transformative, in the sense that it refuses to settle for half-solutions; refuses harmony with *jāhiliyyah* methodologies; refuses coexistence with positivistic doctrines—all positivistic doctrines.

### A Comprehensive Nature

That also necessitates that the nature of work for Islam be comprehensive, in everything that word implies, in all the meanings it carries, and in all the dimensions it presents. Comprehensive in encompassing everything the confrontation requires in terms of resources, and everything the realization of the greater goal requires in terms of means and energies.

Intellectual, political, and activist awareness; psychological and physical readiness; organization and planning; human and material preparation—on every level—are indispensable elements that must all be combined, none of which can dispense with the others, in order to realize the comprehensiveness of Islamic work and to reach the desired goal:

> “[And they are] those who, if We give them authority in the land, establish prayer and give zakah and enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong. And to Allah belongs the outcome of [all] matters.” [Qur’an, Al-Ḥajj 22:41]

### A Global Nature

Another matter that must be clear to the workers is that work for Islam must reach the level of globality in idea, planning, and organization. It is in no way permissible that it remain faint cries that rise and fall here, there, and elsewhere.

For Islam, in its intellectual structure and doctrinal content, is a global system that transcends regional, national, ethnic, and linguistic frameworks. It is a system that, by the breadth and flexibility of its legislative roots, is uniquely capable of embracing the problems of life at every level. The global nature of the call is evident through the horizontal spread of the Islamic community, which covered more than half of the inhabited world while maintaining a unity of leadership over all those lands.

The globality of work for Islam is a necessity imposed by reality, in addition to being an obligation in principle. For we live in an age of global problems, global powers and orientations, global ideas and doctrines. People’s affairs, issues, and politics are intertwined and interlocked; likewise their countries and homes, to the extent that it is difficult to work and move without observing all aspects, conditions, and forces—their outward and inward, their fronts and backgrounds, their local and international dimensions.

The study of the smallest local issue or problem may ultimately reveal external causes and backgrounds behind it, just as it may reveal the need for international activities and measures to solve it.

In view of all this, the workers for Islam—the vanguards of the authentic global Islamic movement—must emerge from the framework of regional thought, planning, and organization, so as to keep Islamic energies far from the whirlpool of local and national erosion and depletion, and to confront with them their major fundamental issues, and to realize the essential goal of their existence:

> “That the word of Allah be the highest and the word of those who disbelieved be the lowest.” [Qur’an, Al-Tawbah 9:40]

The mission of the Islamic movement is to mobilize all the energies and resources capable of removing the rule of false deities (*ṭāghūt*). It is not its mission to seek solutions for the problems of *jāhiliyyah* societies that rule by other than what Allah has revealed.

It is not the mission of Islamic work to seek solutions for educational, media, nutritional, clothing, political, or economic problems, or any other problems produced by corrupt positivistic systems. If it is necessary to address such problems, directly or indirectly, it should only be to the extent that this leads to condemning the systems that produced them, exposing and unmasking these systems, and confirming their invalidity—and never in a way that helps these systems to dominate and persist, or opens for them opportunities to remain and grow.

If the problem of rising prices is presented, for example, the movement must show that it is a natural result of the comprehensive capitalist systems, which by their nature create the appropriate climate for the emergence of monopolies and the control of a group of people over the wealth of the country, exploiting it for their selfish interests; and that Islam is the only *manhaj* capable of creating a clean society in which justice is realized, human rights and sanctities are protected, and woe to whoever aggresses upon human dignity and rights. The movement must not work to help these systems solve the problem, for by doing so it would have given them a justification to remain.

If the cause of Palestine is presented, for example, the Islamic movement must use it to emphasize the colossal failure of the ruling regimes: their failure to mobilize the Ummah psychologically and physically for *jihād* in the cause of Allah and the recovery of the usurped land; their revolving in the orbit of the Eastern and Western colonial camps; and it must emphasize, with full confidence and pride, that Islam is the only *manhaj* capable of preparing and purifying the Ummah to face every challenge and achieve victory in every field.

The fist of the Islamic movement must always and continuously be directed toward the battlefields of the ruling positivistic systems: their fundamental pillars, principles, and starting points.

Beware of a step that becomes a means of prolonging their life rather than a nail in their coffin.

Beware of a step that becomes a justification for their survival rather than a factor in their downfall and defeat.

## The Way of the Messenger ﷺ

We must pause to survey the way of the Messenger ﷺ, to know it and discern the extent of its conformity with the goal, nature, and dimensions of the Islamic *manhaj*.

The truth is that the life of the Messenger ﷺ—in all its features and details—forms a foundation that the caravans of workers for Islam cannot but follow in every time and place. It presents the safest method of working for Islam; it presents the art of *daʿwah* and dealing with people; and consequently it clarifies how to confront and defeat *jāhiliyyah* societies. The essential landmarks of this path can be outlined through the following main lines:

### First: Proclaiming Servitude to Allah from the First Day

Proclaiming servitude to Allah from the first day, without pretense or evasion, so that the goal may be clear—clear in the minds of the callers as it is clear in the minds of those called:

> “[He sent it] so that Allah may make evident those who believe, and [that] He may take to Himself from among you martyrs. And Allah does not like the wrongdoers.” [Qur’an, Āl ʿImrān 3:140]

This proclamation came in various forms of revelation descending upon the pure heart [of the Prophet], all of them resting upon one meaning: making people worship Allah in every matter of their lives—worshipping Him in His divinity (*ulūhiyyah*) and worshipping Him in His lordship (*rubūbiyyah*).

The Messenger ﷺ and his companions were exposed to all kinds of harm and persecution, yet they did not deviate a hand’s breadth nor compromise in bearing the creed and calling people to it:

> “Say, ‘O disbelievers, I do not worship what you worship. Nor are you worshippers of what I worship. Nor will I be a worshipper of what you worship. Nor will you be worshippers of what I worship. For you is your religion, and for me is my religion.’” [Qur’an, Al-Kāfirūn 109:1–6]

For the truth is most worthy of being followed, however great the enmity and sacrifice may be:

> “And say, ‘The truth is from your Lord, so whoever wills—let him believe; and whoever wills—let him disbelieve.’” [Qur’an, Al-Kahf 18:29]

### Second: Establishing an Activist Collective

Establishing an activist collective (*tajammuʿ ḥarakī*) bound to it doctrinally and spiritually, organized and disciplined under conscious leadership, proceeding by the guidance and light of Allah.

An activist collective that does not stumble blindly, nor let transient events and peripheral issues distract it from continuing the march and preparing to realize Islam’s great goals.

An activist collective bound to Islam with a destiny-bound bond, transcending all personal, material, and mental attachments.

A collective whose birth was witnessed by the house of Al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam, where faith took root in the hearts of its companions; whose *jihād* and sacrifices were tested on the battlefields of Badr, al-Qādisiyyah, and Yarmūk; and whose pages of history are filled with the heroism of that generation which the Messenger of Allah ﷺ educated, and through which Allah opened the East and West for the call of Islam.

### Third: Confronting Jahiliyyah Fully, but Consciously

The Messenger ﷺ realized that the goals of Islam require a total transformation in people’s lives: in their ideas and beliefs, in their conduct and customs, in their systems and legislations. Therefore, this confrontation requires preparing the vanguard of believers (*al-ṭalīʿah al-muʾminah*) in a manner commensurate with the weight of the trust, the fierceness of the challenges, and the magnitude of the goals.

Therefore, preparation was comprehensive: through worship and upbringing (*tarbiyyah*), as well as thought and culture; through organization and planning, as well as training and practice in psychological and physical *jihād*—each of these aspects according to its importance and the magnitude of need for it, in its place within the stages of the work, how and when.

### To Realize the Conditions of *Bay'ah* and Membership

To realize the conditions of *bay'ah* (pledge of allegiance, بَيْعَة) and membership: every Muslim is able to share with the Islamic movement (*al-ḥarakah al-Islāmiyyah*, الحَرَكَةُ الإِسْلَامِيَّةُ) the burdens of working for Islam, by joining its ranks, once the qualities previously mentioned are found in him, and once he feels the bitter reality in which the Ummah lives, understands the secret of this backwardness, believes in Islam's ability to revive this Ummah and rescue it, and to elevate it to the highest human, civilizational, and moral levels—together with what this faith requires of total submission to the rulings and principles of Islam in all affairs of his life.

## Quality, Not Quantity

It is only natural that the Islamic movement should seek to expand its ranks and increase its numbers through the various legitimate means and methods. But this must not lead it to gather individuals haphazardly and without criteria. *Taw'iyah* (raising awareness/education, تَوْعِيَةُ) is more important than quantity. The call may exert effort to win an individual, spending time and effort in the hope that this individual will take his place in the community and bear his share of responsibility and his portion of obligations. But it refuses to let the community carry its members throughout their lives, for that is a waste of effort and a squandering of work.

An individual may be an entire Ummah; and he may be a harmful and burdensome weight that does damage and brings no benefit. Not placing him in the ranks is better and more proper. There are many lessons to be taken to heart:

> "[Remember] the day of Ḥunayn when your great number pleased you, but it did not avail you in any way, and the earth seemed narrow to you despite its spaciousness; then you turned back, fleeing." [Qur'an, Al-Tawbah 9:25]

Numerical quantity is not the first criterion. Rather, it belongs to the small group who know Allah, are steadfast, and sincere to Him. Quantity is sometimes a cause of defeat, because some of those swept into it—those who enjoy its abundance without having understood the reality of Islam or committed themselves to it—will have their feet slip and their hearts tremble in the hour of hardship. Thereby they spread disturbance and panic among the souls and in the ranks.

Therefore, evaluating and classifying individuals becomes one of the movement's duties, beneath the appearance of its health, so that it may draw its true manpower from the best human resources before beginning any work. This does not mean that the movement should abandon the rest of its members; rather, it continues to educate and raise them so that they become a permanent resource.

## *Bay'ah* and Its Legal Ruling

*Bay'ah* is a pledge of obedience. It is as if the one pledging makes a covenant with his leader that he will surrender to him the disposal of his own soul and the affairs of the Muslims, not contesting him in anything of that, and obeying him in whatever he is charged with, whether willingly or unwillingly.

*Bay'ah* is a Prophetic *Sunnah* that the Muslims gave to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ repeatedly in his life—such as the First and Second *Bay'ahs* of al-'Aqabah and the *Bay'ah* of al-Riḍwān—and it remained in effect after his death, given by the Muslims to those in authority over them.

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Junādah ibn Abī Umayyah, from 'Ubādah ibn al-Ṣāmit, who said: "He called us, and we pledged allegiance to him, and among the things he took from us was that we pledged to hear and obey, whether in ease or hardship, and in what we like and dislike, and that we would not dispute the command with its people unless we see clear disbelief (*kufr*) for which we have proof from Allah."

Ṣiddīq Ḥasan Khān said in *Al-Dīn al-Khāliṣ*: "This hadīth indicates that asking the Companions for *bay'ah* is a *Sunnah*, and likewise their giving *bay'ah* upon that is a *Sunnah*; and fulfilling it is obligatory, while breaking it is a serious matter."

That is, in a state of our activity and in the state in which we are incapable of doing what we are commanded. Al-Dāwūdī said: "What is meant are the things they dislike." Ibn al-Tīn said: "The apparent meaning is that he meant at the time of laziness and lethargy." Ibn al-Jazarī al-Mālikī said: "It is not permissible to rebel against the rulers even if they are oppressive, unless clear disbelief appears from them. They must be obeyed in what a person likes and dislikes, except if they command a sin; there is no obedience to a created being in disobedience to the Creator."

The sincere activist brother should draw from all these statements, and others besides them, what is obligatory upon him in dealing with his supervisors and the leaders of the call. He must be above devoting his concern to tracking down the slips and faults of his brothers, for *ḥusn al-ẓann* (having a good opinion of Muslims, حُسْنُ الظَّنِّ) is the original obligation to follow, not the partisan methods far from piety and religion. And every *bid'ah* (heresy/innovation, بِدْعَةُ) is misguidance.

## Obedience and Its Legal Ruling

Obedience is compliance with the command. If we do not obtain obedience, sin and *fitnah* (tribulation, فِتْنَةُ) take its place. The believer may not perceive the first steps of *fitnah*; he only senses it once it has actually occurred. Therefore, he must be vigilant in all his words, deeds, and conduct so that he does not become a *fitnah* for those who believe.

Obedience is obligatory unless it involves disobedience or leads to disobedience. Allah—exalted is He—says:

> "O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger, if you should believe in Allah and the Last Day. That is the best [way] and best in result." [Qur'an, Al-Nisā' 4:59]

And Abū Hurayrah—may Allah be pleased with him—reported that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said:

> "Whoever obeys me has obeyed Allah, and whoever disobeys me has disobeyed Allah; and whoever obeys my leader has obeyed me, and whoever disobeys my leader has disobeyed me." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Judgments, Hadith 2956]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Anas ibn Mālik—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Hear and obey, even if an Abyssinian slave is appointed over you." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Judgments, Hadith 7142] And he narrated from Ibn 'Abbās—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Whoever sees something from his leader that he dislikes, let him be patient. For no one separates from the community an arm's length and then dies except that he dies a death of *Jāhiliyyah*." In another narration: "He has removed the bond of Islam from around his neck." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 1849]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from 'Abdullāh ibn 'Umar—may Allah be pleased with them both—from the Prophet ﷺ: "Hearing and obeying are incumbent upon a Muslim in what he likes and dislikes, unless he is commanded with a sin. If he is commanded with a sin, then there is no hearing and no obeying." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Oppressions, Hadith 7144]

Al-Bukhārī narrated from Abū Sa'īd al-Khudrī—may Allah be pleased with him—that the Prophet ﷺ said: "There were people in the time of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ who used to delay [complying with] revelation. Revelation has ceased, and we will only take you according to what appears to us of your deeds. Whoever shows us good, we will trust him and draw him near; and we have nothing to do with what is hidden in his inner self. Even if he says his inner self is good." [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Faith, Hadith 33]

## The Pillars of *Bay'ah*

For belonging to the Islamic movement is, in truth, actual belonging to Islam; the beginning of a new covenant with Allah; and a *bay'ah* upon working and striving (*jihād*, جِهَادُ) in the cause of Allah.

The martyr-Imam summarized the pillars of *bay'ah*, saying:

> The pillars of our *bay'ah* are ten; so preserve them:
> Understanding (*fahm*, فَهْمٌ) — sincerity (*ikhlāṣ*, إِخْلَاصٌ) — work (*'amal*, عَمَلٌ) — *jihād* — sacrifice (*taḍḥiyah*, تَضْحِيَةٌ) — obedience (*ṭā'ah*, طَاعَةٌ) — steadfastness (*thabāt*, ثَبَاتٌ) — selfless dedication (*tajarrud*, تَجَرُّدٌ) — brotherhood (*ukhuwwah*, أُخُوَّةٌ) — trust (*thiqah*, ثِقَةٌ).

The martyr-Imam explained each of these pillars, saying:

### 1. Understanding

That you be certain that our idea is an idea of pure Islam, and that you understand Islam as we understand it within the bounds of these concise fundamentals, which we summarize as follows:

- That Islam is a comprehensive system covering all aspects of life.
- That the Qur'an and *Sunnah* are the reference for every Muslim in learning the rulings of Islam.
- That *taqwā* (God-consciousness, تَقْوَى), sincere worship, and *jihād* are a light and sweetness that Allah casts into the heart of whichever of His servants He wills.
- That *kashf* (unveiling), visions, inspirations, and passing thoughts are not evidence in legal rulings and are not considered unless they do not conflict with the rulings and texts of the religion.
- That *tamā'im* (amulets), *tyarah* (evil omens), *wud'ah* (bird-omens), *raml* (geomancy), *ma'rifah* (clairvoyance), *kuhḥānah* (soothsaying), and everything of this kind are forbidden and must be combated—except what is from a Qur'anic verse or a transmitted *ruqyah* (incantation).
- That the opinion of the leader and his deputy in matters without a text, in matters admitting several possible aspects, and in unrestricted benefits is to be followed as long as it does not conflict with a legal principle. And everyone may be taken from or left aside in his speech except the infallible one. And it is obligatory upon every Muslim who has not reached the level of examining the evidences of legal rulings to follow an imām from the imāms of the religion.
- That juristic disagreement in branches is not a cause of division in religion.
- That every issue upon which action is not required is free of the burden that the Sharī'ah has absolved us from.
- That knowing Allah—blessed and exalted is He—declaring His oneness (*tawḥīd*, تَوْحِيدٌ), and declaring Him transcendent are the highest creeds of Islam; and that the verses of attributes and their authentic hadīths are to be believed as they came, without *ta'wīl* (figurative interpretation) or *ta'ṭīl* (negation of attributes).
- That every *bid'ah* in religion that has no basis is an innovation that must be combated with the best means.
- That the additional *bid'ah* and the omission or neglect of absolute acts of worship are matters of juristic disagreement in which every person has his view; there is no harm in scrutinizing the truth through evidence and proof.
- That visiting the righteous, respecting them, and asking Allah to have mercy upon them for what is known of their good deeds is a means of drawing near to Allah—blessed and exalted is He.
- That visiting graves, whatever they may be, is a legislated *Sunnah* according to the established manner.
- That supplication, when coupled with seeking a means to Allah through one of His creatures, is a subsidiary matter concerning the manner of supplication and not an issue of creed.
- That customary ignorance does not alter the realities of Sharī'ah terms.
- That creed is the foundation of action, and the action of the heart is more important than the action of the limbs; and perfection in both of them is required by the Sharī'ah, even if the levels of requirement differ.
- That Islam liberates the intellect, encourages reflection upon the universe, elevates the rank of knowledge and scholars, and welcomes every beneficial righteous thing.
- That *naẓar shar'ī* (legal investigation) and *naẓar 'aqlī* (rational investigation) may each address what does not fall within the sphere of the other; but they will not differ on decisive matters. A sound scientific reality will never conflict with a firmly established legal principle; the probabilistic aspect of either is interpreted so as to agree with the decisive. If both are probabilistic, then legal investigation is more worthy of following until the rational is established or refuted.
- Do not declare a Muslim an unbeliever who affirms the two testimonies, acts according to their requirements, and performs the obligations—whether with an [erroneous] opinion or a sin—unless he utters a word of disbelief or commits an act that cannot bear any interpretation other than disbelief.

### 2. Sincerity (*Ikhlāṣ*)

That the Muslim brother intend, in his words, his deeds, and all his *jihād*, the Face of Allah alone, without regard to wealth, appearance, fame, or title. Thereby he becomes a soldier of the idea and creed, not a soldier of interest and benefit.

### 3. Work

By it is meant: reforming the self, building the Muslim household, guiding society, liberating the homeland so that it is truly Islamic, restoring the international entity of the Muslim Ummah, and leading the world through spreading the call of Islam in its regions until there is no *fitnah* and the religion is all for Allah.

### 4. *Jihād*

By it is meant the enduring obligation until the Day of Resurrection, as the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Whoever dies without having fought or considered fighting [in Allah's cause] has died upon a branch of hypocrisy." [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 81]

### 5. Sacrifice (*Taḍḥiyah*)

By it is meant giving one's self, wealth, time, life, and everything in the path of the goal.

### 6. Obedience (*Ṭā'ah*)

By it is meant compliance with the command and carrying it out in hardship and ease, whether willingly or unwillingly.

### 7. Steadfastness (*Thabāt*)

By it is meant that the brother remain active and *mujāhid* (one who strives, مُجَاهِدٌ) in the path of his goal, however long the duration and however many the years, until he meets Allah upon that, having attained one of the two good things: the goal or martyrdom.

### 8. Selfless Dedication (*Tajarrud*)

By it is meant that you be devoted to your idea alone, nothing else, and that it be your beginning and end, your lowest and highest.

### 9. Brotherhood (*Ukhuwwah*)

By it is meant that hearts and souls be bound by the bond of creed; for creed is the firmest bond and the most precious. (See: The Duties of Brotherhood and Its Obligations.)

Brotherhood in Islam is the creedal bond that binds the Muslims to one another.

### 10. Trust (*Thiqah*)

By it is meant the soldier's assurance in his leader.

# Chapter 13: Understanding the Conditions of Brotherhood and Membership

## The Meaning of Brotherhood in Islam

Brotherhood in Islam (*al-ukhuwwah*) is a great divine bond that ties hearts together. It is the bond of closeness for the sake of Allah, and it is among the strongest ties of faith, just as prayer and its connection are — for the Prophet said: “The strongest ties of faith are: love for the sake of Allah and hatred for the sake of Allah.” [Musnad Aḥmad]

Brotherhood is one of the supports upon which Islam relies to consolidate the structure of the Muslim society and strengthen the bonds among its children.

The Islamic movement is in the utmost need of deepening this bond, so that it may be like a solid building whose parts reinforce one another, or like a single body: when one of its limbs complains, all the other limbs respond with sleeplessness and fever.

So that brotherhood may play its required role in the formation of the community, Islam has established for it practical, tangible rights and duties — to emphasize that it is responsibilities and burdens, not merely words and theories.

## The Rights and Duties of Brotherhood

1. **Brotherhood helps one to obey Allah.** This is in fulfillment of the Prophet's saying, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever Allah intends good for, He grants him a righteous friend; when he forgets, he reminds him, and when he remembers, he helps him.” [Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi, Book of Asceticism, Hadith 2393] To this ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may Allah be pleased with him, pointed when he said: “Keep to the brothers of truth, and live in their midst, for they are an adornment in prosperity and a refuge in calamity.”

2. **Brotherhood is mutual support and concern for the needs of the brother, striving to fulfill them.** This is in fulfillment of the saying of the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him: “For one of you to walk with his brother in fulfilling a need is better for him than secluding himself in this mosque of mine for a month.” [Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Good Manners, Hadith 6126; Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2543] And brotherhood is the sharing of mutual confidence, reliance upon the truthfulness of the other, his sincerity, and a deep trust that produces affection, esteem, respect, and obedience.

3. **Brotherhood is material mutual support.** This is in fulfillment of the Prophet's saying, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever relieves a Muslim of a distress of this world, Allah will relieve him of a distress on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever makes things easy for one in difficulty, Allah will make things easy for him in this world and the Hereafter. Whoever conceals the faults of a Muslim, Allah will conceal his faults in this world and the Hereafter. Allah is ever helping His servant as long as His servant helps his brother.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Virtues, Hadith 2699; Sunan Abū Dāwūd; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi; Sunan al-Nasāʾī; Sunan Ibn Mājah]

4. **Brotherhood entails social duties that cover the simplest and most important obligations.** The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “The rights of a Muslim upon a Muslim are six: when you meet him, greet him with peace; when he invites you, respond to him; when he seeks your counsel, counsel him; when he sneezes and praises Allah, say *yarḥamuk Allāh*; when he falls ill, visit him; and when he dies, follow his funeral.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Faith, Hadith 2162]

5. **Brotherhood is intimacy, affection, and solidarity.** The Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Do not cut ties, do not turn away from one another, do not harbor hatred, do not envy, and be servants of Allah as brothers. It is not lawful for a Muslim to shun his brother for more than three nights.” [Muwattaʾ Imām Mālik; Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Manners, Hadith 6077; Sunan Abū Dāwūd; Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi; Sunan al-Nasāʾī] And he said: “Do not belittle any good deed, even if it is to meet your brother with a cheerful face.” [Sahih Muslim, Book of Virtue, Enjoining Good Manners, and Joining Ties of Kinship, Hadith 2626] And he said: “Every good deed is charity; and among good deeds is that you meet your brother with a cheerful face, and that you empty your bucket into the vessel of your brother.” [Jāmiʿ al-Tirmidhi, Book on Righteousness and Maintaining Ties of Kinship, Hadith 1911; declared ḥasan by al-Albāni] And he said: “Exchange gifts, you will love one another, and rancor will depart.” [Muwattaʾ Imām Mālik; al-Adab al-Sharʿīyah by Ibn Mufliḥ; declared ṣaḥīḥ by al-Albāni]

## The Obligations of the Muslim Brother

Among the obligations of the Muslim brother — the one who belongs to the Islamic movement — the Martyr Imām set forth, in the Message of Teachings, some twenty obligations. In them he summarized to the truthful brother: that binding yourself by this *bayʿah* (pledge) obligates you to fulfill these duties, so that you may be a strong brick in the structure.

1. **You should have a daily portion (*wird*) from the Book of Allah** no less than one *juzʾ*, and strive not to exceed one month — and not less than three days — in completing it.

2. **You should perfect your recitation of the Qur'an, listening to it, and reflecting upon its meanings.** You should study the pure Sīrah and the history of the righteous predecessors (*al-salaf*) to the extent your time allows; at minimum, the book *Ḥammāt al-Islām* is sufficient for this. You should read abundantly from the *aḥādīth* of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, and memorize at least forty *aḥādīth* — let them be the forty of al-Nawawī. And you should study a treatise on the foundations of creed and a treatise on the branches of jurisprudence.

3. **You should undergo a general medical examination,** and take every means of physical strength and health protection, and keep away from causes of bodily weakness.

4. **You should avoid excess in coffee and tea,** and reduce them from among stimulating drinks, drinking them only when necessary; and you should abstain entirely from smoking.

5. **You should attend to cleanliness in everything:** in your dwelling, your clothing, your food, your body, and your workplace. For Islam has emphasized cleanliness.

6. **You should be truthful in word; do not ever lie.**

7. **You should be faithful to the covenant, the word, and the promise; do not break them,** whatever the circumstances.

8. **You should be courageous and patient in bearing hardship.** The best of courage is speaking the truth, keeping the secret, admitting the mistake, being fair to oneself, and controlling oneself in anger.

9. **You should be dignified,** preferring seriousness always. This does not prevent you from good-natured joking and a smiling laugh.

10. **You should be strongly characterized by modesty,** sensitive in feeling, greatly affected by good and evil — rejoicing in the former and pained by the latter. You should be humble without humiliation, servility, or flattery. You should ask for less than your rank until you attain it.

11. **You should be just and sound in judgment in all situations.** Do not let anger make you forget good deeds, nor let satisfaction close the eye of criticism. Do not let dispute make you forget the burden; speak the truth even if it is against yourself, or against the closest of people to you, even if it is bitter.

12. **You should possess great activity and be trained in public service.** Feel happiness and joy when you are able to offer a service to another person. Visit the sick, help the needy, carry the weak, console the afflicted — even with a kind word — and always hasten to good deeds.

13. **You should be tenderhearted, kind, generous,** pardoning and overlooking, gentle and forbearing, merciful toward human beings and animals. Have good dealings and good conduct with all people, preserving the Islamic social etiquettes: mercy toward the young, reverence for the elderly, making room in gatherings, not spying, not backbiting, not shouting, and asking permission to enter and to leave, and so on.

14. **You should perfect reading and writing,** read extensively in the treatises, newspapers, and magazines of the brothers, and the like. You should have a personal library, however small. You should delve deeply into your knowledge and craft if you are a specialist, and you should be well-acquainted with general Islamic affairs to the extent that enables you to form a conception of them and judge them in accordance with the requirements of the idea.

15. **You should work with your hands, economically,** however rich you may be. You should take up free work, however humble it may be, and plunge into it yourself, whatever your scholarly talents.

16. **Do not be eager for a government position;** regard it as the narrowest of doors to livelihood. Do not reject it if it is made available to you, and do not turn away from it unless it conflicts completely with the duties of the call.

17. **You should be eager above all else to perform your craft in a manner characterized by honesty,** precision, avoidance of cheating, and punctuality.

18. **You should demand your right in a good manner,** and fulfill the rights of people completely, without shortcoming, without being asked or procrastinating.

19. **Keep away from gambling in all its forms,** whatever the motive behind it, and avoid means of unlawful earnings, however immediate their gain may seem.

20. **Keep away from usury (*ribā*) in all transactions,** and purify yourself from it completely.

21. **Serve the general Islamic economy** by encouraging Islamic manufactures and economic institutions, and be keen that your money does not fall into non-Islamic hands, whatever the circumstances. Do not wear or consume except what your Islamic homeland produces.

22. **Contribute a portion of your wealth to the call,** and pay the obligatory *zakāh* due on it, and allocate a known right from it to the asker and the deprived, however small your income.

23. **Set aside a part of your income for emergencies,** however little.

24. **Do what you can to revive Islamic customs** and suppress foreign customs in every manifestation of life: the greeting, language, history, dress, furniture, work and rest hours, food and drink, arrival and departure, sorrow and joy, and so on. Be especially careful to follow the pure Sunnah in all of this.

25. **Boycott civil courts and every non-Islamic judiciary,** and clubs, newspapers, societies, schools, and institutes that oppose your Islamic idea — boycott them completely.

26. **Keep constant watch over yourself before Allah,** remember the Hereafter and prepare for it. Traverse the stages of traveling toward Allah's pleasure with determination and resolve; draw near to Him, glorified be He, through supererogatory acts of worship, including the night prayer (*qiyām al-layl*), fasting at least three days of every month, abundant remembrance of Allah with the heart and tongue, and the authenticated supplication in all circumstances.

27. **Perfect your purification,** and remain in a state of *wuḍūʾ* for most of the time.

28. **Perfect your prayer and be constant in performing it at its appointed times,** and be eager for congregational prayer in the mosque whenever possible.

29. **Fast Ramadan,** and perform *Ḥajj* to the House if you are able to find a way to it; and work toward that if you are not yet able.

30. **Always keep with you the intention of *jihād* and love of martyrdom,** and prepare for that to the extent that preparation is possible for you.

31. **Be constant in repentance and seeking forgiveness,** guard against minor sins as well as major ones, and set aside an hour before sleep in which you take yourself to account for the good and evil you have done. Be keen about time, for it is life. Do not spend any part of it without benefit, and keep away from doubtful matters lest you fall into the forbidden.

32. **Strive against your soul with a mighty striving** until its reins become easy for you. Lower your gaze, restrain your emotion, resist the impulses of instinct in yourself, and elevate it always to the wholesome lawful, keeping it away from the unlawful, whatever that may be.

33. **Avoid wine, intoxicants, drugs, and everything of this sort,** shunning it altogether.

34. **Keep away from bad companions, corrupt friends, places of disobedience, and dens of iniquity.**

35. **Fight places of amusement,** let alone remaining in them; and keep away from all manifestations of luxury and softness.

36. **Know the members of your cell individually,** thoroughly, and let them know you thoroughly as well. Fulfill their rights completely: love, esteem, help, and preferring them. Attend their meetings and do not be absent from them except for an overwhelming excuse; let your treatment of them be affectionate.

37. **Do not sever your connection with any branch, organization, or group** whose connection is not contrary to your idea, especially when you are commanded to do so.

38. **Work to spread your call in every place,** and keep the leadership informed of all your circumstances. Do not undertake any action that would affect it essentially except by permission. Be in constant spiritual contact with it, and consider yourself always a soldier in the line awaiting orders.
# Chapter 14: The Bond's Litany (Wird al-Rābiṭah)

The brother recites in contemplation this noble verse:

> "Say, 'O Allah, Owner of Sovereignty, You give sovereignty to whom You will and take it away from whom You will. You honor whom You will and humble whom You will. In Your hand is all good. Indeed, You are over all things competent. You cause the night to enter the day, and You cause the day to enter the night; and You bring the living out of the dead, and You bring the dead out of the living; and You give provision to whom You will without account.'" [Qur'an, Āl ʻImrān 3:26–27]

Then the brother brings to mind the image of those of his brothers whom he knows, and feels the spiritual connection between himself and those of them whom he has not met. Then he makes a supplication like this:

> "O Allah, You know that these hearts have gathered in Your love, and met in Your obedience, and united upon Your call, and pledged to support Your law. So strengthen, O Allah, their bond, and sustain their affection, and guide their paths, and fill them with Your light that never extinguishes. And expand their breasts with the abundance of faith in You, and the beauty of reliance upon You. And give them life through knowledge of You, and give them death as martyrs in Your path. Indeed, You are the best Protector and the best Helper. O Allah, amen, and may Allah send blessings upon our master Muhammad, and upon his family and companions, and grant them peace."
