# Hasan al-Banna

Hasan al-Banna (1906–1949) was an Egyptian Islamic scholar and the founder of the Muslim Brotherhood (Ikhwan Muslimin). He is the most extensively cited external authority in [[fathi-yakan]]'s "To Be a Muslim," providing the ideological and organizational framework for the [[islamic-movement]], the principles of [[baiah]], and the philosophical critique underlying [[man-vs-materialism]].

## Role in the Book

Al-Banna is referenced throughout Part II ("Living for Islam") as the intellectual architect of Islamic movement thought. [[fathi-yakan]] draws on him not merely for quotations but as the foundational voice on movement structure, member obligations, and strategic orientation.

## Three Types of People

In *Toward the Future of Man*, al-Banna identifies three categories of people based on Qur'anic classification:

1. **Those who live only for eating and enjoyment** — "Those who reject Allah will enjoy (this world) and eat as cattle eat; and the fire will be their abode" (Qur'an 47:12)
2. **Those who live for the glitter of worldly wealth** — drawn to "women and sons; heaped-up hoards of gold and silver" (Qur'an 3:14)
3. **Those who live to spread mischief and destruction** — "the most contentious of enemies" who "spread mischief throughout the world and destroy crops and cattle" (Qur'an 2:204–205)

This tripartite classification underpins the book's analysis of [[man-vs-materialism]] and the imperative of Islamic activism.

see: [[man-vs-materialism]]

## The Islamic Movement's Task

Al-Banna articulates the movement's general task as universal outreach:

> "Our task in general is to stand against the flood of modernist civilization overflowing from the swamp of materialistic and sinful desires. This flood has swept the Muslim nation away from the Prophet's leadership and Qur'anic guidance and deprived the world of its guiding light. Western secularism moved into a Muslim world already estranged from its Qur'anic roots, and delayed its advancement for centuries, and will continue to do so until we drive it from our lands. Moreover, we will not stop at this point, but will pursue this evil force to its own lands, invade its Western heartland, and struggle to overcome it until all the world shouts by the Name of the Prophet and the teachings of Islam spread throughout the world."

see: [[islamic-movement]]

## Five Tools of the Movement

Al-Banna identifies five tools inherited from the Prophet and his companions:

1. **Deep internal love of Allah** and belief in His help and support (Qur'an 3:160)
2. **Conviction that the Qur'an is universally applicable** — "a light and a perspicuous Book" (Qur'an 5:17–18)
3. **Strong sense of brotherhood** — "The believers are but a single brotherhood" (Qur'an 49:10)
4. **Firm reliance on Allah's love and justice** (Qur'an 9:120)
5. **Supreme confidence** in being chosen by Allah to bring truth, justice, and peace

see: [[islamic-movement]]

## Stages of Da'wah (Instructional Discourse)

In his *Instructional Discourse* (*Risalat Ta'alim*), al-Banna outlines three progressive stages:

1. **Introductory** — Disseminating concepts through oratory, writing, and civic action
2. **Preparatory** — Identifying reliable cadre, building wisdom among leaders and military discipline among recruits; no one admitted except those willing to carry out responsibilities in full obedience
3. **Execution** — Relentless combat and constant effort; "this stage will weed out all but the most honest and sincere"

see: [[strategic-planning]], [[baiah]]

## The Principles of Bai'ah

Defining the oath of loyalty, al-Banna states:

> "Our principles of bai'ah are ten: understanding, sincerity, ibadah (worship), jihad (struggle), sacrifice, obedience, perseverance, integrity, brotherhood, and trust."

The first principle — **understanding** — encompasses twenty sub-principles covering Islam as a complete system, the primacy of Qur'an and Sunnah, the rejection of mystical claims contrary to shari'ah, and guidelines on fiqh, bid'ah, and creed.

see: [[baiah]]

## Duties of Members (Educational Discourse / Risalat Ta'alim)

In his *Educational Discourse*, al-Banna prescribes over thirty responsibilities for movement members, covering:

- **Spiritual duties**: Daily Qur'an recitation (at least one juz), studying the Prophet's sirah, memorizing hadith, studying aqidah and fiqh, night prayer, fasting, maintaining wudu
- **Personal conduct**: Truthfulness, bravery, modesty, seriousness tempered by humor, fairness in judgment
- **Community engagement**: Visiting the sick, helping the needy, community service, respecting elders, gentleness with the young
- **Self-reliance**: Earning money regardless of wealth, avoiding luxury, supporting da'wah with wealth, paying zakat
- **Cultural practice**: Greeting with salaam, speaking Arabic, wearing modest clothing, following the sunnah in daily habits

He opens these duties with: "My honest brothers [and sisters], your pledge (bai'ah) requires you to carry out the following duties. Once you have done so, you will be strong and hard as a rock."

see: [[baiah]], [[brotherhood]]

## Movement vs. Specialized Organizations

Al-Banna clarifies the distinct nature of the movement:

> "My brothers, you are not a welfare organization, nor a political party; you are not a limited-goals organization, but a new spirit that moves in the nerves of people. You are the new light that will destroy the darkness of materialism. You are the voices that echo the message of Prophet Muhammad (s)."

see: [[islamic-movement]]

## Trials and Steadfastness

Al-Banna warns members to expect persecution:

> "I want to make it clear to you that today our da'wah is still unknown. When people know and understand the objective of this da'wah, you will be hated and assailed by them. You will face a lot of difficulties and so you will be blocked. Only in this situation will you start to follow the way of true callers to Islam."

see: [[islamic-activism]]

## Strength and Material Power

On the role of material strength, al-Banna teaches that faith is the most important strength, "followed in order by the strength of togetherness, and the strength of manpower and weapons." He emphasizes: "No group can be strong without values." The movement's slogan is "strength in everything."
