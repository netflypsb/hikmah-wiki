# Syed Qutb

Egyptian Islamic thinker, author of *Milestones* (*Ma'alim fi al-Tariq*), and one of the most influential ideologues of the 20th-century Islamic Movement. Executed in 1966 under the Nasser regime. His writings shaped the intellectual foundations of modern Islamic activism, and his ideas on jahiliyyah and Islamic vanguardism remain widely debated.

## Role in the Book

[[fathi-yakan]] cites Syed Qutb as a key authority on the concept of **psychological independence** — the fifth organizational characteristic of the [[islamic-movement]]. Qutb's idea of a "feeling of isolation" is presented as a defining trait that Muslim activists must cultivate in order to remain uncorrupted by the surrounding jahili environment.

## Psychological Isolation

Qutb argued that a vanguard of Muslims must exist **within** society yet remain psychologically separate from it. His formulation, as quoted in the book:

> "There should be a group that takes the lead and proceeds alone in the middle of the ignorance found everywhere on this earth. This group should be in this environment but isolated from it, and should not be of this environment, though it should communicate with it."

This concept of isolation is distinct from nihilist or rejectionist withdrawal. It is **psychological independence** — independence of manners, behavior, and feelings — not physical secession from society. The Prophet (s) reinforced this principle: "Do not be a follower who says, I am with the people right or wrong. But maintain disciplined behavior, so when people do good, you should too and when they do evil, you should avoid it."

The tension between psychological detachment and social engagement is central: effective dawah and movement work require remaining in contact with society, adapting one's message to each audience, while never allowing that contact to erode one's own Islamic commitment. As the broader tradition warns, "the devil is at work wherever good Muslims are active" — vigilance against Satanic influence is essential precisely where Islamic work is most dynamic. This aligns with the theme of [[self-control]], particularly jihad al-nafs and the need to guard the heart's ten vulnerabilities through which Shaitan enters.

## Key Ideas Referenced

- **Psychological independence** from the surrounding jahili environment while maintaining social engagement for dawah
- **Vanguard group** that leads in isolation from corruption but communicates with the broader society
- **Jahiliyyah** as the pervasive ignorance from which Muslims must mentally separate
- The awareness that Satanic assault intensifies wherever Islamic work is most vigorous, reinforcing the necessity of [[self-control]]
