# The Islamic Movement (Section 2.3)

The Islamic movement is a universal organisation that calls people to
Islam, strengthens belief, and organises them for work and jihad. Its duty
is to help people submit to Allah as individuals and groups by establishing
an Islamic community governed by the Book of Allah and the Prophet's sunnah.

## General Task: Universal Outreach

The movement must purify Islam from bid'ah and superstition, and present it
in pure form. It must strive against the remnants of colonial thought —
European socialism and American capitalism — until the Ummah recovers its
independence and identity. The adoption of any man-made system is a denial
of Islam (Qur'an 3:85). [[hasan-al-banna]] summarised the movement's task
as standing against the flood of materialistic civilisation, pursuing it to
its Western heartland, until all religion is for Allah (Qur'an 30:4).

Eight specific duties follow from the general task: internal governance
(Qur'an 5:52), public relations (Qur'an 2:143), judicial system (Qur'an
4:65), military defence (Qur'an 9:41), economic institutions (Qur'an 4:5),
educational infrastructure (Qur'an 96:1), family values (Qur'an 66:6),
and personal empowerment (Qur'an 28:77).

## Five General Characteristics

1. **Guided by Allah** — Direction comes from Revelation, not members'
   interests. Qur'an 6:162: "My prayer and sacrifice, my life and death,
   are all for Allah."

2. **Self-Motivated** — The movement originates from Islam itself, not as
   a reaction to Western ideologies.

3. **Growth-Oriented** — It liberates, not enslaves. It promotes science
   and technology to learn about Allah's greatness, not for power. The
   Prophet said: "Take wisdom from whatever container it comes from."

4. **Comprehensive and Balanced** — It is Salafi in source, Sunni in
   method, Rabbani in spiritual depth, and political in engagement.

5. **Unity in Diversity** — Legitimises different positions on public
   issues but requires unity in usul (fundamentals). [[hasan-al-banna]]:
   "We are an idea and a creed, a system and a syllabus... the system of
   Allah and the way of His Prophet."

## Seven Organisational Characteristics

1. Freedom from special interests of politicians and powerbrokers
2. Gradualism — three stages of da'wah: introductory, preparatory,
   execution. Plus low-profile work to purify intention and ensure safety.
3. Strategic orientation for long-range victory (Qur'an 29:1-3)
4. Openness in work, secrecy in organisation — "Work in public but
   organise in secret"
5. Psychological independence (not nihilist isolation) — [[syed-qutb]]'s
   concept of being in society but not of it
6. Moral consistency — the end never justifies the means. Five principles
   support this: no contamination by un-Islamic means; Islam is indivisible;
   major effort required; guard against diseased hearts; truth comes only
   from revelation.
7. Material strength — "A strong believer is better loved by Allah than a
   weak believer." Strength hierarchy: faith > togetherness > manpower > weapons.

## Five Tools of the Movement

Imam Hasan al-Banna proclaimed five tools:
1. Deep internal love of Allah and belief in His help (Qur'an 3:160)
2. Conviction that the Qur'an is a universally applicable guide (Qur'an 5:17-18)
3. Strong sense of the rights and importance of brotherhood (Qur'an 49:10)
4. Firm reliance on Allah's love and justice (Qur'an 9:120)
5. Supreme confidence in being chosen by Allah to bring truth and justice
