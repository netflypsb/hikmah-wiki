# Ibadah (Worship)

Ibadah is the peak of submission to Allah and the connecting step between creature and Creator. It encompasses all pillars of Islam — prayer, fasting, zakat, and Hajj — as well as the entire shari'ah. The logic of Islam requires life to be all worship and obedience: *"I have created jinns and Men only that they may serve Me"* (Qur'an 51:56-58); *"Truly, my prayer and my death, are all for Allah"* (Qur'an 6:163).

## 1. Dynamic

Worship must be alive, inspired with love of Allah, and productive of ihsan. The Prophet (s) said: *"It is to worship Allah as though you see Him, and though you see Him not, yet truly He sees you"* (Bukhari, Muslim). Worship must also be humble, inspired by both awe and intimacy. 'Aisha reported: *"The Messenger of Allah was talking with us and we were talking with him, but when the time for prayer came he seemed not to know us nor we him"* (Azdi). Lifeless worship yields nothing: *"Many a people get nothing but fatigue from their prayer; and many a people get from their fasting nothing but hunger and thirst"* (Nasa'i).

## 2. Open-Hearted

Worship must be open to Allah and dedicated to Him, free from concerns about business and the hardships of this world. *"Allah will not look at the prayer which does not relate the man's heart with his body"* (Al-Firdausi). Prayer is directed toward the hereafter; when you start praying, you leave this physical world. Al-Hassan warned: *"A prayer without one's heart into it is a fast road to punishment."*

## 3. Fervent

Worship should be impassioned and unsatiable — one must never be complacent but seek ever greater nearness to Allah through nawafil. Hadith qudsi: *"I will declare war against him who shows hostility to a pious worshipper of Mine. And the most beloved things with which My slave comes nearer to Me is what I have enjoined upon him. My slave continued to come closer to Me through Nawafil till I love him. So I become the sense of hearing with which he hears, and the sense of sight with which he sees, and the hand with which he grips, and the leg with which he walks. And if he asks Me, I will give him, and if he asks My protection, I will protect him"* (Bukhari, from Abu Hurairah).

## 4. Dedicated

Worship includes qiyam al-layl (night prayer), the most effective armor of faith for a believer. One should discipline oneself to become accustomed to prayer at night. *"Truly the rising by night is most potent for governing the soul, and most suitable for framing the word of prayer and praise"* (Qur'an 73:6-34). *"They were in the habit of sleeping but little by night, and in the hours of early dawn, they were found praying for forgiveness"* (Qur'an 51:17-18). *"Their limbs do forsake their beds of sleep, the while they call on their Lord, in fear and hope, and they spend in charity out of the sustenance We have bestowed on them"* (Qur'an 32:16).

## 5. Qur'an-Oriented and Full of Awe

Allocate time to read and ponder the Qur'an, especially at dawn: *"The prayer and reading in the morning carry their testimony"* (Qur'an 17:78). *"Had We sent down this Qur'an to a mountain, verily you would have seen it humble itself and cleave asunder for fear of Allah"* (Qur'an 59:21). The Prophet (s) said: *"The Qur'an was revealed in all gravity, so when you read it try to be pensive"* (Abu Ya'la, Abu Naim). *"The Qur'an is the food of Allah… the rope of Allah, bright light, good medicine, and a guide"* (al-Hakim). *"The best worship for my ummah is reading the Qur'an"* (Abu Naim). Whoever declares lawful what the Qur'an prohibits denies it (Tirmidhi).

## 6. Constant in Supplication

Supplication in every detail of life is the essence of worship: *"Call upon Me, and I will respond to you"* (Qur'an 40:60). One should use the supplications of the Prophet (s) when appropriate. The Prophet (s) left 21 supplications for daily situations — upon waking, going to bed, dressing, entering and leaving the bathroom, before and after meals, entering and leaving the house, going to and entering/leaving the mosque, after daily salah, leaving a meeting, setting out on a trip, when unable to sleep, when overwhelmed by sadness, congratulating newlyweds, and before marital relations — embedding remembrance of Allah into every moment of life.

Together these six characteristics ensure that ibadah is not mere ritual but a living, dynamic relationship with Allah that transforms the worshipper from within.
