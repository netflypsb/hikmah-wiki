# Family Life (Section 1.4)

The family is the basic unit of the Muslim community. Islam defines clear
responsibilities at every stage — before marriage, during marriage, and in
raising children.

## Before Marriage

Four principles guide the pre-marital stage:

1. **Niyyah for Allah** — Marry for the sake of Allah, seeking to
   complete half of your [[ibadah]] through a righteous partner.
2. **Protect modesty** — Marriage shields modesty and prevents
   transgression, tying directly to [[morals]].
3. **Choose for piety** — Select a partner for religious commitment, not
   wealth or beauty. The Prophet said: "A woman may be married for four
   things — her wealth, her lineage, her beauty, or her religion. Choose
   the religious one." (Tabrani)
4. **Prioritise din** — The criterion is always faith and character, not
   social status or material assets.

## In Marriage

Three responsibilities within marriage:

1. **Deal kindly** — The Prophet said: "The best of you are those who are
   best to their wives." (Ibn Majah/Tirmidhi) Kindness is not optional;
   it is a core [[morals]] obligation.
2. **Develop harmony** — Build understanding and mutual respect. The home
   should reflect the values of [[aqidah]] and practice of [[ibadah]].
3. **Base on Islam** — The marriage must rest on Islamic principles, not
   convenience or cultural tradition. Stability comes from shared faith.

## Raising Children

Children are born in a state of fitrah — natural innocence (Bukhari/
Muslim). Parents shape their trajectory. Raising a righteous child is
better than ongoing charity (Tirmidhi). Three deeds continue after death:
ongoing charity, beneficial knowledge, and a righteous child who prays
for the parent. This connects family life to [[fathi-yakan]]'s vision of
the Muslim household as the cradle of the next generation.

The family is where [[self-control]] is first learned and where [[morals]]
become lived practice rather than abstract ideals.
