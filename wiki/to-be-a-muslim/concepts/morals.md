# Morals (Section 1.3)

Morality is the core of worship. Without moral character, worship becomes
meaningless — an empty shell devoid of spirit. The book identifies six
sub-topics that define Islamic morality.

## Scrupulous Conscience (Wara')

Wara is avoiding even doubtful matters, not merely the clearly forbidden.
The Prophet (s) said: "What is lawful is plain and what is unlawful is
plain, but between them are things doubtful which many people do not know.
He who guards against doubtful things keeps his religion and honour
blameless." (Bukhari/Muslim) Wara is the foundation of [[morals]] and a
guard for [[aqidah]].

## Modesty and Lowering the Gaze

Allah commands: "Tell the believing men to lower their gaze and guard
their modesty." (Qur'an 24:30) Modesty extends beyond dress; it governs
the eyes, the tongue, and public conduct. It is inseparable from
[[ibadah]] — worship without modesty is defective.

## Wisdom and Guarding the Tongue

The tongue must be guarded against abuse, slander, and useless talk.
Speaking without wisdom erodes the soul. Silence, when uncertain, is a
form of [[self-control]] and an act of worship.

## Fortitude

Fortitude combines perseverance, kindness, and patience. Qur'an 42:43
praises those who endure with dignity. Qur'an 39:10 promises reward for
the patient. Qur'an 25:63 describes the servants of the Most Merciful as
those who walk humbly. Seven stories illustrate the Prophet's fortitude:
at Hunain; the bedouin who pulled his cloak (Anas); the bedouin who
demanded payment (Abu Hurairah); the woman who insulted him; relatives who
abused him; the Jewish man demanding debt repayment; and Prophet Isa's
endurance.

## Honesty, Sincerity, and Grace

Truth leads to goodness and lying leads to evil (Bukhari). Humility means
no pride (Muslim). Trust and kindness demand avoiding suspicion (Qur'an
49:12). Grace is sacrificing self and wealth for Allah (Qur'an 8:4,
2:272). These qualities anchor every [[ibadah]] and reflect [[fathi-yakan]].

## Good Example

Every action of a Muslim portrays Islamic principles. Conduct is the
living da'wah — more persuasive than words alone. See also [[family-life]]
and [[brotherhood]].
