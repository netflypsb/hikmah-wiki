# Self-Control (Section 1.5)

Jihad al-nafs — the struggle against the self — is the greater jihad.
Without mastery over the self, no external struggle succeeds. The book
contrasts two categories of people: those defeated by their desires
(Qur'an 45:23 — "Have you seen he who takes his desires as his god?")
and those who struggle against their impulses (Qur'an 3:135 — those who,
when they commit an evil act or wrong themselves, remember Allah and seek
forgiveness). Self-control is integral to [[aqidah]] and [[ibadah]].

## Two Sources of Strength

1. **The Heart** — A sound heart has three qualities: soft (receptive to
   truth), pure (cleansed of disease), and strong (firm against trials).
   'Ali ibn Abi Talib described this triad as essential for [[morals]].

2. **The Mind and Intellect** — "Those who have knowledge fear Allah"
   (Qur'an 35:28). A single scholar is harder on Satan than a thousand
   worshippers (Tirmidhi). Knowledge empowers [[syed-qutb]]'s emphasis on
   conscious understanding over blind following.

## Signs of Spiritual Defeat

When the heart becomes immoveable — hardened against guidance — spiritual
defeat has set in. Satan enters through eight doors:

1. Greed — wanting more than one's share
2. Love of this life — preferring dunya over akhirah
3. Idleness — wasting time and energy
4. Pride — believing oneself superior to others
5. Haughtiness — arrogance before Allah
6. Envy — resenting others' blessings
7. Boastfulness — showing off one's achievements
8. Covetousness — obsessing over others' possessions

Each door severs [[self-control]] and corrupts [[aqidah]].

## Defences Against the Self

Practical defences include: dhikr (remembrance of Allah), recitation of
the Qur'an, fasting (which restrains desire), avoiding hasty behaviour,
and trusting in Allah (tawakkul). "Verily, those who are pious, when an
evil thought comes to them from Satan, they remember Allah, and indeed
they then see clearly." (Qur'an 7:201) Remembrance is the antidote to
satanic whisperings. See also [[ibadah]] and [[morals]].
