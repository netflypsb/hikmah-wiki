# Man vs Materialism (Section 2.1)

Islam classifies people into three types in their relationship with the
material world. Understanding these types is essential for living for Islam.

## Three Types of People

1. **Materialists** — Those who deny the afterlife entirely. "There is
   nothing but our life in this world; we die and live and nothing
   destroys us but time." (Qur'an 6:29, 45:24) They live as if death is
   the end, severing all [[aqidah]].

2. **Functional Atheists** — Those who claim belief but live
   materialistically. [[hasan-al-banna]] identified three sub-categories
   from the Qur'an: those who see believers as misguided (47:12), those
   consumed by love of material pleasures (3:14), and mischief-makers who
   corrupt society while claiming faith. This second group is the most
   dangerous because their hypocrisy confuses others about true Islam.

3. **Sojourners** — True believers who see the world as temporary. "The
   life of this world is but play and amusement; the home of the Hereafter
   is best for those who are pious." (Qur'an 6:32) Their purpose: "I
   created jinn and mankind only to worship Me." (51:56)

## Five Essential Knowledge Points

To live for Islam, one must acquire five kinds of knowledge:

1. **Divine Purpose** — Qur'an 51:56 and 11:7 establish worship and
   stewardship as the reason for existence.
2. **Value of the World** — The Prophet likened the world to a garbage
   dump and to a lamb cadaver (Muslim) — valueless compared to the
   Hereafter.
3. **Certainty of Death** — "Everyone upon it will perish" (Qur'an 55:26-
   27; 3:185). Abu Dharr reminded that even the Prophets died.
4. **Right vs Wrong** — Every Muslim must learn the essentials of fiqh
   to distinguish halal from haram.
5. **Fallacies of the Ignorant** — Study the thinking of Islam's enemies,
   as [[fathi-yakan]] urges, to refute them effectively.

## Characteristics of Those Living for Islam

They are action-oriented (Qur'an 103:2-3 — loss except those who believe
and do good), problem-oriented, honourable in defence of truth (Qur'an
63:8), and movement-oriented (Qur'an 28:35, 5:2). These traits define
[[islamic-activism]] in practice. See also [[hasan-al-banna]] and
[[islamic-movement]].
