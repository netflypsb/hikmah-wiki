# الناس — An-Nas (114)

> An-Nas — 6 verses, Meccan — the second of the *Mu'awwidhatayn* (pair of refuge-seeking surahs, with [[surah-113-al-falaq|Al-Falaq]])

## Overview

An-Nas (الناس, "Mankind") is the 114th and final surah of the Holy Qur'an. It is a **Meccan** surah comprising **6 verses**, spanning **juz 30** (page 604 of the 604-page Madani mushaf), with **1 ruku**.

The surah is a command to seek refuge (*isti'ādha*) in Allah — identified by three of His attributes: **Lord** (Rabb), **Sovereign** (Malik), and **God** (Ilāh) of mankind — from the evil of the whisperer (*al-waswās al-khannās*) who operates covertly in human hearts, from among both jinn and mankind. Together with Al-Falaq (113), it forms the *Mu'awwidhatayn* — the two surahs of divine protection that the Prophet ﷺ recommended reciting morning and evening.

## Tafsir

### Ibn Kathir (Abridged) — Surah-level commentary

*Note: The raw JSONL duplicates the full Ibn Kathir commentary across all six ayah records (a known artifact for short surahs). The complete commentary is presented once here, as it addresses the surah as a whole.*

> Which was revealed in Makkah. In the Name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful. These are three attributes from the attributes of the Lord, the Mighty and Majestic. They are lordship, sovereignty and divinity. Thus, He is the Lord of everything, the King of everything and the God of everything. All things are created by Him, owned by Him, and subservient to Him. Therefore, He commands whoever is seeking protection to seek refuge with the One Who has these attributes from the evil of the whisperer who withdraws. This (the whisperer) is the devil that is assigned to man. For verily, there is not any of the Children of Adam except that he has a companion that beautifies wicked deeds for him. This devil will go to any lengths to confuse and confound him. The only person who is safe is He Whom Allah protects.
>
> It is confirmed in the Ṣaḥīḥ that he (the Prophet ﷺ) said: «مَا مِنْكُمْ مِنْ أَحَدٍ إِلَّا قَدْ وُكِّلَ بِهِ قَرِينُهُ» (There is not a single one of you except that his companion (a devil) has been assigned to him.) They (the Companions) said, "What about you, O Messenger of Allah?" He replied, «نَعَمْ، إِلَّا أَنَّ اللهَ أَعَانَنِي عَلَيْهِ فَأَسْلَمَ، فَلَا يَأْمُرُنِي إِلَّا بِخَيْرٍ» (Yes. However, Allah has helped me against him and he has accepted Islam. Thus, he only commands me to do good.)
>
> It is also confirmed in the Two Ṣaḥīḥs from Anas, who reported the story of Ṣafiyyah when she came to visit the Prophet while he was performing I`tikaf, that he went out with her during the night to walk her back to her house. So, two men from the Anṣār met him (on the way). When they saw the Prophet, they began walking swiftly. So, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ said, «عَلَى رِسْلِكُمَا، إِنَّهَا صَفِيَّةُ بِنْتُ حُيَي» (Slow down! This is Ṣafiyyah bint Ḥuyay!) They said, "Glory be to Allah, O Messenger of Allah!" He said, «إِنَّ الشَّيْطَانَ يَجْرِي مِنِ ابْنِ آدَمَ مَجْرَى الدَّم، وَإِنِّي خَشِيتُ أَنْ يَقْذِفَ فِي قُلُوبِكُمَا شَيْئًا، أَوْ قَالَ: شَرًّا» (Verily, Shaytan runs in the Son of Adam like the running of the blood. And verily, I feared that he might cast something into your hearts — or he said — evil.)
>
> Sa`īd bin Jubayr reported that Ibn `Abbās said concerning Allah's statement, الْوَسْوَاسِ الْخَنَّاسِ (The whisperer (Al-Waswas) who withdraws): "The devil who is squatting (perched) upon the heart of the Son of Adam. So when he becomes absentminded and heedless he whispers. Then, when he remembers Allah he withdraws." Mujāhid and Qatādah also said this. Al-Mu`tamir bin Sulaymān reported that his father said, "It has been mentioned to me that Shaytan is Al-Waswas. He blows into the heart of the Son of Adam when he is sad and when he is happy. But when he (man) remembers Allah, Shaytan withdraws." Al-`Awfī reported from Ibn `Abbās: "He is Shaytan. He whispers and then when he is obeyed, he withdraws."
>
> As for Allah's saying, الَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ النَّاسِ (Who whispers in the breasts of An-Nas) — Is this specific for the Children of Adam as is apparent, or is it general, including both mankind and Jinns? There are two views concerning this. This is because they (the Jinns) are also included in the usage of the word An-Nas (the people) in most cases. Ibn Jarīr said, "The phrase Rijālun min Al-Jinn (Men from the Jinns) has been used in reference to them, so it is not strange for the word An-Nas to be applied to them also." Then Allah says, مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ (Of Jinn and An-Nas) — Is this explanatory of Allah's statement, الَّذِى يُوَسْوِسُ فِى صُدُورِ النَّاسِ? This is supportive of the second view. It has also been said that Allah's saying, مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ is an explanation of who is it that whispers into the breasts of mankind from the devils of mankind and Jinns. This is similar to Allah's saying: وَكَذَلِكَ جَعَلْنَا لِكُلِّ نَبِىٍّ عَدُوًّا شَيَـاطِينَ الإِنْسِ وَالْجِنِّ (And so We have appointed for every Prophet enemies — Shayāṭīn among mankind and Jinn...) (6:112).
>
> Imām Aḥmad recorded that Ibn `Abbās said, "A man came to the Prophet and said, 'O Messenger of Allah! Sometimes I say things to myself that I would rather fall from the sky than say (aloud openly).' The Prophet said, «اللهُ أَكْبَرُ، اللهُ أَكْبَرٌ الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ الَّذِي رَدَّ كَيْدَهُ إِلَى الْوَسْوَسَةِ» (Allah is Most Great! Allah is Most Great! All praise is due to Allah Who sent his (Shaytan's) plot back as only a whisper.)" (Abū Dāwūd and An-Nasā'ī)

### Fi Zilal al-Qur'an — Sayyid Qutb (companion tafsir)

The full thematic-literary commentary by Sayyid Qutb is in the companion wiki:

→ [[fi-zilal/surah-114-an-nas|Fi Zilal — An-Nas]] — five thematic sections: *The Lord of Mankind*, *The Ancient Battle Between Man and Satan*, *Human Whisperers More Devilish Than the Devil*, *The Everlasting War*, *The Most Perfect Concept of the Battle*.

Key insight from Fi Zilal: the three divine attributes (Lord, Sovereign, God) are deliberately paired against the three-stage identification of the whisperer (its nature as *slinking*, its function as *whispering in hearts*, its origin as *jinn and mankind*). The "slinking" quality (الْخَنَّاس) reveals the whisperer's intrinsic feebleness — it retreats when confronted with God's remembrance, making it a cowardly enemy easily defeated by the believer who seeks refuge.

## Structural Notes

- The surah opens with the command **قُل** (Say) — the same imperative that opens Al-Falaq (113) and Al-Kāfirūn (109), marking a direct divine instruction to the Prophet ﷺ.
- **Triple naming of Allah**: Rabb (Lord) → Malik (Sovereign) → Ilāh (God) — progressing from the general (sustainer) to the specific (the only one worthy of worship). Ibn Kathir notes these correspond to three attributes: lordship, sovereignty, and divinity.
- **Triple identification of the whisperer**: al-waswās al-khannās (the slinking whisperer, v.4) → alladhī yuwaswisu fī ṣudūr an-nās (who whispers in the breasts, v.5) → min al-jinnati wa-n-nās (from jinn and mankind, v.6) — moving from name → function → origin.
- The word **النَّاس** (mankind/people) appears **four times** in six verses — an unusual density that emphasizes the human dimension of the spiritual struggle.
- **Saj' (rhymed prose):** the surah's verses end in the rhyme pattern -āsī (-اسِ), binding the surah together sonically.

## Linguistics

Key Arabic terms in this surah (see [[linguistics/index|linguistics index]] for root studies where available):

- **النَّاس (an-nās)** — root ن-و-س (nāsa): mankind, people. The surah's name and its most repeated word.
- **وَسْوَاس (waswās)** — root و-س-و-س: onomatopoeic — the sound of whispering; the whisperer.
- **خَنَّاس (khannās)** — root خ-ن-س (khanasa): to retreat, withdraw, hide. The one who slinks away when God is remembered. Intensive form (faʿʿāl) — the habitual retreater.
- **صُدُور (ṣudūr)** — root ص-د-ر (ṣadara): breasts, chests; the seat of the heart and conscience in Quranic anthropology.
- **جِنَّة (jinnah)** — root ج-ن-ن (janna): to cover, conceal; the unseen/hidden creation (jinn).
