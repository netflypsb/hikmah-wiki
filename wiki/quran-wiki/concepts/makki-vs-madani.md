# Makki vs Madani — Classification of Surahs

## Definition

The classification of Qur'anic surahs by place and time of revelation: **Makki** (Meccan, before Hijrah) and **Madani** (Medinan, after Hijrah). This is the foundational structural distinction in Qur'anic studies, with significant implications for content, style, and legal methodology.

## Etymology

**Makki** (مَكِّي) — from Makkah (مَكَّة), the city of revelation before Hijrah. **Madani** (مَدَنِي) — from Madinah (الْمَدِينَة), the city of revelation after Hijrah.

## Detailed Explanation

- **Makki surahs:** 86 (75% of total) | **Madani surahs:** 28 (25% of total)

**Makki characteristics:** Short verses, intense rhetorical devices (oaths, questions, parables), focus on tawhid, akhirah, stories of past nations, warnings to disbelievers. Few legal rulings.

**Madani characteristics:** Longer verses, detailed legislation (family law, war, trade, criminal law), address to the believing community, hypocrisy exposed, relations with People of the Book.

**Examples:**
- [[surah-096-al-alaq|Al-'Alaq (96)]] — First revelation; archetypal Makki: short, oath-based, command to read
- [[surah-002-al-baqarah|Al-Baqarah (2)]] — Archetypal Madani: longest, comprehensive legislation
- [[surah-112-al-ikhlas|Al-Ikhlas (112)]] — Makki: pure tawhid in four verses
- [[surah-009-at-tawbah|At-Tawbah (9)]] — Madani: detailed war rules, hypocrisy

## Tafsir Perspectives

Tafsir Ibn Kathir on the opening of Al-Baqarah: 'This is the first surah revealed in Madinah... It contains every matter of the Shari'ah.' In contrast, Makki surahs like Al-Mulk (67) focus on 'He who created death and life to test you' (67:2) — foundational theology before legislation.
