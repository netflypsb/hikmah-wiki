# Qira'at Variations — Canonical Readings of the Qur'an

## Definition

The Qira'at (قِرَاءَات) are the authenticated, multi-variant readings of the Qur'anic text transmitted through chains of recitation (tawatur) from the Prophet (SAW). They represent legitimate linguistic variations in pronunciation, morphology, and occasional wording — all divinely sanctioned.

## Etymology

**Qira'ah** (قِرَاءَة) — reading, recitation, pronunciation; from qara'a (to read, to gather).

## Detailed Explanation

**The Seven (Qira'at Sab'):** Imam Nafi' (d. 169 AH), Ibn Kathir (d. 120), Abu Amr (d. 154), Ibn Amir (d. 118), Asim (d. 127), Hamzah (d. 156), Al-Kisa'i (d. 189).

**The Three (Thalath):** Abu Ja'far (d. 130), Ya'qub (d. 205), Khalaf (d. 229) — added to make Ten.

**The Four (Arba'):** Ibn Wardan, Ibn Jammaz, Al-Yazidi, Al-Hasan — added to make Fourteen.

**Types of variation:**
- Linguistic: vowelling differences (malik / maalik in 1:4), morphology (yaktub / yaktib)
- Syntactic: active vs. passive, singular vs. plural pronouns
- Wording: rare synonym substitution (a'tina / amatina in 2:25)

**Examples:**
- [[surah-001-al-fatihah|Al-Fatihah (1:4)]] — **Maliki** vs **Maaliki** — both valid, both transmitted
- [[surah-002-al-baqarah|Al-Baqarah (2:25)]] — **A'tina** vs **Amatina** — both valid
- [[surah-037-as-saffat|As-Saffat (37:130)]] — **Ilyas** vs **Ilyasin** — both valid

## Tafsir Perspectives

Tafsir Ibn Kathir: 'The Qira'at are all from Allah, revealed to the Prophet (SAW) and transmitted through tawatur. The differences between them are a mercy and an expansion of meaning — each reading illuminates a facet of the divine text.' Ibn al-Jazari in 'An-Nashr fi al-Qira'at al-Ashr' established the science of tajweed and qira'at as a pillar of Quranic studies.
