# Ashab al-Kahf — The Companions of the Cave

## Summary

The Sleepers of the Cave (أَصْحَابُ ٱلْكَهْفِ) are the central narrative of Surah Al-Kahf (18:9-26). A group of young believers fled persecution by a tyrant king, took refuge in a cave, and slept for 309 years. Their story addresses divine protection, the mystery of time, and the debate over the number of sleepers.

## Key Narrative Points

**Fleeing Persecution** — The youths refused to worship anyone but Allah; the cave became their sanctuary (18:10, 16)
**Divine Sleep** — Allah caused them to sleep for 309 lunar years (18:25) — a miracle of time compression
**The Dog** — Their dog stretched his forelegs at the entrance (18:18) — even animals in righteous company are blessed
**The Debate** — Allah intentionally left their number ambiguous: 'Say: Allah knows best how long they stayed' (18:26)
**The Lesson** — 'We made their case a sign... for those who take warning' (18:29); the story is not about the number but about faith under persecution

## Tafsir Perspectives

Tafsir Ibn Kathir on 18:9: 'The people of the cave were a group of young men from a city near Ephesus... They fled the tyrant Decianus (Daqyanus) who demanded idol worship. Their story is one of the greatest proofs of resurrection — sleeping 309 years and waking alive.'
