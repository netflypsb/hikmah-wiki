# Yusuf (AS) — The Most Detailed Narrative

## Summary

The story of Yusuf (AS) (يُوسُف) is the longest continuous narrative in the Qur'an, occupying the entirety of Surah Yusuf (12). It traces his journey from the well of betrayal to the throne of Egypt, demonstrating divine planning, beauty, patience, and forgiveness.

## Key Narrative Points

**The Dream** — Eleven stars, the sun, and the moon prostrating (12:4) — divine promise revealed in childhood
**The Well** — Thrown into the well by his brothers out of jealousy; sold to an Egyptian (12:8-20)
**The Trial of Beauty** — The wife of Al-Aziz attempted seduction; Yusuf preferred prison over sin (12:23-35)
**Interpretation of Dreams** — In prison, he interprets the dreams of the king's cupbearer and baker (12:36-42)
**The Throne** — Called from prison to interpret the king's dream; appointed treasurer of Egypt (12:43-57)
**Reunion and Forgiveness** — 'There is no blame upon you today. May Allah forgive you; He is the Most Merciful of the merciful' (12:92) — the pinnacle of forgiveness.

## Tafsir Perspectives

Tafsir Ibn Kathir on 12:3: 'This is the most beautiful of stories... It contains lessons in patience, trust in Allah, chastity, forgiveness, and the triumph of righteousness over betrayal.' The Prophet (SAW) said: 'The most eloquent of people in speech is Yusuf, when he said: Take this shirt of mine and cast it over the face of my father; he will become seeing' (Sahih al-Bukhari).
