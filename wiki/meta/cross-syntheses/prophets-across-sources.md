# Prophets Across Sources

## Summary

The prophetic narrative exists in three layers: the Quranic account (brief, thematic, spiritually focused), the seerah literature (chronological, biographical detail), and the hadith corpus (authenticated sayings and actions). This synthesis triangulates all three for the major prophets.

## Three Layers of Prophetic Knowledge

### Quranic Account

**Characteristics:** Thematic, not chronological. Stories repeat across surahs with different emphases. Purpose is spiritual instruction, not biography.
**Example:** [[quran-wiki/entities/prophets/musa|Musa (AS)]] — Mentioned in 34 surahs. No single continuous narrative; instead, fragments (birth, staff, conversation, magicians, exodus, Khidr) appear in different contexts.

### Seerah Literature

**Characteristics:** Chronological and biographical. Ibn Hisham / Ibn Ishaq provide narrative continuity, context of revelation, and historical setting.
**Example:** Ibn Hisham on Musa: Full narrative from birth to death, including details not in the Qur'an (e.g., the name of Pharaoh, the identity of the Egyptian who adopted him).

### Hadith Corpus

**Characteristics:** Authenticated sayings of the Prophet (SAW) about previous prophets. Validates, expands, or contextualizes Quranic accounts.
**Example:** Sahih al-Bukhari, Book of Prophets: The Prophet (SAW) said, 'None should say I am better than Yunus ibn Matta' — hadith establishes prophetic humility and ranks.

## Triangulation Results

- **Musa (AS):** Quran provides the spiritual lessons (sabr, divine speech, knowledge); seerah provides the historical setting (Egypt, Midian, Bani Israel); hadith provides the Prophet's commentary on Musa's excellence.
- **Isa (AS):** Quran corrects the Christian narrative (not crucified, not divine); seerah provides the context of the Najran delegation; hadith provides the second coming (Sahih Muslim).
- **Ibrahim (AS):** Quran provides the theological arguments (rational monotheism); seerah provides the chronology of his journeys (Ur, Harran, Sham, Egypt, Makkah); hadith provides the building of the Ka'bah (Sahih al-Bukhari).

## Synthesis

The Qur'an is the **filter**: any seerah or hadith detail that contradicts the Quranic account is rejected. The seerah provides **context**; the hadith provides **authentication and expansion**. All three together produce the complete prophetic picture that no single source can provide alone.
