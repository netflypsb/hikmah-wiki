# Tawhid in Creed and Exegesis

## Summary

Tawhid is the central theme of the Qur'an and the first article of Islamic creed. This synthesis explores how tafsir scholars (exegetes) and theologians (mutakallimun) approach divine unity — complementary but distinct methodologies.

## Approaches to Tawhid

### Tafsir Approach (Ibn Kathir)

**Method:** Textual, verse-by-verse, tracing tawhid through linguistic analysis, hadith evidence, and prophetic precedent.
**Example:** On [[quran-wiki/surah-112-al-ikhlas|Al-Ikhlas (112)]]: Ibn Kathir explains 'Allahu as-Samad' as 'the One to whom all creation turns in need, who has no need of any creation.'
**Strength:** Grounded in the text; every claim is traceable to a verse or authenticated narration.
**Limitation:** Less systematic on philosophical objections (e.g., infinite regress, composition).

### Theological Approach (al-Ash'ari, al-Maturidi)

**Method:** Systematic, responding to philosophical objections (Greek, Christian, Mu'tazili), using rational argumentation alongside revelation.
**Example:** On divine attributes: The Ash'ari position (tafwid — consigning the modality to Allah) vs. the Athari position (affirmation without asking how).
**Strength:** Systematic defense against heresy and philosophical doubt; defines orthodox boundaries.
**Limitation:** Can become abstract; sometimes prioritizes philosophical coherence over plain textual meaning.

### Classical Hanbali/Athari (Ibn Taymiyyah)

**Method:** Return to the salaf: affirm what Allah affirmed, deny what He denied, without metaphor (ta'wil) or denial (ta'til).
**Example:** On Allah's descent to the lowest heaven (hadith): Affirm the descent as befits His majesty; reject the question 'how?' as bid'ah.
**Strength:** Textual fidelity; rejects speculative philosophy.
**Limitation:** Can appear less intellectually satisfying to readers trained in philosophical theology.

## Synthesis

All three approaches serve tawhid: tafsir provides the **textual foundation**, theology provides the **systematic defense**, and the Athari approach provides the **boundary of orthodoxy**.
