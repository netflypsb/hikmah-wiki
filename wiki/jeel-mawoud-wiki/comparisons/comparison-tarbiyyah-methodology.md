# Tarbiyyah Methodology: Hilali vs Qaradawi

## Overview

This comparison examines how two major contemporary Sunni scholars approach **tarbiyyah** (التربية — Islamic education/nurturing). Dr. Majdi al-Hilali, in *al-Jīl al-Maw'ūd*, treats tarbiyyah as the generational instrument that produces the promised generation — intensely practical, attribute-focused, and centred on the Qur'an as the sole formative source. Dr. Yusuf al-Qaradawi, across his extensive corpus (*Approaching the Sunnah*, *Ethics in Islam*, *Fiqh al-Zakah*), treats tarbiyyah through the lens of **wasatiyyah** (moderation), **fiqh** (juristic understanding), and the balance between inner purification and outer legislation.

Though both scholars share the same Sunni foundation and the same ultimate concern — the formation of a faithful, capable Muslim personality — their emphasis, methodology, and practical prescriptions differ significantly.

---

## Hilali's Approach

### Practical and Prophetic

Al-Hilali's tarbiyyah is inseparable from the Prophetic model. He identifies the Prophet ﷺ as **al-murabbī** (the educator) — the second of three instruments that produced the first generation (the Qur'an being the first, the educational environment the third). The Prophet's tarbiyyah was:

- **Practical (ʿamalī):** He was a living model — "his character was the Quran" (Muslim, from ʿĀ'ishah, p. 151)
- **Gradual (tadarrujī):** He did not burden them beyond their capacity
- **Individualised (fardī):** He addressed each person according to their situation
- **Supervised (mushrif):** He watched for deviation and corrected it (p. 160)

> "With the great integrated methodology, there had to be a director and supervisor and follower of the course of the change process, to ensure the quality of the product and the goodness of the fruit." (p. 160)

### Attribute-Focused

Al-Hilali's entire book is structured around **ten attributes** of the promised generation (ikhlāṣ, maḥabbah, khawf, ʿibādah, tawāḍuʿ, zuhd, jihād, ṣabr, iʿtidāl, ukhuwwah) and the **five foundational knowledges** (maʿārif) from which these attributes naturally grow. Tarbiyyah is the process of cultivating these knowledges so the attributes emerge organically — not as artificial performance, but as natural fruit.

The governing principle: **al-muʿāmalah bi-qadr al-maʿrifah** — "treatment is proportionate to knowledge" (p. 99). The deeper the knowledge of Allah, the more sincere, loving, and fearful the believer becomes. Tarbiyyah is therefore not about external discipline but about internal transformation through deepened knowledge.

### Generation-Building

Al-Hilali's ultimate concern is not individual piety but **generational production**. The tarbiyyah he describes is the process that produces *al-jīl al-maw'ūd* — the promised generation that will carry the ummah to naṣr and tamkīn. This generation-building requires:

1. **Ṣafāʾ an-nabʿ (purity of the source):** The Qur'an alone as the sole formative source during the building period — "the servant devotes himself to it completely, giving it his time, his heart, and his mind… not mixing anything else with it — especially in the beginning" (p. 147)
2. **The bonded environment (al-waṭ/biʿah):** Dār al-Arqam, the mosque, the homes — "Let us have tarbiyyah circles in our homes" (p. 190)
3. **Ḥifẓ vs. ʿamal:** Action over memorisation — "Memorising its words is hard for us, but acting on it is easy" (Ibn Masʿūd, p. 156)

### The Qur'an as Methodology of Change

For al-Hilali, the Qur'an is not merely the content of tarbiyyah but its **methodology** (manhaj at-taghyīr). The Qur'an "takes on the task of introducing the servant to his Lord to the furthest extent that his mental capacity can bear" (p. 146) and "links that knowledge to the currents of life" (p. 146). The Sahabah did not speed-read or rote-memorise; they "read slowly, paused to reflect, wept at verses of warning, and rejoiced at verses of promise" (pp. 153–154).

## Qaradawi's Approach

### Wasatiyyah-Centered

Qaradawi's tarbiyyah is embedded in his overarching principle of **wasatiyyah** (وَسَطِيَّة — the middle way). The believer is part of "a middle nation" (*ummatan wasatan*, Q 2:143), charged with avoiding both the excess of over-strictness and the deficiency of laxity. The Prophetic model he most frequently invokes is the hadith of the three men who resolved extreme asceticism — and the Prophet's response: "Your body owns a right over you, your eyes own a right over you, your family own a right over you."

Wasatiyyah governs tarbiyyah by ensuring the educational process itself is moderate: neither spiritually lax nor obsessively intense.

### Fiqh-Oriented

Where al-Hilali organises tarbiyyah around attributes and knowledges, Qaradawi organises it around **fiqh** — juristic understanding. His works treat tarbiyyah as a function of proper religious understanding: knowing the halal from the haram, understanding the maqāṣid (objectives) of the Sharīʿah, and developing the capacity for ijtihād. The famous hadith "Whoever Allah wishes good for, He gives him fiqh in religion" (Bukhārī & Muslim) is a central text for Qaradawi's approach.

Tarbiyyah, in Qaradawi's framework, produces the **faqīh** — the person of understanding — not primarily the murābī (spiritual cultivator) or the jihādī (striver). The tarbiyyah process is therefore more intellectual and juristic than al-Hilali's heart-and-action model.

### Balance Between Inner and Outer

Qaradawi's tazkiyah concept is explicitly dual: **tazkiyat al-nafs** (purification of the soul) and **tazkiyat al-mal** (purification of wealth through zakat). This reflects his consistent pattern of pairing inner and outer dimensions. "Moral life is founded on knowledge and willpower" (*Ethics in Islam*) — tarbiyyah is active, knowledge-driven, and sustained by both worship and legislation.

The Sunnah, for Qaradawi, is "the agreed-upon source for the purification of the soul" — but he explicitly distances this from "the invented practices of later Sufi orders that lack textual basis." His tarbiyyah is Sunnah-grounded but fiqh-regulated.

## Key Differences

| Dimension | Al-Hilali (Jeel Mawoud) | Al-Qaradawi (Qaradawi Library) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary focus** | Generation-building — producing al-jīl al-maw'ūd | Individual and community formation through fiqh |
| **Organising principle** | Attributes growing from five maʿārif (knowledges) | Wasatiyyah — the middle way between extremes |
| **Qur'an's role** | Sole formative source (ṣafāʾ an-nabʿ) during building | Primary source, but alongside Sunnah, ijtihād, and scholarly tradition |
| **Prophet's role** | Al-murabbī — the living, supervising educator | Al-qudwah — the model of moderation |
| **Spirituality** | Heart-centred: knowledge → īmān → attributes → action | Mind-centred: knowledge → fiqh → wasatiyyah → action |
| **Tazkiyah** | Purification of the nafs from shuhh, ʿujb, hubb al-dunyā | Dual: purification of soul (Sunnah-grounded) and wealth (zakat) |
| **Ideal product** | The bonded generation carrying the ummah to naṣr and tamkīn | The faqīh — the person of juristic understanding and moral balance |
| **Attitude to other sources** | Strict exclusion during formative period (ʿUmar's scroll incident) | Open engagement with scholarly tradition, ijtihād, and contemporary realities |
| **Extremism warning** | Iʿtidāl as fruit of proper fiqh of the religion | Wasatiyyah as inherent characteristic of the Sunnah itself |
| **Practical expression** | Home-based tarbiyyah circles (ḥalāqāt) | Institutional: mosques, schools, zakat systems, fiqh councils |

## Shared Ground

Despite their differences, the two scholars share significant common ground:

1. **The Qur'an and Sunnah as the foundation.** Both insist that tarbiyyah must be grounded in the primary texts. Al-Hilali's insistence on the Qur'an as the sole formative source and Qaradawi's insistence on the Sunnah as "the agreed-upon source for the purification of the soul" are two expressions of the same commitment.

2. **The Prophetic model as indispensable.** Both cite the hadith of the three men who practised extreme asceticism and the Prophet's correction as a key text. Al-Hilali uses it to illustrate the Prophet's supervisory role (pp. 166–167, the story of ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn); Qaradawi uses it as the paradigmatic expression of wasatiyyah.

3. **Knowledge must produce action.** Al-Hilali: "treatment is proportionate to knowledge." Qaradawi: "moral life is founded on knowledge and willpower." Neither accepts passive or purely intellectual knowledge.

4. **Rejection of extremism.** Al-Hilali's iʿtidāl (moderation/balance) as a fruit of proper fiqh of the religion (pp. 139–140) parallels Qaradawi's wasatiyyah as a defining characteristic of the Sunnah. Both warn against the excess that sincere but misguided believers might fall into.

5. **Tazkiyah is essential.** Both treat purification of the self as a non-negotiable component of Islamic formation. Al-Hilali places it as the second of his three pivots; Qaradawi treats it as the inner dimension that must accompany all external practice.

6. **Community is essential.** Al-Hilali's insistence on the educational environment (Dār al-Arqam, the mosque, the homes) as the third instrument of change mirrors Qaradawi's consistent emphasis on the communal dimension of Islam — zakat as social purification, the ummah as the middle nation.

## Synthesis

The two approaches are not contradictory but **complementary**. Al-Hilali provides the intense, Qur'an-centred, generational-formation model that is urgently needed when the ummah is in a state of weakness and needs to produce a vanguard generation. Qaradawi provides the wasatiyyah-regulated, fiqh-grounded, institutionally-sustained model that is needed when the ummah has achieved a measure of stability and needs to maintain balance, govern wisely, and avoid the diseases of both extremism and complacency.

A synthesis would suggest:

- **During the formative/building period:** Al-Hilali's model of Qur'an-centred, attribute-focused, generation-building tarbiyyah — with its insistence on purity of the source and intensity of engagement — is the appropriate methodology. The first generation was formed this way.
- **During the consolidation/maintenance period:** Qaradawi's model of wasatiyyah-regulated, fiqh-oriented, institutionally-sustained tarbiyyah becomes essential to prevent the excesses that al-Hilali himself warns against (the diseases of luxury, oppression, and distance from Allah after tamkīn).
- **Both periods require:** The Prophetic model as the living standard, tazkiyah as the inner discipline, and community as the essential environment.

The promised generation, if it is truly to achieve naṣr and tamkīn, must first be built with al-Hilali's intensity and then sustained with Qaradawi's balance.
