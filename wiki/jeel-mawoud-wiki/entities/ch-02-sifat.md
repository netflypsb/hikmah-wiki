## Summary

Chapter 2 is the book's longest and most substantive chapter, presenting the ten attributes (صفات) that the Quranic evidence identifies as essential for the promised generation. The author begins by establishing that these attributes are not theoretical or utopian — they are real, practical qualities that a generation can actualise, as proven by the Ṣaḥābah who were further from Allah in their pre-Islamic state than Muslims today yet transformed themselves and received divine tamkīn within a few years (p. 24–25). The chapter then demonstrates through multiple Quranic verses (24:55, 5:54–56, 8:60, 14:13–14, 21:105–106, 7:137, 32:24, 11:112–113, 9:38–39) that Allah linked His promise of victory to specific attributes, and conversely that abandoning these attributes leads to defeat and loss (p. 28–32). The ten attributes are: (1) Ikhlāṣ (sincerity to Allah), (2) Maḥabbat Allāh (love of Allah), (3) Khawf Allāh (fear of Allah), (4) ʿIbādah (worship), (5) Tawāḍuʿ (humility), (6) Zuhd fī al-Dunyā (asceticism toward the world), (7) Jihād fī Sabīl Allāh (striving in Allah's path), (8) Ṣabr wa Thabāt (patience and steadfastness), (9) Iʿtidāl wa Tawāzun (moderation and balance), (10) Muwālāt wa Tā'khī (loyalty and brotherhood). Each attribute is expounded with Quranic evidence, prophetic hadith, companion narratives, and practical signs. The chapter also distinguishes between individual attributes (applicable to each person) and collective attributes (requiring the generation's mutual cooperation), and clarifies that these ten are not exhaustive — they are the core attributes whose fulfilment naturally produces other virtuous qualities (p. 33–35).

## Key Arguments

- The attributes of the promised generation are not theoretical but practical and achievable — the Ṣaḥābah proved this (p. 24–25).
- When a generation fulfils these attributes, Allah grants it tamkīn; when subsequent generations abandon them, loss and decline follow (p. 26).
- The pattern of Banī Isrā'īl confirms this: when they fulfilled the conditions, Allah granted them victory (7:137); when they deviated into arrogance, oppression, and shirk, they earned humiliation (3:112) (p. 26–27).
- Honour before Allah is proportional to istiqāmah (uprightness) and taqwā; sustained honour requires sustained istiqāmah (p. 27, citing 13:37, 18:49).
- These attributes must exist in a bonded generation, not merely scattered individuals (p. 28).
- The ten attributes are derived from the Quranic verses that link divine promise to specific qualities (p. 28–32).
- Some attributes are individual (ikhlāṣ, love, fear) and some are collective (jihad, brotherhood, enjoining good) — the Quran addresses the collective with plural forms (p. 33–34).
- The ten attributes are not exhaustive; they form the core from which other virtues naturally flow (p. 34–35).

### Attribute 1: Ikhlāṣ (Sincerity/Devotion to Allah)
- 24:55 links the promise of tamkīn to worshipping Allah without associating anything with Him — the word "شَيْئًا" (anything) extends shirk from the narrow to the broad circle (p. 36).
- Shirk al-Tawajjuh (shirk of intention): directing actions partly to Allah and partly to people or worldly gain (p. 36–37).
- Shirk al-Istiʿānah (shirk of seeking aid): relying on oneself or one's abilities instead of Allah — ʿujb (self-admiration) is one of the most dangerous forms (p. 37–38).
- Hidden shirk (riyā' and ʿujb) invalidates deeds (2:264) and incurs Allah's wrath (p. 38–39).
- The Battle of Ḥunayn illustrates how ʿujb led to near-defeat despite numerical superiority (9:25) (p. 39).
- True ikhlāṣ produces visible signs: not seeking praise, hiding one's deeds, fearing fame, equal treatment in front/behind roles, equanimity toward praise and blame, helping others secretly, maintaining private deeds between oneself and Allah, frequent istighfār after good deeds, struggling against ʿujb, and never considering oneself truly sincere (p. 40–42).

### Attribute 2: Maḥabbat Allāh (Love of Allah)
- 5:54 describes the generation Allah loves and who love Him — humble toward believers, mighty against disbelievers, striving in Allah's way (p. 29, 43–44).
- Love of Allah is the primary motive for adherence to religion and obedience (p. 44–45).
- The Ṣaḥābah's love for Allah and the Prophet surpassed all worldly attachments (p. 45–47, 55–57).
- Love of the Prophet is inseparable from love of Allah (p. 47–55).

### Attribute 3: Khawf Allāh (Fear of Allah)
- 14:13–14 and 7:128 identify fear of Allah as a condition for tamkīn (p. 30, 57).
- The required fear is not fleeting emotion but a constant, active fear that drives worship, taqwā, waraʿ, and hastening toward good (23:57–61, 22:32) (p. 58).
- Fear of Allah plays a major role in the believer's istiqāmah and entry into divine wilāyah (3:120) (p. 58).
- The Ṣaḥābah displayed extraordinary fear: Abū Bakr wished to be a tree eaten and digested; ʿUmar wished he had all the earth's gold to ransom himself; ʿUthmān preferred to be ashes; Abū ʿUbaydah wished to be a slaughtered sheep; Ibn ʿAbbās wept until his eyes were like worn-out water-skins; Abū Dharr wished he was a tree; ʿĀ'ishah wished she had never been created (p. 59–60).

### Attribute 4: ʿIbādah (Worship)
- 21:105–106 — the earth inherits those who are ʿābidūn (devoted worshippers) and ṣāliḥūn (p. 30–31, 61).
- The promised generation is a generation of abundant worship: frequent dhikr, prayer, fasting, charity, duʿā', night prayer (p. 61).
- ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib described the Ṣaḥābah as dishevelled, dusty, with marks like goats' knees from prostration, spending the night in qiyām and rukūʿ, weeping until their clothes were soaked (p. 61–62).
- Ibn Masʿūd emphasised the obligation of congregational prayer — "only a known hypocrite" would abandon it (p. 62).
- They were described as "monks by night, warriors by day" (p. 63).
- Hind (wife of Abū Sufyān) embraced Islam after seeing the Muslims' worship in the mosque (p. 63).

### Attribute 5: Tawāḍuʿ (Humility)
- 5:54 describes the promised generation as "أَذِلَّةٍ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ" (humble toward believers) (p. 29, 64).
- True humility is seeing oneself as small — a heart-state that manifests in behaviour toward Allah, oneself, and people (p. 64–65).
- With Allah: recognising one's utter dependence and servitude regardless of wealth, health, or status (p. 65).
- With oneself: self-diminishment, as Mūsā asked for his brother's support despite being qualified (28:34) (p. 65).
- With people: seeing others as better, not disdaining service, sitting with the poor, answering the weak's invitations (p. 66).
- Ṣaḥābah examples: Abū Bakr continued milking his neighbour's sheep as caliph; ʿUmar entered Jerusalem alternating riding with his servant and waded through mud holding the camel's reins; ʿUthmān was seen sleeping in the mosque in a blanket; ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf was indistinguishable from servants; Salmān al-Fārisī as governor carried goods for a man who mistook him for a porter (p. 66–68).

### Attribute 6: Zuhd fī al-Dunyā (Asceticism Toward the World)
- 9:38–39 warns that attachment to the world leads to expulsion from divine wilāyah (p. 31–32, 69).
- Zuhd is turning desire away from the world — the Prophet said: "What have I to do with the world!" (Aḥmad, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ 5669) (p. 69).
- Love of the world is the primary obstacle to entering divine wilāyah — the ummah's weakness stems from loving the world and hating death (Aḥmad, Abū Dāwūd, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ 8183) (p. 69).
- The promised generation's ambitions are not worldly (wealth, position, real estate) but otherworldly — Paradise, seeing the Lord, martyrdom (p. 70–71).
- The generation must not be weighed down by worldly attachments — no bargaining or compromise on matters of faith (p. 71).
- The Prophet ﷺ set conditions for his army: no one should join who has an unfinished building, an unbred animal, or a pregnant wife (muttafaq ʿalayh) (p. 72).
- Family can be an obstacle if not sharing the same vision — Quran: "Indeed among your spouses and children are enemies" (64:14); the Prophet ﷺ said: "The child is the fruit of the heart and a source of cowardice, miserliness, and grief" (Aḥmad, Tirmidhī, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ 7214) (p. 73).
- Wives of the promised generation must share its mission and not demand worldly luxuries (p. 73).
- Ṣaḥābah examples: Ibn Masʿūd said they were fewer in fasting and prayer than later generations but more ascetic and more desirous of the Hereafter (p. 73–74); ʿUmar wept when he saw the wealth of the Qādisiyyah spoils; Salmān's possessions were only a mithara and an injājah (p. 74); Abū Ayyūb explained that "throwing yourselves into destruction" (2:195) meant staying with family and wealth and abandoning jihad (p. 75); Ṣuhayb abandoned all his wealth to the Quraysh in exchange for safe passage (p. 75–76); Saʿīd ibn ʿĀmir gave away all 1,000 dinars sent by ʿUmar, calling the money "the fitnah that entered upon me" (p. 76–77).

### Attribute 7: Jihād fī Sabīl Allāh (Striving in Allah's Path)
- 5:54 — "يُجَاهِدُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَلَا يَخَافُونَ لَوْمَةَ لَائِمٍ" (p. 29, 78).
- The promised generation has a clear goal (khilāfah and global leadership) and dedicates all its energy to achieving it (p. 78).
- It does not withhold any effort; it knows that establishing the religion requires human effort before divine support comes (47:7) (p. 78).
- The generation works day and night, sacrifices comfort, and endures hardship (p. 78–79).
- It is prepared to sacrifice wealth, position, family, comfort, and ultimately life itself (p. 79).
- Ṣaḥābah examples: Abū Mūsā described their food as two black things (dates and water) and clothing as wool (Tabarānī) (p. 80); Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn described a companion going three days without food, eating a charred skin, and binding a stone to his stomach (Ibn Abī al-Dunyā) (p. 80); Rabiʿī ibn ʿĀmir told Rustum: "Allah sent us to bring people from worshipping servants to worshipping Allah, from the constriction of the world to its expansiveness, from the injustice of religions to the justice of Islam" (p. 80–81); Ḥirām ibn Milhān said "Allāhu Akbar!" when fatally stabbed — "I have won, by the Lord of the Kaʿbah!" (p. 81); ʿUmayr ibn al-Ḥumām said "kh-kh" at Badr, eager for Paradise (p. 81–82); ʿAuf ibn al-Ḥārith threw off his armour and fought until killed (p. 82); Jaʿfar ibn Abī Ṭālib at Mu'tah recited: "How sweet is Paradise and near its drink..." (p. 82).

### Attribute 8: Ṣabr wa Thabāt (Patience and Steadfastness)
- 7:137, 32:24 — patience and steadfastness are among the most important attributes of the victorious group (p. 31, 83).
- The path to tamkīn is not easy — it is long, thorny, full of obstacles, with human and jinn devils trying to stop the march (p. 83).
- The promised generation knows the nature of the path and resolves to proceed — either to die as mujāhidīn or reach the goal (33:23) (p. 83).
- The path of daʿwah is one: "Do you think you will enter Paradise without the example of those before you?" (2:214) (p. 84).
- Al-Ṣalābī said: "Ibtilā' is tightly bound to tamkīn — Allah does not give tamkīn to a nation until it passes through the crucible of trials" (p. 84).
- Imam al-Shāfiʿī said: "No one is given tamkīn until he is tested" (p. 84).
- Allah tests believers to distinguish the truthful: "We will test you until We know the mujāhidīn and the patient among you" (47:31) (p. 85).
- Ṣaḥābah examples: Bilāl under torture — "Aḥad! Aḥad!" (p. 85–86); the family of Yāsir — the Prophet ﷺ said: "Patience, O family of Yāsir, Allah has forgiven you" (Ḥākim, Ibn ʿAsākir) (p. 86); Khabbāb ibn al-Aratt complained at the Kaʿbah — the Prophet ﷺ told of previous nations combed with iron combs (Bukhārī) (p. 86–87); at al-Aḥzāb the believers saw the trial as Allah's promise and it increased their īmān (33:22) (p. 87).

### Attribute 9: Iʿtidāl wa Tawāzun (Moderation and Balance)
- 11:112–113 — "Be steadfast as you are commanded" — al-ṭughyān represents extremism/tashaddud, al-rukūn represents laxity/takhyīl, and istiqāmah is the middle path; without it, believers exit divine wilāyah (p. 31, 87–88).
- 2:143 — "We made you a middle nation" (p. 31).
- The promised generation is balanced in all matters — worship, dealings, understanding, movement (p. 88).
- It balances self-reform, family duties, and societal obligations (p. 88).
- It is not isolated from the world — it knows its time, uses modern tools and knowledge, and is intellectually cultured (p. 88).
- It focuses on building the truth rather than destroying falsehood — the Prophet ﷺ did not destroy 360 idols around the Kaʿbah but built tawḥīd in hearts until the idols were naturally removed (p. 89, citing 11:121–123).
- Ṣaḥābah examples: Abū Bakr advised ʿUmar: "Allah's right by day is not accepted at night, and by night not accepted by day — voluntary deeds are not accepted until obligatory ones are performed" (p. 90); ʿUthmān shortened prayer in Ḥajj to avoid people thinking it was always two rakʿahs (p. 90–91); Abū Hurayrah preferred one night of ribāṭ to Laylat al-Qadr at the Black Stone (Ibn Taymiyyah) (p. 91).

### Attribute 10: Muwālāt wa Tā'khī (Loyalty and Brotherhood)
- 5:54–56 — "Your walī is Allah, His Messenger, and the believers" (p. 29, 92).
- The promised generation is not scattered individuals but a bonded, brotherly generation like "a solid structure" (61:4) (p. 92).
- Brotherhood protects against fitnah and westernisation, aids steadfastness, enables mutual encouragement in good and patience (p. 92).
- The Prophet ﷺ established the Mu'ākhāt (brotherhood) between Muhājirūn and Anṣār — 70 men, sharing wealth and even inheritance until Badr (8:75) (p. 92–93).
- Anṣār's iythār (preferring others over oneself): Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ offered ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf half his wealth and one of his wives (Bukhārī) (p. 93–94).
- The Anṣār preferred the Muhājirūn over themselves despite their own need (59:9) — al-Qurṭubī narrated that when Banī al-Naḍīr spoils came, the Prophet offered them a share, but the Anṣār said they were content with the Muhājirūn remaining in their homes (p. 94–95).
- A sheep's head was passed among seven companions because each preferred his brother over himself (Ibn ʿUmar, Iḥyā' ʿUlūm al-Dīn) (p. 95).

## Sahabah Examples

### Attribute 1 — Ikhlāṣ
- **Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī:** Disliked that any deed of his became known (Muslim, Ghazwat Dhāt al-Riqāʿ) (p. 42–43).
- **Muʿādh ibn Jabal:** Wept at the Prophet's grave — the hadith: "The slightest riyā' is shirk; Allah loves the hidden righteous ones who, when absent, are not missed, and when present, are not known" (al-Ṭabarānī, al-Ḥākim) (p. 43).
- **ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb:** Carried water to an Ansārī woman's quarters to break his own pride after delegations came pledging obedience (p. 43).
- **Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq:** Sent a letter to Khālid ibn al-Walīd warning against ʿujb (self-admiration) (p. 43).
- **Abū ʿUbaydah ibn al-Jarrāḥ:** Vowed never to lead prayer again after noticing self-preference during prayer (p. 43–44).
- Fear of nifāq: 30 companions feared hypocrisy for themselves; ʿUmar asked Ḥudhayfah if the Prophet named him among the hypocrites (p. 44).

### Attribute 2 — Maḥabbat Allāh
- **Musʿab ibn ʿUmayr:** Left Meccan luxury for Allah's pleasure (p. 47).
- **Khubayb ibn ʿAdī:** When about to be killed, refused to trade places with Muḥammad ﷺ (p. 47).
- **Ḥanzalah:** Washed by angels after martyring while junub — rushed to jihad immediately upon hearing the call (p. 47–55).
- **ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb at Uḥud:** Told his brother "Take my shield" — brother replied: "I want martyrdom as you do" — they both abandoned their shields (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/32) (p. 56).
- **ʿAbd Allāh ibn Jaḥsh:** Duʿā' before Uḥud: "O Allah, I beseech You to let me meet the enemy tomorrow, let them kill me, slit my belly, cut off my nose and ear, and when You ask me why, I say: for You" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/389) (p. 56).
- **Muʿādh ibn Jabal:** When dying of plague, his neck throbbed and he said: "Choke me, for by Your might You know my heart loves You" (Ibn Qudāmah, al-Raqā'iq 252) (p. 56–57).

### Attribute 3 — Khawf Allāh
- **Abū Bakr:** "I wish I were a tree eaten and digested"; held his tongue saying "This is what brought me to my end"; wept frequently (Ibn al-Qayyim, al-Dā' wa al-Dawā' 80–84) (p. 59).
- **ʿUmar:** "If I had all the earth's gold I would ransom myself from Allah's punishment before seeing it"; last words: "Woe to me and woe to my mother if Allah does not forgive me" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 2/372–373) (p. 59).
- **ʿUthmān:** "If I were between Paradise and the Fire, not knowing to which I am commanded, I would prefer to be ashes before knowing" (p. 59).
- **Abū ʿUbaydah:** "I wish I were a sheep slaughtered by my family who eat my flesh and drink my broth" (p. 59).
- **Ibn ʿAbbās:** His lower eyes were like worn-out water-skins from weeping (p. 59).
- **Abū Dharr:** "I wish I were a tree eaten and digested; I wish I had not been created" (p. 59).
- **Ibn Masʿūd:** A man wished to be among the People of the Right; Ibn Masʿūd said: "Here is a man who wishes he had died and not been resurrected" — meaning himself (p. 60).
- **Shaddād ibn Aws:** When entering his bed, sleep would not come — he said: "O Allah, the Fire has taken my sleep" — and he would pray until dawn (p. 60).
- **ʿĀ'ishah:** "By Allah, I wish I were a tree; by Allah I wish I were a pebble; by Allah I wish Allah had not created me at all." When Ibn ʿAbbās praised her on her deathbed, she said: "Leave me; by the One in Whose hand is my soul, I wish I were forgotten and forgotten" (p. 60).

### Attribute 4 — ʿIbādah
- **ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib's description:** Companions were dishevelled, dusty, with marks on their foreheads like goats' knees; they spent the night in sujūd and qiyām, reciting the Quran, weeping until clothes were soaked (Salāḥ al-Ummah 4/195) (p. 61–62).
- **Ibn Masʿūd on congregational prayer:** "Whoever is pleased to meet Allah tomorrow as a Muslim, let him guard these prayers… only a known hypocrite stayed away" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 3/39–40) (p. 62).
- **Ibn ʿUmar:** "If we missed a man at Fajr and ʿIshā', we would assume the worst about him" (p. 62).
- They were "monks by night, warriors by day" — Abū Sufyān's wife embraced Islam after seeing their worship (Rahbān al-Layl 1/310) (p. 63).

### Attribute 5 — Tawāḍuʿ
- **Abū Bakr:** Continued milking neighbour's sheep as caliph; said in his first speech: "I have been placed over you and I am not the best of you" (Siyar al-Salaf 1/83) (p. 66).
- **ʿUmar:** Entered Jerusalem alternating riding with his servant, wading through mud; said: "Islam gave us honour; we care not for people's contempt" (Tanbīh al-Ghāfilīn 141; Ṣalāḥ al-Ummah 5/433) (p. 67).
- **ʿUmar at death:** Told his son ʿAbd Allāh: "Put my cheek on the ground, perhaps Allah will have mercy on me" (Ibn al-Mubārak, al-Zuhd 147) (p. 67).
- **ʿUthmān:** Seen riding a mule with his servant walking behind; seen sleeping in the mosque in a blanket (Imam Aḥmad, al-Zuhd 127) (p. 67).
- **Abū Hurayrah as amīr of Bahrain:** Rode a donkey and said "Make way for the amīr" (Tanbīh al-Ghāfilīn 142) (p. 68).
- **ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf:** Could not be distinguished from his servants due to his humility (al-Tawāḍuʿ wa al-Khumūl 170) (p. 68).
- **Salmān al-Fārisī as governor of al-Madā'in:** A noble asked him to carry a purchase, thinking he was a porter; Salmān carried it without protest (Tanbīh al-Ghāfilīn 142) (p. 68).

### Attribute 6 — Zuhd
- **Ibn Masʿūd:** Told the Tābiʿūn: "You fast and pray more than the companions, but they were more ascetic in the world and more desirous of the Hereafter" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 2/96) (p. 73–74).
- **ʿUmar at Qādisiyyah spoils:** Wept and said: "This was never given to a people except that it bred enmity and hatred among them" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 2/97) (p. 74).
- **Salmān on his deathbed:** Saʿd ibn Abī Waqqāṣ visited him and wept; Salmān said: "The Prophet ﷺ made a pact with us: 'Let each of you have provisions like a rider'" — and his possessions were only a mithara and an injājah (water containers) (p. 74).
- **Abū Ayyūb al-Anṣārī's tafsīr of 2:195:** "Destruction" (al-tahlukah) is staying with family and wealth and abandoning jihad (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/360) (p. 75).
- **Ṣuhayb al-Rūmī:** When Quraysh tried to bargain — give up migration or leave his wealth — he said: "What if I give you my wealth, will you let me go?" They agreed; the Prophet ﷺ said: "Ṣuhayb has profited! Ṣuhayb has profited!" (2:207 was revealed about him) (Ibn Kathir 1/216) (p. 75–76).
- **Saʿīd ibn ʿĀmir:** ʿUmar sent 1,000 dinars; Saʿīd called it "fitnah entering upon me" and gave it all away to the army (Ibn Qudāmah 258–259) (p. 76–77).

### Attribute 7 — Jihād
- **Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī:** "If you saw us with our Prophet, you would think our smell was the smell of sheep — we wore wool and ate the two black things: dates and water" (al-Ṭabarānī) (p. 80).
- **Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn:** "A companion would go three days without food, take a skin, char it, and eat it; if he found nothing, he would take a stone and press it against his stomach" (Ibn Abī al-Dunyā) (p. 80).
- **Rabiʿī ibn ʿĀmir to Rustum:** "Allah sent us to bring people from worshipping servants to worshipping Allah, from the constriction of the world to its expansiveness, from the injustice of religions to the justice of Islam" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/167) (p. 80–81).
- **Ḥirām ibn Milhān at Bi'r Maʿūnah:** Fatally stabbed, he said "Allāhu Akbar! I have won by the Lord of the Kaʿbah!" (p. 81).
- **ʿUmayr ibn al-Ḥumām at Badr:** When the Prophet ﷺ said "Rise to a Paradise whose width is the heavens and earth," ʿUmayr said "kh-kh"; when asked why, he said "By Allah, I only hope to be among its people." The Prophet ﷺ said: "You are among its people." He threw away his dates and fought until killed (al-Raḥīq al-Makhtūm 242) (p. 81–82).
- **ʿAuf ibn al-Ḥārith:** Asked the Prophet ﷺ what makes the Lord laugh at His servant; the Prophet ﷺ said: "Plunging his hand into the enemy bare-headed." ʿAuf threw off his armour and fought until killed (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/407) (p. 82).
- **Jaʿfar ibn Abī Ṭālib at Mu'tah:** Recited: "How sweet is Paradise and near its drink, cool its shade; the Romans have come near, their punishment far — upon me if I meet them I will strike them" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/407) (p. 82).

### Attribute 8 — Ṣabr wa Thabāt
- **Bilāl ibn Rabāḥ under torture:** Dragged out at midday, thrown on his back, a great stone placed on his chest, told "You will stay until you die or disbelieve" — he said: "Aḥad! Aḥad!" (Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah 1/221, from Ḥilyat al-Awliyā') (p. 85–86).
- **Family of Yāsir:** ʿAmmār, his father, and his mother tortured in the sun to make them leave Islam; the Prophet ﷺ said: "Patience, O family of Yāsir; Allah has forgiven you" (Ḥākim, Ibn ʿAsākir, Ibn Saʿd) (p. 86).
- **Khabbāb ibn al-Aratt:** Complained at the Kaʿbah; the Prophet ﷺ said: "Among those before you, a man would be combed with iron combs and not turn from his religion… but you are impatient" (Bukhārī) (p. 86–87).

### Attribute 9 — Iʿtidāl wa Tawāzun
- **Abū Bakr's advice to ʿUmar:** "Allah's right by day is not accepted at night, and by night not accepted by day; voluntary deeds are not accepted until obligatory ones are performed" (Ibn al-Mubārak, al-Zuhd 119) (p. 90).
- **ʿUthmān ibn ʿAffān:** Shortened prayer in Ḥajj to prevent people thinking it was always two rakʿahs (p. 90–91).
- **Abū Hurayrah:** Preferred one night of ribāṭ to Laylat al-Qadr at the Black Stone (Ibn Taymiyyah, Majmūʿ al-Fatāwā 28/6) (p. 91).

### Attribute 10 — Muwālāt wa Tā'khī
- **Mu'ākhāt between Muhājirūn and Anṣār:** 70 men — half Muhājirūn, half Anṣār — sharing wealth and inheritance until Badr (8:75) (Zād al-Maʿād 2/56; Fiqh al-Sīrah 179–180) (p. 92–93).
- **Saʿd ibn al-Rabīʿ and ʿAbd al-Raḥmān ibn ʿAwf (Bukhārī):** Saʿd offered half his wealth and one of his two wives; ʿAbd al-Raḥmān said: "May Allah bless your family and wealth — where is your market?" (p. 93–94).
- **Anṣār's Iythār (59:9):** When Banī al-Naḍīr spoils came, the Prophet offered them a share; Anṣār said they were content with the Muhājirūn remaining in their homes. The Prophet ﷺ said: "O Allah, have mercy on the Anṣār and the children of the Anṣār" (al-Qurṭubī 18/17–19) (p. 94–95).
- **Seven companions passing a sheep's head:** Each preferred his brother over himself and the head returned to the first after circulating among seven (Ibn ʿUmar, Iḥyā' ʿUlūm al-Dīn 2/271) (p. 95).
