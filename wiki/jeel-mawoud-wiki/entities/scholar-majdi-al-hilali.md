# Dr. Majdi al-Hilali (مجدي الهلالي)

## Biography

Dr. Majdi al-Hilali (مجدي الهلالي) is a contemporary Egyptian Islamic caller (داعية), writer, and medical laboratory physician (طبيب تحاليل طبية). Born in 1961 in Egypt, he has become one of the most prolific authors in the field of Islamic tarbiyyah (تربية) and iman-building (بناء الإيمان), with over 46 published works to his name.

Al-Hilali was active in student Islamic work (العمل الطلابي) during his university years, where he developed his commitment to da'wah and organized Islamic activism. After completing his medical education as a laboratory specialist, he practiced his profession while increasingly devoting himself to writing and calling to Islam.

A transformative period in his intellectual life came during his residence in al-Madinah al-Munawwarah (المدينة المنورة), Saudi Arabia, from 1994 to 2005. He has described the blessed proximity to the Prophet's Mosque (الجوار الطيب المبارك) as having greatly assisted him in his authorship and spiritual development. During and after this period, he produced the bulk of his literary output.

Upon returning to Egypt, he founded the Mu'assasat al-Sahabi 'Uqbah bin 'Amir (مؤسسة الصحابي عقبة بن عامر), an institution dedicated to Qur'anic tarbiyyah for individuals (التربية القرآنية للأفراد). He currently resides in Cairo, where he continues his work in da'wah and tarbiyyah.

He has participated in numerous local and international seminars and conferences, and his lectures, sermons, and articles appear across various print and electronic media platforms.

## Intellectual Methodology

Dr. al-Hilali's intellectual methodology is distinguished by several key characteristics:

1. **Tarbiyyah-Centered Approach (منهج تربوي محوري):** His works center on the individual's inner transformation through systematic tarbiyyah rather than political or structural change alone. He sees the building of the faithful individual as the prerequisite for any collective Islamic renaissance.

2. **Practical Iman-Building (بناء الإيمان العملي):** Unlike theoretical or creed-only treatments of faith, al-Hilali's books provide actionable programs for strengthening iman — including daily practices, self-assessment tools, and step-by-step spiritual regimens. Works like *الإيمان أولاً فكيف نبدأ به؟* and *كيف نحب الله ونشتاق إليه* exemplify this hands-on approach.

3. **Youth-Oriented Discourse (خطاب شبابي):** His writing style is accessible and direct, targeting young Muslims in particular. *واجبات الشاب المسلم* (Duties of the Muslim Youth) and *رسالة إلى أخي في الله* (A Message to My Brother in Faith) reflect his conviction that youth are the primary agents of Islamic renewal.

4. **Anti-Despair Framework (مكافحة اليأس):** A consistent theme across his works is the rejection of despair (القنوط) and psychological defeatism. *ظاهرة الضعف النفسي وسبل علاجها* directly addresses psychological weakness, while *الطوفان قادم* and *أين المخرج؟* confront existential crises facing the ummah with a message of hope rooted in divine promise.

5. **Qur'anic Centrality (مركزية القرآن):** Al-Hilali repeatedly returns to the Qur'an as the primary instrument of personal and civilizational change. *إنه القرآن سر نهضتنا*, *غربة القرآن*, *العودة إلى القرآن*, and *تحقيق الوصال بين القلب والقرآن* form a cluster of works arguing that the ummah's revival depends on restoring the Qur'an to the center of Muslim life.

6. **Tazkiyah as Forgotten Tarbiyyah (التزكية التربية المنسية):** In *التزكية التربية المنسية*, he argues that self-purification (تزكية النفس) — once central to Islamic education — has been neglected, and its revival is essential for producing the generation promised victory.

## Distinctive Contributions

### 1. The "Promised Generation" Paradigm (نموذج الجيل الموعود)

Al-Hilali's most distinctive contribution is the articulation of the "promised generation" concept — the idea that Islamic victory and empowerment (النصر والتمكين) are not random historical events but follow from the cultivation of a generation possessing specific Qur'anic attributes. In *الجيل الموعود بالنصر والتمكين*, he systematically identifies these attributes from the Qur'an and Sunnah, arguing that the ummah's crisis is fundamentally a crisis of the individual, and that collective transformation requires the sequential building of qualified persons. This paradigm bridges the gap between eschatological hope and practical tarbiyyah.

### 2. Practical Iman-Building Programs

While many scholars describe the reality of iman theoretically, al-Hilali operationalizes it. His books offer step-by-step programs for building, renewing, and protecting iman. *الإيمان أولاً* asks "how do we begin?" and provides concrete answers. *كيف نحب الله ونشتاق إليه* treats mahabbah (love of Allah) and shawq (yearning) not as rare mystical states but as achievable goals with identifiable means. This practical orientation distinguishes him from both purely academic theologians and purely emotional preachers.

### 3. Psychological and Spiritual Integration

As a medical doctor, al-Hilali brings a diagnostic sensibility to spiritual ailments. *ظاهرة الضعف النفسي وسبل علاجها* treats psychological weakness as both a medical and spiritual phenomenon, integrating Qur'anic remedies with practical psychological insight. This integration of medical awareness with Islamic spirituality is a hallmark of his writing.

### 4. Da'wah Self-Criticism

Works like *مهلا أخي الداعية*, *صور مرفوضة*, and *طريق الإصلاح* demonstrate a rare willingness among Islamic writers to critique da'wah methodology from within. Al-Hilali warns against common pitfalls — rigidity, lack of wisdom, poor prioritization — and calls for a da'wah grounded in the fiqh of priorities (فقه الأولويات), echoing the broader Islamist intellectual tradition of Yusuf al-Qaradawi and others.

### 5. Qur'anic Tarbiyyah Institution-Building

Through the Mu'assasat al-Sahabi 'Uqbah bin 'Amir, al-Hilali has translated his literary output into an institutional framework for individual Qur'anic tarbiyyah, making his methodology accessible through structured programs rather than books alone.
