## Summary

The introduction establishes the central thesis of the book: Allah has promised a group of believing servants that He will grant them inheritance of the earth and establish them in it (7:128, 24:55). This divine promise is not bound to a specific time or place but is conditional upon the fulfilment of specific attributes and qualities. When any generation, in any era or land, actualises these conditions, Allah's promise will inevitably be realised for them. The author highlights that the generation of the Ṣaḥābah (رضوان الله عليهم) fulfilled these conditions and received Allah's swift and generous reward — inheriting the earth, gaining authority, and mastering it within a few short years (9:111). Given the current state of the ummah — enveloped in darkness, traversing a dark tunnel, and bleeding from a wounded body — the author calls for a concerted effort to raise the promised generation, to sacrifice in forming it, and to study how the Ṣaḥābah completed the requirements of divine victory so that we may emulate them (p. 7).

## Key Arguments

- Allah has promised a group of believers inheritance of the earth, but He did not specify a time or place — rather, He specified conditions and attributes that, when fulfilled by any generation, guarantee the realisation of His promise (p. 5–6).
- Merely making effort and movement for Islam here and there is insufficient to restore Islam's glory unless that effort includes working to complete the attributes of the promised generation (p. 6).
- Allah's promise does not fail — it awaits those who fulfil its conditions (p. 6, citing 21:105).
- History testifies that the Ṣaḥābah generation fulfilled these conditions and received Allah's immediate and generous fulfilment, inheriting the earth and gaining mastery within a few years (p. 6–7).
- The ummah today lives through a critical phase — dark night, dark tunnel, bleeding body — making the need for the promised generation urgent (p. 7).
- The discussion in this book revolves around the promised generation — its attributes, how to realise them, and how to emulate the first generation that achieved them (p. 7).
