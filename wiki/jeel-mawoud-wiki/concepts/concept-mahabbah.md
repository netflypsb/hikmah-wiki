# محبة الله — Maḥabbatullāh: Love of Allah

## Definition

Maḥabbah (محبة) derives from the Arabic root ح-ب-ب (ḥ-b-b), meaning "to love, to cherish, to hold dear." Maḥabbatullāh — love of Allah — is not a mere emotional sentiment but a comprehensive orientation of the heart that dominates all other attachments. It is a love that so governs the believer's inner being that Allah becomes dearer to him than everything else: {وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَشَدُّ حُبًّا لِلَّهِ} (Al-Baqarah:165). This love manifests in obedience, haste to please the Beloved, yearning for intimacy with Him, and preferring what He loves over all competing desires.

Al-Hilali presents maḥabbah as the **second attribute** of the promised generation, arguing that the love Allah requires is not the generalized affection found in all Muslims' hearts, but a dominating, comprehensive love that takes sovereign command of the heart — the kind of love the Companions possessed and that made them sacrifice everything for Allah's sake.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Verse of the Beloved Generation

Al-Hilali anchors his treatment in Al-Ma'idah:54:

> {يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مَن يَرْتَدَّ مِنكُمْ عَن دِينِهِ فَسَوْفَ يَأْتِي اللَّهُ بِقَوْمٍ يُحِبُّهُمْ وَيُحِبُّونَهُ أَذِلَّةٍ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَعِزَّةٍ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ يُجَاهِدُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَلَا يَخَافُونَ لَوْمَةَ لَائِمٍ} (Al-Ma'idah:54)

From this verse, he extracts the attributes of Allah's beloved generation:
1. **Love of Allah** (first and foremost)
2. **Humility** (tawadu')
3. **Continuous jihad**
4. **Worship** (establishing prayer, paying zakah)
5. **Alliance and brotherhood** (muwalah and ukhuwwah) (pp29, 45)

### Not Just Any Love

Al-Hilali directly addresses the objection that all Muslims love Allah (p46): "Yes, there is love of Allah in hearts, but the love that He — Glorified be He — wants from the promised generation is a love that dominates the heart and takes control of it, until Allah becomes dearer to him than everything." He cites: {وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا أَشَدُّ حُبًّا لِلَّهِ} (Al-Baqarah:165) (p46).

### Twelve Signs of True Love (علامات الحب الصادق)

Al-Hilali provides an extensive treatment of the signs of sincere love, each illustrated with Quranic or prophetic evidence (pp46–53):

#### 1. Obedience to the Beloved and Working for His Pleasure
The lover of Allah rushes and hastens to attain His pleasure. He cites Musa (AS): {وَعَجِلْتُ إِلَيْكَ رَبِّ لِتَرْضَى} (Ta-Ha:84). The true lover spares nothing in pleasing his Lord: {وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْرِي نَفْسَهُ ابْتِغَاءَ مَرْضَاتِ اللَّهِ} (Al-Baqarah:207) (pp46–47).

#### 2. Haste in Repentance
When the true lover falls into sin or falls short in an obligation, he rushes in repentance, seeking forgiveness, making excuses, and weeping, fearing his Lord's anger. He cites the Prophet's du'a at Ta'if: "...If You are not angry with me, I care not... I seek refuge in the light of Your Face... You are the one to be pleased until You are pleased, and there is no power and no strength except in You" (pp46–47).

#### 3. Gratitude for His Gifts
The true lover receives his Lord's gifts with joy, happiness, and gratitude, attributing all favor and good to Him, constantly remembering His blessings, praising and thanking Him (p48).

#### 4. Patience in Trials and Acceptance of Decree
The true lover bears his Lord's trial for him and is content with His decree, knowing He intends only good and that the trial is to purify and draw him near: {وَالَّذِينَ صَبَرُوا ابْتِغَاءَ وَجْهِ رَبِّهِمْ} (Al-Ra'd:22) (p48).

#### 5. Frequent Private Supplication (المناجاة)
The true lover loves solitude with Allah, frequent intimate conversation with Him, praising Him, and laying his needs before Him. He waits until voices quiet and eyes sleep, then hastens to his Lord, finding intimate solace — especially in prostration. He cites al-Fudayl ibn 'Iyad taking al-Hasan ibn Ziyad's hand and saying: "Allah descends every night to the lowest heaven and says: 'He lied who claimed love of Me — when night fell, he slept about Me... Is not every lover fond of seclusion with his beloved?'" (pp48–49, from Ruhban al-Layl by al-'Afani 1/408).

#### 6. Love of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ
The true lover loves what Allah loves, and the practical translation of loving Allah and His Messenger is: obeying the Messenger in what he brought from his Lord. He cites: {قُلْ إِن كُنتُمْ تُحِبُّونَ اللَّهَ فَاتَّبِعُونِي يُحْبِبْكُمُ اللَّهُ} (Al Imran:31). He notes the Sahabah came to the Prophet saying: "O Messenger of Allah, by Allah we love our Lord," so Allah revealed this verse (p49, citing Ibn Kathir and al-Qurtubi). Al-Hasan al-Basri said: "A people claimed they love Allah, so Allah tested them with this verse" (p49).

#### 7. Allah and His Messenger Being Dearer Than All Else
Every other love — for spouse, children, parents — must be subordinate to love of Allah, not competing with it. He cites: {قُلْ إِن كَانَ آبَاؤُكُمْ وَأَبْنَاؤُكُمْ... أَحَبَّ إِلَيْكُم مِّنَ اللَّهِ وَرَسُولِهِ وَجِهَادٍ فِي سَبِيلِهِ فَتَرَبَّصُوا} (Al-Tawbah:24) (pp49–50).

#### 8. Loving for Allah and Hating for Allah
The true lover loves what his Master loves and hates what He hates. The Prophet ﷺ said: "The most secure knot of faith is allegiance for Allah, enmity for Allah, love for Allah, and hatred for Allah" (p50, Sahih — narrated by al-Tabarani from Ibn Abbas, authenticated by al-Albani). A person loves another in proportion to the qualities of Allah's love found in them (pp50–51).

He also cites the hadith of the three qualities of the sweetness of faith: "That Allah and His Messenger are dearer to him than all else, that he loves a person only for Allah's sake, and that he hates returning to disbelief as he hates being thrown into the Fire" (p51, Muttafaq 'alayh).

Ibn al-Qayyim's classification of beneficial vs. harmful love is cited (pp50–51, from Ighathat al-Lahfan 2/196–197): Three types of beneficial love (love of Allah, love for Allah, love of what helps obey Allah) and three types of harmful love (love alongside Allah, love of what Allah hates, love that distracts from love of Allah or diminishes it).

#### 9. Love of the Qur'an
"Whoever loves Allah truly will naturally love His word, delight in it, read it frequently, listen to it, and understand its intent." He cites Ibn Mas'ud: "Ask no one about himself except the Qur'an — whoever loves the Qur'an loves Allah and His Messenger" (pp51–52, from Ikhtiyar al-Ula by Ibn Rajab).

#### 10. Frequent Dhikr
"The lover frequently mentions his beloved. Thus you see his tongue always moist with the remembrance of Allah — it is to his heart like water to a fish." He cites: {مَثَلُ الَّذِي يَذْكُرُ رَبَّهُ وَالَّذِي لَا يَذْكُرُ رَبَّهُ مَثَلُ الْحَيِّ وَالْمَيْتِ} — narrated by al-Bukhari (pp52–53).

#### 11. Calling to Allah (الدعوة إلى الله)
The Prophet ﷺ said: "The best of my ummah are those who call to Allah and make His servants love Him" (p53, narrated by Ibn al-Najjar from Abu Hurayrah).

#### 12. Anger for Allah
When Allah's sanctities are violated, His law abandoned, and His Book neglected — the true lover is moved with protective jealousy (ghayrah) for Allah (p53).

#### Also: Love of Jihad and Martyrdom
Jihad in Allah's way is expending effort and energy for His pleasure; martyrdom is the greatest proof that love of Allah is dearer to the servant than everything (p53).

### The Sahabah and Their Love of Allah

Al-Hilali documents how the Sahabah's love of Allah manifested when it clashed with other loves (pp54–56):

- **At Badr**: Abu Ubaydah killed his own father; Abu Bakr intended to kill his son 'Abd al-Rahman; Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr killed his brother 'Ubayd; 'Umar killed a relative. In them was revealed: {لَا تَجِدُ قَوْمًا يُؤْمِنُونَ بِاللَّهِ وَالْيَوْمِ الْآخِرِ يُوَادُّونَ مَنْ حَادَّ اللَّهَ وَرَسُولَهُ...} (Al-Mujadilah:22) (pp54–55).

- **At the Hijrah**: The Muhajirun abandoned wealth, homes, and everything they owned — fleeing with their religion and obeying their Messenger's command. This could not have happened without love of Allah dominating their hearts (pp54–55).

- **Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr**: The Prophet ﷺ said of him: "I saw this one with his parents in Makkah, being honored and pampered, and no young man of Quraysh was like him. Then he left that seeking the pleasure of Allah and supporting His Messenger" (pp55–56, narrated by al-Hakim).

- **Khubayb ibn 'Adi**: When the polytheists were about to kill him (at the Battle of Bi'r Ma'unah), they asked "Would you like Muhammad to be in your place?" He replied: "No, by Allah the Great! I would not like a thorn to prick his foot in my place" (p55, narrated by al-Hakim).

- **Hanzalah**: When he was martyred, the Prophet ﷺ said: "Your companion Hanzalah — the angels are washing him." His wife was asked and said: "He went out while in a state of janabah when he heard the call." The Prophet said: "That is why the angels washed him" (pp55–56, narrated by Abu Nu'aym).

- **'Umar ibn al-Khattab at Uhud**: His brother offered him armor; 'Umar said: "I want martyrdom just as you want it" — and they both left it (p56, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **'Abdullah ibn Jahsh**: Before Uhud, he made du'a: "O Allah, I adjure You that I meet the enemy tomorrow, and they kill me, then they cut open my belly, and mutilate my nose and ear, then You ask me: 'For what?' and I say: 'For You'" (p56, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **Mu'adh ibn Jabal**: When dying of the plague, whenever he regained consciousness, he opened his eyes and said: "Strangle me, O Death, strangle me! By Your majesty, You know that my heart loves You!" (p56, from al-Raqqah wa al-Buka' by Ibn Qudamah).

- **Jaf'ar ibn Abi Talib at Mu'tah**: Fought the Romans while reciting poetry about his longing for Paradise (pp81–82, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

1. **"The most secure knot of faith"**: Loving/hating for Allah's sake — Sahih, narrated by al-Tabarani, authenticated by al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 2539) (p50).
2. **Three signs of the sweetness of faith**: Allah and His Messenger dearer than all else, loving only for Allah, hating disbelief — Muttafaq 'alayh (p51).
3. **The best of my ummah**: Those who call to Allah — narrated by Ibn al-Najjar (p53). Grade not specified in source.
4. **Like the living and the dead**: Comparing the one who remembers Allah and the one who doesn't — narrated by al-Bukhari (p52).
5. **Prophet's du'a at Ta'if**: "If You are not angry with me, I care not..." — (p47). Grade not specified in source.

### Sahabah Examples

See the extensive list above under "The Sahabah and Their Love of Allah" — including Abu Ubaydah, Abu Bakr, Mus'ab ibn 'Umayr, Khubayb ibn 'Adi, Hanzalah, 'Umar, 'Abdullah ibn Jahsh, Mu'adh ibn Jabal, and Ja'far ibn Abi Talib (pp54–56, 81–82).
