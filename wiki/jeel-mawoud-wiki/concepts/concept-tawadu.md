# التواضع — at-Tawāḍu': Humility

## Definition

At-Tawadu' (التواضع) derives from the Arabic root و-ض-ع (w-ḍ-' ), meaning "to lower, to humble, to place down." In the Islamic scholarly tradition, tawadu' is a state of the heart (حالة قلبية) wherein a person considers himself small, recognizes his essential poverty and dependence before Allah, and manifests this internal reality through his conduct toward Allah, himself, and others. It is not self-humiliation or groveling before people, but a dignified awareness of one's true standing before the Creator, expressed through lowered wings (خفض الجناح) toward the believers and firmness toward the rejecters.

Al-Hilali presents tawadu' as the **fifth attribute** of the promised generation, citing the verse: {يُحِبُّهُمْ وَيُحِبُّونَهُ أَذِلَّةٍ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ أَعِزَّةٍ عَلَى الْكَافِرِينَ} (Al-Ma'idah:54) — where "adhillah" (humility) toward believers and "a'izzah" (strength/honor) toward disbelievers are the dual expression of the same generation (p64).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Reality of Humility (حقيقة التواضع)

Al-Hilali defines humility as: "That a person considers himself small... and it is a heart-state that a servant lives, and its effects appear in his behavior and dealings with his Lord, with himself, and with people" (p64).

### Three Dimensions of Humility

#### 1. With Allah (تواضع مع الله)

Humility toward Allah stems from two insights (pp64–65):
- **Seeing one's origin and reality**: One is a created being, powerless, weak, ignorant. One's origin is dust and despicable water.
- **Apprehending the greatness of one's Lord**: His majesty, perfection, and immense favor.

This heart-state must translate into: "Surrender, lowliness, and submissiveness before the Possessor of Might and Majesty; manifesting great need and dependence upon Him; knowing that whatever one is given of health, strength, beauty, or wealth — one remains a humbled servant before a Majestic Lord, and these things change nothing of one's reality" (p65).

#### 2. With the Self (تواضع مع النفس)

Humility with the self means: "Belittling oneself and seeing it with the eye of deficiency, constantly placing it in a station less than what it ought to be" (p65).

Al-Hilali cites the example of **Musa (AS)**: When Musa belittled himself and thought he could not bear the Message alone, he asked Allah to send his brother Harun as a helper: {وَأَخِي هَارُونُ هُوَ أَفْصَحُ مِنِّي لِسَانًا فَأَرْسِلْهُ مَعِيَ رِدْءًا يُصَدِّقُنِي إِنِّي أَخَافُ أَن يُكَذِّبُونِ} (Al-Qasas:34) — even though in reality Musa had performed the duty excellently (pp65–66).

Other forms of humility with the self:
- Not presenting oneself for a task when nominated
- Seeing oneself as unworthy of the position for which one is being considered
- Walking on earth gently — without strutting or swaggering (p66)

#### 3. With People (تواضع مع الناس)

Humility with people stems from: "Seeing them as better than oneself, whatever one's deeds or rank may be. He always looks at the positive side of everyone he deals with and senses their superiority over him" (p66).

This manifests in:
- Not disdaining to serve one's family
- Lowering one's wing to the believers
- Answering the invitation of the poor and weak
- Sitting frequently with the destitute
- Striving to fulfill people's needs (p66)

### The Sahabah's Humility

Al-Hilali provides extensive examples (pp66–69):

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq**: Continued to milk the goats of his neighbors even after becoming Caliph. When they thought he would stop, he said: "By my life, I shall milk them for you, and I hope that what I have entered into will not change the character I was upon." In his first sermon as Caliph, he said: "I have been put in charge over you, and I am not the best of you" — even though he was the best of all people after the Messenger of Allah ﷺ (pp66–67, from Sirr al-Salaf al-Salih by al-Asbahani 1/83).

- **'Umar ibn al-Khattab**: When he entered Jerusalem (Bayt al-Maqdis) at the conquest, he was walking alternately with his servant boy. When the boy's turn to ride came, 'Umar took the camel's rein and waded through the mud with his shoe under his arm. Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah (the commander in Syria) came out and said: "O Commander of the Faithful, the notables of Syria are coming to meet you; it is not good that they see you in this state." 'Umar replied: "We were only given honor by Islam, and we will not seek honor through other than it" (pp67–68, from Tanbih al-Ghafilin). He also passed by a woman making 'asidah (a dish) and told her how to do it properly, taking the stirring stick himself (p68, from Salah al-Ummah). At his death, he told his son 'Abdullah: "Put my cheek in the dust, O my son, perhaps Allah will have mercy on me." His son wiped his cheek with dust (p68, from al-Zuhd by Ibn al-Mubarak).

- **'Uthman ibn 'Affan**: Was seen riding a mule with his servant walking behind him — and he was the Caliph. He was also seen sleeping in the mosque in a plain mantle with no one around him — and he was the Commander of the Faithful (pp68–69, from al-Zuhd by Imam Ahmad).

- **Abu Hurayrah**: When 'Umar sent him as governor of Bahrain, he entered riding a donkey and kept saying: "Make way for the governor! Make way for the governor!" (p68, from Tanbih al-Ghafilin).

- **'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf**: Could not be distinguished from his servants because of his humility in dress (p68, from al-Tawadu' wa al-Khumul by Ibn Abi al-Dunya).

- **Salman al-Farisi**: Was the governor of al-Mada'in. A man of prominence bought something and, thinking Salman was a mere porter, said: "Carry this!" Salman carried it. People recognized him and said: "May Allah rectify the governor — we will carry it for you!" Salman refused. The man realized his mistake and apologized; Salman said: "Go, and never do this again" (pp68–69, from Tanbih al-Ghafilin).
