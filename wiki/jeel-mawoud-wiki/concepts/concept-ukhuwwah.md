# الترابط والأخوة — at-Tarābuṭ wa al-Ukhuwwah: Cohesion & Brotherhood

## Definition

At-Tarābuṭ (الترابط) derives from the Arabic root ر-ب-ط (r-b-ṭ), meaning "to bind, to tie, to connect firmly." Al-Ukhuwwah (الأخوة) derives from أ-خ-و (ʾ-kh-w), meaning "brotherhood, fraternity." Together, they denote the firm bonding and genuine brotherhood that unites the believers as a single body — a cohesive, organized rank (صف) like a solid structure (بنيان مرصوص). This is not mere social affiliation or organizational membership but a bond of faith (رابطة العقيدة) that produces mutual love, sacrifice, protection, and collective striving.

Al-Hilali presents tarābuṭ and ukhuwwah as the **ninth attribute** of the promised generation — "a generation bound together, brotherly, like a solid structure whose parts strengthen one another" (جيل مترابط متآخٍ كالبنيان المرصوص يشد بعضه أزر بعض) (pp92–93).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Foundational Verse: Al-Saff 4

Al-Hilali opens with: {إِنَّ اللَّهَ يُحِبُّ الَّذِينَ يُقَاتِلُونَ فِي سَبِيلِهِ صَفًّا كَأَنَّهُم بُنْيَانٌ مَّرْصُوصٌ} (Al-Saff:4) — "Indeed, Allah loves those who fight in His cause in a rank as if they were a solid structure" (p92). This verse combines two images: the **rank** (الصف) — indicating organization, alignment, and order — and the **solid structure** (البنيان المرصوص) — indicating cohesion, interlocking, and mutual support.

### The Generation Is Not Scattered Individuals

Al-Hilali emphasizes a critical point (p92): "This generation — the generation of tamkin — is not merely scattered individuals here and there who do not feel one another (ليس مجرد أفراد متناثرين هنا وهناك لا يشعر أحدهم بالآخر). Rather, it is a generation bound together, brotherly, like a solid structure whose parts strengthen one another."

Brotherhood (الأخوة) is the bond (الرباط) that binds its individuals to form together a single rank with no unevenness in it.

### The Functions of Cohesion

Al-Hilali identifies three key functions of this cohesion for the promised generation (p92):

1. **Protection from fatigue and negative influence**: It is "a great means of protecting individuals from fatigue or the negative impact of the alienating attacks to which the ummah is exposed." When individuals are isolated, they are vulnerable; when bound together, they protect one another.

2. **Steadfastness and continuity**: It "helps them remain steadfast and continue their journey." The brother who stumbles finds support from those beside him; the one who weakens finds strength from his companions.

3. **Competing in good and mutual counsel**: "Through it there is competition in good, enjoining truth, and enjoining patience" (التنافس على الخير، والتواصي بالحق، والتواصي بالصبر).

### The Prophet's Establishment of Brotherhood: Mu'akhah

Al-Hilali documents the Prophet's establishment of brotherhood between the Muhajirun and the Ansar (pp92–93):

After the Hijrah from Makkah to Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ established brotherhood (المؤاخاة) between the Muhajirun and the Ansar in the house of Anas ibn Malik. They were seventy men — half Muhajirun and half Ansar. He made them brothers on the basis of mutual support (المواساة), and on the basis that they would inherit from each other after death in preference to blood relatives — until the Battle of Badr, when Allah revealed: {وَأُولُو الْأَرْحَامِ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلَى بِبَعْضٍ} (Al-Anfal:75), which returned inheritance to blood relatives while the bond of brotherhood remained in other matters (p93, from Zad al-Ma'ad by Ibn al-Qayyim 2/56).

### Brotherhood as Deed, Not Word

Al-Hilali emphasizes: "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ made this brotherhood a contract (عقدًا), not merely a spoken word, and a deed tied to blood and wealth — not a life that tongues rattle off without any effect" (كان عقدًا لا لفظًا فارغًا، وعملًا يتربط بالدماء والأموال، لا حياة تثرثر بها الألسنة ولا يقوم لها أثر) (p93). "The feelings of preferring others (الإيثار), mutual support (المواساة), and companionship (المؤانسة) were blended in this brotherhood, and the new society was filled with the best examples" (p93).

### The Ansar's Extraordinary Sacrifice for the Muhajirun

Al-Hilali provides the most celebrated example of brotherhood in Islamic history (pp93–95):

#### Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf

Al-Bukhari narrated that when the Muhajirun came to Madinah, the Prophet ﷺ made 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf and Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' brothers. Sa'd said to 'Abd al-Rahman: "I am the wealthiest of the Ansar, so let me divide my wealth in half. And I have two wives — see which of them pleases you and I will divorce her, and when her waiting period ends, you may marry her." 'Abd al-Rahman replied: "May Allah bless you in your family and your wealth — but where is your marketplace?" Sa'd directed him to the market of Banu Qaynuqa'. 'Abd al-Rahman went and returned with a profit from cheese and ghee (p93, from Fiqh al-Sirah by al-Ghazali 179–180).

#### The Ansar's Collective Offer at Banu al-Nadir

When the Prophet ﷺ gained the spoils of Banu al-Nadir, he called the Ansar and thanked them for what they had done for the Muhajirun — sheltering them in their homes and sharing their wealth. Then he said: "If you wish, I will divide what Allah has given me from Banu al-Nadir between you and them, and the Muhajirun will remain as they are in your homes and your wealth. Or if you wish, I will give it to the Muhajirun alone, and they will leave your homes." Sa'd ibn 'Ubadah and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh said: "Rather, divide it between the Muhajirun, and let them remain in our homes as they were." All the Ansar called out: "We are pleased and we submit, O Messenger of Allah!" The Prophet said: "O Allah, have mercy on the Ansar and the children of the Ansar" (p94, from al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Qur'an by al-Qurtubi 18/17–19).

### The Verse of Ithar (Preferring Others Over Oneself)

Al-Hilali cites the Qur'anic description of the Ansar (pp94–95): {وَالَّذِينَ تَبَوَّءُوا الدَّارَ وَالْإِيمَانَ مِن قَبْلِهِمْ يُحِبُّونَ مَنْ هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِمْ وَلَا يَجِدُونَ فِي صُدُورِهِمْ حَاجَةً مِمَّا أُوتُوا وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَى أَنفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ} (Al-Hashr:9).

Al-Qurtubi commented: "The Muhajirun were living in the Ansar's homes. When the Prophet ﷺ gained the spoils of Banu al-Nadir..." (and he recounts the same story above) (p94).

Al-Hilali then adds: "They preferred them over themselves with their wealth and their homes — not from wealth, but despite their need for it (لا عن غنى، بل مع حاجتهم إليها)" (p95).

### The Sheep Circulated Seven Times

Al-Hilali provides another remarkable example of the Sahabah's ithar (p95): Ibn 'Umar narrated that a sheep's head was gifted to a man among the companions of the Messenger of Allah ﷺ. He said: "My brother So-and-so has more need of it than I," and sent it to him. That man sent it to another, and it continued to be passed from one to another until it returned to the first man after circulating among seven people (p95, from Ihya' 'Ulum al-Din by al-Ghazali 2/271).

### Earlier Quranic Foundations for Cohesion

In the earlier part of Chapter 2, al-Hilali had already established the Quranic basis for collective attributes (p34):

- {الَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَمَرُوا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ وَلِلَّهِ عَاقِبَةُ الْأُمُورِ} (Al-Hajj:41) — The empowered generation acts collectively: establishing prayer, paying zakah, enjoining good, forbidding evil.

- {وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ يَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ} (Al-Tawbah:71) — The believers are allies of one another.

He observes: "The Qur'an addresses them collectively because many of the principal attributes of the generation of tamkin cannot be realized except through the cooperation of individuals with one another and their solidarity in a single unity" (p34).

### The Ninth Attribute: Al-Muwalah (الموالاة)

Earlier in the chapter (p32), al-Hilali had listed the ninth attribute as "الموالاة والتآخي" (allegiance and brotherhood), and in the ten-attribute list on p32, item 9 is "الموالاة والتآخي." He treats muwalah and ukhuwwah together as the same concept — allegiance (wala') and brotherhood (ukhuwwah) are two faces of the same coin. The believers are allies of one another, bound by faith, and this alliance manifests as practical brotherhood.

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

No specific hadith with grading is cited in the ukhuwwah section, though the Prophet's actions (mu'akhah) are documented as historical events.

### Sahabah Examples

- **The Prophet's mu'akhah**: Brotherhood between 70 Muhajirun and Ansar in Anas ibn Malik's house (pp92–93)
- **Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' and 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf**: Sa'd offered half his wealth and one of his wives; 'Abd al-Rahman chose the marketplace instead (p93, from al-Bukhari)
- **The Ansar at Banu al-Nadir**: Refused to let the Muhajirun leave their homes; chose to share the spoils (p94, from al-Qurtubi)
- **The sheep circulated seven times**: A single sheep's head passed among seven companions before returning to the first (p95, from al-Ghazali)
