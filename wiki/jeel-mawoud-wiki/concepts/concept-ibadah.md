# العبادة — al-`Ibādah: Worship

## Definition

Al-'Ibadah (العبادة) derives from the Arabic root ع-ب-د ('-b-d), meaning "to serve, to worship, to enslave oneself." In the Islamic context, 'ibadah encompasses everything that Allah loves and is pleased with — whether words, deeds, inward or outward. It is not limited to ritual acts (salah, fasting, zakah, hajj) but extends to every aspect of life when performed in obedience to Allah. The comprehensive definition given by Ibn Taymiyyah — widely cited in Islamic scholarship — defines 'ibadah as: "A comprehensive term for everything that Allah loves and is pleased with from words and deeds, outward and inward."

Al-Hilali presents 'ibadah as the **fourth attribute** of the promised generation, summarizing it with the famous description: **"Monks of the Night, Knights of the Day"** (رُهبان بالليل، فرسان بالنهار) — a generation that combines intense private devotion with vigorous public engagement.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Promised Generation: A Worshipping Generation

Al-Hilali opens with the verse: {وَلَقَدْ كَتَبْنَا فِي الزَّبُورِ مِن بَعْدِ الذِّكْرِ أَنَّ الْأَرْضَ يَرِثُهَا عِبَادِيَ الصَّالِحُونَ} (Al-Anbiya':105–106) (p61). He argues that the generation of tamkin is necessarily a generation of worship — characterized by:

- Frequent remembrance (كثير الذكر)
- Frequent obedience (كثير الطاعة)
- Known to the angels; the mosques testify that they are among those who frequent and anchor them
- Praying Fajr in the mosque; maintaining the Sunnah
- Never missing qiyam al-layl regardless of fatigue
- Maintaining wird (litanies) of nawafil, prayers, adhkar, and Qur'an recitation
- Finding the coolness of his eyes in prayer (قرة عين في الصلاة)
- Prolonging ruku' and sujud; frequent munajah of his Lord
- Frequent spending (إنفاق); frequent fasting; frequent du'a
- Not allowing concern for calling people to distract him from concern for himself — maintaining balance; his iman increases accordingly (pp61–62)

### The Sahabah as the Model: Monks of the Night, Knights of the Day

Al-Hilali cites the famous description of the Sahabah with the phrase: **رُهبان بالليل، فرسان بالنهار** (p63):

> "They lived their night in obedience to their Lord — with recitation, supplication, asking, and their eyes flowing with abundant tears like heavy, pouring rain. In the night, they were monks; in their jihad against their enemies, they were the bravest of knights. And when the time for knowledge came, you saw them among the people of righteous deeds."

He cites Ali ibn Abi Talib's description of the Sahabah: "I saw the companions of Muhammad ﷺ, and I have not seen today anything resembling them. They would wake up disheveled, dusty, with marks like the calluses of goats on their foreheads — having spent the night in prostration and standing, reciting the Book of Allah, swaying between their foreheads and their feet. When morning came, they remembered Allah and swayed like trees sway on a windy day, and their eyes flowed until their garments were soaked" (pp61–62, from Salah al-Ummah by al-'Afani 4/195).

### Devotion to the Mosque and Congregational Prayer

Al-Hilali emphasizes the Sahabah's extraordinary attachment to congregational prayer in the mosque (pp62–63):

Ibn Mas'ud said: "Whoever wishes to meet Allah tomorrow as a Muslim, let him maintain these prayers where they are called, for Allah prescribed for your Prophet the Sunnahs of guidance, and they are among the Sunnahs of guidance. If you were to pray in your houses as this neglectful one prays in his house, you would have abandoned the Sunnah of your Prophet, and if you abandoned the Sunnah of your Prophet you would go astray. No man purifies himself well and then proceeds to any of these mosques except that Allah writes for him with every step a good deed, raises him a degree, and removes a sin. We used to see that only a known hypocrite would stay behind from it. A man would be brought supported between two men until he was stood in the row" (p62, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

'Abdullah ibn 'Umar said: "When we missed a man at Fajr and 'Isha, we thought ill of him" (p62, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

### Night Prayer: The Mark of the Believer

Despite their intense attachment to congregational prayer, the Sahabah were equally devoted to qiyam al-layl, treating it as a source of their joy and happiness. Nothing — not fatigue, not travel — would keep them from it (p63).

### Hind bint Abi Sufyan's Testimony

When Hind (Abu Sufyan's wife) came to accept Islam at the conquest of Makkah, she told her husband she wanted to pledge allegiance. Abu Sufyan said: "You have become an apostate." She replied: "Yes, by Allah — and by Allah, I saw the servants of Allah worshipping Him in this mosque last night. By Allah, they spent the night only in prayer — standing, bowing, and prostrating" (p63, from Ruhban al-Layl by al-'Afani 1/310).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Sahabah Examples

- **Ali ibn Abi Talib**: Described the Sahabah's night worship and morning dhikr (pp61–62)
- **Ibn Mas'ud**: On the obligation of maintaining congregational prayer; the hypocrite was the one who stayed behind (p62)
- **'Abdullah ibn 'Umar**: "When we missed a man at Fajr and 'Isha, we thought ill of him" (p62)
- **Hind bint Abi Sufyan**: Witnessed the Muslims' night worship at the conquest of Makkah (p63)

### Key Narrations

1. **Ibn Mas'ud on congregational prayer**: "Whoever wishes to meet Allah tomorrow as a Muslim, let him maintain these prayers..." — from Hayat al-Sahabah 3/39–40 (p62). Grade not specified in source.

2. **'Abdullah ibn 'Umar on missing people at prayer**: "If we missed a man at Fajr and 'Isha, we thought ill of him" — from Hayat al-Sahabah 3/39–40 (p62). Grade not specified in source.

3. **Hind's testimony**: "I saw the servants of Allah worshipping Him in this mosque last night..." — from Ruhban al-Layl by al-'Afani 1/310 (p63). Grade not specified in source.
