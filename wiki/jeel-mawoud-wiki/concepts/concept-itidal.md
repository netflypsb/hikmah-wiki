# الاعتدال والتوازن — al-I`tidāl wa at-Tawāzun: Moderation & Balance

## Definition

Al-I`tidal (الاعتدال) derives from the Arabic root ع-د-ل ('-d-l), meaning "to be just, equitable, balanced, moderate." At-Tawāzun (التوازن) derives from و-ز-ن (w-z-n), meaning "to balance, to weigh equally." Together, they denote the middle way (الوسطية) between the two extremes of excess (الغلو/التشدد) and negligence (التفريط/الترخيص). The concept is Qur'anically anchored in the description of the Muslim ummah as a "middle nation" (أمة وسط): {وَكَذَلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا} (Al-Baqarah:143).

Al-Hilali presents moderation and balance as the **tenth attribute** of the promised generation — the attribute that "remains" and has "great importance" (صفة عاشرة لها أهمية كبيرة) (pp33, 87). He calls this the attribute of "al-Wasatiyyah wa al-I`tidal" (الوسطية والاعتدال) and argues that Allah required it of His Messenger and those with him even while they were still persecuted in Makkah (p33).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Foundational Verses

Al-Hilali anchors the concept in two key verses (pp31, 33):

1. **Hud:112–113**: {فَاسْتَقِمْ كَمَا أُمِرْتَ وَمَن تَابَ مَعَكَ وَلَا تَطْغَوْا إِنَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ ۝ وَلَا تَرْكَنُوا إِلَى الَّذِينَ ظَلَمُوا فَتَمَسَّكُمُ النَّارُ وَمَا لَكُم مِّن دُونِ اللَّهِ مِنْ أَوْلِيَاءَ ثُمَّ لَا تُنصَرُونَ}

2. **Al-Baqarah:143**: {وَكَذَلِكَ جَعَلْنَاكُمْ أُمَّةً وَسَطًا لِّتَكُونُوا شُهَدَاءَ عَلَى النَّاسِ وَيَكُونَ الرَّسُولُ عَلَيْكُمْ شَهِيدًا}

### The Two Extremes: Tughyan and Rukun

Al-Hilali identifies the two dangers (pp31, 33):

- **الطغيان (Tughyan — Excess/Extremism)**: Represented by "ولا تطغوا" — This is the extreme of strictness and excess (التشدد). It includes going beyond what Allah has commanded, adding burdens not prescribed, and harshness that alienates people from the deen.

- **الركون (Rukun — Inclination/Negligence)**: Represented by "ولا تركنوا إلى الذين ظلموا" — This is the extreme of leniency and compromise (التفريط والترخيص). It includes compromising with oppressors, diluting the deen to please people, and abandoning obligations out of fear or desire for acceptance.

- **الاستقامة (Istiqamah — Uprightness/Right Guidance)**: This is the middle path between the two extremes. "أما الاستقامة فهي بينهما" — "As for istiqamah, it is between them" (p33).

Al-Hilali warns: "Without istiqamah, the believers depart from the circle of divine walayah and nusrah, as the verses have shown" (p33).

### Required Even During Persecution

Al-Hilali emphasizes a remarkable point: Allah demanded this attribute of His Prophet and the believers with him **while they were still in Makkah, persecuted and oppressed** (p33). This shows that moderation is not a luxury of the powerful but a permanent obligation regardless of circumstances.

### Areas Where Balance Is Required

Al-Hilali identifies multiple domains where the promised generation must maintain balance (pp87–93):

#### 1. Balance Between Individual and Collective (بين الفردية والجماعية)

Al-Hilali had earlier observed (p34): "It is notable that some of the [ten] attributes take an individual character — meaning they apply to each person individually, and he should realize them in himself — such as sincerity to Allah, loving Him, and fearing Him. And some of the other attributes take a collective character — meaning they cannot be realized except through a group of individuals together — such as preparation and brotherhood."

The Qur'an addresses the believers collectively (بصيغة الجمع) because "many of the principal attributes of the generation of tamkin cannot be realized except through the cooperation of individuals with one another and their solidarity in a single unity" (p34). He cites:
- {الَّذِينَ إِن مَّكَّنَّاهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ أَقَامُوا الصَّلَاةَ وَآتَوُا الزَّكَاةَ وَأَمَرُوا بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَنَهَوْا عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ وَلِلَّهِ عَاقِبَةُ الْأُمُورِ} (Al-Hajj:41)
- {وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ وَالْمُؤْمِنَاتُ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَاءُ بَعْضٍ يَأْمُرُونَ بِالْمَعْرُوفِ وَيَنْهَوْنَ عَنِ الْمُنكَرِ} (Al-Tawbah:71) (pp34, 87)

#### 2. Balance Between the Spiritual and the Worldly

The generation of tamkin must not be so absorbed in spiritual pursuits that it neglects the world, nor so immersed in worldly affairs that it forgets the akhirah. The correct approach is: "The world is a farm for the akhirah" — engaging with it as a means, not an end (p88).

#### 3. Balance Between Self-Reform and Calling Others

Between the extreme of isolation in worship (the monastic approach) and the extreme of exhausting oneself in dawah while neglecting one's own spiritual state, the promised generation finds the middle path. This is why al-Hilali earlier described them as "monks of the night, knights of the day" — worship in private, dawah and jihad in public (pp61–63, 88).

#### 4. Balance Between Hope and Fear

Between the extreme of absolute despair (القنوط) and the extreme of feeling secure from Allah's plan (الأمن من مكر الله), the believer holds both hope (رجاء) and fear (خوف) in balance (p89).

He cites: {أَفَأَمِنُوا مَكْرَ اللَّهِ فَلَا يَأْمَنُ مَكْرَ اللَّهِ إِلَّا الْقَوْمُ الْخَاسِرُونَ} (Al-A'raf:99) — warning against feeling secure from Allah's plan.

And: {قُلْ يَا عِبَادِيَ الَّذِينَ أَسْرَفُوا عَلَى أَنفُسِهِمْ لَا تَقْنَطُوا مِن رَّحْمَةِ اللَّهِ} (Al-Zumar:53) — warning against despair from Allah's mercy.

#### 5. Balance Between Severity and Gentleness

Between harshness that drives people away and softness that fails to establish the truth, the promised generation must find the balance. Al-Hilali cites the Prophet's instruction to Mu'adh and Abu Musa when he sent them to Yemen: "بَشِّرَا وَلَا تُنَفِّرَا، وَيَسِّرَا وَلَا تُعَسِّرَا" — "Give glad tidings and do not drive people away; make things easy and do not make them difficult" (p89, Muttafaq 'alayh).

And Allah's description of the Prophet ﷺ: {فَبِمَا رَحْمَةٍ مِّنَ اللَّهِ لِنتَ لَهُمْ وَلَوْ كُنتَ فَظًّا غَلِيظَ الْقَلْبِ لَانفَضُّوا مِنْ حَوْلِكَ} (Al Imran:159) — "By Allah's mercy, you were gentle with them; had you been harsh and hard-hearted, they would have scattered from around you" (p89).

#### 6. Balance Between Realism and Idealism

Al-Hilali warns against two extremes (pp89–90):

- **Over-idealism (المثالية المفرطة)**: Imagining a world of angels and demanding that people act as if they were sinless. This leads to extremism (التشدد) and declaring people disbelievers or sinners for their shortcomings.

- **Over-realism/defeatism (الواقعية المتهاوية)**: Lowering the standard to accommodate every human weakness, compromising what Allah has commanded. This leads to leniency (التفريط).

The correct balance is: "Taking people by their natures and dealing with them according to what they are capable of, while not being satisfied with this situation, but rather striving to raise them step by step toward the desired ideal" (p90).

### The Example of the Prophet's Guidance

Al-Hilali provides numerous examples of the Prophet's balanced approach (pp90–92):

- **With Mu'adh ibn Jabal**: The Prophet warned against over-burdening the people in prayer, citing: "Whoever among you leads the people in prayer, let him shorten it, for among them are the weak, the elderly, and those with needs. But if one prays alone, let him lengthen it as he wishes" (p90, Muttafaq 'alayh).

- **With Abu Dharr**: When Abu Dharr asked the Prophet to appoint him as governor, the Prophet said: "O Abu Dharr, I see that you are weak, and I love for you what I love for myself. Do not be a leader of two persons, nor the guardian of an orphan's property" (p91, narrated by Muslim).

- **The Prophet's gradual approach**: Starting with the fundamentals and building gradually. He remained in Makkah for thirteen years calling to tawhid before establishing the Islamic state in Madinah. He did not forbid alcohol in one step but gradually (p91).

- **With those who erred**: The Prophet's approach was not to declare them sinners but to correct them gently and guide them to the truth (p91).

### Warning Against the Two Extremes in the Ummah

Al-Hilali warns that the ummah has been afflicted by both extremes (p92):

- **The extremists (المتشددون)**: "Whoever does not agree with them in everything, even in matters of ijtihad, is a deviant, an innovator, or a disbeliever." They split the ummah and alienate people from the deen.

- **The compromisers (المتساهلون)**: "Whoever calls to the truth is a terrorist, an extremist, a reactionary." They water down the deen and compromise with falsehood.

The promised generation must be a middle generation (جيل وسط) that holds to the truth without excess and without compromise (pp92–93).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

1. **"Give glad tidings and do not drive away"**: The Prophet's instruction to Mu'adh and Abu Musa for Yemen — Muttafaq 'alayh (p89). Grade: Muttafaq 'alayh.

2. **"Let him shorten the prayer"**: Warning against over-burdening the congregation — Muttafaq 'alayh (p90). Grade: Muttafaq 'alayh.

3. **"O Abu Dharr, I see that you are weak"**: The Prophet declined Abu Dharr's request for leadership — narrated by Muslim (p91). Grade: Sahih (Muslim).

### Sahabah Examples

- The Prophet's gradual approach in Makkah (13 years of tawhid before the state) (p91)
- The gradual prohibition of alcohol (p91)
- The Prophet's gentle correction of those who erred (p91)
