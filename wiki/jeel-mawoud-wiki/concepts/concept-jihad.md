# الجهاد — al-Jihād: Striving / Struggle

## Definition

Al-Jihad (الجهاد) derives from the Arabic root ج-ه-د (j-h-d), meaning "to exert effort, to strive, to struggle." In the Islamic lexicon, jihad encompasses exerting one's utmost effort, capacity, and strength for the sake of Allah's pleasure — whether in the battlefield or in the broader struggle to establish Allah's deen. It is not limited to armed combat but includes every form of striving: the jihad of the tongue (calling to Allah), the jihad of knowledge, the jihad of patience against the self, and the jihad of the sword. However, when the term is used without qualification, it generally refers to armed struggle in Allah's path.

Al-Hilali presents jihad as the **seventh attribute** of the promised generation — a generation he calls "الجيل المجاهد" (the striving generation). He argues that the establishment of this deen will not occur except through human effort (جهد البشر), after which divine support (التأييد الإلهي) comes as a consequence of that effort.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Foundational Verses

Al-Hilali cites two key verses (p78):

1. **Al-Ma'idah:54**: {يُجَاهِدُونَ فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَلَا يَخَافُونَ لَوْمَةَ لَائِمٍ} — "They strive in Allah's path and do not fear the blame of any blamer."

2. **Muhammad:7**: {إِن تَنصُرُوا اللَّهَ يَنصُرْكُمْ وَيُثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَكُمْ} — "If you support Allah, Allah will support you and strengthen your footsteps."

### The Law of Striving and Divine Support

Al-Hilali articulates a crucial principle (p78): "He does not expect from that except his Lord's pleasure... a generation that does not withhold any effort it expends to reach its goal. And why not, when he knows that the establishment of this deen will not be except through human effort at the beginning, so that divine support comes as a consequence of this effort."

From this, the children of the promised generation know that **any effort they expend — however small — represents an important step in building the Islamic project**. Therefore, they "connect the night with the day" to reach their goals without neglecting their obligations toward themselves or their families (p78).

### Total Commitment: The Generation That Stands for Its Goal

Al-Hilali describes the comprehensive nature of this generation's sacrifice (pp78–80):

- **He has dedicated his life for his goal** and placed everything else on the margins
- **He has given up many pleasures for his project** and the ummah's great project
- He is driven by "that intense urgency that fills his heart over the conditions of his ummah and what it suffers of humiliation and disgrace unprecedented"
- **He is always fully prepared for sacrifice** — of anything for Allah's pleasure, for implementing His shari'ah, and for raising His banner
- Prepared to sacrifice **his wealth, his time, his effort, his comfort**
- Prepared to sacrifice **his position and lucrative contracts** that would improve his financial standing
- He has prepared himself, his household, and his family for sacrifice and for bearing the roughness of life, as well as the constraints and harm that he can expect to face
- **The sacrifice escalates until it reaches the degree of sacrificing one's life**, longing for his Lord's pleasure and the station of the martyrs with Him

### Clarity of Purpose (وضوح الهدف)

Al-Hilali emphasizes that the first generation knew their goal with complete clarity (p80): "Allah 'azza wa jall had chosen them to spread His deen, to bear witness over the people, and to strive to bring them out of darkness into light." Therefore, "they spread throughout the earth calling creation to Allah and striving in His path. They did not cling to the world, and they did not limit themselves to worship or merely being near the sanctuary. They did not remain in the city of their Prophet ﷺ — despite the great virtue in that — because they knew that Allah 'azza wa jall wanted from them dawah, jihad, and making the earth yield to Him."

He cites **Rabi' ibn 'Amir** addressing Rustam, the Persian commander: "Allah has sent us to bring out whom He wills from the worship of servants to the worship of Allah, from the constriction of the world to its expansiveness, and from the injustice of religions to the justice of Islam. We sent with His deen to His creation — whoever accepts it, we accept from him and turn back, and whoever refuses, we fight him until we reach Allah's promise." "What is Allah's promise?" Rustam asked. Rabi' said: "Paradise for the one who dies fighting the one who refuses, and victory for the one who remains" (pp80–81, from Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum by Safi al-Rahman al-Mubarakpuri and Hayat al-Sahabah).

### Clarity of the Ultimate Goal (وضوح الغاية)

The Sahabah's ultimate goal was crystal clear: **Allah's pleasure and His Paradise** (p81). Al-Hilali provides striking examples:

- **Haram ibn Milhan**: Sent by the Prophet ﷺ with a letter to the enemy 'Amir ibn al-Tufayl. When 'Amir had him stabbed from behind with a spear, Haram saw the blood and said: "Allahu Akbar! I have won, by the Lord of the Ka'bah!" (p81, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **At Badr**: The Prophet ﷺ said: "By the One in whose hand is Muhammad's soul, no man fights them today and is killed while steadfast, seeking reward, advancing and not retreating, except that Allah will enter him into Paradise." Then **'Umayr ibn al-Humam** said: "Khay! Khay!" (an expression of joy). The Prophet asked: "What moves you to say 'Khay, Khay'?" He said: "By Allah, O Messenger of Allah, nothing except the hope that I will be among its people." The Prophet said: "You are indeed among its people." 'Umayr took out some dates he was eating and said: "If I live until I eat these dates, it will be a long life" — then he threw them away and fought until he was killed (pp81–82, from Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum).

- **'Awf ibn al-Harith**: Asked the Prophet "What makes the Lord laugh after this?" The Prophet said: "His hand thrust into the enemy, unarmed." 'Awf took off his armor, took his sword, and fought until he was killed (p82, from Al-Raheeq al-Makhtum).

- **Ja'far ibn Abi Talib at Mu'tah**: Fought the Romans while reciting poetry about his longing for Paradise: "O how beloved is Paradise and its nearness, cool and sweet its drink; the Romans have drawn near, doomed and distant their abode; I must meet them when I meet them!" He fought until he was killed (pp82–83, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

### The Sahabah's Hardship

Al-Hilali documents the extreme hardship the Sahabah endured for Allah's sake (p80; referencing hadith narrations nos. 1769–1784 in source):

- **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari**: "If you had seen us with our Prophet ﷺ, you would have thought our smell was the smell of sheep, for our clothing was wool and our food was the two dark things: dates and water" (p80, narrated by al-Tabarani).

- **Muhammad ibn Sirin**: "A man from the companions of the Prophet ﷺ would go three days without finding anything to eat, so he would take a hide and roast it and eat it, and if he found nothing he would take a stone and tie it to his back" (p80, narrated by Ibn Abi al-Dunya in Kitab al-Ju').
