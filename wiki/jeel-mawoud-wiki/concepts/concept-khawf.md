# الخوف من الله — Khawfullāh: Fear of Allah

## Definition

Khawf (الخوف) derives from the Arabic root خ-و-ف (kh-w-f), meaning "to fear, to be afraid, to dread." In the Qur'anic and prophetic vocabulary, khawf of Allah refers to a reverential awe and dread of Allah's majesty, punishment, and displeasure — not a fleeting emotional tremor, but a permanent state of the heart that produces vigilance, piety (taqwa), and active striving toward obedience. It is paired with love (maḥabbah) as one of the two wings by which the believer approaches Allah.

Al-Hilali presents khawf as the **third attribute** of the promised generation. He distinguishes between a passing fear that merely stirs emotions and departs, and the enduring fear that fills the heart and keeps it in a permanent state of attentiveness. The promised generation requires the latter — a fear that drives to action, produces taqwa, and leads to entering the circle of divine walayah (friendship), nusrah (victory), and kifayah (sufficiency).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### Quranic Foundations

Al-Hilali opens the section on khawf with three verses (p57):

1. **Ibrahim:13–14**: {فَأَوْحَى إِلَيْهِمْ رَبُّهُمْ لَنُهْلِكَنَّ الظَّالِمِينَ ۝ وَلَنُسْكِنَنَّكُمُ الْأَرْضَ مِن بَعْدِهِمْ ذَلِكَ لِمَنْ خَافَ مَقَامِي وَخَافَ وَعِيدِ} — The inheritance of the earth is for those who fear Allah's standing and His warning.

2. **Al-A'raf:128**: {إِنَّ الْأَرْضَ لِلَّهِ يُورِثُهَا مَن يَشَاءُ مِنْ عِبَادِهِ وَالْعَاقِبَةُ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ} — The ultimate outcome belongs to the muttaqin (those with taqwa — the fruit of khawf).

3. **Hud:49**: {تِلْكَ مِنْ أَنبَاءِ الْغَيْبِ نُوحِيهَا إِلَيْكَ مَا كُنتَ تَعْلَمُهَا أَنتَ وَلَا قَوْمُكَ مِن قَبْلِ هَذَا فَاصْبِرْ إِنَّ الْعَاقِبَةَ لِلْمُتَّقِينَ} — The command to be patient accompanies the promise to the muttaqin.

### The Required Fear for the Promised Generation

Al-Hilali articulates the specific nature of the fear required (pp57–58):

> "Through these verses it becomes clear to us that the fear of Allah must fill the hearts of the children of the promised generation with victory and empowerment... a fear motivated by awe and reverence (الإجلال والتعظيم والمهابة) of Allah, and motivated also by the sense of falling short in fulfilling the rights of servitude... and fear of the consequence of sins from which no human is free... and fear of an evil ending (سوء الخاتمة), and fear of the pangs of death and the reckoning of the grave, and fear of the horrors of the Day of Judgment, and fear of standing before Allah and being subjected to account, and fear of the good deeds being found insufficient, and fear of not crossing the Sirat..."

### Crucial Distinction: Productive Fear vs. Passing Emotion

Al-Hilali makes a critical distinction (p58):

> "There is a great difference between a passing fear that shakes feelings and brings tears, then departs on its way, and a permanent fear that fills the heart and keeps it in a constant state of remembrance and attentiveness."

The fear that is required is the fear that:
- **Drives to action**: It pushes the servant to work
- **Produces taqwa and caution**: It yields wara' (scrupulousness) in all words and deeds
- **Seeks precision**: The servant becomes careful in his speech
- **Leaves what is permissible out of fear**: He abandons much of what is permissible, fearing falling into the haram
- **Magnifies Allah's symbols**: {ذَلِكَ وَمَن يُعَظِّمْ شَعَائِرَ اللَّهِ فَإِنَّهَا مِن تَقْوَى الْقُلُوبِ} (Al-Hajj:32)
- **Hastens to good deeds**: {إِنَّ الَّذِينَ هُم مِّنْ خَشْيَةِ رَبِّهِمْ مُشْفِقُونَ... أُولَئِكَ يُسَارِعُونَ فِي الْخَيْرَاتِ وَهُمْ لَهَا سَابِقُونَ} (Al-Mu'minun:57–61)

### The Role of Fear in Istiqamah

Al-Hilali concludes: "The meaning of all this is that the fear of Allah has a great role in the servant's steadfastness on the straight path, and from there his entry into the circle of walayah, nusrah, and divine sufficiency (كفاية إلهية)." He cites: {إِن تَصْبِرُوا وَتَتَّقُوا لَا يَضُرُّكُمْ كَيْدُهُمْ شَيْئًا} (Al Imran:120) (p58).

### The Sahabah's Fear of Allah

Al-Hilali documents extraordinary examples of the Sahabah's khawf (pp59–60):

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq**: Said: "I wish I were a tree that is eaten then reduced to nothing." He used to hold his tongue and say: "This is what has brought me to these calamities." He wept profusely and said: "Weep, and if you cannot weep, then pretend to weep" (p59, from Da' wa al-Dawa' of Ibn al-Qayyim 80–84).

- **'Umar ibn al-Khattab**: On his deathbed said: "By Allah, if I had the entire earth's gold, I would ransom myself with it from Allah's punishment before I see Him." His last words were: "Woe to me! Woe to my mother! If Allah does not forgive me" (p59, from Tanbih al-Ghafilin).

- **'Uthman ibn 'Affan**: Said: "If I were between Paradise and the Fire, not knowing which I would be commanded to, I would choose to be ashes before knowing which of them I would go to" (p59).

- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah**: Said: "I wish I were a sheep that my family slaughtered and ate my meat and drank my broth" (p59, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **'Abdullah ibn 'Abbas**: His lower eyelids were like worn-out water skins from the flow of tears (p59).

- **Abu Dharr**: Said: "I wish I were a tree that is cut, and I wish I had not been created" (p59).

- **'Abdullah ibn Mas'ud**: When a man told him "I would rather be among the people of the Right Hand (As-hab al-Yamin) than the Muqarrabin," Ibn Mas'ud replied: "But here is a man who wishes he had died and not been resurrected" — meaning himself (p60, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **Shaddad ibn Aws al-Ansari**: When he entered his bed, he could not sleep; he would say: "O Allah, the Fire has driven sleep from me," and he would rise and pray until morning (p60).

- **A'ishah (Mother of the Believers)**: Said: "By Allah, I wish I were a tree. By Allah, I wish I were a small pot. By Allah, I wish Allah had not created me at all." When Ibn 'Abbas visited her as she was dying and praised her, she said: "Leave me. By the One in Whose hand is my soul, I wish I were forgotten and forgotten" (p60, from Khawf min Allah by Muhammad Shuman al-Ramli).
