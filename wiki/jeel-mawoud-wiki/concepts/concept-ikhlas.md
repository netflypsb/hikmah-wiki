# الإخلاص — al-Ikhlāṣ: Sincerity to Allah

## Definition

Al-Ikhlāṣ (الإخلاص) derives from the Arabic root خ-ل-ص (kh-l-ṣ), meaning "to purify," "to make sincere," "to be free from mixture or admixture." In the Islamic scholarly tradition, ikhlāṣ signifies directing all acts of worship, intention, and seeking aid solely toward Allah, free from any association (shirk) — whether in the direction of one's deeds (tashajjuh), the seeking of help (isti'ānah), or the hidden corruptions of showing off (riyā') and self-admiration ('ujb). It is the purification of one's internal orientation so that Allah alone is the goal, the sought, the relied upon, and the feared.

Al-Hilali presents ikhlāṣ as the **first and foundational** attribute of the promised generation (p36). Without it, the divine promise of succession (istikhlāf) and empowerment (tamkīn) cannot be realized. The concept extends beyond the narrow definition of "avoiding visible shirk" to encompass a vast circle that includes **every form of hidden association** — anything that competes with Allah for the heart's ultimate orientation.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Foundational Verse: Al-Nur 55

Al-Hilali opens his treatment of ikhlāṣ with the verse of the divine promise:

> {وَعَدَ اللَّهُ الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مِنكُمْ وَعَمِلُوا الصَّالِحَاتِ لَيَسْتَخْلِفَنَّهُمْ فِي الْأَرْضِ... يَعْبُدُونَنِي لَا يُشْرِكُونَ بِي شَيْئًا} (Al-Nur:55)

He observes that the verse conditions the promise of istikhlāf on worship without associating **anything** with Allah. The word "شَيْئًا" (shay'an — anything) is deliberately used to expand the scope of shirk from the narrow circle of overt idolatry to the wide circle encompassing **all** forms of shirk — whether in direction/intention (التوجه والقصد) or in seeking assistance (الاستعانة) (pp36–37).

### Two Dimensions of Shirk that Violate Ikhlas

#### 1. Shirk in Direction/Intention (شرك التوجه والقصد)

This occurs when a person directs an act ostensibly for Allah's pleasure while simultaneously seeking the pleasure of people, their love, elevation of status, or any worldly benefit. Al-Hilali states: "Everything that contradicts complete and absolute direction toward Allah is shirk" (p37). He cites the hadith qudsi:

> قال الله تعالى: أنا أغنى الشركاء عن الشرك، من عمل عملاً أشرك فيه معي غيري، تركته وشركه

> "Allah the Exalted said: I am the most self-sufficient of partners. Whoever does a deed associating someone with Me, I abandon him and his association." (p37, narrated by Muslim)

He also cites the hadith of Shaddad ibn Aws where the Prophet ﷺ wept out of fear for his ummah's shirk — not the worship of idols, sun, or moon, but **showing off in deeds** (الرياء بالأعمال) (p37, narrated by Ibn Majah and al-Hakim).

#### 2. Shirk in Seeking Assistance (شرك الاستعانة)

Just as sincerity demands that one's intention be directed solely to Allah, so too must one's seeking of help be directed to Him alone. Al-Hilali warns: "When a person seeks help from other than his Lord and believes he can reach his goal without Him — Exalted be He — he has committed shirk" (p37).

He identifies a particularly dangerous hidden form: **reliance on one's own self and capabilities**, considering the abilities Allah granted as one's own possession to use and rely upon at will. When a person achieves a goal through such reliance and then views himself with satisfaction and pride, this is the 'ujb (self-admiration) that constitutes one of the most dangerous forms of shirk (pp37–38).

Ibn Taymiyyah is cited: "Showing off (riyā') is from the category of association with creation, and self-admiration ('ujb) is from the category of association with the self. The one admiring himself does not fulfill 'You alone we worship,' just as the one showing off does not fulfill 'You alone we ask for help'" (p38, from Majmu' al-Fatawa 10/277).

### The Danger of Hidden Shirk

Al-Hilali devotes significant attention to the danger of hidden shirk through riyā' and 'ujb (pp38–39):

- **Nullification of deeds**: Any deed mixed with riyā' or 'ujb becomes nullified (مُحبَط), becoming like scattered dust. He cites Al-Baqarah:264.
- **Allah's anger and enmity**: The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever magnifies himself and struts in his walk will meet Allah while He is angry with him" (p39, Sahih — narrated by Imam Ahmad, graded sahih by al-Albani).
- **Deprivation and loss of tawfiq**: The Prophet ﷺ said: "The remorseful awaits mercy; the self-admiring awaits wrath" (p39, narrated by al-Bayhaqi).
- **Defeat and humiliation**: He cites the Battle of Hunayn as a cautionary example: {وَيَوْمَ حُنَيْنٍ إِذْ أَعْجَبَتْكُمْ كَثْرَتُكُمْ فَلَمْ تُغْنِ عَنكُمْ شَيْئًا} (Al-Tawbah:25) (p39).

Al-Hilali concludes this section: "The issue is not only in sincerity of direction toward Allah, but also in sincere seeking of help from Him, and not viewing one's deeds or one's self with joy, satisfaction, and admiration... From here the necessity of the attribute of complete sincerity to Allah in direction and assistance for the children of the promised generation is confirmed, and without it there is no victory and no empowerment" (pp39–40).

### Manifestations of Ikhlas (علامات الإخلاص)

Al-Hilali provides a detailed list of signs that appear when a servant walks the path of sincerity (pp40–42):

1. Not waiting for bosses' approval, people's praise, titles, or preferment
2. Seeking to hide one's place and work as much as possible — neither hinting at nor declaring it
3. Not seeking people's opinion about one's deeds; working and striving to forget and hide the deed
4. Fearing the trial of fame and spotlights, pushing them away
5. Treating work in the background the same as work in the foreground — preferring background work where it escapes people's attention
6. Praise and blame being equal to him; he even pushes away people's praise fearing its negative effects
7. Not feeling distress if his work is attributed to someone else
8. Helping others in their work and striving for its success without anyone noticing
9. Having hidden righteous deeds between him and Allah that no one else knows about
10. Not feeling constricted if someone more experienced is chosen for a task; instead helping and advising the chosen person
11. Not caring about people's pleasure or displeasure with him
12. Not speaking about himself in self-praising terms; not overusing "I... my... I have..."
13. Not boasting to anyone about anything Allah has given him
14. Not trying to display all his capabilities and knowledge before others
15. Not looking for any gain behind his work, nor exploiting his position for worldly benefits for himself or relatives
16. Not reminding others of favors and gifts, in any form
17. Frequent istighfar (seeking forgiveness) after righteous deeds
18. Struggling against his nafs and not surrendering to thoughts of 'ujb — treating them with the "antidote" that restores balance and puts the nafs in the mold of servitude
19. Not considering himself sincere; rather, always thinking poorly of himself and accusing himself of hypocrisy

### The First Generation and Ikhlas

Al-Hilali cites numerous examples from the Sahabah demonstrating ikhlas (pp42–44):

- **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari**: Narrated the hadith about Ghazwat Dhat al-Riqa' where they took turns riding a camel, and their feet were blistered. When Abu Burayah narrated it, Abu Musa disliked it — "as if he hated that anything of his deed should become known" (p42, narrated by Muslim).

- **Umar ibn al-Khattab**: Was seen by Urwah carrying a waterskin on his shoulder. When told "This is not befitting for you, O Commander of the Faithful," Umar explained: "When the delegations came to me with obedience and compliance, some arrogance entered my soul, so I wanted to break it." He then went to the quarters of an Ansari woman and emptied the water into her containers (p43, narrated by al-Tabari).

- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq**: Sent a letter to Khalid ibn al-Walid after his victories in Iraq saying: "...let Abu Sulayman enjoy his ambition and achievement, and may Allah complete it for you. And do not let self-admiration enter you, for you will lose and be humiliated. Beware of claiming credit for your deed, for to Allah belongs the favor and He is the guardian of reward" (pp43–44, narrated from Tarikh al-Tabari).

- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah**: After leading people in prayer, said: "The Satan was pushing me until I thought I had merit over those behind me — I will never lead again" (p44, narrated by Ibn al-Mubarak).

- **Ali ibn Abi Talib**: When praised, he would say: "O Allah, forgive me what they do not know, and do not take me to account for what they say, and make me better than what they think" (p44).

- **Ibn Umar**: When a man said to him "O best of people, son of the best of people," he replied: "I am not the best of people, nor the son of the best of people, but I am a servant of Allah's servants. I hope in Allah and fear Him. By Allah, they will not cease to overwhelm a man until they destroy him" (p44).

- **Fear of nifaq (hypocrisy)**: Ibn Abi Mulaykah said: "I met thirty of the companions of the Prophet ﷺ, all of them fearing hypocrisy for themselves." Umar asked Hudhayfah: "I adjure you by Allah, did the Messenger of Allah ﷺ name me among the hypocrites?" Hudhayfah said: "No, and I will not vouch for anyone after you" (p44, from Da' wa al-Dawa' of Ibn al-Qayyim).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

1. **Hadith Qudsi on partnership**: "I am the most self-sufficient of partners. Whoever does a deed associating someone with Me, I abandon him and his association." — Narrated by Muslim (p37). Grade: Sahih.

2. **The Prophet weeping from fear of hidden shirk**: "I fear for my ummah shirk — they will not worship an idol, sun, moon, or stone, but they will show off in their deeds." — Narrated by Ibn Majah and al-Hakim (p37). Grade not specified in source.

3. **On self-magnification and strutting**: "Whoever magnifies himself and struts in his walk will meet Allah while He is angry with him." — Narrated by Imam Ahmad (p39). Grade: Sahih (al-Albani, Sahih al-Jami' 6157).

4. **Remorseful vs. self-admiring**: "The remorseful awaits mercy; the self-admiring awaits wrath." — Narrated by al-Bayhaqi in Shu'ab al-Iman (p39). Grade not specified in source.

5. **On hidden, pious people**: "The slight showing off is shirk, and Allah loves the pious hidden ones who, when absent, are not missed, and when present, are not recognized. Their hearts are lamps of guidance; they escape from every dark trial." — Narrated by al-Tabarani and al-Hakim (p43). Grade: Sahih isnad (according to the source).

### Sahabah Examples

- **Abu Musa al-Ash'ari**: Hated that any of his deed became known (p42)
- **Umar ibn al-Khattab**: Carried water to break the arrogance that entered his soul from delegations' obedience (p43)
- **Abu Bakr al-Siddiq**: Warned Khalid ibn al-Walid against 'ujb (pp43–44)
- **Abu Ubaydah ibn al-Jarrah**: Refused to lead prayer after sensing self-admiration (p44)
- **Ali ibn Abi Talib**: Responded to praise with du'a for forgiveness (p44)
- **Ibn Umar**: Rejected praise, calling himself a servant (p44)
- **Ibn Abi Mulaykah**: Met 30 companions, all fearing nifaq (p44)
- **Umar asking Hudhayfah about nifaq** (p44)
