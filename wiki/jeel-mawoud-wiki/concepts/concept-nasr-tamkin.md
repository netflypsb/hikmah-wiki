# النصر والتمكين (an-Naṣr wa at-Tamkīn) — Victory & Empowerment

## Definition

النصر (an-naṣr) — "victory, divine help, triumph" — derives from the root ن-ص-ر (na-ṣa-ra), meaning "to help, to support, to grant victory." In Islamic usage, an-naṣr refers to Allah's granting of triumph to His believing servants over their enemies — not merely military success, but the comprehensive divine assistance that enables the establishment of His religion on earth. It is distinct from mere political or military dominance: "Victory is only from Allah" (al-Anfāl 8:10, p. 12).

التمكين (at-tamkīn) — "empowerment, establishment, consolidation" — derives from the root م-ك-ن (ma-ka-na), meaning "to be firm, to be established, to possess authority." At-tamkīn is the state of being firmly established in the land with authority, security, and the ability to govern according to Allah's law. It goes beyond naṣr: while naṣr is the initial divine assistance, tamkīn is its consolidation into lasting civilisational reality.

The compound term an-naṣr wa at-tamkīn represents the complete Quranic promise to believers who fulfill its conditions: divine assistance that leads to firm establishment on earth, enabling the implementation of Allah's religion and the restoration of the ummah's leadership of humanity. In al-Hilali's framework, this is the promised outcome for al-Jīl al-Maw'ūd.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Dual Calculus: Earthly vs. Divine (Ch. 1, pp. 11–12)

Al-Hilali establishes two contrasting sets of calculations:

- **Ḥisābāt ahl al-arḍ** (calculations of the people of the earth): The material and technological superiority of the enemies makes it impossible to catch up. "The area they have allowed us to move in is very limited" (p. 11). These calculations "confirm and establish the truth of the continued miserable state the Islamic ummah is living" (p. 11).

- **Ḥisābāt ahl al-īmān** (calculations of the people of faith): "There is a tremendous power that the human mind cannot comprehend the extent of. This power, if it supports someone, his weakness becomes his strength; so victory becomes his ally regardless of the strength of his enemies. It is the divine power" (p. 11).

The reconciliation: "Our hope in Allah alone does not mean we abandon material causes… rather, the required is the opposite — that we fill every gap available to us, and penetrate all sectors, and exert the utmost effort in possessing the causes of power, not because we will be victorious through them, but because Allah has required that of us" (p. 12).

### Victory Has Conditions (Ch. 1, pp. 13–14)

Al-Hilali insists that naṣr and tamkīn have conditions (shurūṭ): "The Quran has abundantly answered this question, and its verses have informed us that victory and empowerment have conditions, through which they are summoned and attracted" (p. 13). The three foundational verses:

1. **al-Ra'd 13:11:** "Indeed, Allah does not change the condition of a people until they change what is within themselves" — deep internal change is the prerequisite (p. 13)
2. **Muḥammad 47:7:** "If you support Allah, He will support you and plant firmly your feet" — active support for Allah's cause brings divine support (p. 13)
3. **Āl 'Imrān 3:120:** "If you are patient and fear Allah, their plot will not harm you at all" — ṣabr and taqwa protect against enemy schemes (p. 13)

"There must be a support for Allah upon ourselves, and a change of what is in them, and a transition from every state that angers Allah to the state that pleases Him" (p. 13). The change must be 'amīq — deep, reaching into the ummah's very foundations.

### The Example of Banī Isrā'īl (Ch. 1, p. 12)

Al-Hilali cites al-A'rāf 7:137 as a paradigmatic example: the "weak group" (al-fa'ah aḍ-ḍa'īfah) of Banī Isrā'īl inherited the land after Allah destroyed Pharaoh and his people. This demonstrates that divine support can overcome any material disadvantage when conditions are met.

### The Testing Period Before Naṣr (Ch. 1, pp. 19–20)

Before victory comes testing. Al-Hilali cites al-'Ankabūt 29:2–3: "Do people think they will be left to say 'we believe' without being tested?" He explains: "This period is considered a test of the extent of the ummah's sincerity in its love of Allah and its attachment to Him and its reliance upon Him, and the extent of its readiness for sacrifice for His sake" (p. 19). It is "the final birth canal for the birth of the strong, vigorous ummah" (p. 19). The testing period also "cuts the hearts' attachment to other than Allah, and strips them for Him" (p. 19). When material causes are cut off, "the ummah will find before it only Allah, so it will rush to Him, calling upon Him with the call of the drowning person on the verge of going under — then comes relief, and comes naṣr and tamkīn" (p. 19).

He cites al-Baqarah 2:214: "Or do you think you will enter Paradise while the like of those who passed on before you has not yet come to you? — affliction and hardship touched them, and they were shaken until the Messenger and those who believed with him said: 'When is the help of Allah?' Unquestionably, the help of Allah is near."

### The Promise of Khilāfah (Ch. 1, p. 18)

The foundational promise of tamkīn is an-Nūr 24:55: "Allah has promised those who have believed among you and done righteous deeds that He will surely grant them succession (khalīfah) in the land, as He granted it to those before them, and He will surely establish for them their religion which He has preferred for them, and He will surely substitute for them, after their fear, security." Abū al-'Āliyah's commentary: the Prophet ﷺ and his companions were in Mecca about ten years, calling secretly, afraid, not commanded to fight. After the Hijrah, they were commanded to fight while still afraid — wearing arms day and night. A companion asked: "Will we remain afraid forever?" The Prophet ﷺ said: "It will not be long until a man sits in the great gathering with no weapon." Then the verse was revealed and Allah manifested His Prophet over the Arabian Peninsula (p. 18).

### Causes Alone Do Not Bring Naṣr (Ch. 1, pp. 12–13)

Al-Hilali maintains a critical tension: taking causes is commanded, but causes alone do not produce victory. "On the measure of our effort in taking what is available to us of causes, we will have fulfilled a condition of the conditions of naṣr and change, with the understanding that the causes by themselves will not bring us naṣr, but what will bring it is Allah" (p. 12). He cites al-Anfāl 8:10 ("Victory is only from Allah") and Āl 'Imrān 3:160 ("If Allah helps you, none can overcome you") (p. 12).

### The Current Ummah Falls Short (Ch. 5, p. 176)

Al-Hilali directly confronts the contemporary Muslim condition: "We want to serve our religion but while preserving our worldly gains in it — with this state we cannot become the promised generation, for as was mentioned earlier, divine company, support, and victory will not be except for the one who fulfills the conditions, and as long as we have not completed them, we will remain going back and forth in our places" (p. 176). He cites the severe rebuke of at-Tawbah 9:38–39 against the Sahabah who hesitated at Tabuk — and asks: "If this rebuke was for the Sahabah with their great precedence in supporting Islam, then what if the Quran were revealed upon us now? What would Allah say to us in it?" (p. 176).

### The Generation's Role After Tamkīn (Ch. 1, pp. 21–22)

Naṣr and tamkīn are not the end. "The generation of change must not abandon its mission even after sovereignty and world leadership have been achieved. Rather, it must continue building generations, guarding against the diseases of nations — luxury (taraf), oppression (ẓulm), and distance from Allah" (p. 22). The first generation's fear after Badr and Uhud was not defeat, but "the fitnah of the world being spread out upon them" (p. 20). 'Abd ar-Raḥmān ibn 'Awf wept when offered food while fasting, saying: "Muṣ'ab ibn 'Umayr was killed and he was better than me… and Ḥamzah was killed and he was better than me… then the world was spread out for us" — he feared their rewards had been hastened (p. 21).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

- **Abu al-'Āliyah on An-Nūr 24:55 (p. 18):** Ten years of secret da'wah in Mecca, fear and armed vigilance in Madinah, then a companion asks about lasting fear — the Prophet ﷺ promises security. Then the verse of khilāfah is revealed.

- **'Abd ar-Raḥmān ibn 'Awf weeping (p. 21):** Fearing that the world being spread out after victory meant their rewards had been hastened. (Al-Bukhārī)

- **Khabbāb ibn al-Arat weeping (p. 21):** At the martyrs' graves, fearing the reward for their deeds had been given in this world. (Ḥayāt aṣ-Ṣaḥābah)

- **The Ansar at al-'Aqabah (pp. 179–181):** The Prophet ﷺ offered only Paradise as the price of their pledge. As'ad ibn Zurārah warned: "It means parting with all the Arabs and the killing of your best men." They replied: "By Allah, we will never abandon this pledge!" (Reported by al-Bazzār from Jābir)

- **The Ansar's preference of the Muhājirūn (p. 111):** "They gave preference over themselves even though they were in need" (al-Ḥashr 59:9) — all this while poor, not wealthy. Their īmān produced i'thār (preferring others).
