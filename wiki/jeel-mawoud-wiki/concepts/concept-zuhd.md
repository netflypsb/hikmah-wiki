# الزهد — az-Zuhd: Asceticism / Worldly Detachment

## Definition

Az-Zuhd (الزهد) derives from the Arabic root ز-ه-د (z-h-d), meaning "to renounce, to have no desire for, to be ascetic." In Islamic terminology, zuhd is "the turning away of desire from the world and shunning it" — not the prohibition of the lawful, but the liberation of the heart from being enslaved by worldly pursuits. It is not poverty by necessity, but freedom by choice — a detachment that allows the servant to pursue his true goal without being weighed down by worldly attachments. Al-Hilali defines it as: "The turning away of desire from the world and shunning it (انصراف الرغبة عن الدنيا ومجافاتها)" (p69).

Al-Hilali presents zuhd as the **sixth attribute** of the promised generation — a generation he calls "الجيل الزاهد" (the ascetic generation). He argues that love of the world is among the greatest obstacles (أهم العوائق) that prevent the servant from entering the circle of divine walayah, and that the ummah's weakness and humiliation resulted from its love of the world and preferring it over the akhirah (p69).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Prophet's Definition of Zuhd

Al-Hilali cites the Prophet ﷺ: "مَالِي وَالدُّنْيَا وَمَالِي وَالدُّنْيَا" — "What have I to do with the world? What have I to do with the world?" (p69, Sahih — narrated by Imam Ahmad from Ibn 'Abbas, authenticated by al-Albani in Sahih al-Jami' 5669).

### Love of the World as the Root of the Ummah's Weakness

Al-Hilali argues emphatically: "How could the promised generation of victory and empowerment not be like that [i.e., ascetic], when love of the world and desire for it is among the most important obstacles that prevent the servant from entering the circle of divine walayah. And what has afflicted the ummah of weakness and humiliation is only because of its love of the world and its desire for it, and its preferring it over the akhirah" (p69).

He cites the famous hadith: "يوشك أن تداعى عليكم الأمم من كل أفق كما تداعى الأكلة إلى قصعتها. قيل: يا رسول الله! فمن قلة يومئذ؟ قال: لا، ولكنكم غثاء كغثاء السيل، يُجعل الوهن في قلوبكم، ويُنزع الرعب من قلوب عدوكم، لحبكم الدنيا وكراهيتكم الموت" — "It will soon happen that nations will call one another to attack you, from every horizon, as diners are called to a shared dish. It was said: 'Will we be few then, O Messenger of Allah?' He said: 'No, rather you will be plentiful like the scum of a flood. Allah will remove fear of you from the hearts of your enemies and will place weakness (wahn) in your hearts.' It was said: 'What is wahn?' He said: 'Love of the world and hatred of death'" (p69, Sahih — narrated by Imam Ahmad and Abu Dawud from Thawban, authenticated by al-Albani in Sahih al-Jami' 8183).

### The Zuhd of the Promised Generation: Not Poverty but Freedom

Al-Hilali clarifies a critical misunderstanding (pp70–71):

> "The promised generation of tamkin must have love of the world removed from the hearts of its children... His motto in life should be: 'What is little and sufficient is better than what is much and distracting.'"

But he emphasizes: "It does not mean that the generation of tamkin should be a poor generation. Rather, the point is that the world should not occupy his thought or enslave his heart, for the great mission entrusted to him requires that he not be attached to anything other than Allah" (p71).

### The Ambition of the Ascetic Generation (طموح)

Al-Hilali makes a striking argument: the generation of tamkin is a generation of ambition (طموح) — but what are its ambitions? (p70):

- Not in promotion, position, and status
- Not in buying land for children's buildings
- Not in increasing his bank balance

These are worldly ambitions with no value. **His true ambitions** are:
- With Allah 'azza wa jall, in al-Firdaws al-A'la
- In seeing his Lord
- In accompanying His Messenger ﷺ
- In martyrdom in Allah's path, that his soul may be in the crops of green birds

"Therefore, he deals with the world on its true basis — that it is a farm for the akhirah" (p70).

### Not Bartering (لا يُساوم)

The generation of change is a generation that worships Allah and is free from everything besides Him. "Nothing of the world's trappings enslaves him — not a job he fears losing, nor a position he fears missing." His goal is the highest: Allah's pleasure and His Paradise. "As for what is below that, he is ascetic regarding it — it does not concern him much whether he is rich or poor, whether granted children or not, whether in a high position or low" (pp70–71).

The practical consequence: "He does not barter (يساوم) on anything of this world." The result of such bartering is well-known — it leads to compromise on principles (p71).

### What Helps This Generation Remain Free

Al-Hilali provides practical guidance (pp71–72):

1. **Not getting entangled in economic projects** that require heavy expenses which become instruments of pressure and weights binding the heart to the world
2. **Having provisions like a traveler's provisions** — not expanding into luxuries that need heavy expenditure
3. **His wife and children being on the same level of understanding** — if they are not, they form an obstacle (عائق) that impedes his movement

### The Qur'anic Warning About Family as Obstacle

Al-Hilali cites: {يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا إِنَّ مِنْ أَزْوَاجِكُمْ وَأَوْلَادِكُمْ عَدُوًّا لَّكُمْ فَاحْذَرُوهُمْ} (Al-Taghabun:14) (p73).

And the Prophet ﷺ said: "الولد ثمرة القلب، وإنه مجبنة مبخلة محزنة" — "The child is the fruit of the heart, and it is a source of cowardice, miserliness, and grief" (p73, Sahih — narrated by Abu Ya'la, authenticated by al-Albani in Sahih al-Jami' 7160).

This is why the generation of tamkin must choose wives who sense the ummah's concerns and who understand that this generation has different dreams and ambitions. Such wives will not demand expensive furniture, spacious homes, or new cars. They will instead be partners and supporters (pp73–74).

### Choosing Wives for the Promised Generation

Al-Hilali makes a remarkable point: "Generation of tamkin is not limited to men only, but men and women equally" (p74). He argues that both spouses must share the same vision and detachment.

### The Sahabah as the Model of Zuhd

Al-Hilali provides extensive Sahabah examples (pp73–77):

- **Ibn Mas'ud** told the Tabi'in: "You fast and pray more than the companions of Muhammad, but they were better than you." They asked why. He said: "They were more ascetic regarding the world and more desirous of the akhirah" (p73, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **'Umar ibn al-Khattab**: When the spoils of Qadisiyyah came to him, he looked at them and wept. 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf said: "This is a day of joy and delight." 'Umar replied: "Indeed, but no people have ever been given this except that it bred enmity and hatred among them" (pp73–74, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas**: When Salman visited him as he was dying, Salman wept. Sa'd said: "What makes you weep? You will meet your companions and return to the Messenger of Allah ﷺ at the Hawd, and he is pleased with you!" Sa'd replied: "I weep not from fear of death or greed for the world, but the Messenger of Allah ﷺ made a pact with us: 'Let the provision of one of you from the world be like the provision of the rider,' and here are these cushions around me, and around him is only a mat" (pp74–75, from Hayat al-Sahabah). The Prophet ﷺ had said: "لَيْبَلُغَنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ مِنَ الدُّنْيَا كَزَادِ الرَّاكِبِ" — "Let the world reach one of you only like the provisions of a rider."

- **Abu Ayyub al-Ansari**: At the Battle of the Trench, a man fought the enemy at Constantinople until he was cut through. People said: "He has thrown himself into destruction." Abu Ayyub said: "We know better about this verse — it was revealed about us." He explained that after Islam spread and they had families and wealth, they were tempted to return to their families and neglect jihad — and then the verse was revealed: {وَأَنْفِقُوا فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ وَلَا تُلْقُوا بِأَيْدِيكُمْ إِلَى التَّهْلُكَةِ} (Al-Baqarah:195). "So the destruction (tahlukah) was in staying with family and wealth and leaving jihad" (pp75–76, from Hayat al-Sahabah).

- **Suhayb al-Rumi**: Quraysh tried to bargain with him to not emigrate or leave his wealth. He asked: "If I give you my wealth, will you leave me alone?" They said yes. He gave them all his wealth and departed. When the Prophet ﷺ heard, he said: "ربح Suhayب، ربح Suhayب" — "Suhayb has profited! Suhayb has profited!" — twice. About him and those like him, the verse was revealed: {وَمِنَ النَّاسِ مَن يَشْرِي نَفْسَهُ ابْتِغَاءَ مَرْضَاتِ اللَّهِ} (Al-Baqarah:207) (p76).

- **Sa'id ibn 'Amir**: When 'Umar came to Syria, he asked for the names of the poor to receive stipends. The list included "Sa'id ibn 'Amir — their governor." 'Umar was astonished: "How can your governor be poor?" They said: "He holds nothing back." 'Umar wept, then sent him 1,000 dinars. When Sa'id's wife saw them, she asked what was wrong. He said: "The world has come to me! The fitnah has entered upon me!" She offered to help him spend it, and he distributed all of it to the Muslim army — spending every last dinar (pp76–77, from Ibn Kathir's Tafsir and al-Raqqah wa al-Buka' by Ibn Qudamah).

### The Prophet's Guidance on Not Taking Up Real Estate

Al-Hilali cites: "لا تَتَّخِذُوا الضِّيْعَةَ فَتَرْغَبُوا فِي الدُّنْيَا" — "Do not acquire estates, lest you desire the world" (p72, Sahih — narrated by Imam Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, and al-Hakim, authenticated by al-Albani in Sahih al-Jami' 7214).

He also cites the story of a prophet who set conditions for those who wanted to march with him: "A man who married a woman wanting children but hasn't had them, one who built houses but didn't raise the roofs, one who bought sheep or camels awaiting their offspring" — the common element being: "what preoccupies the mind or is a source of pressure that makes the person hesitant in movement, wishing not to die so he can return and tend to his affairs" (p72, Muttafaq 'alayh).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

1. **"What have I to do with the world?"** — Sahih, narrated by Imam Ahmad from Ibn 'Abbas, authenticated by al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 5669) (p69).
2. **"Nations will call one another against you..."** — Sahih, narrated by Imam Ahmad and Abu Dawud from Thawban, authenticated by al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 8183) (p69).
3. **"The child is the fruit of the heart"** — Sahih, narrated by Abu Ya'la, authenticated by al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 7160) (p73).
4. **"Do not acquire estates"** — Sahih, narrated by Ahmad, al-Tirmidhi, al-Hakim, authenticated by al-Albani (Sahih al-Jami' 7214) (p72).
5. **The prophet's conditions for marching** — Muttafaq 'alayh (p72).

### Sahabah Examples

- **Ibn Mas'ud**: The Sahabah were better because of their zuhd, not their worship quantity (p73)
- **'Umar**: Wept at spoils of Qadisiyyah, fearing the enmity wealth breeds (pp73–74)
- **Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas**: Wept at the cushions around him vs. the Prophet's command to live like a rider (pp74–75)
- **Abu Ayyub al-Ansari**: Tahlukah is staying with family and leaving jihad (pp75–76)
- **Suhayb al-Rumi**: Gave all his wealth to emigrate; the Prophet declared "Suhayb has profited!" (p76)
- **Sa'id ibn 'Amir**: Governor of Homs who was poor; spent 1,000 dinars in one day (pp76–77)
