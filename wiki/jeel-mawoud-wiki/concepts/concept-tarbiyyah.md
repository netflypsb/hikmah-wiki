# التربية (at-Tarbiyyah) — Education/Nurturing

## Definition

التربية (at-tarbiyyah) — "education, nurturing, upbringing" — derives from the root ر-ب-و (ra-bā-wa), meaning "to grow, to increase, to raise." In Islamic usage, at-tarbiyyah is the comprehensive, intentional process of forming a human being — intellectually, spiritually, morally, and practically — according to the divine methodology (al-manhaj ar-rabbānī). It is not merely the transfer of information (ta'līm) but the holistic cultivation of a complete personality.

The term shares its root with ربّ (rabb) — Lord, Sustainer, Nurturer — indicating that tarbiyyah is fundamentally an act of rabbāniyyah (divine lordship). The Prophet ﷺ was called al-murabbī (the nurturer/educator) because his role was not merely to convey information but to form people. The Quran describes his mission: "It is He who sent among the unlettered a messenger from themselves, reciting to them His verses, purifying them (yuzakkīhim), and teaching them the Book and the ḥikmah" (al-Jumu'ah 62:2, p. 160). Three dimensions: recitation (talāwah), purification (tazkiyyah), and teaching (ta'līm) — tarbiyyah encompasses all three.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Three Instruments of Change (Ch. 4, p. 146)

Al-Hilali identifies three principal instruments (adawāt) that produced the first generation — the generation of the Ṣaḥābah:

1. **The methodology of change (manhaj at-taghyīr) — and that is the Quran**
2. **The educator supervising the process of change (al-murabbī al-mushrif 'alā 'amaliyyat at-taghyīr) — and that is the Messenger ﷺ**
3. **The environment in which change occurred (al-waṭ alladhī fīhi at-taghyīr) — the bonded, cohesive generation** (p. 146)

Tarbiyyah is the function of the second instrument — the Prophet ﷺ as murabbī — using the first instrument (the Quran) within the third (the community).

### The Quran as Methodology of Change (pp. 146–148)

The Quran performs the transformative work; the Prophet ﷺ supervises and guides. "With Allah, the Quran takes on the task of introducing the servant to his Lord to the furthest extent that his mental capacity can bear, and brings him to the closest that a human after the prophets can be to his Lord" (p. 146). The Quran also "links that knowledge to the currents of life, so the servant sees only Allah's wisdom behind His actions and His will" (p. 146). This produces iḥsān: "he worships Allah as if he sees Him" (p. 146).

Regarding the Hereafter and the self: "The Quran reveals to man the truth of his humble origin, the extent of his weakness and inability… and that he is by Allah not by himself, and if He leaves him for the blink of an eye he perishes. Along with stating these facts, it also informs him of the nature of his self — its love of desires, its inclination toward transgression and tyranny, commanding evil — so he increases his caution against it" (p. 147).

### Purity of the Source (Ṣafā' an-Nab') (pp. 147–149)

For the Quran's tarbiyyah to work, the heart must be exclusively devoted to it during the formative period. "This critical role of the Quran cannot happen unless the servant devotes himself to it completely, giving it his time, his heart, and his mind, approaching it with his entire being, and not mixing anything else with it — especially in the beginning — until the product is sound and pure" (p. 147).

The Prophet ﷺ was intensely committed to this purity: "He was always directing them not to be occupied with anything other than the Quran, so that the Quran could fully play its role in radical change without interference from anything else" (p. 148). When 'Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb brought a scroll from the People of the Book, the Prophet ﷺ became angry: "Are you trying to imitate them, O Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb? By the One in Whose hand is my soul, I have come to you with it white and pure. Do not ask them about anything, lest they tell you a truth and you disbelieve it, or a falsehood and you believe it. By the One in Whose hand is my soul, if Mūsā were alive, he would have no choice but to follow me" (pp. 148–149, reported by Aḥmad).

Sayyid Quṭb comments: "There was an intention from the Messenger of Allah to restrict the source from which that generation drank during the formative period to the Book of Allah alone, to purify their souls for Him alone, and for their natural disposition to be based on His methodology alone" (p. 149).

### The Prophet ﷺ as Murabbī (pp. 160–161)

"With the great integrated methodology, there had to be a director and supervisor and follower of the course of the change process, to ensure the quality of the product and the goodness of the fruit. And this important role was performed by the teacher of humanity, the great educator, the Messenger of Allah" (p. 160). He cites al-Jumu'ah 62:2: "It is He who sent among the unlettered a messenger from themselves, reciting to them His verses, purifying them, and teaching them the Book and the ḥikmah."

The Prophet's tarbiyyah was:

- **Practical (amalī):** He was a living model — "his character was the Quran" (p. 151, from 'Ā'ishah's narration)
- **Gradual (tadarrujī):** He did not burden them beyond their capacity
- **Individualised (fardī):** He addressed each person according to their situation
- **Supervised (mushraf):** He watched for deviation and corrected it

### The Sahabah's Experience of Quranic Tarbiyyah (pp. 153–159)

Al-Hilali provides vivid illustrations of how the Sahabah experienced tarbiyyah through the Quran:

- **Ibn 'Abbās reading at night (p. 153):** "He would recite an āyah, then pause as long as someone might speak to you, then recite another." When asked why, he said: "For reflection (at-ta'wīl) — I think about it."

- **Asmā' bint Abī Bakr repeating an āyah (p. 154):** She kept repeating "So Allah has favoured us and saved us from the punishment of the Scorching Fire" (aṭ-Ṭūr 52:27), calling upon Allah with it. Someone went to the market, conducted his business, and returned while she was still repeating and calling upon Allah with the same āyah.

- **'Abdullāh ibn Mas'ūd (p. 154):** He spent an entire night repeating "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (Ṭā Hā 20:114). 'Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb spent the whole night repeating al-Fātiḥah.

- **'Abbād ibn Bishr (pp. 158–159):** On guard duty at Dhāt ar-Riqā', he was shot by an arrow while praying, but refused to break his prayer. He was shot a second time and continued. Only after the third arrow did he end his recitation and wake his companion. When asked why he didn't wake him sooner: "I was in a sūrah I was reciting, and I did not want to cut it until I finished it." This was tarbiyyah so deep that the Quran's hold on the heart exceeded the fear of death.

### Ḥifẓ vs. 'Amal: The Sahabah's Priority (pp. 155–157)

A distinctive feature of the Sahabah's tarbiyyah: "They dealt with the Quran on its reality as a book of guidance, healing, change, and correction, so their concern was not focused on speed of memorisation or reading the greatest amount" (p. 155). Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī said: "The Messenger of Allah passed away and the memorisers of the Quran from his companions — except for a few — had not completed its memorisation, out of awe for it, and their devotion to understanding its interpretation and acting upon its rulings" (p. 155).

Ibn Mas'ūd said: "It was difficult for us to memorise the words of the Quran, and easy for us to act upon it. And after us, there will come people for whom memorising the Quran will be easy but acting upon it will be difficult" (p. 156). Ibn 'Umar said: "The foremost of the companions of the Messenger of Allah in the early days of this ummah would not memorise more than a sūrah or its like, but they were given acting upon the Quran" (p. 157).

### Tarbiyyah Circles in the Home (Ch. 5, pp. 189–190)

Al-Hilali calls for the revival of tarbiyyah circles (al-maḥāḍir at-tarbawiyyah) similar to Dār al-Arqam and the Sahabah's homes: "Let us have tarbiyyah circles in our homes — the husband with his wife and children, the brother with his siblings" (p. 190). He cites the story of 'Umar's Islam: he went to his sister's house and found Khabbāb ibn al-Arat teaching her and her husband verses of the Quran. "There must be tarbiyyah circles in our homes… and to the extent of our sincerity and dedication, the day will come when we will shake hands with those who walk with us in the path of Allah" (p. 190).

### The Three Pivots of Formation (Ch. 5, pp. 183–185)

Al-Hilali summarises the tarbiyyah process through three maḥāwir (pivots):

1. **Deep īmān in Allah and the Last Day** — binding the heart to Allah, making one worship as if seeing Him
2. **Tazkiyat an-nafs with mujāhadah** — purifying the self and removing self-inflation
3. **Correct understanding of Islam** — comprehensiveness, balance, objectives, priorities, reality

All three must begin with the Quran: "These three pivots must have the Quran as their fundamental starting point. The Quran is the primary instrument of change… without it, the product will not be complete" (p. 185).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

- **'Umar and the scroll (pp. 148–149):** The Prophet ﷺ's anger at 'Umar for bringing a book from the People of the Book — insistence on pure Quranic source during formation. (Aḥmad, 'Abd ar-Razzāq, Ibn Abī Shaybah, al-Dārimī; graded ḥasan by al-Albānī)

- **'Ā'ishah on the Prophet's character (p. 151):** "His character was the Quran." (Muslim) — the Prophet ﷺ embodied the tarbiyyah he taught.

- **Ibn Mas'ūd on the hearts as vessels (pp. 149–150):** "These hearts are vessels — fill them with the Quran and do not fill them with anything else." (Faḍā'il al-Qur'ān, Abī 'Ubayd)

- **'Abbād ibn Bishr at Dhāt ar-Riqā' (pp. 158–159):** Continuing prayer despite being shot by arrows — refusing to cut the sūrah he was reciting. (Ibn Hishām, Ibn al-Qayyim)

- **The Muslims of Madinah before the Prophet's arrival (p. 152):** Muṣ'ab ibn 'Umayr taught them the Quran, and "light entered the hearts of the people of Yathrib, and they were filled with īmān, and their perceptions and priorities changed, and the divided were united" — all before the Prophet ﷺ arrived. The Quran alone, through a single teacher, transformed an entire city.
