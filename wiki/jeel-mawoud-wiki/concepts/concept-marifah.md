# المعرفة بالله (al-Ma'rifah billāh) — Knowledge of Allah

## Definition

المعرفة بالله (al-ma'rifah billāh) — "knowledge of Allah" — refers to the deep, transformative knowledge of Allah's names, attributes, actions, and rights that penetrates the believer's certainty (yaqīn) and inner mind ('aql al-bāṭin), affecting his feelings (mashā'ir), becoming part of his īmān, and producing correct dealings (mu'āmalāt) with Allah: ikhlāṣ (sincerity), ḥubb (love), khawf (fear), rajā' (hope), ta'ẓīm (reverence), tawakkul (reliance), and istislām (surrender).

The term المعرفة (al-ma'rifah) derives from ع-ر-ف ('a-ra-fa), meaning "to know, to recognise, to be acquainted with." Unlike 'ilm (cognitive knowledge), ma'rifah implies relational knowledge — intimate acquaintance that transforms the knower. It is the knowledge that a lover has of the beloved, not the knowledge a scholar has of a subject.

In al-Hilali's methodology, ma'rifat Allāh is the first and most important of the five pillars (al-ma'ārif al-khamsah) from which all attributes of the promised generation emerge. "The first pivot from which the attributes of the promised generation must launch is knowledge of Allah" (p. 102).

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Governing Principle: Mu'āmalah bi-Qadr al-Ma'rifah (pp. 99–102)

Al-Hilali's entire treatment of ma'rifah rests on the principle: "Treatment is proportionate to knowledge" (al-mu'āmalah bi-qadr al-ma'rifah). He illustrates with two analogies:

1. **Two strangers on a train (pp. 99–100):** Initially formal, they gradually warm as they learn about each other. If one discovers the other holds a prestigious position, his entire manner changes. "This without a doubt reflects on how he deals with him."

2. **Our relationship with Allah (p. 99):** "If we look at our dealings and relationship with Allah, we find them cold and limited… what governs them is the degree of our knowledge of Him." Citing al-An'ām 6:91: "They did not appraise Allah with true appraisal."

### The Consequences of Ignorance of Allah (pp. 99–100)

"The more the servants' ignorance of Allah increases, the more their incorrect conception of Him grows" (p. 99). Two Quranic examples:

1. **Al-Baqarah 2:142 (p. 100):** Those who ask about the qiblah change are called "the foolish" (as-sufahā') — because they don't know that Allah owns the heavens and earth, does what He wills.

2. **Fuṣṣilat 41:19–23 (pp. 100–101):** The damned assumed "Allah does not know much of what you do." Their defective assumption (ẓann) about Allah led them to the Fire.

### Mūsā's Story: Knowledge Produces Repentance (pp. 100–101)

The story of Mūsā's request to see Allah (al-A'rāf 7:143) is the paradigmatic example. When Allah manifested Himself to the mountain and it crumbled, Mūsā fell unconscious (ṣu'iq). When he recovered: "His knowledge of the greatness of his Lord had increased, and this was reflected in his words and his dealing with his Lord. When he recovered, he said: 'Glory be to You! I repent to You, and I am the first of the believers.'" (p. 101). He repented from the deficiency in giving Allah His due right of reverence, glorification, and sanctification, and from seeking to see Him. "This repentance was a result of the knowledge that increased after he saw the effects of Allah's greatness" (p. 101).

### Ma'rifah as the Source of All Virtues (p. 102)

"Dealing with Allah with sincerity, love, fear, awe, reverence, taqwa, and surrender — all of that is the product of the strength of knowledge of Him" (p. 102). Conversely: "The weakness of this knowledge leaves many negative effects on the servant — he fears created beings, is anxious about his provision, seeks help from other than Allah, commits many sins" (p. 113).

### The Required Quality of Ma'rifah (pp. 102, 108, 112)

Not any knowledge suffices. The required ma'rifah must be:

- **Deep** ('amīqah) — not superficial
- **Firm** (rāsikhah) — not shaken by doubts or desires
- **Continuous** (mustamirrah) — not momentary
- **Affecting the feelings** (tū'aththir fī al-mashā'ir) — not merely intellectual
- **Part of īmān** (juz'an aṣīliyyan min al-īmān) — integrated into faith
- **Penetrating yaqīn and the inner mind** (fī yaqīn al-insān wa 'aqlihi al-bāṭin) — reaching the deepest level of conviction

### How to Know Allah (pp. 113–118)

Since "there is nothing like Him" (ash-Shūrā 42:11) and "vision cannot encompass Him" (al-An'ām 6:103) and "they encompass Him not in knowledge" (Ṭā Hā 20:110), how can we know Allah? Al-Hilali answers:

1. **Through His names and attributes (pp. 113–115):** Allah has informed us of His beautiful names (al-asmā' al-ḥusnā) and placed their effects and manifestations in His creation. He told us He is merciful (raḥīm) and showed us the effects of mercy — rain, acceptance of repentance, forbearance with sinners. He told us He is overpowering (qahhār) and showed us the effects — the form and colour of newborns, sleep, disease, forgetfulness.

2. **Through reflection on creation (āyāt Allāh, pp. 115–116):** "There is no created thing that does not carry a message introducing Allah to mankind" (p. 115). He cites al-A'rāf 7:185: "Do they not look at the dominion of the heavens and the earth and what Allah has created?" And Āl 'Imrān 3:190–191: "Indeed, in the creation of the heavens and the earth and the alternation of the night and day are signs for those of understanding — those who remember Allah standing, sitting, and on their sides, and reflect on the creation of the heavens and the earth: 'Our Lord, You did not create this in vain! Glory be to You! Save us from the punishment of the Fire.'"

3. **Through life events (aḥāth al-ḥayāh, pp. 116–118):** Allah sends continuous messages (risālāt) through daily events. Al-Hilali identifies six types:

   a. **Messages of mercy (risālāt ar-raḥmah):** Forgotten appointments that prevent harm, fuel running out near a station
   b. **Messages of wisdom (risālāt al-ḥikmah):** Going somewhere reluctantly and finding great benefit
   c. **Messages of warning (risālāt at-takhwīf):** Thunder, lightning, storms, eclipses — citing al-Isrā' 17:59
   d. **Messages of support (risālāt at-ta'yīd):** Ease of affairs, opening of the chest — citing al-Layl 92:5–7
   e. **Messages of punishment (risālāt al-'uqūbah):** Difficulty, constriction, deprivation — citing an-Nisā' 4:123
   f. **All combined (p. 118):** "He shows you His signs — so which of Allah's signs do you deny?" (Ġāfir 40:81)

   These messages come "continuously so that they may awaken from their slumber and recover from their heedlessness" (p. 118), citing ar-Ra'd 13:2: "He arranges affairs, He details the signs so that you may be certain of meeting your Lord."

### True Knowledge: The Most Beneficial Science (p. 113)

"From here, the truly beneficial knowledge (al-'ilm an-nāfi') which seekers of knowledge in particular and all Muslims in general should seek is knowledge of Allah. Through this knowledge, the servant's dealing with his Lord improves — he fears Him, is mindful of Him, takes heed of Him, relies on Him, hopes in Him" (p. 113). He cites Fāṭir 35:28: "Indeed, those who fear Allah among His servants are the scholars" and al-Ḥujurāt 49:13: "The most noble of you in the sight of Allah is the most righteous."

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

- **The Prophet ﷺ on īmān and sin (p. 103):** "A servant does not commit zina while he is a believer…" — moments of sin show the victory of desire over the knowledge of Allah that should have prevented it. (al-Bukhārī and Aḥmad from Ibn 'Abbās)

- **The Prophet's du'ā for fear of Allah (p. 103):** "O Allah, grant us a portion of Your fear that comes between us and our sins." (al-Tirmidhī and al-Ḥākim from Ibn 'Umar; graded ḥasan by al-Albānī, Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmi' 1268)

- **The Prophet ﷺ on complete īmān (p. 104):** "Whoever loves for Allah, hates for Allah, gives for Allah, and withholds for Allah has completed īmān." (Abū Dāwūd) — complete īmān means all sentiments directed by knowledge of Allah.
