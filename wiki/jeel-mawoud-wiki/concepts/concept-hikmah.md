# الحكمة (al-Ḥikmah) — Wisdom

## Definition

الحكمة (al-ḥikmah) — "wisdom" — derives from the root ح-ك-م (ḥa-ka-ma), meaning "to judge, to decide wisely, to prevent." Al-ḥikmah in Islamic usage encompasses: (1) comprehensive understanding of the religion in its totality; (2) knowledge of priorities, balances, and realities (fiqh al-awlawiyyāt, fiqh al-mawāzināt, fiqh al-wāqi'); (3) the correct application of knowledge in the right measure, at the right time, in the right way; and (4) the attribute of moderation, balance, and the middle way (al-i'tidāl, at-tawāzun, al-wasaṭiyyah) that prevents excess.

In al-Hilali's methodology, al-ḥikmah is not an independent attribute of the promised generation but rather the **fruit of the fifth pivot** — correct understanding of Islam (al-fahm aṣ-ṣaḥīḥ lid-dīn). It functions as a **regulator** (ḍābiṭ) that prevents the other four knowledges from driving the believer to extremism. Without ḥikmah, the strength of īmān in Allah and the Hereafter, combined with caution against the world and the self, might push a person to abandon the world entirely or impose excessive strictness.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Attribute of Moderation as Fruit of Understanding (Ch. 3, pp. 109–111)

Al-Hilali treats ḥikmah under the tenth attribute of the promised generation: moderation and balance (al-i'tidāl wa at-tawāzun). He writes: "As for the attribute of moderation and balance — it is the tenth attribute — it is a fruit of correct understanding of the religion in its comprehensiveness, and the ranks of its rulings. It is also a fruit of knowledge of the fiqh of priorities, the fiqh of balances, the fiqh of reality, the fiqh of objectives, and the fiqh of disagreement" (p. 109).

### Ḥikmah as Regulator (Ḍābiṭ) (pp. 109–110)

The critical function of ḥikmah: "The importance of this pivot — the pivot of understanding — as a regulator that prevents the extremism of actions that the strength of īmān in Allah and the Last Day, and the strength of caution against the world and the self, might drive one to" (p. 109).

Without this regulator, the four knowledges can produce distortion: "It is expected that when these four knowledges take hold of the servant, they may drive him to abandon the world or impose strictness on the self and prevent it some of its permissible pleasures. They may drive him to strictness where strictness is not appropriate, and leniency where leniency is not appropriate" (p. 109).

### Ḥikmah, I'tidāl, Tawāzun, and Wasaṭiyyah as Fruits of Fahm (p. 109)

"From here, wisdom and moderation and balance and the middle way became good fruits of correct understanding of the religion" (p. 109). This means ḥikmah is not merely intellectual — it is the practical outworking of comprehensive religious understanding. The one who understands Islam correctly will naturally be balanced, moderate, and wise.

### The Five Fiqhs That Produce Ḥikmah (p. 109)

Al-Hilali identifies five specific types of understanding (fiqh) that produce ḥikmah:

1. **Fiqh al-awlawiyyāt** — understanding priorities: knowing what comes first and what comes later, what is essential and what is secondary
2. **Fiqh al-mawāzināt** — understanding balances: knowing how to weigh competing goods and competing harms
3. **Fiqh al-wāqi'** — understanding reality: knowing the actual conditions, constraints, and opportunities of one's context
4. **Fiqh al-maqāṣid** — understanding objectives: knowing the higher purposes (maqāṣid) of the Sharī'ah and working toward them
5. **Fiqh al-khilāf** — understanding disagreement: knowing how to navigate legitimate differences of opinion without extremism or takfīr

### The Fifth Pivot (p. 109)

"Meaning that correct understanding of Islam is the fifth pivot from which the attributes of the promised generation launch" (p. 109). Without this pivot, the other four knowledges — however deep — will produce a deformed character: zealous but imbalanced, sincere but extreme, cautious but rigid.

### In Chapter 5: Ḥikmah in the Generation's Formation (pp. 183–185)

When al-Hilali outlines the three formation pivots (al-maḥāwir) for building the promised generation, ḥikmah appears as the third:

1. Deep īmān in Allah and the Last Day (p. 183)
2. Tazkiyat an-nafs with mujāhadah (p. 184)
3. **Correct understanding of Islam in its comprehensiveness, balance, and moderation, and knowledge of the fiqh of objectives, balances, priorities, and the reality that surrounds the da'wah** (p. 185)

He stresses that all three pivots must have the Quran as their starting point: "These three pivots must have the Quran as their fundamental starting point. The Quran is the primary instrument of change… without it, the product will not be complete and the fruits will not be ripe" (p. 185).

### Ḥikmah in Da'wah (Ch. 1, pp. 14–15)

In Chapter 1, al-Hilali mentions ḥikmah in the context of the generation's approach to da'wah: "Especially if the members of the promised generation adopt the method of wisdom in da'wah, and present the concept of change to people in a loved and simple way that everyone can understand" (p. 15). Ḥikmah here means presenting the message in a way appropriate to the audience's capacity and circumstances — not overwhelming them and not diluting the truth.

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

- **The Prophet's method of ḥikmah (implicit throughout Ch. 4):** The Prophet ﷺ was the model of ḥikmah in tarbiyyah. He did not rush the Sahabah's formation, did not overburden them, and tailored his guidance to each individual's capacity. His insistence on the Quran as the sole source during the formative period (p. 148) was itself an act of ḥikmah — protecting the purity of the foundational input.

- **The Sahabah's understanding of moderation (implicit):** The Sahabah did not abandon the world entirely. They married, worked, traded, and enjoyed what Allah permitted — but without letting these become their primary concern. Their ḥikmah was in maintaining the balance: using the world as a means, not an end.
