# الصبر والثبات — aṣ-Ṣabr wa ath-Thabāt: Patience & Steadfastness

## Definition

Aṣ-Ṣabr (الصبر) derives from the Arabic root ص-ب-ر (ṣ-b-r), meaning "to be patient, to endure, to persevere, to restrain." In the Qur'anic vocabulary, sabr encompasses three dimensions: patience in obedience to Allah (الصبر على الطاعة), patience against sin (الصبر عن المعصية), and patience in adversity (الصبر على البلاء). Ath-Thabāt (الثبات) means "firmness, stability, steadfastness" — the quality of remaining fixed and unshaken on one's course regardless of the pressures.

Al-Hilali presents sabr and thabat together as the **eighth attribute** of the promised generation — a generation he calls "الجيل الصابر" (the patient generation). He argues that the path to tamkin is not "carpeted with roses and basil" but is a difficult, rugged, long road filled with thorns and obstacles, and that sabr and thabat are the only viable response.

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### The Nature of the Path

Al-Hilali opens with a vivid description (pp83–84): "One of the fundamental attributes of the promised generation of victory and empowerment is that it is a patient generation... that endures the pressures it faces, overcomes the obstacles it encounters, and stands firm before the trials and tribulations it is exposed to on its path to achieving its desired goal."

The path: "a difficult, rugged, long road... filled with thorns and obstacles... on its sides stand the devils of jinn and mankind working hard to stop the journey, and laying traps to prevent them from reaching the goal for which they have dedicated their lives."

The promised generation knows the nature of this path and therefore: "They prepared themselves for it, held fast to their Lord, and made a covenant with Him to proceed forward on this path — either to die in it, striving in the path of their Lord, patient and steadfast on their covenant, or to reach their goal" (p83).

He cites the verse: {مِنَ الْمُؤْمِنِينَ رِجَالٌ صَدَقُوا مَا عَاهَدُوا اللَّهَ عَلَيْهِ فَمِنْهُم مَّن قَضَى نَحْبَهُ وَمِنْهُم مَّن يَنتَظِرُ وَمَا بَدَّلُوا تَبْدِيلًا} (Al-Ahzab:23) (p83).

### The Path of the People of Da'wah

Al-Hilali cites: {أَمْ حَسِبْتُمْ أَن تَدْخُلُوا الْجَنَّةَ وَلَمَّا يَأْتِكُم مَّثَلُ الَّذِينَ خَلَوْا مِن قَبْلِكُم مَّسَّتْهُمُ الْبَأْسَاءُ وَالضَّرَّاءُ وَزُلْزِلُوا حَتَّى يَقُولَ الرَّسُولُ وَالَّذِينَ آمَنُوا مَعَهُ مَتَى نَصْرُ اللَّهِ أَلَا إِنَّ نَصْرَ اللَّهِ قَرِيبٌ} (Al-Baqarah:214) (p84).

He then quotes al-Salabi: "Trial is firmly connected to empowerment — a close connection. For the sunnah of Allah has always been that He does not grant empowerment to a nation until it passes through various stages of selection, and until its metal is refined in the crucible of events, so that Allah separates the impure from the pure. This is a sunnah that runs through the Islamic ummah without exception. Allah has willed to test the believers and try them, to purify their faith, and then empowerment in the earth comes after that" (p84, from al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah by 'Ali al-Salabi 1/262–263).

### Al-Shafi'i on Trial and Empowerment

Al-Hilali cites Imam al-Shafi'i: A man asked him: "Which is better for a person — to be empowered or to be tried?" Al-Shafi'i said: "One will not be empowered until he is tried. For Allah tried Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, and Muhammad — blessings and peace upon them all — and when they were patient, He empowered them. So let no one think he will be free of pain entirely" (p84, from al-Sirah al-Nabawiyyah by al-Salabi).

### The Only Response: Sabr and Thabat

Al-Hilali cites: {وَلَنَبْلُوَنَّكُمْ حَتَّى نَعْلَمَ الْمُجَاهِدِينَ مِنكُمْ وَالصَّابِرِينَ وَنَبْلُوَ أَخْبَارَكُمْ} (Muhammad:31) — "We will test you until We know the mujahidin among you and the patient, and We will test your reports" (p85).

And: {رَبَّنَا أَفْرِغْ عَلَيْنَا صَبْرًا وَثَبِّتْ أَقْدَامَنَا وَانصُرْنَا عَلَى الْقَوْمِ الْكَافِرِينَ} (Al-Baqarah:250) — "Our Lord, pour patience upon us, make our feet firm, and help us against the disbelieving people" (p85).

### The Sahabah's Patience in Makkah

Al-Hilali documents the extreme trials the early Muslims endured in Makkah (pp85–87):

- **Bilal ibn Rabah**: When the sun was at its most intense, Umayyah ibn Khalaf would drag Bilal out and throw him on his back in the scorching sands of Makkah, place a massive rock on his chest, and say: "You will stay like this until you die or disbelieve in Muhammad and worship al-Lat and al-'Uzza." Bilal — in that trial — said only: "Ahad! Ahad!" (One! One!) (p85, from Hayat al-Sahabah by al-Kandhlawi).

- **The family of Yaṣir**: 'Uthman ibn 'Affan narrated: "I was walking with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ by the Bat-ha' of Makkah when 'Ammar, his father, and his mother were being tortured in the sun to make them leave Islam. Abu 'Ammar said: 'O Messenger of Allah, is this how it always is?' The Prophet said: 'صَبْرًا آلَ يَاسِرَ، اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِآلِ يَاسِرَ وَقَدْ فَعَلْتِ' — 'Patience, O family of Yaṣir. O Allah, forgive the family of Yaṣir, for You have already done so'" (p85, narrated by al-Hakim and Ibn 'Asakir).

- **The Prophet's counsel to Khabbab ibn al-Aratt**: Khabbab came to the Ka'bah complaining of the severe persecution and asking the Prophet to supplicate Allah for them. The Prophet's face turned red and he said: "There were people before you — one of them would be combed with iron combs so that nothing of his flesh or sinews remained, and that would not turn him from his religion! By Allah, this matter will be completed, until the rider travels from San'a' to Hadramawt fearing nothing except Allah and the wolf for his sheep — but you are hasty!" (pp86–87, narrated by al-Bukhari).

### Patience at the Battle of the Trench (Al-Ahzab)

Al-Hilali cites the extraordinary steadfastness at the Battle of the Trench (Al-Ahzab/Al-Khandaq): When the mushrikin gathered against the believers from every side, the believers recalled what Allah had told His Prophet about the trials they would face on their path to establishing the Islamic state. "This increased them in faith, submission to Allah, patience, and anticipating His reward." He cites: {وَلَمَّا رَأَى الْمُؤْمِنُونَ الْأَحْزَابَ قَالُوا هَذَا مَا وَعَدَنَا اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ وَصَدَقَ اللَّهُ وَرَسُولُهُ وَمَا زَادَهُمْ إِلَّا إِيمَانًا وَتَسْلِيمًا} (Al-Ahzab:22) (p87).

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

### Hadith

1. **Patience, O family of Yaṣir**: "صَبْرًا آلَ يَاسِرَ، اللَّهُمَّ اغْفِرْ لِآلِ يَاسِرَ وَقَدْ فَعَلْتِ" — narrated by al-Hakim and Ibn 'Asakir (p85). Grade not specified in source.

2. **The Prophet's counsel to Khabbab**: "There were people before you combed with iron combs..." — narrated by al-Bukhari (p87). Grade: Sahih.

### Sahabah Examples

- **Bilal ibn Rabah**: "Ahad! Ahad!" under torture in the midday sun (p85)
- **The family of Yaṣir**: Tortured for their faith; the Prophet promised them forgiveness (p85)
- **Khabbab ibn al-Aratt**: Came complaining of persecution; the Prophet reminded him of those before who suffered more (pp86–87)
- **The believers at the Battle of the Trench**: Trial increased their faith and submission (p87)

### Scholarly Opinions Cited

- **Al-Salabi**: "Trial is firmly connected to empowerment" (p84)
- **Imam al-Shafi'i**: "One will not be empowered until he is tried" (p84)
