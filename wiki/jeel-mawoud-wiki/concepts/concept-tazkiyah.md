# تزكية النفس (Tazkiyat an-Nafs) — Soul Purification

## Definition

تزكية النفس (tazkiyat an-nafs) — "purification of the self/soul" — derives from the root ز-ك-و (za-ka-wa), meaning "to grow, to increase, to purify, to make flourish." The noun form tazkiyyah/tazkiyah carries two complementary meanings: (1) **purification** (taṭhīr) — cleansing the self from its defects, diseases, and attachments to other than Allah; and (2) **growth** (tanmiyah) — nurturing the self's positive qualities: sincerity, love of Allah, fear of Him, reliance upon Him, humility, and all the virtues that constitute the attributes of the promised generation.

The term نفس (an-nafs) — "self, soul" — in this context refers specifically to the lower self (an-nafs al-ammārah bi-sū') with its inherent tendencies: love of desires (ḥubb ash-shahawāt), inclination to transgression (al-mayl ilā al-fujūr wa aṭ-ṭughyān), and commanding evil (al-amr bi-s-sū'). Tazkiyah is the mujāhadah (striving) against these tendencies to purify the self for Allah alone.

In al-Hilali's methodology, tazkiyat an-nafs is the **second of the three formation pivots** (al-maḥāwir ath-thalāthah) for building the promised generation — positioned between deep īmān (the first pivot) and correct understanding of Islam (the third). It is also the Prophet's mission as described in al-Jumu'ah 62:2: "yuzakkīhim" — "He purifies them."

## Al-Hilali's Treatment

### Īmān Alone Is Not Sufficient (Ch. 5, pp. 183–184)

Al-Hilali makes a critical point that many overlook: "Īmān alone is not sufficient, but it must be accompanied by striving against the self for sincerity and truthfulness. Otherwise, the self takes over good deeds and diverts their course from the path of sincerity to Allah and working for His pleasure, to a path that serves its own interests and magnifies its own status" (pp. 183–184). This is "a matter of extreme importance that many are unaware of" (p. 184).

The danger is that without tazkiyah, the self — even in the context of da'wah and righteous work — will seek its own glorification: "Going out with the da'wah among people will expose its possessor to praise and commendation and the spotlight… and this, if the possessor does not have humility and self-diminishment and seeing the self with the eye of deficiency and caution — it will be a disaster for its possessor" (pp. 184–185).

### Tazkiyah as the Second Formation Pivot (Ch. 5, p. 183)

Al-Hilali outlines three pivots (maḥāwir) for building the promised generation:

1. **Deep īmān in Allah and the Last Day** — produces reverence, love, hope, sincerity, and hastening in good deeds
2. **Tazkiyat an-nafs with the inherent striving against it (al-mujāhadah al-majbūlah 'alayhā)** — removing manifestations of self-inflation that, if left, "would become like an idol (ṣanam) that the person associates with his Lord in worship and seeking help" (p. 183)
3. **Correct understanding of Islam** — comprehensiveness, balance, priorities, reality

### The Self as Potential Idol (Ṣanam) (Ch. 5, p. 183)

A striking metaphor: "Removing any manifestations of its self-inflation, which if left would become like an idol (ṣanam) that the person associates with his Lord in worship and seeking help" (p. 183). The unchecked self becomes a rival to Allah — not openly, but subtly: the person worships Allah but also serves his own reputation; he seeks Allah's help but also depends on people's opinion. This hidden shirk (shirk al-khafī) is the most dangerous disease because it is invisible to the sufferer.

### The Quran as the Instrument of Tazkiyah (Ch. 4, pp. 146–148)

The Quran itself performs tazkiyah: "The Quran reveals to man the truth of his humble origin, and the extent of his weakness and inability, and his ignorance and the size of his needs required for survival, and that he is by Allah not by himself, and if He leaves him for the blink of an eye he perishes" (p. 147). It also "informs him of the nature of his self — its love of desires, its inclination toward transgression and tyranny, commanding evil — so he increases his caution against it, does not ascribe any merit to it, but rather strives against it and disciplines it upon truthfulness and sincerity" (p. 147).

This is a crucial insight: tazkiyah is not merely self-denial or ascetic practice. It is knowledge-driven transformation. The Quran reveals the self's true nature; the believer, recognising it, takes appropriate action.

### Knowledge of Self as the Foundation of Tazkiyah (Ch. 3, pp. 105–106)

Tazkiyah is built on the second of the five knowledges: ma'rifat an-nafs. "If one of us knew the truth of his origin, and the extent of his weakness, his need for his Lord, the severity of his poverty to Him… he would increase his caution against himself, his fear of it, and his sense of need for Allah" (p. 105). Knowledge of self produces:

- **Ikhlāṣ** — because the self can take credit for nothing
- **Tawakkul** — because the self is utterly dependent
- **Tawāḍu'** — "the origin of humility is the servant's considering himself small, seeing himself with the eye of deficiency, and seeing others as better than him" (p. 105)

### The Sahabah's Tazkiyah Through the Quran (Ch. 4, pp. 153–159)

The Sahabah's engagement with the Quran was itself an act of tazkiyah — not merely intellectual exercise but transformative encounter:

- **Ibn 'Abbās (p. 153):** He would recite an āyah, then pause to reflect on it. His tazkiyah was in the pause — letting the āyah work on his heart before moving on.

- **Asmā' bint Abī Bakr (p. 154):** She kept repeating āyah 27 of aṭ-Ṭūr — "So Allah has favoured us and saved us from the punishment of the Scorching Fire" — calling upon Allah with it for an extended period while someone went to the market and returned. Her tazkiyah was in the repetition — letting the gratitude and fear penetrate.

- **'Abbād ibn Bishr (pp. 158–159):** Shot by arrows while praying on guard duty, he refused to break his prayer because "I was in a sūrah I was reciting, and I did not want to cut it." His tazkiyah had reached such depth that the Quran's hold on him exceeded physical danger.

- **'Abdullāh ibn Mas'ūd (p. 154):** He spent an entire night repeating "My Lord, increase me in knowledge" (Ṭā Hā 20:114) — not for information but for the transformative knowledge that increases īmān and purifies the self.

### The Sahabah's Approach: Action Over Memorisation (Ch. 4, pp. 155–158)

Their tazkiyah prioritised 'amal (action) over ḥifẓ (memorisation): "It was difficult for us to memorise the words of the Quran, and easy for us to act upon it. And after us, there will come people for whom memorising the Quran will be easy but acting upon it will be difficult" (Ibn Mas'ūd, p. 156). "The foremost of the companions of the Messenger of Allah in the early days of this ummah would not memorise more than a sūrah or its like, but they were given acting upon the Quran" (Ibn 'Umar, p. 157).

Abū 'Abd ar-Raḥmān as-Sulamī, one of the Sahabah's students, said: "We used to learn the Quran from people who informed us that when they learned ten āyahs, they would not pass beyond them to another ten until they learned what was in them of action. So we learned knowledge and action together" (p. 158).

### Tazkiyah Especially Critical in the Beginning (Ch. 5, pp. 184–185)

Al-Hilali warns that the formative period (marḥalat at-takwīn al-ūlā) is when tazkiyah is most critical: "The self's exposure to praise and commendation and the spotlight — if the possessor does not have humility and self-diminishment and seeing the self with the eye of deficiency and caution — it will be a disaster for its possessor" (p. 184). Without tazkiyah early on, the self will co-opt the da'wah for its own glorification.

## Hadith & Sahabah Examples

- **Ibn Mas'ūd on the hearts as vessels (pp. 149–150):** "These hearts are vessels — fill them with the Quran and do not fill them with anything else." Tazkiyah requires exclusive devotion to the purifying source.

- **Ibn Mas'ūd on ḥifẓ vs. 'amal (p. 156):** "It was difficult for us to memorise the words of the Quran, and easy for us to act upon it." Tazkiyah is measured by action, not memorisation.

- **Abū 'Abd ar-Raḥmān as-Sulamī (p. 158):** "They would not pass beyond ten āyahs until they learned what was in them of action." Tazkiyah is gradual and action-based.

- **'Abbād ibn Bishr (pp. 158–159):** Continuing prayer despite arrows — tazkiyah so deep that the Quran's hold exceeds fear of death.

- **Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī (p. 155):** "The Messenger of Allah passed away and the memorisers from his companions had not completed memorisation, out of awe for it and their devotion to understanding its interpretation and acting upon its rulings." Tazkiyah was their priority over memorisation.
