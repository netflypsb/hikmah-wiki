# Chapter 5: Where Are We in Relation to the Promised Generation?

We want to serve our religion, but while keeping hold of our worldly life and our gains in it.

In such a state, we cannot become part of the Promised Generation. As mentioned earlier, divine support, aid, and victory will only be granted to those who fully meet the conditions. As long as we have not completed them, we will remain stuck where we are.

Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, rebuked the Companions—may Allah be pleased with them—severely because some of them did not want to go out with the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, on the expedition to Tabūk: partly because of the intense heat, and partly because the fruits were ripe.

Reflect with me on what Allah said to them:

> “O you who have believed, what is [the matter] with you that, when you are told to go forth in the cause of Allah, you adhere heavily to the earth? Are you satisfied with the life of this world rather than the Hereafter? But what is the enjoyment of worldly life compared to the Hereafter except a [very] little. If you do not go forth, He will punish you with a painful punishment and will replace you with another people, and you will not harm Him at all. And Allah is over all things competent.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:38-39]

If this was the rebuke given to the Companions despite their tremendous precedence in supporting Islam, what then if the Qur’an were revealed upon us today? What would Allah say to us in it?

So let us begin from now.

## A Sincere Reckoning

From here it becomes clear to us that if we wish to be among the children of the Promised Generation—the generation promised victory and empowerment—then a serious reckoning with ourselves is essential: rearranging our priorities, and honestly defining our goal.

We must have a clear vision of our role, and of the nature and function of this generation. It is not like other people in its dreams and ambitions, in its striving and movement.

Each of us—by the will of Allah—is capable of being a generation of change. All that is required is resolve, determination, and persistence; insistence upon Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, that He make us among them; turning to the Qur’an and its meanings with passion and longing, and surrendering our leadership to it; devoting much of our time to it; and joining hands with those who came before us and are striving to restore the glory of Islam anew.

Pay attention: there is a point that must be taken into account. Some of us may see many of the attributes of the Promised Generation already realized in himself; if that happens, it is still not enough, nor does it justify him walking alone. A generation of change is not scattered individuals here and there, unaware of one another. Rather, it is an interconnected, supportive generation that moves with balanced steps, each individual standing at one of the frontiers of the Muslim Ummah: awakening the heedless, guiding the aware, and uniting efforts.

There must be cooperation among all and mutual understanding around the Islamic project—the project of rebuilding the Ummah’s glory anew—so that each individual plays his role in it. And this, without doubt, calls for gathering efforts and coordinating between them.

## The Need to Lighten One’s Burdens

If the Promised Generation has particular attributes—as we have passed over—total attachment to Allah, the Exalted and Majestic; sincerity to Him; fear of Him; love for Him; humility; renunciation of the world; longing for the Hereafter; and unrelenting action and *jihād*—then those of us most prepared to complete these attributes are those who have not drowned in the sea of this world, and upon whose hearts heavy burdens have not been placed that pull them down to the earth and prevent them the freedom to move.

Reflect with me, dear reader, on this hadith whose authenticity is agreed upon. Abu Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, said that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> “A Prophet from among the Prophets went out on a military expedition and said to his people: ‘Let no man follow me who has recently taken possession of a woman’s private part and desires to consummate the marriage but has not yet done so; nor one who has built a building but has not yet raised its roof; nor one who has bought pregnant ewes or camels and is awaiting their offspring.’ He then set out, and when he drew near the village at the time of the afternoon prayer—or close to it—he said to the sun: ‘You are commanded and I am commanded. O Allah, hold it back for me a little.’ So it was held back for him until Allah granted him victory.” [Sahih al-Bukhari; Sahih Muslim]

Here, this Prophet—peace be upon him—set conditions for whoever wished to go out with him on the expedition. The core of these conditions is that the heart must not be occupied with, or attached to, anything of this world.

Imam al-Nawawī emphasized this meaning in his commentary on this hadith. He said—may Allah have mercy on him:

> “In this hadith is the lesson that important matters should only be entrusted to people of resolve and free hearts, and not to one whose heart is attached to something else, for that weakens his determination and prevents him from giving his full effort.” [Sahih Muslim, with commentary of Imam al-Nawawī, 12/279, Dar al-Ma‘rifah, Beirut]

This is what is required to be found in abundance in the Promised Generation.

## Paradise Is the Price

The same meaning occurred with the people of the Pledge of al-‘Aqabah. When the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, asked them to protect him and support his religion, he did not promise them anything of this world in return, so that their hearts would not become attached to it. Instead, he made the reward Paradise, despite his knowledge, peace and blessings be upon him, of what Allah would open for him and his Companions.

Jābir ibn ‘Abdullah said:

> “We said: ‘How long shall we leave the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, to be driven out in the mountains of Mecca in fear?’ So seventy men from among us went out until, in the season of pilgrimage, we went to meet him at the defile of al-‘Aqabah. We gathered with him one by one or two by two until we all met. We said: ‘O Messenger of Allah, we pledge allegiance to you.’ He said: ‘Pledge to me that you will hear and obey in ease and hardship, spend in difficulty and plenty, enjoin good and forbid evil, and speak the truth in Allah without fearing the blame of any blamer; and that you will support me when I come to you against whatever you protect of yourselves, your wives, and your children. For you is Paradise.’ We stood and pledged allegiance to him. He took the hand of Sa‘d ibn Zurārah—the youngest among us—and said: ‘Slowly, O people of Yathrib, for we did not tire the backs of our camels except knowing that he is the Messenger of Allah, and that to bring him out today is to break with all the Arabs and to have your best men killed and your swords bite one another. So either you are a people who will bear that and your reward is with Allah, or you fear cowardice in yourselves; make that clear, for it is an excuse for you before Allah.’ They said: ‘Give us respite, O Sa‘d. By Allah, we will never abandon this pledge nor strip it from ourselves.’ He said: ‘So we stood and pledged allegiance to him. He took a pledge and a condition from us, and promised us Paradise in return.’” [Al-Sīrah al-Nabawiyyah by Al-Ṣallābī, 1/460]

And the Anṣār remained true to their pledge, defending Islam and protecting its gains. They grew numerous in times of fear and few in times of greed. They continued to support their immigrant brothers, even preferring them over themselves. All of that in hope of the condition upon which they pledged allegiance: Paradise.

Al-Bazzār narrated on the authority of Jābir, may Allah be pleased with him, that he said:

> “When the Anṣār divided their date palms, a man’s share would be divided into two portions, one of them smaller than the other. Then they would put the spadix with the smaller portion, and offer the Muslims the choice. The Muslims would take the larger portion, and the Anṣār would take the smaller one, because of the spadix, until Khaybar was conquered. Then the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: ‘You have fulfilled your duty to us. So if you wish, you may take your share of Khaybar and the produce of your palms will be yours.’ They said: ‘We had a condition upon you and you had a condition upon us, that we would have Paradise. We have done what you asked, so we must have our condition.’ He said: ‘Then Paradise is yours.’” [Ḥayāt al-Ṣaḥābah, 1/292]

This is what Islam wants today: a generation that looks to nothing of the rubble of this world; a generation whose gaze is fixed on the heavens, always striving for Allah’s pleasure and for the attainment of His Paradise. Its model is the saying of the Exalted:

> “Indeed, Allah has purchased from the believers their lives and their properties [in exchange] for that they will have Paradise.” [Qur’an, At-Tawbah 9:111]

There is no doubt that all of us are capable of being among the children of this generation if we are able to remove the burdens that pull our feet, our bodies, and our hearts down to the earth.

And since young people are the least burdened among us, they are without doubt the most ready to complete the attributes of the Promised Generation. They are also distinguished by intense emotion, determination, courage, and high ambition. This does not mean that we are not fit for it. Rather, we are fit—by Allah—if we resolve, purify our intention, lighten our burdens, and make Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, alone our goal. Then we will be like the young in ambition, emotion, and burning resolve.

## This Is Not a Call to Monasticism

This does not mean, dear reader, that what is required of the generation of hope is that it should not marry, work, or the like. Rather, the point is that one should prepare himself for the great role entrusted to him, sense the importance of that role, and know that it takes precedence over everything else; and that he should do his utmost to complete whatever attributes he lacks before entering the arena of life and having his heart attached to this world.

There is no doubt that if he enters the arena of life after having taken great steps in building himself, completing what he lacks, and feeling the weight of the trust placed upon his shoulders, he will adapt his life according to the reality and the time that Allah has placed him in. He is not an ordinary person whose role in life is merely to work, marry, have children, strive to secure their worldly future, and then die. No; his sublime goal in this world is to awaken his Ummah and guide its emotions and leadership through hardship and ease.

This goal requires him to adapt all of his circumstances in service of it.

Yes, he will marry, but he will choose a wife who helps him achieve his goal. He will search for her extensively until he finds one like himself, who strives as he strives and thinks as he thinks, so that together they may form a model Muslim home concerned with a single cause: the cause of Islam. They will not care whether their dwelling is spacious or narrow, or whether their furniture is new or old. Their home will be whatever is available, and their clothing whatever they can find. They will bear the hardship of a rough life and the austerity resulting from the husband’s limited income, because he has accepted a simple job that does not consume much of his time, leaving it free for the greatest mission entrusted to him.

As for their children, together they will work on raising them on the love of Allah, attachment to Him, striving for Paradise, renunciation of the world, and caution against their own souls, so that they may become, with them, children of the Promised Generation.

## Axes of Formation

For these promising young generations, it is appropriate to focus with them on the three axes from which the attributes of the Promised Generation emanate:

### First: Deep Faith in Allah and the Last Day

Deep faith in Allah and in the Last Day moves the heart, binding it constantly to Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and causing its possessor to worship his Lord as though he sees Him, and to weave the events of his life around Him, glorified be He.

What is required is a faith that produces magnification of Allah’s greatness in the soul, driving one to love, hope, and sincerity toward Him, glorified be He, as well as to compete in good deeds.

Along with deep faith comes faith in the Last Day as a driving force that pushes the servant to strive and persistently pursue Paradise and salvation from the Fire.

### Second: Purification of the Soul

Purification of the soul along with the natural stinginess embedded in it, and removing any manifestations of its inflation, which—if left unchecked—would become like an idol that a person associates with his Lord in worship and in seeking help. This is a matter of the utmost importance, and many people are heedless of it. Faith alone is not enough; rather, it must be accompanied by a *jihād* of the soul for truthfulness and sincerity. Otherwise, that soul will dominate over righteous deeds, diverting them from the path of sincerity to Allah and action for His pleasure into a path that serves its own interests and magnifies its own importance.

This is something that demands great attention and focus, especially at the beginning—during the first stage of formation—because going out to call people among others will expose one to praise, compliments, and spotlight. If this is not accompanied by humility and belittling of the self, and viewing it with an eye of deficiency and caution, it will become a calamity for him.

### Third: Correct Understanding of Islam

Correct understanding of Islam in its comprehensiveness, balance, and moderation; knowledge of the *fiqh* of objectives, balances, and priorities; and knowledge of the reality surrounding the caller.

## Returning to the Qur’an

These three axes must have their fundamental starting point in the Qur’an. For the Qur’an is the primary factory of change—as we have passed over—and without it the result will not be complete nor the fruit ripe.

Yes, there is no harm in using any means that serves these three axes, but let its starting point be the Qur’an. The Prophetic Sīrah, for example, has a great role in presenting the model of the first generation. This Sīrah must be connected to the Qur’an and studied as a practical, applied model of it. Islamic history is likewise. As for the Sunnah, it is the twin of the Qur’an; we must approach it from this perspective and regard it as an elucidation of what has been summarized in the Qur’an.

## The Qur’an Is the Starting Point

The greatest guarantee for the continuance of an individual’s growth and for his carrying out the various directives and duties is the strength of his faith in Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and his attachment to the Hereafter. And the best continuous and renewed source of faith is the Qur’an:

> “The believers are only those who, when Allah is mentioned, their hearts become fearful, and when His verses are recited to them, it increases them in faith.” [Qur’an, Al-Anfāl 8:2]

The Qur’an has an amazing ability to stir the emotions, to dominate them, and to direct them toward Allah, the Exalted and Majestic. And that is not all. The Qur’an has a wondrous quality—found nowhere else—which is its ability to keep whoever deals with it properly in a permanent state of ambition, activity, fervor, and positivity. This is through its continuous generation of energy within the soul of its reader every time he reads it, responds to it, and his emotions are moved by it. This energy will drive him to spend it in various good deeds, which the Qur’an has indicated, making him the most active and productive of people with himself, with his family, and in his da’wah environment. Therefore, the greatest effort at the beginning should be directed toward a true return to the Qur’an, benefiting from its miracle, entering into the circle of its influence, and drawing from the springs of faith within it. When that is achieved, completing the three axes—faith, purification, and understanding—becomes the easiest of things.

## The Meaning of Returning to the Qur’an

It should be pointed out that returning to the Qur’an does not mean that an individual sets for himself a program to memorize the Qur’an in a short period, or to complete it in a few days. Rather, the point is to return to the true value of the Qur’an, which Allah revealed for it to be a divine means and instrument for guidance, healing, correction, and change. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> “Indeed, this Qur’an guides to that which is most suitable.” [Qur’an, Al-Isrā’ 17:9]

This is the true role of the Qur’an. Its recitation or memorization are only means to facilitate benefiting from its miracle. This means that the generation promised victory and empowerment must turn to the Qur’an with its entire being, devote the greatest amount of its time to it, occupy itself with it, and make it its first source of receiving. Among the things that help in this is setting aside a specific time for it every day, reading it in a quiet place far from noise so that one can benefit from it better, and expressing one’s emotions toward the verses through weeping and supplication. One should also prepare his heart before recitation by remembering death and the Hereafter. Allah, the Exalted, said:

> “But remind by the Qur’an whoever fears My threat.” [Qur’an, Qāf 50:45]

Along with these means, the reading should be calm, deliberate, and in *tartīl*; likewise with focus and without distraction. The overall meaning of the verse should be the intended object, and one should not stop at the interpretation of every word. Rather, one should allow the verses of the Qur’an to flow within him and for their effect to rise gradually until they dominate the emotions and direct them toward Allah, the Exalted and Majestic. This is the highest purpose of reading. Reflection alone is not enough; rather, there must be emotional impact, through which faith increases, energy is generated, and effective change takes place.

When one of us finds his heart moved and stirred by a verse, he should repeat it again and again, for every time he does so, faith increases in his heart. There is no upper limit to this repetition. As long as the response is present, the repetition continues; when the response stops, we move on to the verses after it.

As for memorization, one should follow the method of the Companions in this: taking a few verses, living with them, reading their commentary, and applying what they indicate of character and behavior; and not moving on to others until he is certain that he is acting upon them. All of this is so that his benefit from the Qur’an may be good and its verses become an argument for him, not against him.

## The Project of Revival

My Muslim brother… my Muslim sister…

The Qur’an is the project of revival for the entire Ummah, and it is the rope that Allah has sent down from heaven to save us from what we are in. So what are we going to do?

First, we must hold fast to this rope and turn to it with the eagerness of a thirsty man to water—or greater—and give it the best and most of our time. For the solution—certainly—to emerge from the impasse we are living in begins with clinging to this Book. If we do that—and it is easy for everyone who sincerely wills it—

Second, we must call people to the Qur’an, spread its spirit among them, and lift up whoever we can from the pull of the earth and clay, connecting them to the rope of the Qur’an; and continue in that until Allah’s promise comes.

## Where Do I Begin?

But perhaps you say: “Yet I am alone now. I see what is happening to my Ummah of humiliation and disgrace, and I possess nothing but myself. I want to be a generation of change. So what do I do?”

The solution lies in clinging to the Qur’an and holding fast to it, acting upon what its verses indicate, and making use of the energy generated by it to undertake the various good deeds.

Yes, this is not enough, for a generation of change is interconnected, cohesive, and mutually supportive. It completes one another; its individuals stand at the frontiers of Islam and hold fast to one another. So it is necessary to search for them and be among them. If we cannot find them, then we must hold fast to the Qur’an and return to it in truth—not a formal return—and call those around us to it: fathers and mothers, wives and children, relatives, neighbors, and colleagues.

Let us move with this call—the call to return to the Qur’an—in every place, and let us not stop there. Rather, let us have our educational incubators, like the first incubators that existed in the house of al-Arqam and in the homes of the Companions. We have in the story of the Islam of ‘Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb, may Allah be pleased with him, a proof of the importance of these incubators: he went to his sister and found Khubāb ibn al-Aratt teaching her and her husband verses of the Qur’an.

So there must be educational incubators in our homes: the husband with his wife and children, the brother with his brothers. According to the sincerity and selflessness we show in that, the day will come when we meet those who walk with us on the path of Allah, and we will place our hands in theirs to form, together…

*The generation promised victory and empowerment.*
