# Chapter Four: The First Generation and Instruments of Change

## Page 146

When we look at the first generation — the generation of the Companions (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum) — who stand before us as the model that fulfilled the conditions of victory and empowerment (an-naṣr wa at-tamkīn), so Allāh fulfilled His promise with them and empowered them upon the earth to settle therein wherever they wished…

…If we look at them and investigate the tools they used in order to reach this refined level, we find that three essential matters shared in producing that result: the methodology of change, which is the Qur'an; the educator supervising the process of change, who is the Messenger (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam); and the environment in which change took place and through which the cohesive, mutually supportive generation was formed.

### Methodology of Change

If most of the attributes of the Promised Generation endowed with victory and empowerment arise from the strength of knowledge of Allāh, Exalted and Majestic is He, and of the Last Day, and likewise arise from the strength of *zuhd* in this world and caution against the self, then the Qur'an accomplishes all of that with ease and facility.

With Allāh: the Qur'an introduces the servant to his Lord to the utmost limit his mental capacity can bear, bringing him to the closest point a human being can reach after the Prophets — peace and blessings of Allāh be upon them.

And the Qur'an also connects that knowledge with the events of life, so the servant sees only the wisdom of Allāh behind his actions and His will, Glorified is He. That is reflected in his dealings until he reaches the level of *iḥsān* of worshipping Allāh as though he sees Him, so he seeks nearness to Him from close by, feels His closeness to him and His sustaining care over him, finds comfort in Him, and increases in longing for Him…

## Page 147

With the Hereafter: the Qur'an reveals to man the reality of his lowly origin, the extent of his weakness and incapacity, his ignorance, and the magnitude of his needs required for continuing to live, and that he is by Allāh, not by himself — and if He were to forsake him for the blink of an eye he would perish.

Along with clarifying these realities, it also makes him acquainted with the nature of his self that loves desires, inclines to immorality and transgression, and commands evil, so his caution against it intensifies. He does not attribute any virtue to it; rather, he strives against it and disciplines it upon truth and sincerity…

Thus when the servant links this knowledge with what happens in his life, the reality of his self becomes firmly established for him, and he lives forever a humble, broken servant to Allāh, free from anything besides Him.

### Purity of the Source

This grave role of the Qur'an cannot occur unless the servant devotes himself to it with great devotion, gives it his time, heart, and mind, and approaches it with his entire being and feeling, not mixing anything else with it — especially in the beginning — so that the result is sound, pure, and fruitful…

This is what happened with the Companions (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum). They realized the value of the Qur'an and that the true reason for its revelation is to guide people to Allāh and to lead them safely to Him, so they devoted themselves to it and dedicated themselves to it. Their teacher and educator, the teacher of humanity Muḥammad (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam), helped them in that, for he was keen that their reception be unified and the source from which the Companions drank be pure.

## Page 148

Look at him (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam): ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu) came to him one day with a book he had obtained from some of the People of the Book. He said, “O Messenger of Allāh, I have come by a good book from some of the People of the Book.” The Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) became angry and said, “Are you wavering in it, O Ibn al-Khaṭṭāb? By the One in whose hand is my soul, I have come to you with it bright and pure. Do not ask them about anything, for they may tell you something true and you will disbelieve in it, or something false and you will believe in it. By the One in whose hand is my soul, if Mūsā were alive he would have no choice but to follow me.”[1]

Thus the Qur'an was not alone in their arena; there were also the books of the Jews and Christians, and there was the heritage of neighboring civilizations. Yet he (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) was keen that the source from which the Companions drank, the sources of their formation, be pure so that the product would be pure.

And the martyr Sayyid Quṭb — may Allāh have mercy on him — emphasizes this meaning in his comment on this incident, saying:

“Thus the Messenger of Allāh intended to restrict the source from which that generation drank during the formative period to the Book of Allāh alone, so that their souls would be devoted solely to it and their disposition would be straight according to its methodology alone. Hence he was angry when he saw ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu) drinking from another source.”

The Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) wanted to forge a generation pure in heart, pure in mind, pure in conception, pure in feeling, and pure in formation from any influence other than the divine methodology contained in the Qur'an.[3]

### Whoever Tastes, Knows

The Companions tasted the sweetness of occupying themselves with the Qur'an, realized its value, and witnessed the fruit of that in their reality, which changed radically. They became other personalities than those they had been before Islām, as if they had been born anew.[4]

From this arose their intense keenness to convey this message to those after them, and they were distressed by people’s occupation with anything other than the Qur'an. Here is ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu): two men from the *Tābiʿīn* came to him with a scroll, wanting him to read it. They said, “This is a scroll containing fine words.” He said, “Bring it. O servant, bring the basin and pour water into it,” and he began erasing it with his hand, saying: “We relate to you, [O Muḥammad], the best of stories in what We have revealed to you of this Qur'an.” (Qur'an, Yūsuf 12:3). We said…

## Page 150

“Look at it, for it contains amazing speech,” and he kept erasing it, saying: “Indeed these hearts are vessels; fill them with the Qur'an and do not fill them with anything else.”[5]

### The Qur'anic Miracle

Thus the Companions’ keenness to convey their Prophet’s counsel to occupy themselves with the Qur'an alone, especially during the first formative period, was a result of their vision of the fruit of the Qur'anic miracle that had produced in them a radical change and revived in them true life: “And is one who was dead and We gave him life and made for him light by which to walk among the people like one who is in darkness, never to emerge therefrom?” (Qur'an, al-Anʿām 6:122).

They realized the value of light after living long years in darkness.

If you say: Was not the tremendous change that happened to the Companions, which was reflected upon their reality, due to the presence of the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) among them?!

## Page 151

Yes, the presence of the Messenger (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) among the Companions had a great role in regulating, refining, and rightly guiding the process of change by virtue of his being a teacher and educator — as will be explained. But as for the methodology of change and the factory into which the Companions entered and which transformed them, that was without doubt the Qur'an, about which Allāh the Exalted said: “If We had sent down this Qur'an upon a mountain, you would have seen it humbled and coming apart from fear of Allah.” (Qur'an, al-Ḥashr 59:21).

And Sayyid Quṭb — may Allāh have mercy on him — emphasizes this meaning, saying:

“If the presence of the person of the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) were indispensably necessary for the establishment of this call and the bearing of its fruits, Allāh would not have made it a call to all people, nor made it the final message, nor entrusted the affairs of people on this earth to it until the end of time.”

But Allāh, Glorified is He, undertook to preserve the Reminder, and knew that this call could continue after the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) and could bear its fruits, so He chose him to be near Him after twenty-three years of messengership, and preserved this religion after him until the end of time.

Indeed, the first source from which that generation drank is the spring of the Qur'an… The hadīth of the Messenger and his guidance were but traces of that spring. When ʿĀʾishah (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhā) was asked about the character of the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam), she said: “His character was the Qur'an.”[6]

## Page 152

### Al-Madīnah Was Opened by the Qur'an

Perhaps one of the clearest examples confirming this meaning — that the Qur'an is what opened al-Madīnah — is what Imām Mālik and others said, and that was before the migration of the Messenger (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) to it. After the Pledge of ʿAqabah, he (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) sent Muʿṣab ibn ʿUmayr to Yathrib with what he had of the Qur'an… So what happened?

Light entered the hearts of the people of Yathrib and they were filled with faith; concepts and concerns changed, the factions united, and they all gathered upon one word. They held fast to the strong rope of Allāh, which is the Qur'an, so there occurred among them what was of amazing degree in giving, sacrificing, and preferring others… All of that happened before he (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) came to them. The proof of that is what they did with their emigrant brothers in solidarity and preference regarding houses, wealth, and fruits despite their poverty and severe need; and this would not have happened without the high level of faith they reached through the Qur'an: “And [also for] those who were settled in al-Madinah and [adopted] the faith before them. They love those who emigrated to them and find not any want in their breasts of what the emigrants were given but give [them] preference over themselves, even though they are in privation. And whoever is protected from the stinginess of his soul — it is those who will be the successful.” (Qur'an, al-Ḥashr 59:9).

## Page 153

### The Concept of Occupation with the Qur'an

Thus, occupation with the Qur'an, benefiting from its miracle, and entering into the sphere of its effect is the principal factor that changed the Companions and made them that unique generation of which humanity is proud to this day.

And when we speak of the Companions’ occupation with the Qur'an, this means, first and foremost, their occupation with its recitation — the true recitation. As Ibn ʿAbbās said concerning the meaning of “true recitation”: “That is, they follow it as it deserves to be followed.”[7]

The true value of the Qur'an lies in its ability to change, and this undoubtedly requires understanding its meanings, being affected by them, and acting according to their requirements… ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu) said: “Do not be deceived by one who recites the Qur'an; it is merely speech that we speak, but rather look at one who acts by it.”[8]

### An Example of the Companions’ Way of Reading the Qur'an

And if you want an example of the Companions’ way of reading the Qur'an, here is this report from Abū Dharr — may Allāh have mercy on him — from Ṣāliḥ, who said: “I was a neighbor of Ibn ʿAbbās (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhuma), and he would perform *tahajjud* at night, reciting a verse then remaining silent for as long as I have told you — and that was long — then he would recite. I said: For what reason did he do that? He said: Because of interpretation; he reflects upon it.”[9]

## Page 154

And ʿIbād ibn Ḥamzah said: “I entered upon Asmāʾ (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhā) while she was reciting: ‘So Allah conferred favor upon us and protected us from the punishment of the Scorching Fire.’ (Qur'an, aṭ-Ṭūr 52:27). I stood before her repeating it and supplicating, and that lasted long, so I went to the market and did my need, while she was still repeating it and supplicating with it.”[10]

### No Substitute for Reflection

Thus, the meaning of occupation with the Qur'an is not merely frequent recitation with the tongue without reflecting on its meanings with the mind or moving the heart with it. If that happens, it will not achieve the purpose of the Qur'an and what was revealed to realize.

It was said to the lady ʿĀʾishah (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhā) that some people recite the Qur'an two or three times in a night. She said: “They read and do not read. The Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) used to stand at night in prayer reciting al-Baqarah, Āl ʿImrān, and an-Nisāʾ; he did not pass by a verse containing glad tidings except that he asked Allāh the Most High and desired, nor by a verse containing fear except that he sought refuge.”[11]

## Page 155

And when a man said to ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu), “Indeed I recite al-Mufaṣṣal in one rakʿah,” ʿAbd Allāh said: “This is like hasty poetry? There are people who recite the Qur'an and it does not pass beyond their throats, but when it settles in the heart it benefits.”

And al-Nawawī commented in his explanation of Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim on the saying of Ibn Masʿūd, saying: “Its meaning is that there are people whose share of the Qur'an is only its passage over the tongue, so it does not pass beyond their throats to reach their hearts; and that is not what is sought. Rather, what is sought is to understand it and reflect upon it until it settles in the heart.”[12]

### The Spring of Faith

The Companions dealt with the Qur'an according to its true reality as a book of guidance, healing, change, and correction; therefore their concern was not directed toward speed of memorization or reading the greatest number of its verses, and there were not many memorizers among them despite their intense occupation and devotion to the Qur'an. Why? Because their primary concern was directed toward acting by it and changing through it.

They tasted the sweetness of faith through this book, so that preoccupied them from memorizing it, despite the importance of memorization as a means facilitating recitation of the Qur'an at any time.

## Page 156

Al-Ḥasan al-Baṣrī — may Allāh have mercy on him — said: “Indeed the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) died and none of his Companions (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum) had completed memorizing the Qur'an except the very few, out of reverence for it and attending to themselves with memorizing its interpretation, acting by its firm verses, and [reflecting upon] its ambiguous verses.”[13]

And Ibn Masʿūd said: “Indeed, memorizing the words of the Qur'an was hard for us, while acting by it was easy for us; and those after us will find memorizing the Qur'an easy, while acting by it hard for them.”

If you say: And why was memorizing the Qur'an hard for them while they witnessed its revelation over twenty-three years, and they were the people of the Arabic language and the most entitled to it?

## Page 157

The answer: yes, it is natural that they would be the most people to memorize the Qur'an, but they were not like that because their greatest concern was directed toward acting by the Qur'an, especially since they realized that the people of the Qur'an are the people who follow it. If one of them memorized its letters, that is a favor and blessing from Allāh, as ʿAbd Allāh ibn ʿAmr ibn al-ʿĀṣ (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhuma) said: “Whoever gathers the Qur'an has indeed carried a tremendous matter, for prophethood has been placed between his shoulders, yet it is not revealed to him.”[14]

And what confirms this meaning is that many of the senior Companions died without completing memorization of the Qur'an… Ibn Saʿd narrated in his *Ṭabaqāt* from Muḥammad ibn Sīrīn, who said: “ʿUmar was killed and had not compiled the Qur'an.”[15]

And Ibn ʿUmar (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhuma) said: “The virtuous among the Companions of the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) in the early part of this Ummah did not memorize from the Qur'an except a sūrah or so, and they were granted action by the Qur'an; and indeed the latter part of this Ummah will be granted the Qur'an — among them the child and the blind — but they will not be granted action by it.”[16]

## Page 158

### This Is Not a Call to Neglect Memorization

My dear reader, this should not be understood as a call to neglect memorization. Rather, the purpose is that each of us should review himself regarding his manner of dealing with the Qur'an, and that he should begin his matter from its beginning and not from its end. The first matter of the Qur'an is benefiting from its great miracle in guidance, healing, and change; and reading and memorization are only auxiliary means to this benefit. It is a serious error to make auxiliary means into intended goals, so that our concern becomes reading the greatest number of its verses without finding in it any positive change resulting from memorizing the Qur'an or frequent recitation with the tongue. For the Qur'an is not a drink of water that we drink quickly to quench our thirst; rather, it is a weighty word that needs slow sipping and careful consideration in its recitation, and likewise in its memorization — nay, even more so — and this was the state of the Companions (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum), who were the true people of the Qur'an and the most entitled to it…[17]

Abū ʿAbd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī, one of the students of the Companions, said: “Indeed we took the Qur'an from people who informed us that when they learned ten verses they did not pass beyond them to the next ten until they knew what was in them of action. So we learned knowledge and action together, and indeed there will come after us people who will drink the Qur'an as water is drunk; it will not pass beyond this — and he pointed to his throat.”[18]

## Page 159

### With ʿIbād ibn Bishr

From what preceded it becomes clear to us that the Companions benefited from the Qur'an when they dealt with its matter from the beginning, lived with its reality, and drank from the renewable springs of faith in it, so they became its people and were drawn to it. It dominated their feelings in an unbelievable manner. And to you, dear reader, is a wonderful example of that:

The Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) returned with those of the Companions who were with him from the expedition of Dhāt ar-Riqāʿ, and after severe effort night came and everyone wanted to sleep. Guards had to be posted to protect the Muslims while they slept, so two Companions, ʿIbād ibn Bishr and ʿAmmār ibn Yāsir, undertook this task. The two took turns at guarding, beginning with ʿIbād while ʿAmmār slept. When he saw that the place was suitable for prayer, one of the polytheists came and shot him with an arrow, so he removed it and completed his prayer. Then he shot him with a second arrow, so he removed it and completed his prayer. Then he shot him with a third, so he removed it and ended the recitation and woke ʿAmmār while he was prostrating. When ʿAmmār asked him why he had not woken him at first — he had been shot?

He answered: “I was in a sūrah that I was reciting, and I did not wish to interrupt it until I finished it. When the shooting continued against me, I bowed to inform you, and by Allāh, had the Messenger of Allāh (ṣallá Allāhu ʿalayhi wa sallam) not commanded me to guard it [the post], I would have cut off my soul before interrupting it or finishing it.”[19]

### Control Over the Emotions

So if you are in doubt about the Qur'an’s ability to dominate a human being’s feelings and control them, ask yourself: Why did ʿAbd Allāh ibn Masʿūd (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu) keep repeating the saying of the Exalted: “My Lord, increase me in knowledge.” (Qur'an, Ṭā Hā 20:114) through the night until morning? And why did ʿUmar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhu) keep repeating [the opening of] al-Fātiḥah throughout the night?[20]

And others, and others.

Indeed it is the sweetness of faith, the humility of the heart, the pleasure of true nearness to Allāh, and the feeling of change that occurs to them every time they repeat the verse that moved their hearts… So is there one who lives in these atmospheres and sees the light with his own eye, yet returns backward and exchanges what is inferior for what is better, reciting the Qur'an with his tongue while heedless of it?!

From here we realize the importance of the Qur'an as a fundamental methodology for the change that happened to the generation of the Companions (raḍiya Allāhu ʿanhum).
