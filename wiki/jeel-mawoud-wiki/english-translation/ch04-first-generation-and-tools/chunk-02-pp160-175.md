# Chunk 2 — Pages 160–175

## Translation of: Chapter Four — The First Generation and the Instruments of Change

**Quranic verses in this chunk:** 62:2, 9:40, 6:162–163

--- PAGE 160 ---

Along with this great and integrated *manhaj* (methodology) there had to exist a guide, a supervisor, and a follower of the progress of the process of change, to ensure the quality of the outcome and the goodness of its fruits. This important role was fulfilled by the teacher of humanity, the great educator, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him:

> ﴿هُوَ الَّذِي بَعَثَ فِي الْأُمِّيِّينَ رَسُولًا مِنْهُمْ يَتْلُو عَلَيْهِمْ آيَاتِهِ وَيُزَكِّيهِمْ وَيُعَلِّمُهُمُ الْكِتَابَ وَالْحِكْمَة﴾

*It is He who has sent among the unlettered a Messenger from themselves reciting to them His verses and purifying them and teaching them the Book and wisdom.* (al-Jumuʿah 62:2)

He, peace and blessings be upon him, was not merely a teacher who gave instructions to people and left them; rather, he was with them, living alongside them, following them, and guiding them. He explained the Qur'an and affirmed its meanings; he connected the events of life to Allah, Mighty and Majestic, made people desire the Hereafter, instilled *zuhd* (ascetic detachment) in worldly things, purified souls, corrected concepts, and organised activity. Here are examples that confirm this:

**In the sphere of connecting the events of life to Allah, Mighty and Majestic:**

We see him, peace and blessings be upon him, affirming to his Companions the meanings of faith in Allah, His names and attributes, and the meanings of servitude to Him, Glorified be He, and connecting these meanings with what happens in life. Thus during the Hijrah, when he and his companion Abū Bakr al-Ṣiddīq, may Allah be pleased with him, entered the cave of Thawr, Abū Bakr felt the polytheists near the cave, almost within sight of them, so he, peace and blessings be upon him, said to him as it came in the Qur'an:

> ﴿لَا تَحْزَنْ إِنَّ اللَّهَ مَعَنَا﴾

*Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us.* (al-Tawbah 9:40)

--- PAGE 161 ---

Anas ibn Mālik said: "I served the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and he never said to me about something I had done, 'Why did you do such-and-such?' nor about something I had not done, 'Why did you not do such-and-such?'" [Sahīh, narrated by Imām Aḥmad, al-Tirmidhī, and al-Ḥākim; authenticated by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 7957]

And he said: "When some member of his family rebuked me, he would say, 'Leave him, for if something had been decreed, it would have happened.'" [Narrated by Imām Aḥmad in his *Musnad*]

Ibn ʿAbbās, may Allah be pleased with them both, said: "I was riding behind the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, when he said: 'O young man, I shall teach you some words: Be mindful of Allah and He will be mindful of you; be mindful of Allah and you will find Him before you; when you ask, ask of Allah; when you seek help, seek help of Allah. Know that if the whole nation were to gather together to benefit you with anything, they would not benefit you except with something that Allah has already written for you; and if they were to gather together to harm you with anything, they would not harm you except with something that Allah has already written against you. The pens have been lifted and the pages have dried.'" [Sahīh, narrated by Abū Dāwūd and Ibn Mājah and Imām Aḥmad; authenticated by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 3564]

Look at him, peace and blessings be upon him, connecting what happens in life to Allah, Mighty and Majestic. Abū Hurayrah, may Allah be pleased with him, said: I heard the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, say:

> ﴿الرِّيحُ مِنْ رُوحِ اللَّهِ تَعَالَى: تَأْتِي بِالرَّحْمَةِ، وَتَأْتِي بِالْعَذَابِ، فَإِذَا رَأَيْتُمُوهَا فَلَا تَسُبُّوهَا، وَاسْأَلُوا اللَّهَ مِنْ خَيْرِهَا، وَاسْتَعِيذُوا بِاللَّهِ مِنْ شَرِّهَا﴾

"The wind is from the spirit of Allah, the Exalted: it comes with mercy and it comes with punishment. When you see it, do not curse it, but ask Allah for its good and seek refuge in Allah from its evil." [Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, and Imām Aḥmad; authenticated by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 3564]

--- PAGE 162 ---

He, peace and blessings be upon him, said:

> ﴿إِنَّ الشَّمْسَ وَالْقَمَرَ آيَتَانِ مِنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ: لَا يَخْسِفَانِ لِمَوْتِ أَحَدٍ وَلَا لِحَيَاتِهِ، وَلَكِنَّهُمَا آيَتَانِ مِنْ آيَاتِ اللَّهِ، يُخَوِّفُ اللَّهُ بِهِمَا عِبَادَهُ، فَإِذَا رَأَيْتُمْ ذَلِكَ فَصَلُّوا وَادْعُوا حَتَّى يُنْكَشِفَ مَا بِكُمْ﴾

"The sun and the moon are two signs among the signs of Allah: they are not eclipsed because of anyone's death or life; rather, they are two signs among the signs of Allah by which Allah frightens His servants. So when you see that, pray and supplicate until what has befallen you is relieved." [Agreed upon]

And besides these and many other hadīths with which he directed those around him from among his Companions, and his community after him, connect the course of events to Allah, Mighty and Majestic, so that attachment to Him, Glorified be He, and pure monotheism for Him increase.

**Encouraging hope in the Hereafter:**

As for his encouraging his Companions with Paradise and warning them of the Fire, how numerous are the hadīths that have reached us to confirm this meaning.

From Qubayṣah, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, when a third of the night had passed would stand and say: 'O people, remember Allah! The earthquake has come; whoever fears flees; whoever flees reaches shelter. Indeed the merchandise of Allah is precious; indeed the merchandise of Allah is Paradise. The earthquake has come, followed by the aftershock; death has come with all that it contains.'" [Narrated by al-Tirmidhī; he said: a *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ* hadīth]

--- PAGE 163 ---

In the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s, from al-Barāʾ, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: "A garment of silk was presented to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and they began to admire its softness, so he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Do not admire this; the handkerchiefs of Saʿd ibn Muʿādh in Paradise are better than this.'"

He, peace and blessings be upon him, used to spur the Companions' ambition to enter Paradise. From Usāmah ibn Zayd, may Allah be pleased with him, who said: "The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: 'Is there anyone who will gird himself for Paradise? For Paradise has no equal: by the Lord of the Kaʿbah, it is a light that shines, a basil-plant that sways, a palace firmly built, a flowing river, a ripe fruit, a beautiful and lovely spouse, many garments, an eternal abode in a secure dwelling, fruits and delight, and a blessing in a lofty and resplendent state.' They said: 'Yes, O Messenger of Allah, we are those who gird themselves for it.' He said: 'Say, if Allah wills.' The people said: 'If Allah wills.'" [Narrated by Ibn Mājah]

--- PAGE 164 ---

**Ascetic detachment from the world:**

He, peace and blessings be upon him, was keen to instill *zuhd* in the Companions and to detach their hearts from it. From Jābir, may Allah be pleased with him, that the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, passed through the market while the people were on his two sides—that is, on both sides of him—and he passed by a dead kid with drooping ears; he took it and seized it by the ear, then said: "Which of you would like to have this for a dirham?" They said: "We would not like to have it for anything; what would we do with it?" Then he said: "Would you like it to be yours?" They said: "By Allah, if it were alive it would be defective, for its ears droop—so how much more when it is dead!" He said: "By Allah, the world is more contemptible to Allah than this is to you." [Narrated by Muslim. *Asakk* means having small ears.]

Imagine with me the impact of this scene on the Companions present, and how they would deal with the world thereafter!

And one day, in the Fajr prayer, the mosque was filled with the Companions—may Allah be pleased with them—and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, knew that many of them had come from distant places because they knew that Abū ʿUbaydah ibn al-Jarrāḥ had brought wealth from Bahrain. He, peace and blessings be upon him, smiled when he saw them, then said: "I think you have heard that Abū ʿUbaydah has brought something." They said: "Yes, O Messenger of Allah." He said: "Rejoice and hope for what pleases you; for by Allah, I do not fear poverty for you, but I fear that the world will be spread out before you as it was spread out before those who were before you, so you will compete for it as they competed for it and it will destroy you as it destroyed them." [Agreed upon]

And when he sent Muʿādh ibn Jabal, may Allah be pleased with him, to Yemen, he said to him: "Beware of luxury, for the servants of Allah are not those who live in luxury." [Narrated by Imām Aḥmad from Muʿādh; graded *ḥasan* by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 2668]

--- PAGE 165 ---

**Purification of the soul:**

As for the sphere of purifying the soul from its predisposition to wickedness and from the greed ingrained in it, we find him, peace and blessings be upon him, directing his Companions to the deeds by which their souls are purified and trained in truthfulness and sincerity to Allah, Mighty and Majestic. The hadīths in this chapter are many; among them is his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> ﴿مَنْ تَعَظَّمَ فِي نَفْسِهِ، وَاخْتَالَ فِي مِشْيَتِهِ، لَقِيَ اللَّهَ وَهُوَ عَلَيْهِ غَضْبَانُ﴾

"Whoever exalts himself in his own soul and walks with an arrogant gait will meet Allah while He is angry with him." [Narrated by Imām Aḥmad from Ibn ʿAmr; authenticated by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 6157]

And his saying:

> ﴿مَنْ تَعَلَّمَ الْعِلْمَ لِيُبَاهِيَ بِهِ الْعُلَمَاءَ، أَوْ يُمَارِيَ بِهِ السُّفَهَاءَ، أَوْ يَصْرِفَ بِهِ وُجُوهَ النَّاسِ إِلَيْهِ، أَدْخَلَهُ اللَّهُ جَهَنَّمَ﴾

"Whoever seeks knowledge in order to boast with it before scholars, or to argue with it before fools, or to turn people's faces toward him, Allah will cast him into Hell." [Narrated by Ibn Mājah from Abū Hurayrah; authenticated by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 6158]

And in the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s it is reported that one day Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī and two men from the tribe of his uncle entered upon him. One of them said: "O Messenger of Allah, appoint me over some of what Allah, Mighty and Majestic, has entrusted to you." And the other said the like. So he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: "By Allah, we do not appoint anyone to this work who asks for it, or who is eager for it."

--- PAGE 166 ---

This is from the angle of protecting his Companions from themselves and closing the doors of temptation before them. As for his directing them to deeds that strengthen their sincerity to Allah, Mighty and Majestic, they are many; among them is his saying, peace and blessings be upon him:

> ﴿مَنْ اسْتَطَاعَ مِنْكُمْ أَنْ يَكُونَ لَهُ خَبْءٌ مِنْ عَمَلٍ صَالِحٍ فَلْيَفْعَلْ﴾

"Whoever among you is able to have a hidden store of good deeds, let him do so." [Ṣaḥīḥ, narrated by al-Ḍiyāʾ and al-Khaṭīb al-Baghdādī from al-Zubayr; included by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 6081]

And in the sphere of curing the soul from the greed ingrained in it, he, peace and blessings be upon him, would always urge his Companions to spend in the path of Allah. Look at him, peace and blessings be upon him, as he says to Bilāl, may Allah be pleased with him:

> ﴿أَنْفِقْ يَا بِلَالُ، وَلَا تَخْشَ مِنْ ذِي الْعَرْشِ إِقْلَالًا﴾

"Spend, O Bilāl, and do not fear diminution from the Owner of the Throne." [Narrated by Imām Aḥmad in his book *al-Zuhd*]

And from ʿAbdullāh ibn ʿUbayd, who said: "A man from the Anṣār came to the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and said: 'O Messenger of Allah, why is it that I do not love death?' He said: 'Do you have wealth?' He said: 'Yes, O Messenger of Allah.' He said: 'Then present your wealth before you.' He said: 'I cannot bear that, O Messenger of Allah.' He said: 'Then a person is with his wealth: if he gives it forward he will love to catch up with it, and if he leaves it behind he will love to lag behind with it.'" [Narrated by ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak in *al-Zuhd*, no. 634]

**Correcting understanding and teaching wisdom:**

He, peace and blessings be upon him, was keen to correct the understanding of his Companions and to teach them wisdom and the fiqh of dealing with the rulings of religion and the needs of the soul. So when the excessive austerity of ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn, may Allah be pleased with him, upon himself reached him, he said to him:

> ﴿يَا عُثْمَانُ! أَرَغِبْتَ عَنْ سُنَّتِي؟ فَإِنِّي أَنَامُ وَأُصَلِّي، وَأَصُومُ وَأُفْطِرُ، وَأُنْكِحُ النِّسَاءَ، فَاتَّقِ اللَّهَ يَا عُثْمَانُ! وَإِنَّ لِأَهْلِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَقًّا، وَإِنَّ لِضَيْفِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَقًّا، وَإِنَّ لِنَفْسِكَ عَلَيْكَ حَقًّا، فَصُمْ وَأَفْطِرْ، وَصَلِّ وَنَمْ﴾

"O ʿUthmān, have you turned away from my Sunnah? For I sleep and pray, fast and break the fast, and marry women; so fear Allah, O ʿUthmān. Indeed your family has a right upon you, and your guest has a right upon you, and your own soul has a right upon you; so fast and break the fast, pray and sleep." [Ṣaḥīḥ, narrated by Abū Dāwūd from ʿĀʾishah; included by al-Albānī in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ*, no. 7946]

Look at him teaching the Companions correct understanding and the fiqh of priorities when they saw him giving more of the spoils to those whose hearts were to be reconciled than to some of the early Emigrants, and he would say to them: "Indeed I give a man while another is dearer to me than he, for fear that he will be cast into the Fire on his face." [Agreed upon]

And when Allah opened Makkah and it became an abode of Islam, he, peace and blessings be upon him, resolved to demolish the Kaʿbah and return it to the foundations of Ibrāhīm, peace be upon him; but he was prevented from that—as Ibn al-Qayyim said—despite his ability to do it, for fear that something greater would result from Quraysh's inability to bear it because of their recent Islam and their being newly separated from disbelief. [*Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn*]

--- PAGE 168 ---

For in the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s he, peace and blessings be upon him, said to the Lady ʿĀʾishah: "O ʿĀʾishah, were it not that your people are recently come from Jāhiliyyah, I would have ordered the House to be demolished and would have inserted into it what was taken out of it, fastened it to the ground, and made for it two doors, an eastern door and a western door, so that it would reach the foundations of Ibrāhīm."

And among what he, peace and blessings be upon him, taught his Companions and his community after him is that he forbade cutting off hands during military expeditions, for fear that what is more hateful to Allah than it would follow from that—namely, disabling the expedition or delaying a Companion from catching up with the polytheists out of zeal and anger. [*Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn*, 3/3]

**Employing energies and directing activity:**

Along with his teaching, peace and blessings be upon him, and his directing his Companions to the doors of good, and along with his following them and undertaking the duties mentioned above, he also employed energies and organised the movement of individuals. Thus he commanded a group of them to emigrate to Abyssinia as a safe place that would protect them and protect the call from extermination; and among what he said, peace and blessings be upon him, was: "In the land of Abyssinia there is a king before whom no one is wronged; so go to his land, until Allah makes for you relief and a way out from what you are in." [Narrated by Abū Dāwūd, al-Nasāʾī, and al-Tirmidhī; authenticated by al-Albānī in *al-Mishkāt*, no. 3601]

--- PAGE 169 ---

And after the second Pledge of al-ʿAqabah he chose Muṣʿab ibn ʿUmayr to send to Yathrib to teach and call to Allah.

In al-Aḥzāb he chose Ḥudhayfah ibn al-Yamān—exclusively—to bring him news of the polytheists; and in Khaybar he gave the banner to ʿAlī ibn Abī Ṭālib. And he sent Abū Mūsā al-Ashʿarī and Muʿādh ibn Jabal to call the people of Yemen and teach them.

Thus he, peace and blessings be upon him, would employ energies, placing each person in the place appropriate to his capabilities. From here it becomes clear to us the importance of the educator's role and his functions, which appeared with clarity in the greatest educator humanity has ever witnessed: Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him.

**The educational incubators:**

Along with the methodology and the educator, the third element in the process of forming the generation promised victory and empowerment remains: namely, the educational atmosphere, the environment in which formation takes place. One who examines the biography of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his noble Companions and the manner in which this unique generation was formed will notice that he, peace and blessings be upon him, was not content with the Islam of the individual; rather, he would take him to the house of al-Arqam ibn Abī al-Arqam to be with his brothers, learning, purifying himself, and forming brotherhood with them.

Munīr al-Ghaḍbān said: "The house of al-Arqam became a new centre for the call in which the Muslims would gather and receive from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, every new revelation, and listen to him—peace and blessings be upon him—as he reminded them of Allah,

--- PAGE 170 ---

recited the Qur'an to them, and placed before him whatever was in their souls and their circumstances, so he would nurture them—peace and blessings be upon him—under his own eye, as they were nurtured under the eye of Allah, Mighty and Majestic." [*al-Tarbiyyah al-Qiyādiyyah li-Munīr al-Ghaḍbān*, 1/198, Dār al-Wafāʾ, Egypt]

The study material which the Prophet, peace and blessings be upon him, taught in the house of al-Arqam was the Noble Qur'an; for it was the sole source of reception. The Beloved Chosen One, peace and blessings be upon him, was keen to unify the source of reception and to make it exclusive, so that the Noble Qur'an alone would be the methodology and central idea upon which the individual Muslim, the Muslim family, and the Muslim community would be nurtured. The spirit of holiness would bring down verses, fresh and tender, upon the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and the Companions would hear them from the mouth of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, directly; so they would be poured into their hearts, seep into their spirits, and flow in their veins like blood. Their hearts and spirits would interact with the Qur'an and be moved by it, so that one of them would be transformed into a new human being in his values, feelings, aims, behaviour, and aspirations. The Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, was intensely keen that the Noble Qur'an alone should be the study material and the methodology upon which the souls of his Companions were nurtured, and that their instruction should not be mixed with anything other than the Qur'an. [al-Ṣallābī, *al-Sīrah al-Nabawiyyah*, 1/148, Dār al-Īmān, Alexandria, quoting *Dawlah al-Rasūl min al-Takwīn ilā al-Tamkīn*, p. 225]

--- PAGE 171 ---

**Education in Madīnah:**

After the Hijrah the educational environment expanded to include the Anṣār, so brotherhood was established between them and the Muhājirūn, and education continued in the newborn Muslim society, so that the signs of the dawn of empowerment began to appear gradually.

Yes, the picture of education may have differed from what it had been in the house of al-Arqam in Makkah, but its essence continued through the mosque, through the directions of the Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him, to the Companions, his living with them, and his concern for their conditions.

**Conclusion:**

In conclusion, the basic elements that contributed to forming the first generation are: the Qur'an, the educator, and the educational environment. Through these three elements there appeared in this generation the qualities that qualified it to enter into the circle of divine patronage and victory; so Allah fulfilled His promise with them, established them on the earth, and made them heirs to its kingdoms.

Yes, the most important of these elements—without doubt—is the Qur'an. Why should it not be, when it is the greatest miracle that descended from heaven, and whose greatest secret lies in its ability to revive hearts and change people radically? Then after that come the educator and the educational environment as refining and regulating elements for the change that the Qur'an brings about.

--- PAGE 172 ---

And there is no doubt that the Qur'an cannot fulfil its role in change with any human being unless he turns to it with the eagerness of one who desires to truly benefit from its miracle—as the Companions did. This requires him to occupy himself intensely with the Qur'an, to make it the first and principal source of reception, especially in the first formative period, and that his concern when reciting it should be to understand its meanings, be affected by its verses, and move his heart with them; that he beware of being concerned only with its wording; that he be deliberate in memorising its verses as much as possible so that he may act upon them; and that he constantly work to connect the Qur'an with the events of life. He must be patient with this manner of dealing so that the Qur'an may change his concerns and conceptions, gain control over his feelings, and entrench faith in Allah and the Last Day in them; so that the result after that will be a sincere servant of Allah, Mighty and Majestic, who repeats the saying of the Exalted:

> ﴿قُلْ إِنَّ صَلَاتِي وَنُسُكِي وَمَحْيَايَ وَمَمَاتِي لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ * لَا شَرِيكَ لَهُ وَبِذَلِكَ أُمِرْتُ وَأَنَا أَوَّلُ الْمُسْلِمِينَ﴾

*Say, "Indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds. No partner has He. And this I have been commanded, and I am the first [among you] of the Muslims."* (al-Anʿām 6:162–163)

**Translator's notes for Chunk 2:**

1. **"The teacher of humanity" (*muʿallim al-bashariyyah*) and "the great educator" (*al-murabbī al-ʿaẓīm*):** The author describes the Prophet as both the supreme teacher and the personal guide who accompanied, followed, and directed his students. This reflects the classical tarbiyyah model: not merely instruction but formation through presence.

2. **Page 160 Quran verse (62:2):** The author cites the verse that describes the Prophet's four functions—reciting, purifying, teaching the Book, and teaching wisdom. This is the foundational Quranic proof for the Prophet as educator.

3. **Anas ibn Mālik hadīth:** Cited as *ṣaḥīḥ* in the source, authenticated by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 7957). It illustrates the Prophet's gentle, non-blaming tarbiyyah with servants.

4. **Ibn ʿAbbās hadīth:** Famous *ḥadīth al-tawakkul* and the "lifted pens, dried pages" narration. Cited via Aḥmad, Abū Dāwūd, Ibn Mājah, and al-Tirmidhī; authenticated by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 3564).

5. **Wind and eclipse hadīths:** Both are narrated through multiple collections and authenticated by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 3564). They show the Prophet connecting natural events to Allah, teaching *tawḥīd* and proper supplication.

6. **Qubayṣah's hadīth on death:** Narrated by al-Tirmidhī as *ḥasan ṣaḥīḥ*. The phrase "the merchandise of Allah is Paradise" is preserved as in the original Arabic.

7. **Saʿd ibn Muʿādh's handkerchiefs:** Cited from al-Barāʾ in the two *Ṣaḥīḥ*s; the silk episode and the comparison to Paradise appear in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*.

8. **Usāmah's narration on girding for Paradise:** Cited as narrated by Ibn Mājah. The vivid description of Paradise's delights is kept as an extended quotation.

9. **Dead kid hadīth:** Narrated by Muslim. *Asakk* is explained in the source as meaning small-eared; this note is preserved in the translation.

10. **Abū ʿUbaydah wealth hadīth:** Agreed upon (*muttafaq ʿalayh*)—narrated in both *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*. It warns against the world spreading out before the Muslims as it did for previous nations.

11. **Muʿādh's instruction to avoid luxury:** Narrated by Imām Aḥmad from Muʿādh; graded *ḥasan* by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 2668).

12. **Self-exaltation and knowledge-seeking hadīths:** Both authenticated by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 6157 and 6158). They warn against pride and insincere seeking of knowledge.

13. **No appointment for those who ask for office:** A famous *ṣaḥīḥ* hadīth in *Ṣaḥīḥ al-Bukhārī* and *Ṣaḥīḥ Muslim*. The author uses it to close the door of fitnah for those eager for leadership.

14. **Hidden store of good deeds (*khabʾ min ʿamal ṣāliḥ*):** Narrated from al-Zubayr by al-Ḍiyāʾ and al-Khaṭīb al-Baghdādī; included by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 6081).

15. **Bilāl and the Anṣār man on spending:** The first narration is from Imām Aḥmad's *al-Zuhd*; the second from ʿAbdullāh ibn al-Mubārak's *al-Zuhd* no. 634. They illustrate the cure of the soul from greed.

16. **ʿUthmān ibn Maẓʿūn hadīth:** Narrated by Abū Dāwūd from ʿĀʾishah; included by al-Albānī (*Ṣaḥīḥ al-Jāmiʿ* 7946). It corrects extreme renunciation and establishes the rights of family, guest, and self.

17. **Giving more to those whose hearts are reconciled:** Agreed upon. The Prophet explains that he sometimes gives to a person whom he loves less because he fears that person may fall headlong into the Fire.

18. **Kaʿbah demolition narration:** Cited from *Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn* by Ibn al-Qayyim. The Prophet's restraint due to the recent Islam of Quraysh is presented as a lesson in *ḥikmah* and prioritisation.

19. **Cutting off hands in expeditions:** Also cited from *Iʿlām al-Muwaqqiʿīn*, 3/3. The Prophet forbade it because disabling the expedition or delaying a Companion out of anger was worse in Allah's sight.

20. **Emigration to Abyssinia / Muṣʿab in Yathrib / Ḥudhayfah in al-Aḥzāb / ʿAlī in Khaybar / Abū Mūsā and Muʿādh in Yemen:** These examples demonstrate the Prophet's strategic deployment of talents. The Abyssinia hadīth is narrated by Abū Dāwūd, al-Nasāʾī, and al-Tirmidhī; authenticated by al-Albānī (*al-Mishkāt* 3601).

21. **House of al-Arqam:** The first Muslim educational centre in Makkah. Munīr al-Ghaḍbān's description is cited from his *al-Tarbiyyah al-Qiyādiyyah*, 1/198.

22. **Qur'an as sole study material:** The author stresses that the Prophet made the Qur'an the exclusive source and central idea, quoting al-Ṣallābī's *al-Sīrah al-Nabawiyyah*, 1/148.

23. **Education in Madīnah:** The author notes that the form changed but the essence continued through the mosque and the Prophet's direct living with the Companions.

24. **Conclusion and final Quranic verse (6:162–163):** The author concludes that the three elements—Qur'an, educator, and educational environment—formed the first generation, and then exhorts the reader to approach the Qur'an as the Companions did. The closing verse, al-Anʿām 6:162–163, is verified against the Sahih International translation via Al-Quran Cloud API.

25. **Key Arabic terms preserved:** *manhaj* (methodology), *zuhd* (ascetic detachment), *tawḥīd* (pure monotheism), *tazkiyah* (purification), *ʿibādah* (worship/servitude), *khabʾ* (hidden store), *ḥikmah* (wisdom), *fiqh* (deep understanding), *murabbī* (educator), *wasīṭ* (environment), *muʿallim* (teacher), *mutābaʿah* (following up), *tarbiyyah* (education/nurturing), *mawʿūd* (promised), *tamkīn* (empowerment), *naṣr* (victory).
