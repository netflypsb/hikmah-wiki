**Sixthly: Asceticism in the World (al-zuhd fī al-dunyā)**

**An Ascetic Generation (jeel zāhid)**

The generation of change is an ascetic generation in the world, in the true meaning of asceticism (al-zuhd) — namely, turning desire away from the world and shunning it, as he, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "What have I to do with the world!" (1)

How could the generation promised victory and empowerment be otherwise, when love of the world and desire for it are among the most important obstacles that stand between the servant and entering the sphere of divine guardianship (al-wilāyah al-ilāhiyyah). And the ummah was only afflicted with what afflicted it of weakness and humiliation because of its love for the world and its desire for it, and its preferring it over the Hereafter.

Our Messenger, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, foretold this, so he said: "The nations will soon call one another against you, just as diners call one another to their dish." It was said: "O Messenger of Allah, will it be because of our small number that day?" He said: "No, rather you will be many, but you will be like foam, like the foam of a torrent. Allah will place weakness in your hearts and remove fear of you from the hearts of your enemies — because of your love for this world and your hatred of death." (2)

It is therefore necessary that this attribute be present in the promised generation, and that love of the world depart from the hearts of its children. Its motto in life must be: "Little that suffices is better than much that distracts."

He should not think much about wealth; rather, he should look at it according to its reality, namely as a means by which he obtains his essential needs and the essential needs of his family, and by which he preserves his dignity from asking people and begging them.

**Ambition, but…! (ṭumūḥ, wa lākin…!)**

The generation of empowerment is an ambitious generation, but what are its ambitions?

Are they in high rank, position, and prestige? Are they in buying land to build property for his children?

Are they in increasing his bank balance?

No, all of these are worldly ambitions that have no value and will not benefit him in his true future there in the Hereafter.

As for his true ambitions, they are with Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He: in the highest Paradise… in seeing his Master… in accompanying his Messenger and beloved, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him.

His ambitions are in martyrdom in the path of Allah, and that his soul be in the crops of green birds…

From here, he deals with the world according to its reality: that it is a farm for the Hereafter…

**He Does Not Bargain (lā yusāwim)**

The generation of change is a generation that worships Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, and is free from everything besides Him.

## Page 71

…Nothing of the world's debris captivates it. No job holds it captive that it fears losing, nor a position it dreads missing.

The goal has become clear to this generation, and its gaze is attached to the heavens, so its highest goal is the pleasure of Allah and His Paradise. As for what is beneath that, it is ascetic toward it. It does not concern itself much whether it is rich or poor… whether it has been given children or not… whether it is in a high position or a lowly one.

And among the effects of this generation's freedom is that it is not shackled by burdens that prevent its movement: it does not bargain over anything of this world, for the result of bargaining is known.

Among what helps this generation to free itself from the captivity of the world is that it should not become entangled in economic projects, and that its provision in the world should be like the provision of a traveller, so that it does not expand into luxuries that require from it many expenses which become a tool pressuring it and burdens binding its heart to the world.

And this does not mean that the generation of empowerment should be a poor generation. Rather, the intent is that the world should not occupy its mind or captivate its heart, for the great task entrusted to it requires that it not become attached to anything besides Allah. Reflect with me on this noble Prophetic hadith to know the importance of the heart not becoming attached to the world…

**Seventhly: A Generation of Strivers (jeel mujāhid)**

Among the characteristics of the promised generation is that it is a generation of mujahids in the path of Allah, exerting its effort, capacity, and strength for the pleasure of its Lord. Allah's words apply to it: "They strive in the path of Allah and do not fear the blame of a blamer." (Quran, Al-Ma'idah 5:54).

The vision has become clear to this generation, and the banner of caliphate and global leadership has been raised for it, so it has rolled up its sleeves to reach it, and made its life and capabilities in accordance with executing this goal. It expects from that nothing but the pleasure of its Lord upon it.

It is a generation that does not withhold any effort it exerts to reach its goal. And why not, when it knows that establishing this religion will only be by human effort in the beginning, so that divine support comes following the presence of this effort, as Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, said: "If you help Allah, He will help you and plant your feet firmly." (Quran, Muhammad 47:7).

From here the children of this generation know that any effort they exert — however small — represents an important step in building the Islamic project. Therefore, they connect the night with the day in order to reach their goals without neglecting their duties toward themselves or their families.

## Page 79

**Eighthly: Patience and Steadfastness (al-sabr wa al-thabāt)**

**A Patient Generation (jeel ṣābir)**

Among the essential attributes of the generation promised victory and empowerment is that it is a patient generation. It bears what befalls it of pressures, overcomes the obstacles it encounters, and remains steadfast before the trials and calamities it encounters on its path to achieving its desired goal.

The children of this generation have learned that the path that will take them to their goal is not strewn with roses and basil; rather, it is a difficult, bitter, long path, full of thorns and obstacles. Devils of humankind and jinn stand on its two sides, working hard to stop the march and to prevent them from reaching the goal for which they dedicated their lives — the pleasure of their Master and winning His Paradise.

The children of this generation have learned the nature of the path, so they have accustomed themselves to it, clung to their Lord, and pledged to Him to proceed forward on this path. So either they die in it as mujahids in the path of their Master, patient, steadfast upon their covenant, or they reach their goal: "Among the believers are men true to what they pledged to Allah. Among them is he who has fulfilled his vow, and among them is he who awaits. And they did not alter by any alteration." (Quran, Al-Ahzab 33:23).

**The Path of the People of Da'wah (tarīq aṣḥāb al-daʿawāt)**

Among the most important characteristics of this generation is that it is a moderate and balanced generation in all its affairs — in its worship and its dealings, in its understanding and its movement. It gives every possessor of a right his right, does not magnify what is small or belittle what is great, and places everything in its proper place: "And whoever is given wisdom has certainly been given much good." It balances between the requirements of reforming itself and the requirements of its household and society, and it knows that the first circle is itself, then its household, then its society.

It does not exaggerate where exaggeration is not appropriate, nor does it burden itself with what it cannot bear.

**A Scholar of His Time (ʿālim bi-zamānihi)**

The generation of empowerment is not a generation isolated from people, so you do not see it living in seclusion far from them. Rather, it is the child of its environment, knows its time well, deals well with the variables of life and modern means of knowledge, cultured in thought. It lives the concerns of its ummah, and knows well how its enemies plot.

It uses everything new to convey its call and reach its goal.

And this attribute was well represented in the generation of the companions — may Allah be pleased with them — and its manifestations appeared in many of their sayings and actions. Perhaps one of the strongest manifestations was their delay in burying their Messenger and beloved, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, until they finished choosing a caliph and imam for them, fearing that they would spend a night without an imam, because of the great corruption in that for the ummah.

And this Abu Bakr al-Siddiq — may Allah be pleased with him — at his death, when the hour came and death visited him, he bequeathed to Umar ibn al-Khattab — may Allah be pleased with him — after appointing him his successor as caliph of the Muslims, saying to him: "Indeed, Allah has a right by day that is not accepted at night, and a right by night that is not accepted by day. And voluntary deeds are not accepted until obligatory deeds are performed." (1)

And Uthman ibn Affan — may Allah be pleased with him — shortened the prayer at Hajj, although the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, used to shorten at Hajj, lest that become a means for the common people to believe that the prayer is two rak'ahs at all times and circumstances.

**(1)** Al-Zuhd by Abdullah ibn al-Mubarak 119.

## Page 91

And Abu Hurayrah — may Allah be pleased with him — said: "To stand guard for a night in the path of Allah (ribāṭ) is more beloved to me than to spend the Night of Decree in prayer at the Black Stone." (2)

## Page 92

**Tenthly: A Generation Bound Together in Brotherhood (jeel mutarābiṭ mutaʾākhī)**

This generation — the generation of empowerment — is not merely scattered individuals here and there, none of them feeling the other. Rather, it is a generation bound together in mutual brotherhood like a solid structure, each part supporting the other. For brotherhood is the bond that binds its individuals so that they form together a single straight row with no crookedness in it: "Indeed, Allah loves those who fight in His path in a row as though they are a solid structure." (Quran, As-Saff 61:4).

This bonding that should exist among the children of the promised generation has a great role in achieving the desired goal, for it is a tremendous means of protecting individuals from lethargy or negative influence by the civilizational attacks to which the ummah is exposed. It also helps them to remain steadfast and continue the journey, and through it comes competition in good, mutual counsel in truth, and mutual counsel in patience.

**Noble Models (numādhij mushrifah)**

And because the first generation is the model that we see before us and in whom Allah's promise of victory and empowerment was fulfilled, it is natural that we see this attribute clearly manifested in it, and its effects appeared in the sayings and deeds of its children. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, established, after the emigration from Mecca to Medina, the brotherhood (al-mu'ākhāt) between the Muhajirun and the Ansar in the house of Anas ibn Malik.

## Page 93

And they were seventy men, half of them from the Muhajirun and half from the Ansar, forming brotherhood based on mutual consolation and on inheriting from one another after death instead of relatives, until the Battle of Badr. Then when Allah, Exalted and Majestic is He, revealed: "And those of kinship are closer to one another in the Book of Allah." (Quran, Al-Anfal 8:75), inheritance by the bond of brotherhood was revoked. (1)

And the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, made this brotherhood a contract, not an empty phrase, and a deed binding in blood and wealth, not a greeting that tongues chatter with and has no effect. Feelings of selflessness, consolation, and companionship mingled in this brotherhood, filling the new society with the most wonderful examples. (2)

Al-Bukhari narrated that when the Muhajirun came to Medina, the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, formed brotherhood between Abd al-Rahman ibn Awf and Sa'd ibn al-Rabi' — may Allah be pleased with them both. Sa'd said to Abd al-Rahman: "I am the wealthiest of the Ansar, so divide my wealth in half, and I have two wives, so look at whichever of them pleases you and name her to me, I will divorce her, and when her waiting period ends, marry her." He said: "May Allah bless you in your family and wealth. Where is your market?" So they directed him to the market of Banu Qaynuqa', and he returned with some butter and tallow…

**(1)** Zad al-Ma'ad by Ibn al-Qayyim 2/56.

**(2)** Fiqh al-Sirah by Muhammad al-Ghazali 179-180.

## Page 94

**Selflessness with Need (al-ithār maʿa al-ḥājah)**

The bond of brotherhood and love for Allah's sake is among the strongest bonds that gather the believers in general, and the generation of empowerment in particular. This bond can intensify and intensify until it reaches horizons so high that the mind cannot believe them.

Reflect on Allah's description of the Ansar and how they dealt with their Muhajirun brothers: "And those who settled in the home and in faith before them love those who emigrated to them and find not in their breasts any want of what they were given, and prefer them over themselves, even though they are in privation." (Quran, Al-Hashr 59:9).

Al-Qurtubi said: The Muhajirun were in the houses of the Ansar. So when the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, obtained the wealth of Banu al-Nadir, he called the Ansar and thanked them for what they had done with the Muhajirun in giving them their homes and sharing their wealth, then he said: "If you wish, I will divide what Allah has bestowed upon me from Banu al-Nadir between you and them, and the Muhajirun will remain as they are in your homes and your wealth. And if you wish, I will give it to them and they will leave your homes." So Sa'd ibn Ubadah and Sa'd ibn Mu'adh said: "No, we will divide it between the Muhajirun, and let them remain in our homes as they were." And the Ansar cried out: "We are content and submit, O Messenger of Allah." So the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, said: "O Allah, have mercy on the Ansar and the children of the Ansar." (1)

**(1)** Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Quran by al-Qurtubi 18/17-19.

## Page 95

They had preferred them over themselves with their wealth and homes, not out of plenty, but while they themselves needed them. (1)

And among what confirms that selflessness (al-ithār) was a general characteristic in the dealings of the first generation with one another is what Ibn Umar — may Allah be pleased with them both — reported, that a sheep's head was presented to a man from the companions of the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings of Allah be upon him, and he said: "So-and-so my brother is more in need of it than I." So he sent it to him. Then that person sent it to another, and it kept being sent from one to another until it returned to the first after it had circulated among seven. (2)

**(1)** Al-Jami' li-Ahkam al-Quran by al-Qurtubi 18/17-19.

**(2)** Ihya' Ulum al-Din by Abu Hamid al-Ghazali 2/271.
