**Second: The dominance of the love of Allah in the heart**

*(A generation that loves Allah, exalted and majestic)*

Allah, exalted, says: "O you who have believed, whoever of you should revert from his religion, Allah will bring forth a people whom He will love and who will love Him, humble toward the believers, mighty against the disbelievers, striving in the cause of Allah and not fearing the blame of a critic. That is the favour of Allah; He gives it to whom He wills. And Allah is all-Encompassing and Knowing. Your ally is none but Allah and His Messenger and those who have believed — those who establish prayer and give zakāh, and they bow. And whoever is an ally of Allah and His Messenger and those who have believed — indeed, the party of Allah will be the predominant." (Quran, Al-Mā'idah 5:54–56).

These verses speak of the generation and the group whom Allah, exalted and majestic, loves and is pleased with, and for whom He writes victory over their enemies.

This group has several attributes that the previous verses have clarified, and the first of these attributes is their love of Allah, exalted and majestic: "whom He will love and who will love Him."

If you say: But all Muslims love Allah, exalted and majestic, so what is the point of this attribute?

## Page 46

...Yes, there is love of Allah in hearts, but the love that He — exalted is He — wants from the promised generation is a love that overwhelms the heart and dominates it, until Allah becomes more beloved to him than everything: "And those who believe are stronger in love for Allah." (Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:165).

This true love has signs by which it is known... among them:

**1. Obedience to the beloved and working for His pleasure**

The servant who loves Allah hastens and rushes to attain His pleasure. Look at Moses, peace be upon him, as he leaves the Children of Israel behind him to meet his Lord, saying: "And I hastened to You, my Lord, that You might be pleased." (Quran, Ṭā Hā 20:84).

And the true lover continues to be eager to please his Lord, even if he expends in that cause all that he owns: "And among the people is he who sells himself, seeking the pleasure of Allah." (Quran, Al-Baqarah 2:207).

**2. Swiftness in turning back to Allah**

Among the signs of true love is the servant's hastening to repentance, seeking forgiveness, apologising, pouring out tears, and seeking to appease his Master when he falls into a sin or falls short in performing a duty, due to his constant fear of his Lord's anger against him. Look at Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him, as he privately converses with his Lord and seeks to please Him after what befell him at al-Ṭā'if of rejection and aversion, and his fear

...that the cause of that lay with himself. He said, peace and blessings be upon him: "...If You are not angry with me, I do not care; rather, Your well-being is more expansive for me... I seek refuge in the light of Your face, by which the darknesses are illuminated and by which the affairs of this world and the Hereafter are set right, that Your wrath should descend upon me or Your displeasure befall me. Yours is the reproach until You are pleased, and there is no power and no strength except with You..."(1)

(1) Sunan al-Tirmidhī; also narrated by al-Ḥākim and others.

**3. Gratitude for His gifts**

The true lover receives the guidance and gifts of his Lord with joy, happiness, and gratitude, and he attributes to Him every favour and good that comes to him. We find him ever-remembering His blessings, praising and thanking Him for them.

**4. Patience in tribulations and contentment with the decree**

For the true lover of Allah bears the tribulation his Lord sends to him and is content with His decree, for he knows that He does not intend anything but good for him, and that He only tried him to purify him and draw him nearer to Him: "And those who are patient, seeking the countenance of their Lord." (Quran, Ar-Raʿd 13:22).

**5. Abundant private devotion (munājāh)**

Among the signs of true love of Allah is love of solitude with Him, abundant private conversation with Him and praise of Him, and laying oneself bare before Him... And since private devotion is specific to the beloved alone, the lover always waits until sounds quiet down, eyes fall asleep, and the place becomes empty, then he rushes to his Lord and finds intimacy with Him. How beautiful are the moments of intimacy in the vastness of prayer, and especially in prostration... For that reason the best prayer after the obligatory prayer was the night prayer (qiyām al-layl), when the atmosphere is prepared for contact... at that time our Lord descends to the lowest heaven in a manner befitting His glory and perfection.

**Third: Intense fear of Allah, exalted and majestic**

*(A generation humble, fearing Allah)*

Allah, exalted, says: "And their Lord had inspired them: We will surely destroy the wrongdoers, and We will surely settle you in the earth after them. That is for whoever fears My station and fears My threat." (Quran, Ibrāhīm 14:13–14).

And Allah, exalted, says: "Indeed, the earth belongs to Allah; He causes to inherit it whom He wills of His servants. And the outcome is for the righteous." (Quran, Al-Aʿrāf 7:128).

And Allah, exalted, says, addressing His Messenger: "That is from the news of the unseen which We reveal to you. You knew it not, neither you nor your people, before this. So be patient; indeed, the outcome is for the righteous." (Quran, Hūd 11:49).

Through these verses it becomes clear to us that fear of Allah should fill the hearts of the sons of the generation promised victory and enablement... a fear aroused by veneration, magnification, and awe of Allah, exalted and majestic; and aroused likewise by the feeling of falling short in fulfilling the rights of servitude... and fear of the consequences of sins from which no human can escape... and of exposure to an evil end, the stupor of death, the reckoning of the grave, the terrors of the Day of Resurrection, of being presented before Allah and exposed to reckoning, of the scales of good deeds not tipping in one's favour, of not being permitted to cross the Ṣirāṭ...

## Page 58

There is a great difference between a passing fear that stirs the emotions, sends forth tears, and then passes on its way, and a constant fear that fills the heart and keeps it always in a state of remembrance and alertness.

Indeed the fear required to be present in the hearts of the sons of the generation of enablement is the fear that drives to action, produces taqwā, caution, and waraʿ in all words and deeds, so that its possessor is careful in his speech and leaves much of the permissible out of fear of falling into the forbidden.

What is required is a fear that drives its possessor to magnify the symbols of Allah: "That is so, and whoever honours the symbols of Allah — indeed, it is from the piety of hearts." (Quran, Al-Ḥajj 22:32). And it makes him hasten toward good deeds: "Indeed, those who are apprehensive from fear of their Lord, and those who believe in the signs of their Lord, and those who do not associate anything with their Lord, and those who give what they give while their hearts are fearful because they will be returning to their Lord — it is those who hasten to good deeds, and they are foremost in them." (Quran, Al-Mu'minūn 23:57–61).

That means fear of Allah plays a major role in the servant's steadfastness on the straight path, and from it enters into the circle of divine wilayah, support, and protective care: "If you are patient and fear Allah, their plotting will not harm you at all." (Quran, Āl ʿImrān 3:120).

**Fourth: A worshipping generation**

Allah, exalted, says: "And We had already written in the Psalms after the mention that the earth shall be inherited by My righteous servants. Indeed, in this is a notification for a worshipping people." (Quran, Al-Anbiyā' 21:105–106).

The generation of enablement is a generation that worships Allah, exalted and majestic... abundant in remembrance, abundant in obedience... a generation known to the angels, and the mosques bear witness that it is among their caretakers and pillars.

...it rises early for prayer, and preserves the Sunnah prayers... it does not miss the night prayer no matter how tired it is.

...it has devotional practices it preserves of supererogatory prayers, and likewise the adhkār and recitation of the Qur'an.

...its delight is in prayer... it prolongs bowing and prostration, and increases in private converse with its Lord.

...abundant in spending... abundant in fasting... abundant in supplication.

...its concern for people and its call to them do not distract it from concern for itself, so it always preserves its balance, and its increase in faith.

Thus were the companions of Muḥammad, peace and blessings be upon him:

**Fifth: Humility (al-tawāḍuʿ)**

*(A humble generation)*

Among the most important attributes that should be found in the generation promised victory and enablement is humility, which is emphasised by what came in His saying, exalted: "O you who have believed, whoever of you should revert from his religion, Allah will bring forth a people whom He will love and who will love Him, humble toward the believers, mighty against the disbelievers." (Quran, Al-Mā'idah 5:54).

For humility toward the believers and lowering the wing for them indicates the humility of its possessor.

The reality of humility is that a person sees himself as small... It is a state of the heart that the servant lives, and its effects appear in his conduct and dealings with his Lord, with himself, and with people.

**With Allah:**

The servant's humility in his relationship with his Lord stems from his perception of his reality and origin: that he is a created being, powerless, weak, ignorant... His origin is dust, and despicable water... It stems likewise from his sensing the greatness of his Lord, His majesty, His perfection, and the immensity of His favour upon him.

## Page 65

This inner state should be translated by the servant into an image of submission, humbleness, and submissiveness to Allah, exalted and majestic, showing his utter poverty and need of Him, and that no matter what he is given of forms of health, strength, beauty, or wealth, he remains as he is: a humbled servant of a glorious Lord, and these things do not change his reality in the slightest.

**With oneself:**

The reality of humility with oneself is that a person sees it as small, views it with an eye of deficiency, and always places it in a station lower than it should be. Sufficient for you in that is what Moses — peace be upon him — did when he saw himself as small and considered it too great a burden to bear the message alone, so he asked his Lord to let his brother Hārūn — peace be upon him — help him carry it: "And my brother Hārūn is more fluent than me in tongue, so send him with me as a helper to confirm me, for I fear they will deny me." (Quran, Al-Qaṣaṣ 28:34). Although the reality shows that he — peace be upon him — carried it in the best way.

Among the forms of a person's humility is not putting himself forward to undertake something, and likewise seeing himself as unworthy of the work proposed to him.

Among them also is that he walks upon the earth humbly, not swaggering or strutting.

## Page 66

**With people:**

A person's humility with people stems from his view of them as better than him no matter what his deeds or rank may be; he always looks at the positive side of everyone he deals with and senses their superiority over him.

That appears in his conduct with them... so he does not disdain serving his family, he lowers his wing for the believers, he responds to the call of the poor and weak, he often sits with the needy, and he hastens to fulfil the needs of people.

**The humility of the Ṣaḥābah:**

This attribute clearly appeared and its effects were plainly visible in the generation of the Ṣaḥābah, may Allah be pleased with them. Look at Abū Bakr, may Allah be pleased with him: he continued milking sheep for his neighbour after assuming the caliphate, and he said to them when they thought he would not continue: By my life, I will milk them for you; I hope that what I have entered into will not change me from a character I used to be upon. So he continued milking for them.(1)
