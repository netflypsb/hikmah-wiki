# Chapter 3: How Are the Attributes of the Promised Generation Achieved? (Pages 128–144)

## Page 128

### How Do We Believe in the Hereafter?

It is of great importance to know the stages of man’s journey to Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and to the Hereafter (al-akhirah), and to know the nature of the stage of worldly life (al-dunya) and its relationship to the Hereafter. In light of this knowledge, the servant’s attentiveness and rolling up of his sleeves for Paradise (al-jannah) will take place.

**The Fundamental Task**

Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, sent us down to earth to perform a specific task, namely His worship and servitude (ubudiyyah) to Him in the unseen (al-ghayb); whoever succeeds in performing this task wins Paradise, and whoever fails is punished by imprisonment in the Fire (al-nar), and we seek refuge with Allah.

The world is nothing but a house of test: “Indeed, We have made that which is on earth an adornment for it, that We may test them as to which of them is best in deed.” (Quran, al-Kahf 18:7)

What is required of man — any man — is that he obey his Lord, follow His law, carry out His commands, and avoid His prohibitions,

## Page 129

and what is also required of him is that he deal well with the means and tools that Allah gives him or withholds from him. In the case of being given and provided with various blessings, what is required of the servant is gratitude (shukr), and that is through praising Allah for His blessings, not being tyrannical because of them, feeling grateful toward Him, Exalted is He, frequently humbling himself before Him, and not being arrogant over others or feeling superior to them.

As for in the case of being deprived of some blessings, such as being deprived of children, abundant provision, or security, what is required of the servant is patience (sabr), so he does not resent, complain, or object to the decree of Allah.

This test begins from the age of puberty to the moments of death and the extraction of the soul, and through the righteous deeds and correct answers that the servant performs, his reward will be in the Hereafter, and his bliss in Paradise: “And whoever does righteousness — then for themselves they are preparing.” (Quran, al-Rum 30:44)

As for the supervision over the test, Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, undertakes it Himself, for He is the Watchful Witness: “Indeed, Allah is ever, over you, an Observer.” (Quran, al-Nisa’ 4:1)

And the angels record the answers: “He does not speak a word but that there is with him a ready observer.” (Quran, Qaf 50:18)

Rather, the body itself testifies against its owner: “On a Day when their tongues, their hands, and their feet will testify against them as to what they used to do.” (Quran, al-Nur 24:24)

This test may end at any time, so the servant must always be ready for the moment of its end so that he does not lose his Hereafter.

## Page 130

After death comes the stage of the barzakh (barzakh), then the resurrection (al-baith) and the Day of Judgment (yawm al-qiyamah), with its tremendous events and reckoning for all people concerning the task they were asked to perform; after the reckoning, the successful will depart to Paradise, and the failing ones will be driven to the Fire, and we seek refuge with Allah.

In Paradise there will be bliss that no mind can grasp the essence of, and along with lasting bliss there will be eternity without death; and in the Fire — and we seek refuge with Allah — there will be unbearable punishment that does not end: “It will not be eased for them, and they will be therein in despair.” (Quran, al-Zukhruf 43:75)

This means that any reasonable person must have his ultimate ambition there in Paradise, and must work to win it and enjoy its bliss, and must search for the means that lead to that; and the most important of these means is utilizing everything within his reach to make it a means of achieving his goal.

**The Traveler’s Rest**

These realities require constant attentiveness, wakefulness, and clarity of vision regarding the reality of the world — that it is a house of passage and a test — and that we were not sent down to it to remain in it, but rather to spend some time in it and then leave it and depart, like a traveler who rests from the fatigue of travel at any rest stop he meets along the road.

## Page 131

This traveler will think only of stocking up with what benefits him in his journey and brings him to his goal and objective. Likewise, the world is nothing but one of the stations of the journey to the Hereafter, and we must stock up from it with what benefits us there.

If you say: We all know that, but we forget it amid the successive events of life, so how can our remembrance and attentiveness to these realities endure [1]?

The solution lies in three means:

**First:** taking lesson from those who preceded us, frequently thinking about death and visiting graves, reflecting on the conditions of their occupants and how the Angel of Death came to them without them being ready to meet him, and sensing their regret at wasting the opportunity that was in their hands [1],

## Page 132

and remembering their wishes, which are confined to returning to the world even for a single moment in which they would glorify Allah or seek His forgiveness. The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Two light rak’ahs that you belittle or consider insignificant, added by this — he pointed to a grave — to one’s deeds, are dearer to him than the rest of your world.” [2]

**Second:** frequent reading of the Quran and reflecting on its meanings. It is observed that discussion of the Hereafter, the Day of Reckoning, the bliss of Paradise, the punishment of the grave, the nature of existence in the world, and urging people to race to Paradise — all of this occupies a large space in the Quran, and hardly a surah is free of it, so that it leads to certainty in those matters: “No! If you only knew with knowledge of certainty, you would surely see the Hellfire.” (Quran, al-Takathur 102:5–6)

Thus when a person becomes certain of these realities, that will be reflected in his conduct and the way he deals with the elements of life; you will see him striving, worshipping, rolling up his sleeves to do good deeds, and treating others well.

**Third:** righteous companionship (al-suhbah al-salihah). Along with taking lesson from those around us and remembering death, and with constant reflection on the Quran, the mutual reminding among believers of these realities comes as a means that helps the servant maintain his wakefulness, attentiveness, and good preparation for death, as the Exalted said to His Messenger, peace and blessings be upon him: “And keep yourself patient with those who call upon their Lord morning and evening,

## Page 133

seeking His countenance; and let not your eyes pass beyond them, desiring adornments of the worldly life, and do not obey one whose heart We have made heedless of Our remembrance and who follows his desire and whose affair is ever in neglect.” (Quran, al-Kahf 18:28)

Indeed, a person’s being in a wholesome environment and righteous companionship tends to stir his ambition to race toward the Gardens. Look at Umar ibn al-Khattab, may Allah be pleased with him, and his keenness to outstrip Abu Bakr al-Siddiq, may Allah be pleased with him, in spending. He said, may Allah be pleased with him: “The Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, commanded us one day to give charity, and that coincided with my having some wealth, so I said: Today I will outstrip Abu Bakr, if I can outstrip him. So I came to the Messenger of Allah with half of my wealth, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: ‘What have you left for your family?’ I said: The like of it. He said: And Abu Bakr came with all that he had, so the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: ‘What have you left for your family?’ He said: ‘I have left for them Allah and His Messenger.’ I said: ‘I will never compete with you over anything ever again.’” [3]

These means and others increase faith in the Hereafter in the heart and spur one to strive and roll up his sleeves for Paradise.

### How Do We Practice Zuhd in the World?

There is no doubt that the servant’s knowledge of the world in its true reality plays a major role in his asceticism (zuhd) and the turning away of his desire from it, and in increasing his desire for what is with Allah, the Exalted and Majestic.

And whoever reads the verses and hadiths that describe the world will be amazed at the intensity of its contemptibility to Allah, the Exalted and Majestic. If you are in doubt about this, then reflect with me on this hadith.

“Indeed, Allah, the Exalted, has made what comes out of the children of Adam a parable for the world.” [4]

Al-Mustawrid ibn Shaddad said: “I was with the group who stopped with the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, at the dead sheep, and the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: ‘Do you see how contemptible this is to its owners, so that they threw it away?’ They said: Who would throw it away because of its contemptibility, O Messenger of Allah? He said: ‘By the One in whose hand is the soul of Muhammad, the world is more contemptible to Allah than this is to its owners.’” [5]

Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, created the world and did not give it any value, nor did He esteem it by the slightest thing, as he, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “If the world were equal in the sight of Allah to the wing of a mosquito, He would not have given the disbeliever a sip of water from it.” [6]

## Page 135

Thus the world is contemptible, of no value, like a stinking carcass that people loathe and avoid passing near; if they are forced to do so, you see them hastening their steps and turning their faces away from it.

**The House of Grief and Sorrows**

Along with this amazing reality of the world, it is, beyond that, never pure for anyone; it is full of turbidness: joys are followed by sorrows, well-being is pursued by sickness, strength is followed by weakness. The gathering of loved ones is not complete in it, for if they gather, separation is inevitable for them.

If you say: Why then did Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, create it with this contemptibility and that turbidness? The answer came from the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him: “Whoever loves his world harms his Hereafter, and whoever loves his Hereafter harms his world. So prefer what remains over what perishes.” [7]

So if the world had a considerable value, and if it were free of its turbidness and pure, people would love it and become attached to it, which would lead to their competing and struggling over it, and increased eagerness to remain in it; thus their hatred of death would increase, and their desire for the Hereafter and its bliss would decrease, so their trade would stagnate and their deeds would be abandoned.

## Page 136

This means that the world’s being in this contemptible state and with that turbidness is a great blessing from Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, to His servants. For if the world were pure, no one would roll up his sleeves for the Hereafter; yet despite that, Satan has been able to deceive very many people and make them forget these realities, and to adorn the world for them and display it before them so that they lean toward it, cling to it, and struggle over it, and forget the mission for which they were created.

**Means of Knowledge**

As was said previously, for these realities to be a motive for changing a person’s dealing with his world and for his asceticism in it, they must take hold of his certainty and affect his faith. Hence, it was necessary for there to be strong and continuous means that carry out this task.

These means proceed — like the previous means — from two axes: an emotional axis that constantly reminds one of the reality of the world, blending thought with emotion, stirring feelings, and generating the driving energy for action; and a practical axis that confirms the emotional meaning, thereby embedding its significance in a person’s certainty so that his perceptions, concerns, and consequently his actions flow easily and spontaneously, expressing his asceticism in the world.

## Page 137

As for the theoretical axis capable of constant reminding of the reality of the world, it is the Eternal Book that Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, sent down as mercy and guidance for people: “And thus We have sent it down as an Arabic Quran, and have varied therein the warnings, that they might fear Allah or it would cause them to remember.” (Quran, Ta Ha 20:113)

It is observed in the Quran that the subject of the world and its reality occupies a large space in it, through describing it, censuring those who are eager for it, and presenting stories of previous peoples who were deluded by it and regretted that most intensely after their death.

Along with the Quran comes the Sunnah likewise, and what it contains of numerous hadiths that speak of the reality of the world and call to asceticism in it.

As for the practical axis, it is taking lesson from the events of the world: the careful observer of the events of life and what happens to people sees the world in its reality, and that it is a house of sorrows, worries, griefs, turbidness, and separation.

Thus the people of strength yesterday are the weak ones today, and status and authority have never lasted for anyone.

Hardly a house is free of someone sick, and it is rare to see a person without worries or griefs. Loved ones separate: a son leaves his father and marries, and a father leaves his sons and dies, and …

When the servant views these scenes with the eye of reflection, the reality of the world will always be manifested before him.

## Page 138

And among the things that embed this meaning in the soul is looking at garbage dumps and carcasses every so often and connecting that with the reality of the world and its contemptibility to Allah, as the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, and his noble Companions used to do.

It was narrated from al-Hasan al-Basri that he said: “Umar ibn al-Khattab passed by a garbage dump and stopped at it, and it was as if that pained his companions and they were hurt by it, so he said to them: This is your world that you are so eager for.” [8]

### How Is Proper Understanding of Religion Achieved?

As for the qualities of moderation and balance, and what they yield of wisdom in dealing with the events of life, they are nothing but a natural product of proper understanding of religion, as the Messenger of Allah, peace and blessings be upon him, said: “Whoever Allah wishes good for, He gives him understanding (fiqh) in religion.” [9]

The intended meaning of proper understanding of religion is realizing the reality of its comprehensiveness for all aspects of life, knowing the sources of legal rulings and their levels, and also recognizing the non-legislative sources that some rely on in receiving rulings, such as inspiration and dreams, and how serious that is.

Proper understanding of religion also requires knowledge of the jurisprudence of priorities (fiqh al-awlawiyyat) and how to balance and give precedence between benefits and harms when they conflict, for as Shaykh al-Islam Ibn Taymiyyah said: “The intelligent one is not the one who knows good from evil, but rather the intelligent one is the one who knows the better of two goods and the worse of two evils.” [10]

Along with the jurisprudence of priorities, there is the jurisprudence of objectives (fiqh al-maqasid), the jurisprudence of reality (fiqh al-waqi), and the jurisprudence of disagreement (fiqh al-khilaf). All of these are matters that contribute to forming the balanced mentality of the sons of the generation promised victory and empowerment.

## Page 140

**Means of Achieving Understanding**

Among the most important means of achieving proper understanding of religion is the Noble Quran, for it is a Book that addresses the intellect, in which it draws the map of Islam with its correct proportions, and gives its possessor a comprehensive conception of everything required of him and his relationship to everything around him; and it is not content with that, but places every matter in its appropriate size within the tree of Islam, for it arranges priorities and forms a moderate, balanced mentality that gives every right-holder his due. The Exalted said: “And whoever is given wisdom has certainly been given much good.” (Quran, al-Baqarah 2:269)

Ibn Abbas said: “Wisdom is knowledge of the Quran.” [11]

Along with the Quran, there are studies by scholars on the principles of understanding, and perhaps the most prominent of these is what al-Shatibi wrote in his book *al-Muwafaqat* and what Imam al-Shahid Hasan al-Banna wrote in *Risalat al-Ta’alim*, in which he laid down principles of understanding. These principles have been explained by many scholars, and they generally form the balanced scholarly personality of the Muslim.

It is also good to study the jurisprudence of priorities and objectives, and the jurisprudence of disagreement, while taking into account that grounding proper understanding of Islam and learning wisdom in dealing with the events of life require — along with the previous means — a teacher [11].

## Page 141

and a murabbi (teacher-mentor) who waters this knowledge into his students and follows them in their dealings with the course of events to make sure of their proper application of the quality of moderation and balance.

**Brotherhood… How Is It Achieved?**

Brotherhood (ukhuwwah) in Allah is a natural fruit of the fruits of faith in Him, Exalted is He, and a necessary consequence of it; and the more faith takes root in the heart, the greater the degree of love for Allah’s sake, as was the case of the Ansar (the Helpers) with the Muhajirun (the Emigrants). Their strong faith in their Lord was reflected in their dealings with their Muhajirun brothers to the extent that they preferred them over themselves, despite their own poverty and need.

Thus the quality of brotherhood and bonding is a product of the first axis, which is knowledge of Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and what it leads to of strong faith and love for Him, Exalted is He; and yet this love must be translated into actions that embed its meaning in a person’s certainty, for just as faith is word and deed, so is brotherhood.

Hence it becomes necessary to have an environment and a setting in which the means and works of brotherhood are practiced: mutual support (takafull), assistance, preferring others (ithar), striving to fulfill needs, and …

And there must be an environment and incubators in which brotherhood and bonding are nurtured among the sons of the generation promised victory and empowerment, otherwise the spirit will dry up among individuals.

## Page 142

**The Basic Means**

From what has preceded, it is clear to us that the means by which the basic axes for forming the promised generation are achieved revolve around: good dealing with the Quran and everything that serves it, good dealing with the events of life and taking lesson from them, and righteous companionship.

Thus the Quran is the effective means of knowledge: knowledge of Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, knowledge of the self, knowledge of the world, and knowledge of the Hereafter.

What distinguishes the knowledge of the Quran is that it is repeated in many surahs in various styles, addressing both the intellect and the emotions, so that through this those knowledges are embedded in a person’s certainty and his feelings are directed toward them, producing a living, strong faith.

## Page 143

As for the events of life: everything that happens to a person is by the will of Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and there is no will of Allah except that behind it is a wisdom that flows into the benefit of mankind, as the Exalted said: “Indeed, Allah is, to the people, Kind and Merciful.” (Quran, al-Baqarah 2:143)

So life is nothing but successive messages from Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, by which He makes us know Him, Exalted is He, ourselves, the value of the world, and the value of the Hereafter. “And how many signs in the heavens and earth do they pass over while they turn away from them.” (Quran, Yusuf 12:105)

But through good dealing with the Quran, and the gradual attentiveness that will occur to the individual as a product of this dealing, it will become easy for him to read the divine messages and take lesson from them, to understand well concerning Allah, the Exalted and Majestic, and to connect the events of life to Him, so that knowledge and certainty in Him, Exalted is He, and in the Hereafter increase, and caution against the self and the world increases.

As for righteous companionship, it is considered the environment through which the sons of the promised generation practice the quality of brotherhood, producing a strong, bonded generation like a building whose parts reinforce one another.

And through righteous companionship and the educational environment, some means are also practiced that help increase the previous knowledges and connect them to reality, so that faith and attentiveness increase.

In it, everything that serves the Quran and confirms the wisdom and faith-based meanings in it is studied, such as the Seerah (prophetic biography), history, and principles of understanding; and through it, mutual counsel toward truth and patience takes place, and individuals support one another.

## Page 144

**The Murabbi**

Along with these three means — “the Quran — reflection — companionship” — comes the role of the teacher (mu’allim) and the murabbi (teacher-mentor) to bind the means to one another. He takes the weak by the hand until his resolve becomes firm and enters him into the world of the Quran, until the resources of faith are renewed in him, and he is able to reflect on the events of life, read the divine messages, and draw near to his Lord.

The presence of the murabbi is also important for sharpening ambitions to roll up one’s sleeves for the Hereafter and to practice asceticism in the world.

And among his most important roles are: regulating understanding among individuals, teaching them wisdom, and making sure they practice it well in the course of their lives.
