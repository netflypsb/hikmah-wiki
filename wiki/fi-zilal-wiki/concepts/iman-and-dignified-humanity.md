# Iman and Dignified Humanity

A cross-cutting theme developed in **Surah Al-`Asr (103)** and **Al-Humazah (104)** — the Quranic equation of faith (*īmān*) with human dignity, and the diagnosis that the loss of faith is the loss of humanity itself.

## The Theme

Sayyid Qutb's commentary on Al-`Asr is the fullest statement of what may be called his **theological anthropology**: faith is not merely a creed but the constitutive quality that makes human beings *fully human*. Its absence is not neutral difference but degradation below the animal — what the Qur'an calls *khusr* (loss).

Al-Humazah dramatizes the reverse: a person "devoid of human ideals and generosity and stripped of faith" whose wealth-worship produces a "vile and debased picture" — the living embodiment of *khusr*.

## The Seven Qualities (Al-`Asr)

Qutb identifies seven qualities of dignified humanity that faith establishes:

1. **Worship of one God** — elevates man above servitude to anyone other than God; establishes liberty as an inner conviction, not a political status.
2. **Godliness** — the source of all concepts, values, and laws. Equity and justice replace personal desires. The believer stays above jāhiliyyah values "even when a believer finds himself alone, with no one else of his kind." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]
3. **Clarity of the Creator-creature relationship** — connection without mediator; gives confidence, purpose, and eliminates fear, anxiety, arrogance.
4. **Following God's ordained path** — with steadfastness and clear vision; goodness from definite motives toward certain aims.
5. **Dignity in God's sight** — "the loftiest concept he may attain of himself." Any ideology that abases this — Darwinism, Freudianism, Marxism — is "among the most horrid disasters human nature has encountered." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]
6. **Purity of motivation** — God's awareness of the inner self produces self-purification, refined moral sense, responsibility toward all humanity.
7. **Elevation above worldly greed** — the believer acts because good *is* good and God requires it, not for immediate results. "God, for whom he performs the good, neither dies, forgets nor ignores anyone's deeds." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]

## The Inverse: The Slanderer (Al-Humazah)

Al-Humazah (104) is the photographic negative of Al-`Asr's seven qualities:

| Al-`Asr quality | Al-Humazah inversion |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Worship of one God | Wealth as false god |
| Godliness (values from God) | Values from wealth accumulation |
| Creator-creature clarity | Delusion of immortality through wealth |
| Steadfast path | Aimless counting of money |
| Dignity in God's sight | Mocking others' dignity |
| Purity of motivation | "Wicked vanity let loose" |
| Elevation above greed | "Keeps counting it again and again" |

The slanderer is not merely a sinner but a *type*: the person in whom faith has been replaced by its counterfeit. "Islam despises this type of person whose characteristics are diametrically opposed to its own high standards of morality." ^[raw/surah-104-al-humazah.md]

## Faith as Dynamic

Qutb insists faith is not static belief but active, creative, productive:

"Faith is a positive and active concept which, once it has pervaded the human conscience, hastens to reflect itself to the outside world in good deeds... It must be dynamic. If it is not, then it is either phoney or nonexistent, just as a flower cannot withhold its fragrance which naturally spreads. Otherwise, it is not in the flower at all." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]

This is what connects *īmān* to *'amal ṣāliḥ* (righteous deeds) in the sūrah's formula. Faith without deeds is not weak faith but non-faith.

## The Community Dimension

Faith produces not isolated individuals but a community — the *tawāṣī* (mutual counsel) of Al-`Asr verse 3. Qutb: "Counselling one another to follow the truth and to persevere in the face of adversity reveals a picture of Islamic society which has its own very special entity, a unique inter-relationship between its individual members and a single objective." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]

The slanderer of Al-Humazah is the anti-community figure: he mocks and ridicules rather than counselling and encouraging. His punishment — "tied to a column, as animals are tied, without respect" — is the precise inversion of the dignity faith confers.
