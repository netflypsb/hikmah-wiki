# Akhirah and the Fleeting World

A cross-cutting theme developed across **Surahs Al-`Asr (103), Al-Takāthur (102), and Al-Qāri'ah (101)** — three adjacent Meccan surahs that form a triptych on the transience of worldly life and the absolute reality of the hereafter.

## The Theme

Sayyid Qutb identifies a recurring Quranic movement across these surahs: the **demotion of dunya (this world)** and the **exaltation of ākhirah (the hereafter)** as the only theatre of real profit and loss. The three surahs escalate the theme:

- **Al-`Asr (103):** Man is in *khusr* (loss) — except those who believe and act righteously. Life is fleeting; the hereafter is where real gain or loss is suffered.
- **Al-Takāthur (102):** "You are preoccupied by greed for more and more, until you go down to your graves." Worldly rivalry is exposed as drunken distraction from the certainty of accounting.
- **Al-Qāri'ah (101):** The Striker — the Day of Resurrection itself —strips all worldly anchors bare. People become scattered moths; mountains become carded wool. Only *mīzān* (the balance) matters.

## Qutb's Treatment

### The fleeting wink

In Al-Takāthur, Qutb writes: "It portrays the life of this world as a fleeting wink in the long span of existence: 'You are preoccupied by greed for more and more, until you go down to your graves.' (Verses 1-2) The wink of this life is over and its small leaf is turned. Thereafter time stretches on and the burdens become heavier." ^[raw/surah-102-al-takathur.md]

In Al-`Asr, this same wink is measured against eternity: "All that has been said so far concerned gain and loss in this life which, though of great importance, is very trivial in comparison with the hereafter. There is an everlasting life and a world of reality where real profit is made or real loss is suffered." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]

### The Striker as cosmic equalizer

Al-Qāri'ah strips all worldly weight: "The scene portrayed here is one of horror directly affecting man and mountains. In this scene people look dwarfish in spite of their great number. For they are like scattered moths. They fly here and there having no power or weight." ^[raw/surah-101-al-qariah.md]

The mīzān (balance) is the only measure that survives the Striker. Qutb warns: "To indulge in a sophisticated, logical and linguistic argument about the meaning of the Qur'ānic term, mawāzīn, used here is in itself a departure from the Qur'ānic spirit." ^[raw/surah-101-al-qariah.md]

### The questioning

Al-Takāthur ends with the ultimate audit: "Then on that day you will certainly be questioned about your joys and comforts." (Verse 8) Qutb unpacks: "How did you get it? How did you dispense with it? Was it obtained from a lawful source and dispensed with lawfully? Or was it gained unlawfully and used in a sinful manner?" ^[raw/surah-102-al-takathur.md]

## The Escalation Pattern

| Surah | Order | Worldly focus | Akhirah reality |
|-------|-------|--------------|-----------------|
| Al-`Asr (103) | Constitution | Generic "loss" | Faith + deeds + mutual counsel = the only path |
| Al-Takāthur (102) | Warning | Greed for more | Graves → hellfire → questioning |
| Al-Qāri'ah (101) | Striker | (all worldly anchors) | Resurrection → balance → abyss or paradise |

The three surahs move from **principle** (Al-`Asr) to **warning** (Al-Takāthur) to **vision** (Al-Qāri'ah) — from the abstract declaration of loss to the concrete depiction of the Day that proves it.
