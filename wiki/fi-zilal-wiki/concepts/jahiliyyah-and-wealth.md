# Jahiliyyah and Wealth

A cross-cutting theme developed across **Surahs Al-Takāthur (102), Al-Humazah (104), and Al-Fīl (105)** — the Qur'an's diagnosis of wealth-worship as the hallmark of *jāhiliyyah* (ignorance) and the principal obstacle to human dignity.

## The Theme

Sayyid Qutb reads these surahs as a sustained critique of the jāhiliyyah mindset: the substitution of material accumulation for faith, the delusion that wealth confers immortality and mastery, and the moral degradation it produces — from rivalry (takāthur) to mockery (humazah) to imperial aggression against God's House (fīl).

## Qutb's Treatment

### Rivalry as drunkenness (Al-Takāthur)

"You drunken and confused lot! You who take delight and indulge in rivalry for wealth, children and the pleasures of this life, from which you are sure to depart! You who are absorbed with what you have, unaware of what comes afterwards!" ^[raw/surah-102-al-takathur.md]

Qutb treats *takāthur* (rivalry for worldly gain) as a species of intoxication — not metaphorical but real: the worldly mind is narcotized, eyes closed at the precipice. The sūrah's rhythm "travels upwards in space at the beginning and downwards to the deep, deep level at the end" — mimicking the descent into the grave that awaits all such accumulation.

### Wealth as false god (Al-Humazah)

"He imagines that his money and his wealth is a god, capable of everything without exception, even of resisting death, making him immortal and stopping God's judgement and His retribution." ^[raw/surah-104-al-humazah.md]

Qutb sees in the slanderer (humazah) not merely a bad character but a *type*: the person for whom wealth has replaced God as the supreme value. The moral symptoms — mockery, ridicule, taunting — follow inevitably. "Islam despises this type of person whose characteristics are diametrically opposed to its own high standards of morality." ^[raw/surah-104-al-humazah.md]

### Imperial arrogance (Al-Fīl)

Abrahah's march on the Ka'bah is the political-military form of the same wealth-worship: the assumption that material power (elephants, armies) can override what is sacred. Qutb frames it as jāhiliyyah's culmination: "God did not want the idolaters to take responsibility for protecting His House... If they had done so, they would have been prompted by fanatic jāhiliyyah impulses." ^[raw/surah-105-al-fil.md]

The destruction of Abrahah by birds carrying clay stones is the Qur'an's answer to the delusion of imperial power: "Divine power is exhibited more strikingly when those who manifested their might by recruiting elephants, the largest quadruped animals, were destroyed and crushed by a tiny animal, invisible to human eyes." ^[raw/surah-105-al-fil.md]

## The Escalation

| Surah | Jahiliyyah form | Wealth's delusion | Divine answer |
|-------|-----------------|--------------------|---------------|
| Al-Takāthur (102) | Social rivalry | "More and more" → graves | Questioning on the Day |
| Al-Humazah (104) | Moral degradation | Wealth = immortality | The crushing fire |
| Al-Fīl (105) | Imperial aggression | Military might vs the House | Birds + clay stones |

The progression: from the *private* disease (greed) to the *social* symptom (mockery of the virtuous) to the *political* expression (war on the sacred).

## Qutb's Broader Critique

This theme links directly to Al-`Asr's diagnosis: faith is what "keeps him above all jāhiliyyah concepts, interests and mundane values. This is so even when a believer finds himself alone, with no one else of his kind." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md] The three wealth-surahs (102, 104, 105) dramatize what Al-`Asr states constitutionally: without faith, wealth becomes the jāhiliyyah substitute, and the result is loss (*khusr*).

Qutb explicitly names modern ideologies as heirs to this jāhiliyyah: "the effects of Darwinism, Freudianism and Marxism are among the most horrid disasters human nature has encountered. For they teach mankind that all abasement and downright animalism are natural phenomena." ^[raw/surah-103-al-asr.md]
