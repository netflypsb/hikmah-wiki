# الفَاتِحَة — Al-Fātiḥah (1)
## In the Shade of the Qur'an — Sayyid Qutb

> Al-Fātiḥah — 7 verses, Meccan

**Cross-reference:** [[quran-wiki/surah-001-al-fatihah|Quran Wiki: Al-Fatihah]] for Uthmani text, Ibn Kathir, and Muyassar tafsir.

---

### Verses

> *In the Name of God, the Merciful, the Beneficent.*

> **(1)** In the Name of God, the Merciful, the Beneficent.
> **(2)** Praise be to God, the Lord of all the worlds.
> **(3)** The Compassionate, the Merciful.
> **(4)** Master of the Day of Judgement.
> **(5)** You alone do we worship and to You alone do we turn for help.
> **(6)** Guide us on the straight path.
> **(7)** The path of those on whom You have bestowed Your favours, not those who have incurred Your wrath, nor those who have gone astray.

---

## Overview

Every Muslim recites this short *sūrah* of seven verses at least seventeen times a day. An authentic *ḥadīth* of the Prophet Muḥammad (peace be upon him), says: "A prayer by one who does not read the Fātiḥah is invalid."

This short *sūrah* contains a great deal of the central basic ideas of Islam, its beliefs and concepts. It outlines many of its essential perceptions and attitudes. All this makes it clear why it is chosen for frequent recitation and why it is essential for the validity of prayer. ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

The *sūrah* is recognised by the majority of scholars as the "seven oft-repeated verses" referenced in Qur'ān 15:87: "We have given you seven oft-repeated verses and this sublime Qur'ān."

## The Seven Fundamental Principles

Qutb's commentary identifies seven fundamental Islamic principles, each corresponding to one of the surah's seven verses:

### 1. The Basmalah — Invocation of God's Name (Verse 1)

> In the name of God, the Compassionate, the Merciful.

**First principle:** The invocation of God's name at the beginning of every action. This is established by the very first revelation: "Read in the name of your Lord..." (96: 1) and is in line with the principle that God is "the first and the last, the outward and the inward." (57: 3)

**The divine attributes *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Raḥīm*:** These encompass all aspects and dimensions of mercy. *al-Raḥmān* is exclusive to God; *al-Raḥīm* may be used for human beings. When combined, they encompass all aspects and dimensions of mercy. ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**Second principle:** The restriction of the use of *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Raḥīm* to God alone — defining the relationship between God and man. ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

### 2. Praise and Lordship (Verse 2)

> Praise be to God, the Lord of all the worlds.

**Third principle:** Praise of God at the beginning and at the end of every action. "He is God; there is no deity but He. To Him belongs praise in the first and in the last..." (28: 70) ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**The narration of `Umar ibn al-Khaṭṭāb:** The Prophet told how a man once said, "Lord, I praise You as befits the majesty of Your face and the greatness of Your power." The two angels accompanying that man could not evaluate the remark and referred to God Almighty, who commanded them: "Register it in his record as he said it, and I shall reward him as he deserves when he returns to Me." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**Lord of all the worlds:** The belief in absolute universal Godhead — God is the sole, absolute and ultimate owner with full independent authority to act in the whole cosmos. He has not created the world and abandoned it; He continues to be an active living authority over His creation. This living and dynamic relationship is "the perpetual fountain of life for all creation." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**The correction of polytheism:** People often acknowledged God as sole creator while believing in multiple deities with authority in their lives. The Qur'ān addresses this: "They only worship them [minor gods] so that they may bring us nearer to God." (39: 3) and "They have taken their rabbis and priests as gods alongside God." (9: 31) ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

### 3. Mercy as the Creator-Creation Link (Verse 3)

> The Compassionate, the Merciful.

These two attributes "define the link between the Lord as Creator and His creation. It is a link based entirely on love, peace, reassurance and care, and which inspires within man a spontaneous feeling of gratitude and praise towards God." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**Contrast with other traditions:** "Unlike Greek mythology or the Old Testament (Genesis, 4: 11–12), Islam does not depict God as an enemy who pursues man with relentless vengeance, or plots and schemes against His creation with spite and vindictiveness." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

### 4. Belief in the Hereafter (Verse 4)

> Master of the Day of Judgement.

**Fourth principle:** Belief in the hereafter. "Belief in the hereafter is essential because it engages the human soul and mind and concentrates man's attention on a future existence. This in turn helps to rein in man's obsession with the present life, and to transcend his immediate earthly desires." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

Qutb frames this as the distinction "between a well-balanced and conscientious humanity and an egotistic, self-seeking one." Human life cannot be balanced until people believe "that what they earn in this transient life is not the be-all and the end-all." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

### 5. Liberation Through Worship (Verse 5)

> You alone do we worship and to You alone do we turn for help.

**Fifth principle:** "This verse expresses another fundamental principle that follows logically from the preceding ones. It is also a decisive and crucial principle that draws a line between the freedom man gains in submitting to God and the abuse and debasement implicit in man's servitude to man." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**Total liberation:** "Once man has submitted himself to God and sought help and guidance from Him alone, he has achieved total liberation from the tyranny of all religious, intellectual, moral and political powers." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**Two categories of human power:** A rightly-guided power that recognizes God, and an arrogant, rebellious one that does not. A Muslim supports the former "no matter how weak or disadvantaged it may be" and rejects the latter "regardless of its strength or dominance." Qur'ān: "Many a small band, by the grace of God, has vanquished a large one." (2: 249) ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**The Muslim's relationship with nature:** One of "curiosity and friendliness rather than fear or hostility." The forces of nature are "by-products of God's own power, and subject to His will." The notion of "conquering nature" is "cynical and negative" and "alien to Islamic perceptions." Qur'ān: "He has all that is on the earth subservient to you." (45: 13) ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**The Prophet and Mount Uḥud:** "This is best expressed by the Prophet when he once faced Mount Uḥud, the scene of a bitter defeat for Muslims, and said, 'How we love this mountain, and how it loves us!' This expresses in a nutshell the affinity the Prophet felt towards nature even in its most rugged forms." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

### 6–7. Guidance and the Straight Path (Verses 6–7)

> Guide us on the straight path; the path of those on whom You have bestowed Your favours, not those who have incurred Your wrath, nor those who have gone astray.

**Sixth principle:** Guidance. "With these words a believer pleads to be shown the Right Way and to be helped along it, which cannot be achieved without God's guidance, care and mercy." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

**The nature of the straight path:** "one taken by those whom God has favoured, not the way of those who have earned His displeasure by their deviation from the Truth, nor that of the heedless who have no knowledge of the truth. It is the path of happiness and salvation." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

## The Ḥadīth of the Fruits of Prayer

The Prophet Muḥammad is reliably reported to have quoted God as saying that the fruits of prayer are shared equally between Me and My servant, and My servant will be granted what he asks for:

- As the worshipper recites: "Praise be to God, the Lord of all the worlds" → God says: 'My servant has praised Me.'
- "The Compassionate, the Merciful" → 'My servant has thanked Me.'
- "Master of the Day of Judgement" → 'My servant has glorified Me.'
- "You alone do we worship, and to You alone do we turn for help" → 'This is between Me and My servant, and My servant will receive what he asks for.'
- "Guide us on the straight path..." → 'This is for My servant, and he will be granted his wish.'

This *ḥadīth* explores some of the meanings of al-Fātiḥah and helps explain "why God has chosen this *sūrah* for recitation by Muslims at least seventeen times a day." ^[raw/surah-001-al-fatihah.md]

## Cross-Cutting Themes

This commentary develops several themes that recur across Fi Zilal's treatment of other surahs:

- **Tawhid as liberation** — the equation of God's oneness with human freedom; servitude to God alone as the only true liberation from servitude to man. (cf. Al-ʿAṣr's "worship of one God" quality)
- **Mercy as the Creator-creation relationship** — the divine attributes *al-Raḥmān* and *al-Raḥīm* as the defining link between God and His creation, contrasted with vengeful depictions in other traditions.
- **The hereafter as balance** — belief in the Day of Judgement as the principle that prevents human life from becoming "egotistic, self-seeking" and makes it "well-balanced and conscientious."
- **The Muslim and nature** — the friendly, constructive relationship with the natural world; the rejection of "conquering nature" as alien to Islamic perception.
- **Guidance as the practical means** — prayer and the request for guidance as the believer's active response to the fundamental principles.
