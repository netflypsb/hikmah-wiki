## Overview: The Final Passage of Al-Anfal

Verses 55–75 constitute the **final passage** of Surah Al-Anfal, a Medinan surah revealed in the context of the Battle of Badr (2 AH). Sayyid Qutb, in *Fi Zilal al-Qur'an*, titles this passage **"Delineation of Loyalties"** — and indeed, the entire passage is a systematic drawing of boundaries: between those who keep their covenants and those who break them, between peace and war, between the community of believers and the camp of disbelief, and ultimately between the inheritance claims of faith-brotherhood and those of blood kinship.

The passage can be divided into **six thematic sections**:

| Section | Verses | Theme |
|---------|--------|-------|
| I | 55–57 | The worst of creatures: covenant-breakers |
| II | 58–60 | Treaty ethics: treachery, deterrence, and preparedness |
| III | 61–63 | Peace, trust in Allah, and the unity of believers' hearts |
| IV | 64–66 | Divine sufficiency and the ratio of steadfastness |
| V | 67–71 | Captives of war: the Badr precedent |
| VI | 72–75 | The demarcation of loyalties and inheritance |

## Section I: The Worst of Creatures — Covenant-Breakers (Verses 55–57)

### Arabic Text

> **8:55** إِنَّ شَرَّ ٱلدَّوَآبِّ عِندَ ٱللَّهِ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ فَهُمْ لَا يُؤْمِنُونَ
>
> **8:56** ٱلَّذِينَ عَـٰهَدتَّ مِنْهُمْ ثُمَّ يَنقُضُونَ عَهْدَهُمْ فِي كُلِّ مَرَّةٍ وَهُمْ لَا يَتَّقُونَ
>
> **8:57** فَإِمَّا تَثْقَفَنَّهُمْ فِي ٱلْحَرْبِ فَشَرِّدْ بِهِم مَّنْ خَلْفَهُمْ لَعَلَّهُمْ يَذَّكِّرُونَ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **55** Indeed, the worst of living creatures in the sight of Allah are those who have disbelieved, and they will not [ever] believe —
>
> **56** The ones with whom you made a treaty but then they break their pledge every time, and they do not fear Allah.
>
> **57** So if you, [O Muhammad], gain dominance over them in war, disperse by [means of] them those behind them that perhaps they will be reminded.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** explains that Allah declares the "worst of moving creatures" (شَرَّ ٱلدَّوَآبِّ) on the face of the earth to be the disbelievers who persistently reject faith and break every covenant they make. The term *dawabb* (دَوَابّ) — literally "walking creatures" — is deliberately used to strip covenant-breakers of their human dignity: they behave like animals, governed by no moral law, fearing no consequence. Ibn Kathir reports that the verse applies to groups such as the Jewish tribes of Qaynuqa`, al-Nadir, and Qurayzah, as well as Bedouin Arabs around Madinah, all of whom broke treaties with the Prophet ﷺ.

The command in verse 57 — "disperse by means of them those behind them" (فَشَرِّدْ بِهِم مَّنْ خَلْفَهُمْ) — means to punish treaty-breakers so severely that it serves as a deterrent to others contemplating similar treachery. As-Suddi comments: "They might be careful not to break treaties, so that they do not meet the same end."

**Tafsir al-Muyassar** states:
> "Indeed, the worst of creatures that walk upon the earth in Allah's sight are the disbelievers who persist in their disbelief — they do not believe Allah's messengers, do not affirm His oneness, and do not follow His law."

**Fi Zilal al-Qur'an (Sayyid Qutb)** adds a profound dimension: the Arabic term *dawabb* connotes "those that walk on earth," making the description "the worst of all animals that walk on earth." These are humans who have shed two distinctively human qualities: **faith** (they "will not believe") and **faithfulness** (they "break their treaty at every occasion"). They run loose like animals — except that animals do not make covenants and therefore cannot be guilty of treachery. The covenant-breaker, then, sinks below the animal: he possesses the capacity for moral commitment but deliberately violates it.

### Cross-Reference

The description of disbelievers as the "worst of creatures" appears in a parallel passage earlier in the same surah:

> **8:22** — "Indeed, the worst of living creatures in the sight of Allah are the deaf and dumb who do not use reason."

The repetition reinforces the surah's central moral framework: disbelief is not merely an intellectual error but a degradation of human nature itself.

## Section II: Treaty Ethics — Treachery, Deterrence, and Preparedness (Verses 58–60)

### Arabic Text

> **8:58** وَإِمَّا تَخَافَنَّ مِن قَوْمٍ خِيَانَةً فَٱنۢبِذْ إِلَيْهِمْ عَلَىٰ سَوَآءٍۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ لَا يُحِبُّ ٱلْخَآىِٕنِينَ
>
> **8:59** وَلَا يَحْسَبَنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ سَبَقُوٓا۟ۚ إِنَّهُمْ لَا يُعْجِزُونَ
>
> **8:60** وَأَعِدُّوا۟ لَهُم مَّا ٱسْتَطَعْتُم مِّن قُوَّةٍ وَمِن رِّبَاطِ ٱلْخَيْلِ تُرْهِبُونَ بِهِۦ عَدُوَّ ٱللَّهِ وَعَدُوَّكُمْ وَءَاخَرِينَ مِن دُونِهِمْ لَا تَعْلَمُونَهُمُ ٱللَّهُ يَعْلَمُهُمْۚ وَمَا تُنفِقُوا۟ مِن شَيْءٍ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ يُوَفَّ إِلَيْكُمْ وَأَنتُمْ لَا تُظْلَمُونَ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **58** If you [have reason to] fear from a people betrayal, throw [their treaty] back to them, [putting you] on equal terms. Indeed, Allah does not like traitors.
>
> **59** And let not those who disbelieve think they will escape. Indeed, they will not cause failure [to Allah].
>
> **60** And prepare against them whatever you are able of power and of steeds of war by which you may terrify the enemy of Allah and your enemy and others besides them whom you do not know [but] whom Allah knows. And whatever you spend in the cause of Allah will be fully repaid to you, and you will not be wronged.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** outlines two distinct scenarios in these verses:

1. **Actual treaty violation** (vv. 55–57): dealt with by punitive military action.
2. **Fear of treachery** (v. 58): when there are clear signs that a party intends to betray a treaty, the Muslims must **openly declare** the termination of the treaty — "on equal terms" (عَلَىٰ سَوَآءٍ) — so that both sides know a state of war exists. Islam forbids **treacherous surprise attacks** on a treaty partner. The treaty must be formally and publicly repudiated before any military action.

Ibn Kathir narrates a **hadith from Imam Ahmad** in which Mu`awiyah was leading an army in Roman lands while a bilateral peace treaty was still valid. A man approached Mu`awiyah and said: "I heard the Messenger of Allah ﷺ say: 'Whoever has a treaty with a people must neither break it nor untie it until its term expires, or declare its termination to them on equal terms.'" Mu`awiyah and the people listened and returned home. This hadith, recorded by Ahmad and Abu Dawud, establishes the Islamic legal principle: **no secret betrayal of treaties; only open and fair termination**.

Verse 59 refutes the disbelievers' delusion that they can "outstrip" Allah's plan. Ibn Kathir cross-references:
- **29:4** — "Or think those who do evil deeds that they can outstrip Us? Evil is that which they judge!"
- **24:57** — "Consider not that the disbelievers can escape in the land. Their abode shall be the Fire."
- **3:196–197** — "Let not the free disposal of the disbelievers throughout the land deceive you. A brief enjoyment; then their ultimate abode is Hell."

Verse 60 commands military preparedness. Ibn Kathir narrates several hadith on this verse:

**Hadith 1** (Imam Ahmad, Muslim — `Uqbah bin `Amir):
> The Prophet ﷺ said on the minbar: "And make ready against them all you can of power — Verily, **power is shooting** (الرَّمْيُ), power is shooting!" (أَلَا إِنَّ الْقُوَّةَ الرَّمْيُ)

**Hadith 2** (Imam Malik, Al-Bukhari, Muslim — Abu Hurayrah):
> The Prophet ﷺ said: "Horses are kept for three purposes: for some they are a source of reward (for jihad), for some a means of shelter (self-sufficiency), and for some a source of sin (pride and enmity)."

**Hadith 3** (Imam Ahmad — `Abdullah bin Mas`ud):
> The Prophet ﷺ said: "Horses are of three types: a horse of the Most Merciful (kept for Allah's cause), a horse of Satan (used for gambling), and a horse of man (for livelihood)."

**Hadith 4** (Al-Bukhari, Muslim — `Uqbah bin `Amir):
> The Prophet ﷺ said: "Good is tied to the forelocks of horses until the Day of Resurrection — reward and spoils."

**Tafsir al-Muyassar** on verse 60:
> "Prepare, O Muslims, to face your enemies with all the strength and equipment you can muster, so that you may strike terror into the enemies of Allah and your enemies who watch you, and frighten others whose enmity you do not yet see but Allah knows them. Whatever you spend in Allah's cause, little or much, Allah will repay you in full and reserve your reward until the Day of Judgment."

**Fi Zilal** emphasizes that Islam's need for military power is not for aggression but for four purposes: (1) to secure peace for those who accept Islam; (2) to deter aggression against the land of Islam; (3) to prevent enemies from obstructing the propagation of the faith; and (4) to break any force that claims divine sovereignty for anyone other than Allah. Sayyid Qutb stresses: "No Muslim should ever mince words when he declares this basic truth. There is nothing to be ashamed of in the nature of the Islamic approach."

## Section III: Peace, Trust, and the Unity of Hearts (Verses 61–63)

### Arabic Text

> **8:61** ۞ وَإِن جَنَحُوا۟ لِلسَّلْمِ فَٱجْنَحْ لَهَا وَتَوَكَّلْ عَلَى ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّهُۥ هُوَ ٱلسَّمِيعُ ٱلْعَلِيمُ
>
> **8:62** وَإِن يُرِيدُوٓا۟ أَن يَخْدَعُوكَ فَإِنَّ حَسْبَكَ ٱللَّهُۚ هُوَ ٱلَّذِيٓ أَيَّدَكَ بِنَصْرِهِۦ وَبِٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ
>
> **8:63** وَأَلَّفَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِهِمْۚ لَوْ أَنفَقْتَ مَا فِي ٱلْأَرْضِ جَمِيعًا مَّآ أَلَّفْتَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِهِمْ وَلَـٰكِنَّ ٱللَّهَ أَلَّفَ بَيْنَهُمْۚ إِنَّهُۥ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **61** And if they incline to peace, then incline to it [also] and rely upon Allah. Indeed, it is He who is the Hearing, the Knowing.
>
> **62** But if they intend to deceive you — then sufficient for you is Allah. It is He who supported you with His help and with the believers
>
> **63** And brought together their hearts. If you had spent all that is in the earth, you could not have brought their hearts together; but Allah brought them together. Indeed, He is Exalted in Might and Wise.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** notes that verse 61 establishes the third ruling: if the enemy genuinely inclines to peace, the Muslims must reciprocate. The word *janah* (جَنَحُ) connotes a gentle leaning, an inclination. The instruction is coupled with *tawakkul* — trust in Allah — because Allah hears their words and knows their intentions.

**Hadith** (Imam Ahmad — `Ali bin Abi Talib):
> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "There will be disputes after me, so if you have a way to end them in peace, then do so."
> (إِنَّهُ سَيَكُونُ بَعْدِي اخْتِلَافٌ أَوْ أَمْرٌ فَإِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ أَنْ يَكُونَ السِّلْمَ فَافْعَل)

**Hadith** (Al-Bukhari, Muslim — the Ansar):
> The Prophet ﷺ said to the Ansar: "Did I not find you astray and Allah guided you through me? Did I not find you poor and Allah enriched you through me? Did I not find you disunited and Allah united you through me?"
> (يَا مَعْشَرَ الْأَنْصَارِ أَلَمْ أَجِدْكُمْ ضُلَّالًا فَهَدَاكُمُ اللهُ بِي، وَعَالَةً فَأَغْنَاكُمُ اللهُ بِي، وَكُنْتُمْ مُتَفَرِّقِينَ فَأَلَّفَكُمُ اللهُ بِي)

Verse 62–63 addresses the fear of deception: even if the peace offer is a cover for treachery, Allah is sufficient. It was He who strengthened the Prophet with divine help and the believers, and it was He who **united their hearts** (أَلَّفَ بَيْنَ قُلُوبِهِمْ). Ibn Kathir stresses: "If you were to spend all that is on earth, you could not have so united their hearts, but Allah united them." This refers to the Aws and Khazraj tribes of Madinah, whose pre-Islamic enmity was transformed into brotherhood through faith — a feat impossible through any human means.

**Fi Zilal** underscores that the bond of faith is qualitatively different from all other bonds — blood, race, land, language, or economic interest. These latter ties are shared with animals; faith alone appeals to the highest human qualities. The verse affirms that the unity of the Muslim community is not manufactured by political maneuvering or financial incentive but is a **divine act** — a direct intervention by Allah in the hearts of believers.

### Cross-Reference

- **3:103** — "And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together and do not be divided. And remember the favor of Allah upon you — when you were enemies and He brought your hearts together and you became, by His favor, brothers."
- **59:9** — Those who "love those who emigrate to them and have no jealousy in their breasts for that which they have been given."

## Section IV: Divine Sufficiency and the Ratio of Steadfastness (Verses 64–66)

### Arabic Text

> **8:64** يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِيُّ حَسْبُكَ ٱللَّهُ وَمَنِ ٱتَّبَعَكَ مِنَ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ
>
> **8:65** يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِيُّ حَرِّضِ ٱلْمُؤْمِنِينَ عَلَى ٱلْقِتَالِۚ إِن يَكُن مِّنكُمْ عِشْرُونَ صَـٰبِرُونَ يَغْلِبُوا۟ مِا۟ئَتَيْنِۚ وَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُم مِّا۟ئَةٌ يَغْلِبُوٓا۟ أَلْفًا مِّنَ ٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ بِأَنَّهُمْ قَوْمٌ لَّا يَفْقَهُونَ
>
> **8:66** ٱلْـَٔـٰنَ خَفَّفَ ٱللَّهُ عَنكُمْ وَعَلِمَ أَنَّ فِيكُمْ ضَعْفًاۚ فَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُم مِّا۟ئَةٌ صَابِرَةٌ يَغْلِبُوا۟ مِا۟ئَتَيْنِۚ وَإِن يَكُن مِّنكُمْ أَلْفٌ يَغْلِبُوٓا۟ أَلْفَيْنِ بِإِذْنِ ٱللَّهِۗ وَٱللَّهُ مَعَ ٱلصَّـٰبِرِينَ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **64** O Prophet, sufficient for you is Allah and for whoever follows you of the believers.
>
> **65** O Prophet, urge the believers to battle. If there are among you twenty [who are] steadfast, they will overcome two hundred. And if there are among you one hundred [who are] steadfast, they will overcome a thousand of those who have disbelieved because they are a people who do not understand.
>
> **66** Now, Allah has lightened [the hardship] for you, and He knows that among you is weakness. So if there are from you one hundred [who are] steadfast, they will overcome two hundred. And if there are among you a thousand, they will overcome two thousand by permission of Allah. And Allah is with the steadfast.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** explains the progression: verse 64 declares Allah's absolute sufficiency — "Allah is enough for you and those who follow you." Verse 65 then sets the initial ratio: **1:10** — twenty steadfast believers can overcome two hundred, and one hundred can overcome a thousand. The disbelievers are "a people who do not understand" (قَوْمٌ لَّا يَفْقَهُونَ) because they fight for worldly gain, not for a transcendent cause.

Verse 66 introduces a **concession** (rukhsah): Allah lightens the burden from 1:10 to **1:2**, knowing the believers' weakness. Ibn Kathir reports from Ibn `Abbas: "When this verse (65) was revealed, it was difficult for the Muslims, for they thought it was burdensome since twenty would have to face two hundred." So Allah revealed verse 66 as a mercy.

**Tafsir al-Muyassar** on verse 66:
> "Now Allah has lightened your burden, O believers, because of the weakness among you. If there are one hundred steadfast among you, they will overcome two hundred of the disbelievers. And if there are a thousand of you, they will overcome two thousand by Allah's permission. And Allah is with the steadfast, with His support and victory."

**Fi Zilal** reflects on the contrast between God's invincible power and the small human force: "The difference is too huge to allow any meaningful comparison. Hence, the outcome of the battle is a foregone conclusion." The reduction from 1:10 to 1:2 is not a retreat but a recognition of human limitation — steadfastness (*sabr*) remains the decisive factor, and Allah is "with the steadfast."

### Asbab al-Nuzul (Occasion of Revelation)

The curated Quran Wiki records that verses 65–66 are connected to the asbab al-nuzul entry: **"Allah Has Lightened Your Burden"** — the initial ratio of 1:10 was found too demanding and Allah condescended to 1:2, demonstrating divine mercy even in the context of military obligation.

## Section V: Captives of War — The Badr Precedent (Verses 67–71)

### Arabic Text

> **8:67** مَا كَانَ لِنَبِيٍّ أَن يَكُونَ لَهُۥٓ أَسْرَىٰ حَتَّىٰ يُثْخِنَ فِي ٱلْأَرْضِۚ تُرِيدُونَ عَرَضَ ٱلدُّنْيَا وَٱللَّهُ يُرِيدُ ٱلْـَٔاخِرَةَۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَزِيزٌ حَكِيمٌ
>
> **8:68** لَّوْلَا كِتَـٰبٌ مِّنَ ٱللَّهِ سَبَقَ لَمَسَّكُمْ فِيمَآ أَخَذْتُمْ عَذَابٌ عَظِيمٌ
>
> **8:69** فَكُلُوا۟ مِمَّا غَنِمْتُمْ حَلَـٰلًا طَيِّبًاۚ وَٱتَّقُوا۟ ٱللَّهَۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ
>
> **8:70** يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّبِيُّ قُل لِّمَن فِيٓ أَيْدِيكُم مِّنَ ٱلْأَسْرَىٰٓ إِن يَعْلَمِ ٱللَّهُ فِي قُلُوبِكُمْ خَيْرًا يُؤْتِكُمْ خَيْرًا مِّمَّآ أُخِذَ مِنكُمْ وَيَغْفِرْ لَكُمْۚ وَٱللَّهُ غَفُورٌ رَّحِيمٌ
>
> **8:71** وَإِن يُرِيدُوا۟ خِيَانَتَكَ فَقَدْ خَانُوا۟ ٱللَّهَ مِن قَبْلُ فَأَمْكَنَ مِنْهُمْۗ وَٱللَّهُ عَلِيمٌ حَكِيمٌ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **67** It is not for a prophet to have captives [of war] until he inflicts a massacre [upon Allah's enemies] in the land. Some Muslims desire the commodities of this world, but Allah desires [for you] the Hereafter. And Allah is Exalted in Might and Wise.
>
> **68** If not for a decree from Allah that preceded, you would have been touched for what you took by a great punishment.
>
> **69** So consume what you have taken of war booty [as being] lawful and good, and fear Allah. Indeed, Allah is Forgiving and Merciful.
>
> **70** O Prophet, say to whoever is in your hands of the captives, "If Allah knows [any] good in your hearts, He will give you [something] better than what was taken from you, and He will forgive you; and Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."
>
> **71** But if they intend to betray you — then they have already betrayed Allah before, and He empowered [you] over them. And Allah is Knowing and Wise.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** provides extensive historical context from the **Battle of Badr**:

After Badr, the Prophet ﷺ consulted his Companions about what to do with the captives. Three opinions emerged:
- **Abu Bakr**: "They are your people and tribesmen. Spare them and offer them the chance to repent." (Mercy)
- **`Umar**: "They accused you of lying and drove you from your city. Put them to death." (Severity)
- **`Abdullah ibn Rawahah**: "Light a fire and throw them into it." (Harshness)

The Prophet ﷺ went to his quarters, then returned and said:
> "God makes some people's hearts so soft that they become softer than milk, and He makes others' hearts harden until they become harder than bricks. Abu Bakr, your attitude is like Abraham's: 'He that follows me belongs to me. As for him who disobeys me, well, You are surely most Forgiving, Merciful' (14:36). And like Jesus: 'If You punish them, they are Your servants; and if You forgive them, You are indeed Almighty, Wise' (5:118). As for you `Umar, you are like Moses: 'Our Lord, wipe out their wealth and firm up their hearts so they would not believe until they see painful torment' (10:88). And like Noah: 'My Lord, do not leave on earth a single unbeliever' (71:26). You are in poverty, so no one may be set free without a ransom, or else, he should be beheaded."

The Prophet ﷺ initially accepted the ransom, but verse 67 was revealed as a mild reproach: a prophet should not take captives for ransom until he has thoroughly subdued the enemy in the land. The believers "desired the commodities of this world" (عَرَضَ ٱلدُّنْيَا), while Allah desired the Hereafter.

**Hadith** (Imam Ahmad — Anas):
> The Prophet ﷺ said: "Allah has made you prevail above them." `Umar said: "O Allah's Messenger! Cut off their necks." The Prophet turned away. He asked again: "O people! Allah has made you prevail above them, and they are your brothers of yesterday."

**Hadith** (Abu Hurayrah):
> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "War booty was never allowed for any among mankind except us."
> (لَمْ تَحِلَّ الْغَنَائِمُ لِسُودِ الرُّؤُوسِ غَيْرَنَا)

Verse 68 reveals that the prior decree of Allah — which had already permitted spoils and ransoming — was what saved the Muslims from severe punishment. Verse 69 then makes the booty **lawful and good** (حَلَـٰلًا طَيِّبًا), as long as the believers remain God-fearing.

Verses 70–71 address the captives directly: if Allah finds **goodness** (خَيْر) in their hearts — i.e., faith — He will give them something better than the ransom taken from them. Ibn Kathir narrates that this promise was fulfilled for **Al-`Abbas** (the Prophet's uncle), who accepted Islam after Badr and received far more than the ransom he paid.

**Tafsir al-Muyassar** on verse 70:
> "Say to the captives of Badr: Do not grieve over the ransom taken from you. If Allah finds goodness in your hearts, He will give you something better from His bounty — and He did fulfill this promise to Al-`Abbas and others — and He will forgive your sins."

**Fi Zilal** emphasizes that the Islamic approach to captives is fundamentally different from that of other conquerors: "No vengeance is to be exacted on prisoners of war. Such was the practice of the Byzantines and other conquerors. But Islam follows a totally different approach." The captive is invited to faith, which is "goodness at its purest." The purpose of holding captives is to open their hearts to the light of Islam — not to humiliate or destroy them.

## Section VI: The Demarcation of Loyalties and Inheritance (Verses 72–75)

### Arabic Text

> **8:72** إِنَّ ٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَهَاجَرُوا۟ وَجَـٰهَدُوا۟ بِأَمْوَ ٰ⁠لِهِمْ وَأَنفُسِهِمْ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَاوَوا۟ وَّنَصَرُوٓا۟ أُو۟لَـٰٓىِٕكَ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍۚ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَلَمْ يُهَاجِرُوا۟ مَا لَكُم مِّن وَلَـٰيَتِهِم مِّن شَيْءٍ حَتَّىٰ يُهَاجِرُوا۟ۚ وَإِنِ ٱسْتَنصَرُوكُمْ فِي ٱلدِّينِ فَعَلَيْكُمُ ٱلنَّصْرُ إِلَّا عَلَىٰ قَوْمِۭ بَيْنَكُمْ وَبَيْنَهُم مِّيثَـٰقٌۗ وَٱللَّهُ بِمَا تَعْمَلُونَ بَصِيرٌ
>
> **8:73** وَٱلَّذِينَ كَفَرُوا۟ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍۚ إِلَّا تَفْعَلُوهُ تَكُن فِتْنَةٌ فِي ٱلْأَرْضِ وَفَسَادٌ كَبِيرٌ
>
> **8:74** وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ وَهَاجَرُوا۟ وَجَـٰهَدُوا۟ فِي سَبِيلِ ٱللَّهِ وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَاوَوا۟ وَّنَصَرُوٓا۟ أُو۟لَـٰٓىِٕكَ هُمُ ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ حَقًّاۚ لَّهُم مَّغْفِرَةٌ وَرِزْقٌ كَرِيمٌ
>
> **8:75** وَٱلَّذِينَ ءَامَنُوا۟ مِنۢ بَعْدُ وَهَاجَرُوا۟ وَجَـٰهَدُوا۟ مَعَكُمْ فَأُو۟لَـٰٓىِٕكَ مِنكُمْۚ وَأُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَرْحَامِ بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلَىٰ بِبَعْضٍ فِي كِتَـٰبِ ٱللَّهِۚ إِنَّ ٱللَّهَ بِكُلِّ شَيْءٍ عَلِيمُۢ

### Translation (Sahih International)

> **72** Indeed, those who have believed and emigrated and fought with their wealth and lives in the cause of Allah and those who gave shelter and aided — they are allies of one another. But those who believed and did not emigrate — for you there is no guardianship of them until they emigrate. And if they seek help of you for the religion, then you must help, except against a people between yourselves and whom is a treaty. And Allah is Seeing of what you do.
>
> **73** And those who disbelieved are allies of one another. If you do not do so, there will be fitnah on earth and great corruption.
>
> **74** But those who have believed and emigrated and fought in the cause of Allah and those who gave shelter and aided — it is they who are the believers, truly. For them is forgiveness and noble provision.
>
> **75** And those who believed after [the initial emigration] and emigrated and fought with you — they are of you. But those of [blood] relationship are more entitled [to inheritance] in the decree of Allah. Indeed, Allah is Knowing of all things.

### Tafsir

**Ibn Kathir** divides the believers into three categories:

1. **The Muhajirun** — those who left their homes, wealth, and families in Makkah to emigrate to Madinah, sacrificing everything for the sake of Allah and His Messenger.
2. **The Ansar** — the Muslims of Madinah who gave asylum, shelter, and wealth to the Muhajirun, and fought alongside them.
3. **Believers who did not emigrate** — those who believed but remained in their lands among the disbelievers. The Muslims owe them **no duty of protection** (وَلَـٰيَة) until they emigrate. However, if they appeal for help on grounds of faith, the Muslims must help — **except against a people with whom the Muslims have a treaty**.

Verse 73 declares the disbelievers to be "allies of one another" (بَعْضُهُمْ أَوْلِيَآءُ بَعْضٍ), and warns: if the Muslims do not organize themselves as a mutual support network, there will be **fitnah** (فِتْنَة) — trial, discord, and persecution — and **great corruption** (فَسَادٌ كَبِير) on earth.

**Hadith** (Al-Hakim — Usamah bin Zayd):
> The Prophet ﷺ said: "No followers of two religions inherit from each other. Therefore, neither a Muslim inherits from a disbeliever nor a disbeliever from a Muslim."
> (لَا يَتَوَارَثُ أَهْلُ مِلَّتَيْنِ، وَلَا يَرِثُ مُسْلِمٌ كَافِرًا، وَلَا كَافِرٌ مُسْلِمًا)
> Al-Hakim said: "Its chain is Sahih."

This hadith is also recorded in **the Two Sahihs** (Bukhari and Muslim):
> "Neither a Muslim inherits from a disbeliever nor a disbeliever inherits from a Muslim."
> (لَا يَرِثُ الْمُسْلِمُ الْكَافِرَ وَلَا الْكَافِرُ الْمُسْلِم)

**Hadith** (Imam Ahmad — Jarir bin `Abdullah Al-Bajali):
> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "The Muhajirun and Al-Ansar are the supporters of each other, while the Tulaqa' of Quraysh (whom the Prophet set free after conquering Makkah) and `Utaqa' from Thaqif (freed after Hunayn) are supporters of each other until the Day of Resurrection."

**Hadith** (Imam Ahmad — Buraydah bin Al-Hasib):
> When the Messenger of Allah ﷺ would send a commander with an army, he would advise him: "Fight in the name of Allah, in the cause of Allah. When you meet your enemy of the polytheists, call them to one of three options: accept Islam, or pay jizyah, or fight."

Verse 74 reaffirms the faith of the Muhajirun and Ansar as **true faith** (ٱلْمُؤْمِنُونَ حَقًّا), promising them **forgiveness** and **noble provision** (رِزْقٌ كَرِيم) in the Hereafter.

**Hadith** (Two Sahihs — Mutawatir):
> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "One will be in the company of those whom he loves."
> (الْمَرْءُ مَعَ مَنْ أَحَب)
>
> Another narration: "He who loves a people is one of them."
> (مَنْ أَحَبَّ قَوْمًا فَهُوَ مِنْهُم)
>
> And in another: "...will be gathered with them."
> (حُشِرَ مَعَهُم)

Verse 75 extends the community to include **later believers** who emigrate and strive alongside the original community — "they are of you" (فَأُو۟لَـٰٓىِٕكَ مِنكُمْ). The final ruling: **blood relatives** (أُو۟لُوا۟ ٱلْأَرْحَامِ) have priority in inheritance, as ordained by Allah. This abrogated the earlier practice in which Muhajirun and Ansar inherited from each other through the bond of faith-brotherhood.

**Hadith** (Abu Dawud, Tirmidhi):
> The Messenger of Allah ﷺ said: "Indeed Allah has alloted every right to the one who deserves it, so there may be no will for an heir."
> (إِنَّ اللهَ قَدْ أَعْطَى كُلَّ ذِي حَقَ حَقَّهُ فَلَا وَصِيَّةٍ لِوَارِث)

### Fi Zilal: The Nature of Islamic Society

Sayyid Qutb closes his commentary on this passage with a profound reflection on the nature of Islamic society. The essential tie that binds the Muslim community is **not** blood, race, homeland, language, or economic interest — it is **faith** (*iman*). These other ties are shared with animals; faith alone appeals to the highest human qualities. The practical result was a civilization that was not "Arab" but "Islamic" — open and all-inclusive:

> "In this unique Islamic society Arabs, Persians, Syrians, Egyptians, Moroccans, Turks, Chinese, Indians, Romans, Greeks, Indonesians, Africans and people of other nations and races were gathered together. Their various characteristics were united, and with mutual cooperation, harmony and unity they took part in the construction of the Islamic community and the Islamic culture."

The final verse — assigning inheritance to blood relations — does not negate the primacy of faith; it simply restores a natural order within the community of faith. The bond of faith remains supreme in the social and political sphere; the bond of blood governs inheritance and family obligation. Both are recognized, each in its proper place.

## Summary of Key Themes

### 1. Covenant and Treachery (vv. 55–58)
The worst creatures before Allah are not merely disbelievers but **covenant-breakers** — those who combine disbelief with habitual treaty violation. Islam requires transparent treaty ethics: no secret betrayal, only open declaration of termination. The command to punish treaty-breakers severely is not vengeance but **deterrence** — to protect the peace from those who would exploit it.

### 2. Military Preparedness and Deterrence (vv. 59–60)
Muslims must maintain a position of strength — not for aggression but to secure peace, protect the faith's propagation, and deter hostile forces. The hadith emphasizes that "power is shooting" (الرَّمْيُ) and that preparedness is a communal obligation. Spending in Allah's cause is guaranteed full repayment.

### 3. Peace and Trust in Allah (vv. 61–63)
Islam inclines to peace when the enemy genuinely inclines to peace. Even if the enemy intends deception, Allah is sufficient. The unity of the believers' hearts is a divine miracle — not manufactured by politics or wealth but directly wrought by Allah.

### 4. Divine Sufficiency and Human Weakness (vv. 64–66)
Allah is sufficient for the Prophet and the believers. The initial 1:10 ratio was reduced to 1:2 as a concession to human weakness. Steadfastness (*sabr*) — not numbers — is the decisive factor, and Allah is "with the steadfast."

### 5. Captives and the Ethics of War (vv. 67–71)
The Battle of Badr established the precedent: captives are not to be slaughtered or humiliated but are to be **invited to faith**. The ransom of captives was initially criticized but then made lawful by divine decree. If a captive finds faith, Allah promises something better than what was taken from them — fulfilled in the case of Al-`Abbas and others.

### 6. The Architecture of the Ummah (vv. 72–75)
The Muslim community is defined by three categories: Muhajirun (emigrants), Ansar (helpers), and later believers. The bond of faith replaces tribal and blood bonds as the primary social tie, while blood relations retain their role in inheritance. Disbelievers are warned that they too form alliances — and if the Muslims do not organize their own mutual support, **fitnah** and **corruption** will spread on earth. The passage ends with the promise of forgiveness and noble provision for the true believers, and the extension of the community to all who join it in the future.
